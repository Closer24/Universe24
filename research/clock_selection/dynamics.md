# Clock-written state, later probes and recurrence

This note proposes two bounded mechanisms under the existing model. It changes
no dynamics or acceptance rule. The baseline is main commit
`ff89e2ff661c8b60af84aff738f9040ad7f15bcc`, tree
`a8c6add7a4c2c1ec2d9791cdbaa297dd375e944c`.

## Candidate 1: write a phase, then interrogate it

Use the prepared-clock cycle

`C = ((0,1), (1,0), (0,-1), (-1,0))`.

The existing phase study supports, within its finite registered domain, a
post-encounter target phase offset `p_written = -d_write (mod 4)`. The separately
validated transmission component with center remainder zero enables a complete
outgoing carrier when its prepared phase and packet delay satisfy
`p + d_probe = 2 (mod 4)`.

Composing those statements gives a **candidate prediction**, not a proved
two-input device:

`d_probe - d_write = 2 (mod 4)` may enable the later probe;

`d_probe - d_write = 0 (mod 4)` may suppress its exact carrier template.

Both sources can be prepared before the first update. The meaningful new
question is whether the writer's interaction changes the later probe's output
under identical apparatus and probe preparation. Merely observing a later
prepared packet is not a computed acknowledgment. All four writer/probe source
controls must preserve the initial remainder field, and all arithmetic events
must remain recorded.

For a strong enabling comparison, choose initial phase `p = -d_probe (mod 4)`
and gap two modulo four. For suppression, choose `p = 2-d_probe (mod 4)` and gap
zero modulo four. Both settings make the initial writer phase favorable for
transmission. Its early output therefore must remain visible and must be
distinguished from the later probe by a prospectively fixed space-time port.
These settings do not support a claim of no output before approval.

Initial offsets satisfying `p+d_probe = 1 or 3 (mod 4)` are exploratory options
for seeking suppression of both singleton outputs. Their behavior cannot be
inherited from the two validated transmission families.

## Existing-record interface diagnostic

A read-only post-analysis compared the complete 336-channel target region at
state 32 in all 64 joint phase-study recordings to the canonical prepared
clock/guide interface with that observed center phase. Every counter and
remainder matched: 32 cases at center remainder zero and 32 at remainder four,
with zero differing triples. No trajectory was generated by this diagnostic.

The comparison used `region_addresses` and `region_trace[32]` in the old records
and `assemble(interactions_phase.case_spec(...)).initial`, with the canonical
case's phase set to `C.index(observed_center[:2])` and control set to `01`.
This compares an existing producer's canonical preparation against recorded
observations; it is not an independent proof or a new acceptance test.

The last nonzero external term entering the target region in those records
occurred at updates `delay+17` through `delay+21`. The latest designated-center
C occurred at `delay+18`; some aligned cases had no such C. These are finite
diagnostics, not universal response-time bounds.

Local matching removes one possible interface mismatch. It does not remove
outgoing information from the full board. A later probe can meet the writer's
wake before reaching the clock, and distant activity can return. New evidence
must use an uninterrupted two-packet run with all those states retained. In
particular, never replace the post-write board with a canonical local patch.
The transmission evidence used remainder zero; the remainder-four comparison
alone does not extend that component's validated domain.

## Candidate 2: a periodic guide supplies a returning probe

For a pristine exact carrier on its periodic guide,
`S_t = T_x^t S_0`, so the complete carrier state returns after `L` updates.
Putting a clock on that guide suggests a recurrence test. Conditional on the
same carrier surviving and the entire interface being ready, a returning
packet sees relative phase `L (mod 4)`. The simple phase ansatz therefore
suggests enabling for `L=2 (mod 4)` and suppression for `L=0 (mod 4)`.

An interaction can also emit other disturbances. Those disturbances wrap too,
and may invalidate the ansatz before the returning carrier arrives. A periodic
boundary experiment must explicitly retain and test them. Carrier-only and
clock-only controls are necessary. Such an experiment would test an internally
returning probe; it would not establish bulk propagation, physical light,
complexity preference or a one-input splitter. No recurrence run is claimed by
this note.

## Limits of the evolution metaphor

On any finite invariant set of complete states, an invertible update permutes
the states, and every orbit is periodic. A scalar score that is nondecreasing
at every step of a periodic orbit must be constant on that orbit. It cannot
strictly improve forever or yield a dissipative full-state attractor there.

This is not a general impossibility theorem for Universe24: its integer
counters are unbounded. Local transient selection, useful coarse observations
and information carried into other channels remain possible. A suppressed
carrier template does not imply erased alternatives or an empty output region.

The acceptance target should therefore specify finite, internally conditioned
candidate discrimination and a recorded consequence. A passive complexity
score may evaluate a resulting preference, but may not choose, delete or
restart a candidate. Phase compatibility by itself is not a demonstrated
preference for greater complexity.
