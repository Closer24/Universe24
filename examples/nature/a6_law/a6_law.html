1. The clock of a thing at rest

Each clock is a light thing of content 1 launched between two mirror bodies one Link aside of a half-axis at r from the mass, read at its centre every other interval; the rate is the intervals it moved over the intervals it stayed between its mirrors, and a transverse push turns it out. What came out is a step, not a slope: inside r = 5 at X = 256 (r = 3 at X = 64) the clock is frozen, reading one to four whole quanta in every interval it stays; from there outward it reads two or three quanta of the transient's tail and is turned out of the open cavity; at r = 8 and beyond it reads nothing. The two readings of the wait differ nowhere a clock is frozen and nowhere it is sparse, and by a third of the waits at the one cavity that read tens of quanta over hundreds of intervals. At the GR w every whole quantum read costs 87 intervals.
