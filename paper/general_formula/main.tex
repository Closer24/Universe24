From it follow, in lattice form, the Klein-Gordon equation with Schr\"odinger's slow limit, Planck's relation in the band's form and, under one assumption fixed by two measurements, the exponential weak-field metric, $\gamma = \beta = 1$, and, with no number put in, frame dragging's form for a plane configuration of one rotation sense, which read on bodies is a finding against nature (Section~\ref{sec:misses}). \subsection{The question and the claim}\label{sec:question}

This paper is about one method of computing physics. A cubic lattice of Nodes is stepped by one local rule in whole numbers, Rule3, which is exactly reversible. The lattice is read only through clicks, the reports of declared counting regions, which the model calls NodeDetectors. The title names the parts. The integer cellular automaton is the lattice stepped by Rule3. Universe24 is its clicks, written by the NodeDetectors from the fields of its declared universe. The formulas it shares with nature are the known formulas reached from the one line in lattice form: the Klein-Gordon equation with Schr\"odinger's slow limit, Planck's relation in the band's form (the adiabatic invariant \cite{ehrenfest}) and, under one named assumption, the exponential weak-field metric of gravity (Section~\ref{sec:shared}). The formulas it adds are the lattice's own terms, which depart from the continuum or have no counterpart there (Section~\ref{sec:added}). The paper has one claim: that Universe24 helps compute things in nature. Its content is what the model computes of nature, set against measurement, each quantity with its status and its number, the hits and the misses alike. The method is how it computes, and not the claim. The paper does not claim that nature is such a lattice.

The criterion is the same throughout: a derived, click-fenced formula that disagrees with a measurement beyond its stated order refutes the model at that formula. Section~\ref{sec:limits} lists the refutations found. Universe24 is one world built by the paper's method: two declared universes, the two slits' (the one run, read by its NodeDetectors) and Bell's (no run), and the forms of Sections~\ref{sec:builds} and~\ref{sec:formulas}, derived from the law's lines and computed from no run. Other worlds closer to nature can be built by the same method. Building one needs the calculations named in Section~\ref{sec:closer}, which this work does not do.

The paper starts from one statement and never contradicts it. Universe24 is the meeting of the past with the future; a click is that meeting, the only measurement there is in the model, and in it only clicks are seen (assumption, the paper's first statement; clicks; the key of Section~\ref{sec:key}). A Node holds a field's present and its past, two integer levels. The rule makes the field's future from these two levels and from the six neighbouring Nodes. Run forward from the source Node of its initial data, the split events are the future, every path at once. Read backward from a click that happened, by the same rule, they are the past, and through two slits both of them pass through both gaps. A click is where the two meet at a declared NodeDetector. Which meeting happens is the only thing the model does not determine, the only non-local element of the description (Section~\ref{sec:click}). In the model, what looks like a wave is the future. What looks like a particle is the present, the click. The track is the past.

\section{The model and the rule}\label{sec:model}

The physical location is a \emph{Node}, and its complete local information is its NodeState. For every field and every part, the NodeState holds two integer levels, the present and the past, and one remainder. A part is one level pair of a field: a potential field's time and axis parts, a bound state's own levels, a pair field's two lines. The potential fields' levels are the potential level a Node holds for the other fields to read. Nothing else is kept at a Node, and every count, current, charge or stress is a reading computed from these numbers when it is needed. Nodes are joined by \emph{Links} through six directional \emph{Ports} per Node, and the cubic array of Nodes is the lattice. A \emph{field} is one kind of physical value with its rational pair and its shape, and its \emph{configuration} is its levels over the lattice. A \emph{pace} is the step factor of a Node (its clock) or of a Link (its ruler). A \emph{NodeDetector} is the model's counter, a connected region of Nodes declared in the universe's declaration, and its \emph{click} is one quantum credited to it, the only measurement.

\subsection{The lattice and its group of 48}\label{sec:lattice}

The lattice is a box of $N_x \times N_y \times N_z$ Nodes, each joined to its six neighbours by Links through its Ports $\pm x$, $\pm y$, $\pm z$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:lattice}(a)). An axis is declared periodic, open or folded. Beyond an open face a field's rest is read, $0$ for a field without one; a folded axis is one layer, whose two Ports return the Node itself. An inner face is a plane of Nodes declared closed except for declared gaps, a wall with slits. A face may be declared receding (an assumption): the box grows by a layer of zeros whenever a nonzero level reaches the Node next to it, so that the step on such a box is the step on the unbounded lattice, with no cosmological redshift, since nothing stretches.

The symmetry group of the cube has $48$ elements: the $24$ rotations and, with the reflections, the signed permutations of the three axes. It is $O_h$, the group of the octahedron of the Nodes one interval away (Fig.~\ref{fig:lattice}(b)), and with the translations it is the space group of the simple cubic lattice. It acts by permuting the Nodes and their Ports and carries every level pair with its Node. Rule3 commutes with it, since its line reads the six Ports symmetrically and names no axis. Under the group the form $D$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law} is a scalar, even under the time reversal, which exchanges a field's next and before levels, keeps its present, and carries the step to its inverse. The current $F$ is a vector, odd under the time reversal. The Wronskian $W$ is a scalar under the cube's $48$, odd under the time reversal and under the plane's reflection. The click is a scalar, naming a region and an interval, never a Node or an axis. No boost, no rotation beyond the cube's $24$ and no scaling is in the group, so Lorentz's group and the continuum's rotations are no symmetries of the lattice, and what the paper finds of the moving clock it finds as a property of the clicks (theorem for the covariance and the kinds of the readings; lattice; S.4; the moving clock derived, the second order a definition; clicks; Section~\ref{sec:shared}, S.21).

\subsection{Rule3 and the vacuum band}\label{sec:rule3}

The paper follows from one line of integer arithmetic, Rule3, and the inputs named beside it: one write, three readings, the one assumption of the forms (Section~\ref{sec:paces}), the counting rule of the click and the declarations named where they stand. The line holds at every Node, in every interval and for every field:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
&\text{Rule3:} && w\,\anext + r' = \textstyle\sum_{j \sim i} R_{ij}\,\mathrm{arr}_j + S\,\anow - w\,\abefore + r,\\
&&& 0 \le r' < w = 6\,\den\,\Gamma^2,\\
&&& R_{ij} = 2\,\num\,p_a(i, j)^2 = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2,\\
&&& S = 12\,\den\,\Gamma^2 - 12\,(\den - \num)\,p_0^2 - \textstyle\sum_{j \sim i} R_{ij};\\
&\text{the one write:} && \text{a potential field's part at a Node gains}\\
&&& \Bigl(\textstyle\sum_f w_f\,q_f(\text{Node}) + r\Bigr) \ddiv E, \text{ the remainder } r \text{ kept there};\\
&\text{the readings:} && D_i = \mathrm{now}_i^2 - \mathrm{next}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i,\\
&&& W_i = \mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{before}} - \mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{before}},\\
&&& F_{ij} = \num\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j).
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
In the first line $\anow$, $\abefore$ and $\anext$ are the field's level at the Node now, one interval before and one interval later. The level of the neighbour $j$ is $\mathrm{arr}_j$, the six Ports summed. The remainder kept at the Node before and after the step is $r$ and $r'$. The Node clock is $\Gamma$. The field's pair is $[\num, \den]$, with $\cos\omega_0 = \num / \den$ its rotation per interval at wave number zero. The Node's clock pace is $p_0$, its own pace $p_i$, and $p_a(i, j)$ is the pace of the Link through the Port, the Node's pace times the Link's factor $q_{ij} / \Gamma$. At the vacuum's paces the two Links of an axis share one $R_a = 2\,\num\,\Gamma^2$ and $S = 0$, and $\ddiv$ is floor division. One whole number is formed and divided once by the wall $w$. The quotient is the next level, the remainder stays at the Node, nothing is rounded away, and the step back is the same division (Theorem~\ref{th:direction}).

The second line is the only way a potential field's level is ever written (a field that holds a value for others to read). The sourcing fields' quantities $q_f$ at the Node, each a form $D_f$, a Wronskian $W_f$ or a stress $T_{aa,f}$, are read in the Node's own units (Section~\ref{sec:paces}; S.34), weighted by $w_f$, summed with the part's one remainder and divided by the part's wall $E$ (a divisor, not the form $E$ of Section~\ref{sec:form}), $E_s T$ for a time part and $E_s \Wc$ for an axis part. $w_f$ carries the write weight $k_w$ and the proper factors of S.34 (d); the energy line $E_s T\,\num = k_w\Gamma\,\den$ is stated in S.26. The third line is what is read from a configuration and never written: its form $D_i$, which sources the potential fields; its Wronskian $W_i$, its charge (for a plane field $\mathrm{re}$ and $\mathrm{im}$ are its two level pairs); and its current $F_{ij}$ into the Node $i$ from its neighbour $j$. A NodeDetector reads that current through its boundary in quanta of the field's unit $\Wc = 3\,\den\,T$, $T$ the quantum's norm. One quantum's inflow is $\Wc\sin\omega_0$ in the current's unit and its energy $T\sin\omega_0$ in the quantum's, and a massless field's quantum is $\Wc$ itself.

At the vacuum's paces the line reads $\anext + \abefore = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a (\mathrm{arr}_{+a} + \mathrm{arr}_{-a})$. Its rotation at the wave vector $\mathbf k$ is $\cos\omega = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a \cos k_a$, with $\cos\omega_0 = \num / \den$ at wave number zero; along one axis it is Meyer's dispersion relation \cite{meyer1997} at $\theta - \rho = \omega_0$ and $\tan\theta\tan\rho = 2$, his $\tan\theta$ the mass, so the inertia $3\tan\omega_0$ below is the lattice's and the three axes' equal share from one pair is the line's own (Section~\ref{sec:adds}; Online Resource 1, B.0). For the massless pair $[1, 1]$ along one axis $\cos\omega = (2 + \cos k) / 3$, so $\omega = |k| / \sqrt 3$ as $k \to 0$, and along the cube's diagonal $\cos\omega = \cos k$ exactly, with no dispersion (derived; lattice; S.1, S.54). Light's speed at long wavelength is $1 / \sqrt 3$ Link per interval (derived; lattice; S.1). It lies below the causal bound of one Link per interval (assumption, postulate 4 of Online Resource 1, C.1, the step's reach; lattice). Its group speed falls at shorter wavelengths as $1 - 0.083\,k^2$. In nature's names the vacuum's line is the discrete Klein-Gordon equation (a quantum walk's lattice form of it in \cite{arrighi2013}), exact but for the remainder term, with $c_K^2 = \num / (3\,\den)$ and $m^2 = 2(1 - \num / \den)$, the rest rotation the mass, and its slow limit at small wave number is Schr\"odinger's equation with the inertia $m^* = 3\tan\omega_0$ under three named conditions (derived; lattice; S.1, S.53). By construction $m^* c_m^2 = \omega_0$ for every pair, with $c_m^2 = \omega_0 / (3\tan\omega_0)$ the band's kinetic scale: this is the model's $E = mc^2$, read in three click numbers, nature's its small-gap limit, the departure $\omega_0 / \tan\omega_0$, $1 - \omega_0^2 / 3$ to the order $\omega_0^2$ (derived for $m^*$ from the band, $c_m$ defined, the identity by construction and no test; clicks; S.18, S.21). $c_K$ is the discrete equation's coefficient at every wave number and $c_m$ the band's kinetic scale at rest; they differ at a finite gap and agree at nature's (derived; lattice; S.1, S.18, S.21, S.34 (c)). At the vacuum's pace Rule3 is the leapfrog scheme of the Klein-Gordon equation, exact in the variable $s = 2\sin(\omega / 2)$. What the law adds is one rational $[\num, \den]$ that fixes mass and speed together, an equation that is exact, departures that are predictions with bounds (Sections~\ref{sec:shared} and~\ref{sec:added}), the integers with the remainder, and the paces (derived; lattice; S.1).

\subsection{The paces and the one assumption}\label{sec:paces}

A potential field enters a field's step by changing the field's paces at a Node, the coefficients of Rule3 there, $c_i$ the potential level the field reads at the Node, $U = c / \Gamma$ the same in the potential's unit, and $t_a(i, j)$ the Link's stress (the level the Link holds), one number per Link from the parts at its two ends (S.7; the Link's number the mean of its two ends' readings, S.33):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paces}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
p_0 = \Gamma\,(1 - 1 / \Gamma)^{c}, \qquad p_i = \frac{p_0(c_i)^2}{\Gamma}, \qquad p_a(i, j) = \frac{p_i\,q_{ij}}{\Gamma}, \quad q_{ij} = \Gamma - t_a(i, j), \\ c = \sum_{\text{reads}} \text{weight} \times \text{the read field's time level}.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
A positive weight makes a hollow, an attraction, and a negative weight a hill; every field reads the potential fields its declaration names at the weights it names, a plane field also the charge potential, and sources what it reads, so every pair of fields that read each other is reciprocal.

Nothing in Rule3 fixes how a level enters the paces. Beside the line, the model holds that a potential level is a count of units and that every act is measured in the Node's own units, $hN = 1$ in its units form: the one assumption of the forms (assumption, fitted; the composition derived from it; lattice; S.34). From it the clock composes, and a uniform potential level $U$ changes only the units, time by the lapse $N = p_0 / \Gamma$ and length by $h = p_0 / p_a$ per Link (S.34). One sentence is not in Rule3: a Link is clocked twice by its reading Node's own clock, $p_i = p_0^2 / \Gamma$, the ruler going with the clock. The lattice says why twice, since a Link has two ends. It does not say why the reading Node's clock stands at both ends rather than the product of the two ends, which gives $\beta = -1$ at the small gap (the post-Newtonian $\beta$ as the orbits read it, Section~\ref{sec:shared}).

The sentence is one discrete choice. The one assumption, $hN = 1$, is fixed by the bending's measurement, $4U_b$ for ``twice'' ($\gamma = 1$). The perihelion's $43$ arcseconds per century \cite{will} choose the Node's own reading of the Link's pace over the two ends' product ($\beta = -1$ at the small gap, $72$ arcseconds) and then stand as the test of the choice: $\beta = 1$ as the gap closes, $\beta_K = 1.25$ at the computed pair, where the band's $k^4$ term adds $1 / 8$ of the advance to first order, $13$ percent by the ray integration on the exact band in a coordinate plane (orientation dependent within a percent) and $8$ to $10$ in the $(111)$ plane (derived under the one assumption; clicks for $\gamma$, $\beta$ and the redshift's factor, lattice for the metric's form at the computed pair; S.19). It is the first of three choices made with a known result in view (Section~\ref{sec:inputs}). \subsection{Reversibility}\label{sec:direction}

Let $\sigma$ be the direction, $+1$ forward and $-1$ backward, $\ddiv$ and $\dmod$ the floor division and its remainder in $[0, w)$, and $\rho$ the remainder carried in. \begin{theorem}[the direction]\label{th:direction}
The backward call returns $(\abefore, r)$ exactly, integer for integer; the reversibility of every step needs no second function, the odd lines' source recomputed by the backward step as the paces are (S.56 (b)). (theorem for the step and where the writes change no level; the write an assumption; lattice; S.45).
\end{theorem}
