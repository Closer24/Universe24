\begin{abstract}
We present one method of computing physics: a cubic lattice whose Nodes hold whole numbers, stepped by one integer recurrence and read only through declared detectors' clicks. On it the classical wave equation, the quantum of action and the metric of gravity are three readings of one line, with nothing continuous beneath; every formula below exemplifies what the method yields, with a list of what it does not yet give. The paper claims the method and not nature: it does not assert that the world is such a lattice; every result is a supporting finding, offered for the reader's test. The recurrence steps every Node from its present, its past and six neighbours by one integer division, reversibly and covariantly, conserving a quadratic form. Measurement is the meeting of the record run forward with the one read backward from the click. Newton's gravitation is reflected, $G$ as a reading. Einstein's weak field is reflected in the clock's potential under one named assumption, a Link clocked twice by its Node: the exponential metric, $\beta = \gamma = 1$ in that potential, a shadow $4.6$ percent above Schwarzschild's; against Kepler's potential the computed pair's bending, perihelion and redshift depart by 25 to 33 percent, closing below $10^{-18}$. The three computed worlds are derivations, not experiments: the two-slit pattern is the line stepped on its world, and Bell's $S = 2\sqrt 2$ and GHZ's $M = -4$ follow, for a pair laid alike as one event, from the line's linearity under one declared credit, every local credit giving at most $2$.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} integer lattice rule; reversible cellular automaton; measurement as a meeting of past and future; Bell's inequality; weak-field gravity; limiting speed on a lattice

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The question.} The object of this paper is one method of computing physics: a GameBoard of Nodes stepped by one local, reversible rule in whole numbers, read only through clicks, the one measurement there is. On it the classical wave equation, the quantum of action and the metric of gravity are three readings of one line, Rule3: the line itself is the lattice wave equation, its count read in quanta is the click, and its paces read as a clock and a ruler are the metric, with nothing continuous beneath. The question is whether that method can give the clicks that nature shows, and with how little state, non-locality and randomness; the fifteen formulas and the three computed worlds below are examples of what the method yields, not its scope, and Section~\ref{sec:open} lists what it does not yet give. The paper's one claim is the method: a GameBoard of integers stepped by one rule and read into the world through clicks, from which the forms of nature are derived in the order stated. It does not claim that nature is such a lattice; every derivation, comparison and prediction below is a supporting finding for the method, each with its status, and none is the paper's thesis. The judgement is the reader's: the law's document, the implementation and the files are public, and the paper is written to be used for further derivations and tests, as the papers it cites are used here. Of Newton the law computes gravitation: Poisson's equation as a field's rest on the lattice, the inverse-square pull as a free packet's fall in the field's gradient at the band's inertia, the mass as the count, Kepler's orbits, and $G$ as a reading of the law's constants. Of Einstein it computes the weak field in the clock's potential, the redshift, the bending, Shapiro's delay and the perihelion, with $\gamma = \beta = 1$ under one named assumption, a Link clocked twice by its reading Node's own clock, and the special-relativistic part only as the matter family's gap closes, the moving clock's factor reaching Lorentz's as the family's kinetic scale reaches light's. The structure is a scalar pace and not the tensor field equations, so it departs from Einstein's at the second order and in the strong field, has no frame dragging and not the tensor's polarisations, and against the Newtonian potential the orbits measure the computed pair's departures are the band's gap, closing with it (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). It starts from one statement and never contradicts it (\claimmark{assumption}): the world is the meeting of the past with the future; a click is that meeting, the one measurement there is; and an observer sees only clicks (the words are defined at the end of this section). Every physical number of the paper is a reading of one integer rule with its declared integers, the pairs, $\Gamma$, $T$, the two holders' weights, gravity's $E_g$ and the sign holder's $k$, and the vacuum content (Section~\ref{sec:choice}); the implementation holds no number of physics, no floating-point arithmetic and no random draw in the GameBoard's step; that these are fewer declared integers than the standard model's nineteen parameters is an explicitly named hypothesis a reader can check (\claimmark{hypothesis}); of nature's other clicks it gives the forms of Section~\ref{sec:forms} at the computed pair, with the findings of Section~\ref{sec:open} against them. A Node holds a record's present and its past, two integer levels, and the rule makes the record's future from the two and from the six neighbouring Nodes. The rule is covariant under the lattice's translations and under the symmetry group of the cube, the $24$ rotations and, with the reflections, the $48$ signed permutations of the three axes; no boost is among them (Section~\ref{sec:objects}). The lattice holds nothing but events that split: at every Node a level splits by the rule to the six neighbours. Run forward from an emitter, the split events are the future, every path at once; read backward from a realised click by the same rule, they are the past, and through two slits both pass both gaps. A click is where the two meet at a declared detector, and which meeting is realised is the one thing the model does not determine, the one non-local element of the description (Section~\ref{sec:meeting}). What appears as a wave is the future, what appears as a particle is the present, the click, and the track is the past (Section~\ref{sec:nowaves}). Least is meant in three named counts, the state per Node, the non-local elements and the random draws, among the models we know of; no theorem of minimality is claimed, and why nature is so we do not claim to know.

\paragraph{The rule in one line.} Everything in the paper follows from one line of integer arithmetic, Rule3, together with one write and three readings. At every Node, in every interval and for every family of physical values \cite[the line; the booking; the primitives]{algebra}:
{\small\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
&\text{Rule3:} && w\,\anext + r' = \textstyle\sum_{j \sim i} R_{ij}\,\mathrm{arr}_j + S\,\anow - w\,\abefore + r, \quad 0 \le r' < w,\\
&&& w = 6\,\den\,\Gamma^2, \quad R_{ij} = 2\,\num\,p_a(i, j)^2 = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2,\\
&&& S = 12\,\den\,\Gamma^2 - 12\,(\den - \num)\,p_0^2 - \textstyle\sum_{j \sim i} R_{ij};\\
&\text{the one write:} && \text{a held part at a Node gains } \Bigl(\textstyle\sum_f w_f\,q_f(\text{Node}) + r\Bigr) \ddiv E, \text{ the remainder } r \text{ kept there};\\
&\text{the readings:} && D_i = \mathrm{now}_i^2 - \mathrm{next}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i, \qquad W_i = \mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{before}} - \mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{before}},\\
&&& F_{ij} = \num\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j).
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}}
In the first line $\anow$, $\abefore$ and $\anext$ are the family's level at the Node now, one interval before and one interval later: the present, the past and the future. $\mathrm{arr}_j$ is the arrival through the Port to the neighbour $j$, that neighbour's level, the six Ports summed. $r$ and $r'$ are the remainder kept at the Node before and after the step, $\Gamma$ the Node clock. $[\num, \den]$ is the family's pair, and $\cos\omega_0 = \num / \den$ is its rotation per interval at wave number zero. $p_0$ is the Node's clock pace, $p_i$ its own pace and $p_a(i, j)$ the pace of the Link through the Port, the Node's pace times the Link's factor $q_{ij} / \Gamma$, read the same from both ends. All are the Node clock lowered by the field levels the family reads there (Section~\ref{sec:paces}). At the vacuum's paces the two Links of an axis share one $R_a = 2\,\num\,\Gamma^2$ and $S = 0$. $\ddiv$ is the floor division. The second line is the only way a field's level is ever written: the sum over the sourcing families $f$ of each family's own quantity $q_f$ at the Node, weighted by $w_f$, plus the part's one remainder, divided by the part's wall $E$. The third line is what is read from a record and never written: its form $D_i$, which sources the fields; its Wronskian $W_i$, its charge; and its current $F_{ij}$ into the Node $i$ from its neighbour $j$, which a detector reads through its boundary in quanta of $\Wc = 3\,\den\,T$, with $T$ the quantum's norm, the normalisation of the quadratic form.

\paragraph{What the paper contributes.} We state the rule and what is exact about it, proved in the supplement, reversibility to the bit, the conserved form with its current, the momentum flux as the wave's own stress (Section~\ref{sec:theorems}). We formulate measurement as the meeting of the forward and the backward record at a declared detector, the one non-local element named (Section~\ref{sec:click}). We obtain the families as bands of the rule and choose a universe with as few free numbers as the rule allows (Section~\ref{sec:families}). We show that bodies are fixed points, prove their stability under the dilation and state how they cluster (Section~\ref{sec:bodies}). We compute the weak-field forms of gravity and the moving clock's factor, each marked by what it rests on, in the model's own potential and in the one nature's tests read (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). And we state the two slits and Bell as derivations of what each must give, neither experiment performed here (Section~\ref{sec:slits}). The comparison with nature is partial by design: each form is compared at the order stated, and what is outside the present scope is listed as open in Section~\ref{sec:open}. The method does not depend on the outcome of any one comparison: a family is one row of a file whose constants a reader may set and compute, and the implementation builds any universe of this kind from one file. This paper studies a single one, the rule's own, the matter pair $[2, 3]$ over one denominator $\Gamma = 6{,}000$, four families, every divisor $1$ but gravity's and the file's few numbers of nature and not of the rule, and reports where it agrees with nature and where it does not; what is general is the structure, a GameBoard of integers read into the physical world through clicks alone.

\paragraph{What is put in, and the three kinds of claim.} The paper's claims are of three kinds, and the text keeps them apart. \emph{Found}: what is proved or derived from the rule with no input chosen for it, the fifteen formulas above and the click formulas of Table~\ref{tab:clicks} marked derived; the findings against nature are comparisons, listed in Section~\ref{sec:open}. \emph{Assumed, chosen or declared}: the assumption and the credit below, the universe's rows, the detector's four declarations, its unit of credit among them, the electron as the free quantum of matter and the matter pair's denominator as the family's own integer (Section~\ref{sec:constants-law}). \emph{Held to be computable and not yet computed}: the bodies at the closing gap, the moving fixed point of a body and the absorbed fraction with the body's write; the explicitly named hypotheses are stated where they stand, among them the three predictions entered as derived hypotheses (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}), the carrier that would mend the lattice's frame and the nuclear holders (Section~\ref{sec:open}). One assumption is named with the forms, that a Link is clocked twice by its reading Node's own clock, $hN = 1$; the clock's composition and the acts' factors are derived from the rule, and under the sentence the model's static metric is the exponential metric, so the bending, the delay and the perihelion are Einstein's in the clock's own potential, the sentence itself fixed by those two clicks (Section~\ref{sec:paces}); the forms against Kepler's potential reach Einstein's only as the matter family's gap closes (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). One choice was made with a known result in view, and we state it as an input, not a prediction: a click's share is the square of the meeting, a credit chosen to reach nature's correlations, so Bell's $2\sqrt 2$ follows from it and does not test it. The file's numbers are nature's and not the rule's: the vacuum content, a declaration; the vacuum write's rate, $\Lambda$, the one declared number of the hypothesis of Section~\ref{sec:clusters}; the binding range, a world's choice; gravity's weight $E_g$ and the sign holder's write weight $k$, read from $G$ and from $\alpha$; and the gaps, read from the masses (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}, Table~\ref{tab:calibration}). Table~\ref{tab:results} lists every result of the paper with its kind, its fence and its inputs. What is not claimed: that nature is a lattice; that any number of the computed pair $[2, 3]$ is nature's; that a comparison with nature is more than a consistency statement at the stated order; or that a formula known in the continuum is new where the paper gives its lattice form, the known form standing beside it in every row. Every derivation was checked twice, independently, within the project and, where a world exists, by the implementation's checks, not by an independent referee. Where the text says exact it means exact in the rational arithmetic before the rounding, with the integer remainders bounded as stated. The identifications of Section~\ref{sec:constants-law} rest on the electron as the free quantum of matter and change with it. And a line decided after a derivation is stated as the law with the implementation's run pending, a run that differed from its prediction being a defect of the implementation to repair and never a result.

\paragraph{The three layers of the method.} The method has three layers, each with a fixed part and a free part. The board's rules (Section~\ref{sec:model}): which families and of what dimension, which holders, who reads what and how; Rule3 with its reads is fixed, the file's rows are free. The numbers (Section~\ref{sec:families}): $\Gamma$, the pairs, the holders' weights, gravity's $E_g$ and the sign holder's $k$, $T$, the vacuum content, the ranges, all free and as few as the rule allows. The click (Sections~\ref{sec:click}, \ref{sec:detector} and~\ref{sec:bodyclicks}): how the board becomes a click; what the detector declares, its window, region, floor and unit of credit; what is credited, the share, a bilinear form of the two sides' sums and so the double angle of Bell's $E$; how a board mass is read as a click mass, $M\sin\omega_b$, the three masses; and the draw by the shares. Fixed here, common to every world, is the click's one form: the meeting, the bilinear credit, the draw by the shares, the detector's one write at the Node of the exchange and the postulate that only a click is a measurement; free, the world's, are the detector's four declarations, the unit it counts in among them. So a gap at the free part, the counter's unit or how spin would show in a click, is open to construction, and a gap of the one form, no spontaneous click and so no lifetime, is fixed. Every comparison with nature passes through this layer.

The tables' status columns use the same marks, with calibration (a number taken from nature), declaration (a world's own number) and implemented (checked by the implementation) beside them. The runs of the implementation reported in this paper are the checks' (Section~\ref{sec:engine}), the prediction written first and the run agreeing with it to the rounding, the implementation's check of the algebra and no experiment against nature. \paragraph{The words.} The physical location is a \emph{Node}, and its complete local information is its \emph{NodeState}. Nodes are joined by \emph{Links} through six directional \emph{Ports} per Node, and the lattice of Nodes is the \emph{GameBoard} (Fig.~\ref{fig:lattice}(a)). A local change is an \emph{Event}, and there is one local rule, Rule3. A \emph{family} is one kind of physical value with its pair and what it holds. Its \emph{record} is its levels over the GameBoard, two per Node with a remainder. A family of quanta has a \emph{dimension}, $1$ for one real line and $2$ for a plane of two lines. A \emph{body} is the set of Nodes on which a family's record stands bound, with no law of its own; a \emph{cluster} is bodies in one another's wells; a \emph{message} is a travelling record laid by the world. A \emph{detector} is a region of Nodes declared in the file to report, and its \emph{click} is one quantum credited to it, the one measurement; an \emph{interval} is one step of the whole GameBoard; a family that holds a value for others to read is a \emph{holder}. The words in full are S.49 \cite[the objects]{algebra}\cite[the words]{engine}.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Reversible cellular automata and digital physics.} The idea that the physical world is a discrete computation goes back to Zuse \cite{zuse}; it was developed as reversible cellular automata by Fredkin, Toffoli and Margolus \cite{fredkin}\cite{margolus}\cite{toffoli}, as a programme by Wolfram \cite{wolfram}, and as a deterministic foundation beneath quantum theory by 't Hooft \cite{thooft2013}\cite{thooft}. Quantum cellular automata put a unitary step on a lattice, from the lattice Weyl, Dirac and Maxwell equations \cite{bialynicki} and quantum lattice gases \cite{meyer} to the structure theorems of the reviews \cite{arrighi}\cite{farrelly} and the derivation of Weyl's and Dirac's equations from an automaton's symmetries \cite{dariano}; the present rule's step is an integer recurrence on two levels, not a unitary on amplitudes. Rule3 is a second-order cellular automaton in the sense of Toffoli and Margolus, $\anext = f(\anow) - \abefore$ \cite{toffoli}, with an integer alphabet and one Euclidean division whose remainder stays at the Node. Feynman noted that a local classical computer reproduces no correlations beyond Bell's bound \cite{feynman}; the model agrees, and places the excess in the declared credit of the click rather than in the lattice (Section~\ref{sec:bell-blind}).

\paragraph{Time-symmetric accounts of measurement.} Wheeler and Feynman described radiation as an interaction between the source and the absorber, with advanced and retarded fields together \cite{wheeler}; Cramer's transactional interpretation has an offer wave forward and a confirmation wave backward, meeting at the absorber \cite{cramer}. The two-state vector formalism evolves one state forward from the preparation and one backward from the outcome \cite{abl}\cite{vaidman}; Price argued that a time-symmetric account is what Bell's theorem leaves open \cite{price}, and Wharton and Argaman showed that every locally mediated reformulation of quantum theory must let some mediating parameters depend on measurement settings in their future \cite{wharton}. The model belongs to this class, the backward reading from a realised click depending on the detector's setting alone; it differs in that both directions are one integer line, exact to the bit, that their product is a reading and not a wave, and that the choice among the meetings is the one non-local element; the record is an ontic state in the terms of \cite{harrigan}.

\paragraph{Bell's theorem and local models.} Bell's inequality bounds every local account of a pair's correlations \cite{bell}, in the form that experiments test \cite{chsh}, and a local model, a joint distribution and the inequalities are one condition \cite{fine}. The model's local credits give $S = \sqrt 2$ and $S = 2$, as they must (Section~\ref{sec:bell-blind}); its credit by the contrast reaches the quantum correlation only by discarding pairs, Pearle's road \cite{pearle}, below the efficiency bounds of Eberhard and of Garg and Mermin \cite{eberhard}\cite{gargmermin}, which the loophole-free tests of 2015 close for nature \cite{hensen}\cite{giustina}\cite{shalm}.

\paragraph{Discrete gravity and its tests.} Regge calculus \cite{regge} and causal sets \cite{bombelli} discretise the geometry of space-time itself; here the lattice is never curved, and the weak-field forms are read from the coefficients (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). In the parametrised post-Newtonian framework \cite{will} the one assumption gives $\gamma = \beta = 1$ exactly in the model's own potential, with the matter band's factors against the potential the tests read closing with its gap. A lattice breaks Lorentz invariance at order one unless its terms are tuned \cite{collins}, and the bounds are reviewed in \cite{mattingly}; the paper's answer is the closing gap of Section~\ref{sec:forms}.

\section{The model}\label{sec:model}

This section states the model: the lattice, the families, the rule, the paces through which one family acts on another, the one write, the readings and the order of acts in an interval \cite[the GameBoard and the families; Rule3; the primitives]{algebra}.

\subsection{The GameBoard}\label{sec:objects}

The GameBoard is a box of $N_x \times N_y \times N_z$ Nodes. Two Nodes that differ by one step along one axis are joined by a Link, and each Node has six Links, one through each of its six Ports, $\pm x$, $\pm y$ and $\pm z$. Each axis is declared periodic, open or folded. Beyond an open face no Node lies and a level there is read as $0$; a folded axis has one layer, and its two Ports return the Node itself. An inner face is a plane of Nodes declared closed except for declared gaps, which is how a wall with slits is made. A face may also be declared receding (\claimmark{assumption}): the box then grows by a layer of zeros whenever a nonzero level reaches the Node next to the face, so no event ever meets the face, and a run on such a box is a run on the unbounded lattice. The box grows and nothing stretches, so a receding face gives no cosmological redshift \cite[the objects; the unbounded board]{algebra}\cite[the receding face]{decisions}.

\paragraph{The group and its action.} The symmetry group of the cube has $48$ elements, the $24$ rotations and, with the reflections, the signed permutations of the three axes, $O_h$, the octahedron's group as it is the cube's (Fig.~\ref{fig:lattice}(b)); with the translations it is the space group of the simple cubic lattice. It acts on the state space by permuting the Nodes and their Ports and carrying every level pair with its Node; the time reversal exchanges a record's present and its past and carries the step to its inverse (Theorem~\ref{th:direction}); and a rational rotation of a plane record's two parts, $(p, q) / r$ with $p^2 + q^2 = r^2$, carries the rational solutions of the line without its division to solutions and leaves the Wronskian unchanged. Rule3 commutes with the space group's action, since its line reads the six Ports symmetrically and names no axis: a world turned by an element of the group runs as the turned world. Under the group the form $D$ is a scalar, even under the time reversal; the current $F$ a vector, odd under it; the Wronskian $W$ a pseudoscalar; and the click a scalar, since it names a region and an interval and never a Node or an axis. No boost, no rotation beyond the cube's $24$ and no scaling is in the group, so Lorentz's group and the continuum's rotations are no symmetries of the lattice, and what the paper finds of the moving clock it finds as a property of the clicks (Section~\ref{sec:forms}) (\claimmark{theorem} for the covariance and the readings' kinds; \fence{GameBoard}; S.4) \cite[the direction; the four acts]{algebra}\cite[the one algebra]{decisions}.


The NodeState holds the model's own numbers and nothing else: for every family and every part, two levels and one remainder, and for every part of a field family, the remainder of its one write. A count, a current, a charge or a tension is a reading computed from these numbers when it is needed; no register, table, formula or random draw is kept at a Node \cite[the objects]{algebra}\cite[nothing at a Node but the law's numbers]{decisions}.

\subsection{Families and records}\label{sec:records} A family is declared by two keys: its pair $[\num, \den]$ and its shape. A family of quanta, such as matter or light, declares its dimension, $1$ for one real line or $2$ for a plane of two lines, and holds nothing. A family of dimension $3$, three real lines with a rotation among them on each Link, is declared by no file and enters only as an explicitly named hypothesis of Section~\ref{sec:open} (\claimmark{hypothesis}). A field family declares its sources, the form, the form with the three tensions, or the Wronskian, which fix its parts, one real line per source, with its divisor $E_s$ and, for the massless field, its rest; a field sourced by the form or the tensions holds the content, which slows the clocks of the families that read it, and a field sourced by the Wronskian holds the sign, the charge's field. A family's record is its levels over the GameBoard, at every Node a level now and a level one interval before, with a remainder; a family of dimension $2$ lays two level pairs $(\mathrm{re}, \mathrm{im})$, two solutions of the same line, and only a plane carries a charge (Section~\ref{sec:sign}). A body is a connected set of Nodes on which a family's record stands bound, and a message is a travelling record laid by the world \cite[the objects]{algebra}.

\subsection{The paces: how a field acts on a record}\label{sec:paces}

A field acts on a family by changing the family's paces at a Node, the coefficients of Rule3 there:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paces}
\begin{aligned}
p_0 = \Gamma\,(1 - 1 / \Gamma)^{c}, \qquad p_i = \frac{p_0(c_i)^2}{\Gamma}, \qquad p_a(i, j) = \frac{p_i\,q_{ij}}{\Gamma}, \quad q_{ij} = \Gamma - t_a(i, j), \\ c = \sum_{\text{reads}} \text{weight} \times \text{the read family's time level},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $c_i$ is the content the family reads at the Node, the sum over its reads of the weight times the read field's level. The clock $p_0$ composes, each unit of the level slowing it by the same factor $1 - 1 / \Gamma$, to the unit. The Node's pace $p_i$ is the clock twice, $p_0^2 / \Gamma$, so that the ruler goes with the clock; $t_a(i, j)$ is the Link's tension, one number per Link from its two ends' parts (Section~\ref{sec:form}), read into the Link's factor $q_{ij}$ the same from both ends. And the Link's pace is the product, $\Gamma - 2c_i - t_a$ to first order \cite[every row reads the content; the clock is the Node's, the tension is the Link's]{algebra}. A positive weight makes a hollow, an attraction; a negative weight makes a hill. The pace is the clock's slowing: a record at a Node of pace $p$ rotates and moves as at $\Gamma$, with its intervals $p / \Gamma$ as long. Every family reads the holders of the content its declaration names at the weights it names, gravity's and the binding field's at the weight $1$ for every family, the fields among the readers and a holder of the content its own level among it (the law's line, implemented; Section~\ref{sec:engine}; the nuclear holders of Section~\ref{sec:open} by the nucleon families alone), so $x = (L_b + L_g) / \Gamma$ with $L_b$ the binding field's level and $L_g$ gravity's. A family of dimension $2$ also reads the holder of the sign plainly, and light, the holder's own record, reads the holders of the content as every family does and not the holder of the sign. And a family sources what it reads at the weight it reads with, so every pair of families that read each other is reciprocal \cite[the paces; the reads are the held row's pair's]{algebra}.

Two parts of the paces follow from that. The clock composes, $p_0 = \Gamma(1 - 1 / \Gamma)^c$, each unit slowing the clock as it stands by the same fraction, since the write of one unit is an act and every act is measured in the Node's own units, the read included (\claimmark{derived}). \subsection{The write, the readings and the interval}\label{sec:interval}

\paragraph{The one write.} Every write into a field's level is one act, the second line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}: at a Node in each interval a held part gains $(\sum_f w_f\,q_f + r) \ddiv E$, one division per part with one remainder $r$ carried at that Node. Here $q_f$ is the sourcing family's own quantity at the Node: its form $D_f$ for a holder of the content, its Wronskian $W_f$ for the holder of the sign, or its tension $T_{aa,f}$ for an axis part. $w_f$ is the weight with which that family reads the written field, and $E$ is the part's wall, $E_s T$ for the time part and $E_s \Wc$ for an axis part. The sources are summed in the numerator and never divided apart, so no remainder stands per sourcing family \cite[the primitives]{algebra}\cite[the one algebra]{decisions}.

\paragraph{The readings.} A reading is a bilinear form of the levels with declared coefficients; it writes nothing on the GameBoard. The law reads three, the third line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}. The form $D_i = \mathrm{now}_i^2 - \mathrm{next}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i$, the present against the product of the future and the past, is $B^2\sin^2\omega_b$ at every interval for a standing record of peak $B$; its quotient over $T$, the well, sources the fields. The Wronskian $W_i$ is the charge (Section~\ref{sec:sign}). The current $F_{ij} = \num\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j)$ into the Node $i$ from its neighbour $j$ is antisymmetric and, with the Link's factor $q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$, is the Link term of the conserved form (Theorem~\ref{th:form}), the plain current where no tension stands; a wave travelling from $i$ to $j$ has $F_{ij} < 0$. A static record has $\mathrm{now} = \mathrm{next} = \mathrm{before}$, so its form and its current vanish: a static field carries no quanta and no detector sees it \cite[the booking; the conserved form]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The interval.} Every read is of the values at the interval's start; Rule3's line reads one Link per interval, and nothing in zero time; every reading of the interval, the step, the tensions and the hold's write, is read from the record as it stands at the interval's start, so a Node's NodeState after one interval depends on the Node and its six neighbours alone, the interval's dependency radius one Link (the law's line, \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the interval's dependency radius is one Link]{algebra}. \paragraph{What is conserved, and where.} On the GameBoard the conservations are per family: the weighted form of a family while its reads are static (Theorem~\ref{th:form}), the Wronskian of a plane family under a static level, exactly in the line and to first order in a level's change (Section~\ref{sec:sign}), the quanta through the Ports, the wave number of a free record and reversibility bit for bit. The GameBoard conserves no total: the sum of the families' forms is not conserved and not assumed, the share moving with the paces with no current to compensate (Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift}). \subsection{Reversibility}\label{sec:direction}

Let $\sigma$ be the direction, $+1$ forward and $-1$ backward; let $\ddiv$ and $\dmod$ be the floor division and its remainder in $[0, w)$; and let $\rho$ be the remainder carried in. With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direction}
u = \sigma\Bigl(\sum_j R_{ij}\,\mathrm{arr}_j + S\,x - w\,y\Bigr) + \rho, \qquad z = \sigma\,(u \ddiv w), \qquad \rho' = u \dmod w,
\end{equation}
the forward call with $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \abefore, r)$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\anext, r')$, and the backward call with $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \anext, r')$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\abefore, r)$.

\begin{theorem}[the direction]\label{th:direction}
The backward call returns $(\abefore, r)$ bit for bit; the reversibility of every step needs no second function.
\end{theorem}

\noindent (Proved in S.48.) Theorem~\ref{th:direction} holds for the GameBoard's step at every interval; a detector's write and a body's click (Section~\ref{sec:nothing}) are the instruments' acts outside the reversal, as the lay is; the back-in-time gate is exact between clicks and runs across a click from the output's click line (\claimmark{theorem} for the step; the write \claimmark{assumption}). The whole run therefore goes back: $N$ intervals forward and $N$ back return every array of every family bit for bit, provided every number an act read at an interval's start is one the state after the interval still holds (\claimmark{theorem}, by construction from Theorem~\ref{th:direction}). The implementation checks this as a test, running a world forward and back and comparing every array at every interval; on the worlds of Section~\ref{sec:slits} it reports \textsc{match} over their whole runs, worlds whose write stays under one level (Section~\ref{sec:choice}) and whose detectors write nothing; the interval's reversal with a level-changing write is not yet gated (\claimmark{computed}, \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the inverse; the objects]{algebra}\cite[how to run a world]{engine}\cite[the Node knows no family]{decisions}. The condition rules out one read: a coefficient read from a Node's own state across a step cannot be recovered at the step back, so a record's rotation sense is never read at a Node and the holder of the sign is read by the record's shape, a plane or a line (Section~\ref{sec:sign}) \cite[the interval]{algebra}\cite[the board goes back in time, as a gate; there is no sense]{decisions}.

\subsection{What the acts can form}\label{sec:acts}

Rule3's line is one act, and declared coefficients make it four: (a) the read, the line with its six arrivals; (b) the one-Node step, the line with the arrivals declared $0$, which rotates a level by Chebyshev's recurrence; (c) the division, the line with $R = S = 0$, Euclid's division with the remainder kept; and (d) the load, a declared integer in the numerator, which is how a count enters a level \cite[the four acts]{algebra}.

\begin{theorem}[what the acts can form]\label{th:acts}
Every composition of the four acts is a piecewise-linear function of the levels with integer slopes: a sum of linear forms and of floors of linear forms, composed. No such function is quadratic in the levels.
\end{theorem}

\noindent (Proved in S.48.) So every product of two levels is a reading and never a write, and the three readings of Section~\ref{sec:interval} are the only products the law takes. A line enters the law only if it passes three tests: it is generic (one primitive with declared integers, no family's name), vector (an act on the whole state, with no root and no floating-point number) and local (its own record and the six neighbours, nothing kept at a Node beyond the law's numbers). A line that needs more is a hypothesis under its own name. No act of the interval takes a square root; the integer square root appears only when a world is laid, as the fixed point of the division act $x \leftarrow (x + n \ddiv x) \ddiv 2$ \cite[the three tests of every rule]{algebra}\cite[the six verbs; the root leaves everywhere]{decisions}.

\subsection{The conserved form and the momentum flux}\label{sec:form}

The read coefficient $R_a(i \to j) = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ carries the Node's factor and the Link's; the Node's factor is not symmetric where the paces differ and the Link's is, so the conserved form carries the weight $1 / p_i^2$ on its Node terms and the Link's factor $q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ on its Link terms:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:form}
E = \sum_i \frac{w\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - S_i\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i}{p_i^2} - 2\,\num\sum_i \sum_{j \sim i} \frac{q_{ij}^2}{\Gamma^2}\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j .
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[the conserved form]\label{th:form}
At constant paces for which a positive Node weight $d_i$ with $d_i R_a(i \to j) = d_j R_a(j \to i)$ on every Link exists, $E$ with the weights $d_i$ is exact under the line without the rounding. Under the law's read coefficient, the Node's pace squared times the Link's factor squared, $R_a(i \to j) = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ with $q_{ij} = q_{ji}$ and $p_i = p_0(c_i)^2 / \Gamma$ the composed Node factor, the weight $d_i = 1 / p_i^2$ cancels the Node's factor on both sides of every Link, so $E$ is exact for any field and any tensions. Its Link term carries the Link's factor, so the current of Rule3's own conservation law through the Link $ij$ is $\num\,(q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2)\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j)$, the plain current $F_{ij}$ where no tension stands, $q_{ij} = \Gamma$; the form with the plain current holds there alone, and with a tension it is not conserved (two witnesses, S.32).
\end{theorem}

\noindent (Proved in S.48.) With the rounding each Node's remainder adds at most one unit of the level per interval to the walk of $E$ (\claimmark{derived}, the remainder's term in S.5). $E$ is a diagnostic. Where the paces move, the total form $D = |\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}|^2 - \langle \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}\rangle$ changes exactly by $D(t + 1) - D(t) = \langle \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, (\bM(t) - \bM(t + 1))\,\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}\rangle$, which is $0$ at fixed uniform paces and negative where a well deepens under a standing record (\claimmark{theorem}, by substitution; \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the conserved form]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The momentum flux is the wave's own stress.} The momentum density along the axis $a$ at the Node $i$ is the current per axis, $P_a(i) = (F_{i,i+a} - F_{i,i-a}) / \num$, and the line without its division gives exactly, at constant paces, the identity below; the integer step adds the remainder term $\delta_i\,\Delta_a(\mathrm{now})_i - \mathrm{now}_i\,\Delta_a(\delta)_i$ with $\delta = r - r'$ and $\Delta_a$ the centred difference, which the implementation's test includes (\claimmark{theorem} for the line, \fence{GameBoard}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum}
\begin{aligned}
w\,[P_a(t + 1) - P_a(t)] &= \sum_b R_b\,[G_{ab}(i - b) - G_{ab}(i)],\\
G_{ab}(i) &= \mathrm{now}_i\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a+b} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a+b}) - \mathrm{now}_{i+b}\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $G_{ab}(i)$ the stress reading on the $b$-Link $(i, i + b)$, the levels now alone. The proof is in S.48. The tension on the axis $a$ at a Node, the part a field reads into the Link's pace and the source of a field's axis part, is the momentum flux averaged over the Node's two $a$-Links,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tension}
\begin{aligned}
T_{aa}(i) &= -\num\,\bigl(G_{aa}(i) + G_{aa}(i - a)\bigr) \ddiv 2,\\
G_{aa}(i) &= \mathrm{now}_i\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+2a} - \mathrm{now}_i) - \mathrm{now}_{i+a}\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and each Node sources its own part, $-\num\,h_a(i)$ with $h_a(i) = \mathrm{now}_{i-a}\,\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_i^2$, read from its two neighbours along the axis at the interval's start; the Link's tension is its two ends' parts, one number per Link read the same from both ends by the pace at the next interval. A plane wave of amplitude $b$ at the wave number $k$ has $G_{ab} = -2\,b^2\sin k_a \sin k_b$, so the flux along the wave is positive, as a wave's pressure is (S.7); a packet of count $c$ and velocity $\mathbf v$ has $c\,v_a v_b$, the stress of dust. With the sign as written a wave's stress deepens the content around it, so light gravitates by its pressure too. The off-diagonal parts and the vector part, which Rule3 never reads, are not written \cite[the tension; the final unification]{algebra}\cite[the tensor part is the record's own stress]{decisions}.

\subsection{The count is the record's share}\label{sec:share}

A family's count of quanta is a reading of its record and no line of its own. At every Node the record's share is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:share}
e_i = \Bigl[w\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - S_i\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i - \mathrm{now}_i \sum_{j \sim i} R_{ij}\,\mathrm{before}_j\Bigr] \ddiv (2p_i^2),
\end{equation}
the conserved form's term at the Node with its weights (Theorem~\ref{th:form}), the Node's $1 / p_i^2$ and each Link's $q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ inside $R_{ij}$, read in quanta over $\Wc$ as the nearest whole number; at the vacuum's paces it is $3\,\den\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - \num\,\mathrm{now}_i\,S_6(\mathrm{before})_i$ exactly, $S_6$ the six-neighbour sum, and read with the vacuum's coefficients instead of the Node's own paces the same expression is the vacuum's share, the unit of the tables of Section~\ref{sec:bodies}. The implementation reads it so; the law's document prints the Node term over the mean of the Link paces squared with the plain Link term, the same where no tension stands. Over one step of Rule3 the share's rational value, before its floor, changes by exactly the six currents into the Node, each weighted by its Link's factor, plus Rule3's own rounding (\claimmark{theorem} for the rational identity, \fence{GameBoard}; S.5); the integer share of Eq.~\eqref{eq:share} adds the reader's own floor, a residual strictly between $-1$ and $1$ per Node and level pair, distinct from Rule3's remainder:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:share-identity}
\begin{aligned}
e_i(\mathrm{next}, \mathrm{now}) - e_i(\mathrm{now}, \mathrm{before}) &= \sum_{j \sim i} \frac{q_{ij}^2}{\Gamma^2}\,F_{ij} - \frac{(\mathrm{next}_i - \mathrm{before}_i)(r'_i - r_i)}{2p_i^2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $F_{ij}$ at the levels the step started from and $r$, $r'$ Rule3's remainder before and after the step. So over any region the total share changes only by the weighted currents through the region's boundary, the remainder terms and the readers' floors (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the count is the record's share; the share's change is the currents]{algebra}. A formulation moving whole quanta by a division of the current would put numbers at every Node that no act of Rule3 reads back; the law does not carry it (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[the count's line and the sense's line leave the law]{decisions}. Where the paces move the share drifts with no current crossing any Link, Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift}, a diagnostic \cite[the paces' own change moves the share with no current]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The signed integers.} The levels are signed integers, the currents are signed by their direction and the Wronskian by its sense, and a click is one whole quantum, never negative. The total share of a closed or periodic GameBoard is non-negative for $|\num| \le \den$, with equality for a static uniform level of light and, on an even periodic board, for its checkerboard (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}; S.6). One Node's share can be negative at a lay's edge, and a region's by at most half a level-square per boundary Port. Over the whole passage of one forward packet the inflow accumulated at a region is non-negative up to the rounding, since the lay's current is forward at every Link, so the credit by the shares is well defined; where a window is cut inside a passage the instrument credits $\max(s_R, 0)$ (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}) \cite[the law's integers are signed]{decisions}.

\section{Measurement: the click as a meeting}\label{sec:click}

The click is the meeting of the future with the past at a declared detector, and no act of the GameBoard. This section states what is on the lattice, the meeting, the detector, what a click leaves behind, the charge and light, and what locality costs \cite[the click; the reading]{algebra}.

\subsection{Events, and no waves}\label{sec:nowaves} On the GameBoard there are events and amplitudes. An event is a level split by Rule3 to the six neighbours; an amplitude is the level Rule3 produces at a Node, the sum of the split events there; and the record is the amplitudes over the GameBoard. In our world there are clicks, the detectors' reports, and nothing else. In the two slits the emitter's events pass both gaps, every path of the future at once; the fringes are where split events from the two gaps meet at the screen with the same sign. The past is made of the same split events: read backward from the realised click by the same line, Rule3 at $-1$, they form the past's tree, which also passes both gaps back to the emitter. What stays on the GameBoard, the amplitudes no click realised, is not in our world: after a click they are a future that cannot be realised, an empty wave, a GameBoard diagnostic (Section~\ref{sec:nothing}) (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[no waves on the event board]{algebra}\cite[no waves on the event board]{decisions}.

\subsection{The click is the meeting of the future with the past}\label{sec:meeting}

\begin{assumption}[the click is a meeting, the law and not a hypothesis]\label{as:meeting}
A record runs on the GameBoard in both directions by the one line: forward from the emitter, the possibilities, the future; backward from the realised click, what happened, the past, Rule3 at $-1$. A click is the meeting of two quanta at a declared detector: the incoming record's quantum run forward, the future, and the detector's own transition, the click's unit, the past read back from it; its share is their product at the region, at resonance (Section~\ref{sec:bodyclicks}), the form $D = \mathrm{now}^2 - \mathrm{next} \times \mathrm{before}$, the now against the future times the past; which meeting is realised is the detector's draw with its declared seed, and the GameBoard draws nothing; the one non-local place is the credit's one draw over the detectors that read one record, through the root. The detector only asks whether the future and the past met at it.
\end{assumption}

In plain words (Fig.~\ref{fig:meeting}): a click happens when the past meets the future; the click is the now; the detector only asks whether the future and the past met at it; and a click is created only at their meeting. This is a line of the model and not a hypothesis \cite[the click is the meeting of the future with the past]{decisions}.

\paragraph{The click, algebraically.} Let $X$ be the state space: at every Node, for every part of every family, one integer level pair and the write remainders, and nothing else. The step $S_t$ is Rule3 over the interval with the paces read from the fields at its start, a bijection of $X$, exact by the division with its remainder; the line without its division is linear in the levels at fixed paces, the linearity the backward reading uses, the integer map not affine; its forward orbit is the future and its inverse orbit the past. The readings $D$, $F$ and $W$ are bilinear forms on $X$, covariant under the symmetry group of Section~\ref{sec:objects} and never written. An instrument $R$ is a declared region with its front Ports and its coefficients, which define $Q_R$, the inflow through its front over the window, quadratic in the levels for one line and quartic for a pair (S.50). A click is the pair $(R, t)$, the credit of one whole quantum to the instrument $R$ at the interval $t$, drawn with the probability $s_R / \sum_{R'} s_{R'}$, where $s_R = Q_R(x_t) / \Wc$ is the instrument's form on the forward orbit over the quantum's measure of a current. In the meeting's words, the inverse orbit from the credited state coincides with the forward orbit on the realised path, and the share is the bilinear form's value at the coincidence. The click's decision is the one map in the model that is not a bijection, from the vector of shares to a unit vector, a scalar under the group, so no click names a Node to us; its write is one act on $X$, at the one Node of the region from which the quantum reached the detector, which the detector knows and we do not: the levels that arrived at that Node are taken, whatever they are, as the receding face takes a share, the record's count, conserved, falls by one, the detector's record takes its transition, and the change spreads by Rule3 as an event (Section~\ref{sec:nothing}; \claimmark{assumption}, the instrument's declaration). Every observable is $Q_R / \Wc$ on the forward orbit or a ratio of two such. The window is the whole passage of a packet, or the credit floors at $0$ (Section~\ref{sec:share}); a Node whose pace rounds to $0$, a frozen Node, stands in no instrument's front and is never clicked (\claimmark{assumption} for the definition, \claimmark{derived} for the precisions; \fence{clicks}) \cite[the click's algebraic definition]{decisions}.

\paragraph{One form for every click.} At the credit the parts of a record are read with the detector's coefficients, each part paired with the same part through the common origin, summed and squared; the share is that density, and the draw is by the shares. The two slits are the case with nothing to pair: light is one real line, and the share is the record's own inflow. Bell's check is the case through the common origin: a pair born as one event is two trees with one root, and the backward reading from the first click passes the root and continues forward on the partner's tree (Section~\ref{sec:bell}). In the implementation the joint share is computed from the forward records alone, by the linearity of the rule, the check of Section~\ref{sec:direction} showing that the backward reading exists to the bit (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}).

\paragraph{No duality.} The wave is the future, the record run forward with every possibility; the particle is the present, the click, one whole quantum at one region in one interval; the track is the past, the chain of realised clicks and the record read backward through them: one record read in two directions, the particle where the two readings coincide. After a click the record goes on by Rule3 with the arrived levels taken at the Node of the exchange and its count down by one, the other paths a future that cannot be realised; nothing collapses at a distance, the click is a credit, and the past has grown by one click (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[the wave is the future, the click is the present, the track is the past]{decisions}.

\subsection{The detector is a declared instrument}\label{sec:detector}

\begin{assumption}[the detector's declaration]\label{as:detector}
A detector is one connected region of Nodes declared in the file, never one Node, at least half the wavelength of the family it reports across the beam ($4$ Links at $\lambda = 8$); a narrower region, a single Node or a region in pieces is refused at load. It is opaque, backed by a receding face or by the wall's own face, so that what enters it leaves the declared board and never returns. Its click is its report, per interval and family: the net current into the region through its front boundary Ports, the Ports leading in from the declared board, summed in integers, with the region's name and the family and never a Node; one wall $\Wc = 3\,\den\,T$ of the inflow over a passage is one quantum, so a screen saw $N$ quanta where its regions' inflows sum to $N\,\Wc$. The credit: one quantum to one detector, drawn by the shares of the regions' inflows, read outside the GameBoard over every declared detector of the world, one credit per quantum and never two; the detector draws with its declared seed, the GameBoard draws nothing and the run is deterministic per seed. After a realised click the detector writes once, at the one Node from which the quantum reached it (Section~\ref{sec:nothing}). The credit is the one non-local place of the description and no line of the law.
\end{assumption}

Why a region: a record confined to one Node contains every wave number at once, $\Delta x\,\Delta k \ge 1 / 2$ on the lattice as in nature; so no click names a position finer than a region, and every per-Node number is a diagnostic \cite[the click is the detector's report; no click names a Node]{algebra}\cite[no click names a Node]{decisions}.

What produces a click is deterministic, the meeting; which of the possibilities becomes the click no line of the law gives, and the instrument draws. Exclusivity, one click per quantum, and Born's rule, the credit's chance equal to the intensity's share \cite{born}, are declarations of the instrument and not findings of the model \cite[what produces a click is not known]{algebra}. The uncertainty relation is the detector's three declarations, a region of at least half a wavelength, a credit per interval and one draw per quantum (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[the uncertainty relation is not a rule of its own]{decisions}; what each declaration costs is derived in S.8 and S.50, a pattern in the histogram and never in one click (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}) \cite[the output]{engine}.

\subsection{The click decides in the credit and writes at one Node}\label{sec:nothing}

\begin{assumption}[the click's decision and its write]\label{as:nothing}
The GameBoard is deterministic and local: Rule3 with no draw, Theorem~\ref{th:direction}'s bijection at every interval. The click is the meeting of two quanta at the detector, the incoming record's quantum and the detector's own transition; the detector, an instrument declared in the world file, draws with its declared seed whether the meeting is realised, and the randomness is the detector's, the board has none. Forward, to the future, one write: the exchange at the one Node from which the quantum reached the detector, which the detector knows and we do not (the uncertainty principle: for us the click is a region and a window); the detector's record takes its transition, the levels that arrived at that Node are taken, whatever they are, as the receding face takes a share, the record's count, conserved, falls by one, and the change spreads by Rule3 as an event, one Link per interval, with no global act; the other paths stay on the board as a future that cannot be realised, an empty wave, a GameBoard diagnostic. Backward, to the past, a reading and never a write: the credit's reading through the root, which chooses which tree is the past and gives Bell at a distance (Section~\ref{sec:bell}), the one non-local place; it cannot be a write, the board being deterministic forward.
\end{assumption}

A single quantum's count stands at $0$ after its click, the count conserved and read by the credit through the root, the detectors' shared other side on the GameBoard, the Nodes' levels carrying the shares and never the count: antibunching and one quantum one click between far detectors \cite{grangier}, the credit's one draw over all the detectors that read one record (\claimmark{assumption} for the one draw, \claimmark{derived} from it for the rest; \fence{clicks}; the numbers of Sections~\ref{sec:slits-blind} and \ref{sec:bell-blind} unchanged, one click per record). No signal passes: nothing at one detector changes the amplitudes at another except by Rule3 from the Node of the exchange. A detector is a body of the GameBoard and stands nowhere outside it: it has two sides, the front where the incoming quantum meets its own transition and the other side where its own record connects to the rest of the board, the apparatus and the observer's bodies \cite[the observer is a cluster]{algebra} and, through the record it took, that record's root, the emitter's event, so that two detectors reading one record are connected through the root, their common past; it can be entangled on both sides. Bound bodies click by quanta from outside meeting their own transition, never by clicks from outside, the meeting the resonance of Section~\ref{sec:bodyclicks} (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}); a body in the dark is only the future and still gravitates; emission is the same meeting with the roles reversed, two local writes and one draw of the credit binding them. The Node stays generic, knowing no family and no click; the write is the instrument's act (\claimmark{assumption}, the instrument's declaration at the model owner's word; the decision's form \claimmark{derived}, the one form of Section~\ref{sec:meeting}). The GameBoard holds only the future, every possibility, and has no arrow: Rule3 is a bijection at every interval (Theorem~\ref{th:direction}). The clicks have the arrow: each is forward only, the one act outside the reversal; at each click what was future becomes past, the present being the meeting; the chain of clicks is the past advancing into the future, written once and never undone. The direction of time is the clicks' and not the board's (\claimmark{theorem} for the step; the clicks' arrow the instrument's declaration). The engine round of the click's write is in progress on the branch \texttt{click-ends}; nothing of it is claimed until its gates pass, and the checks reported in this paper are those of detectors that report and write nothing (Section~\ref{sec:engine}) \cite[the two hands close the line of the click's write; the detector only writes, and it writes only to the one Node from which it received the click; on the GameBoard there is no draw; the one who makes this draw is the detector; there is no family of clicks at the Node]{decisions}\cite[the click is the meeting of the future with the past; clicks go only forward; the backward run is exact]{algebra}.

\subsection{The sign is the rotation sense}\label{sec:sign}

A family of quanta laid as a plane carries its record as two level pairs, $(\mathrm{re}, \mathrm{im})$, stepped by one call of Rule3 with one set of coefficients, each with its own remainder; a field's parts stay real. The charge density at a Node is the reading
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wronskian}
W_i = \mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{before}} - \mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{before}},
\end{equation}
the Wronskian of the two pairs, which the line without its division conserves exactly with the conserved form's weights, under Theorem~\ref{th:form}'s condition on the paces (\claimmark{theorem}, since $\bD\bM$ is then symmetric; \fence{GameBoard}; S.9). The integer step adds to each part the remainder term $\epsilon = (r - r') / w$, $|\epsilon| < 1$, so the integer $W$ changes at a Node by $\epsilon_{\mathrm{re}}\,\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{now}} - \epsilon_{\mathrm{im}}\,\mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{now}}$ per interval, not zero in general, one witness computed in S.9 (\claimmark{computed}, \fence{GameBoard}); an exactly conserved integer charge is open (Section~\ref{sec:open}). Its local change is the Link current, $(2\,\num / w)\,(q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2)\,(\mathrm{im}_i\,\mathrm{re}_j - \mathrm{re}_i\,\mathrm{im}_j)$ per Port for the weighted $W_i / p_i^2$, $\num / (3\,\den)$ times the bracket for the integer $W_i$ at the vacuum's paces (S.9), and its total over the GameBoard is invariant under any change of the paces that keeps them uniform at each Node, since the bracket $\mathbf{im}_{\mathrm{now}}^{\top}\bM\,\mathbf{re}_{\mathrm{now}} - \mathbf{re}_{\mathrm{now}}^{\top}\bM\,\mathbf{im}_{\mathrm{now}}$ vanishes for every symmetric $\bM$ (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}). A real record ($\mathrm{im} = 0$) has $W = 0$, so light, the real record of the holder of the sign, is neutral; a rotating record has $W$ of either sign by its sense \cite[the sign is the rotation sense; the sign's count is the record's own]{algebra}.

Who reads the holder of the sign is decided by the family's dimension and by no name (\claimmark{assumption}, \fence{GameBoard}): a family of dimension $2$ has a Wronskian and reads it, a family of dimension $1$ has $W = 0$ and does not. The holder of the sign does not read its own level, so light does not read it and a static charge does not bend light.
