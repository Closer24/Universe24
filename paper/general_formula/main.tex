\begin{abstract}
\begin{abstract}
We present a model of physics as one local rule in whole numbers on a cubic lattice: Rule3 steps every Node from its present, past and six neighbours by one Euclidean division; we prove every run reversible to the bit, a quadratic form conserved at fixed paces, and the rule covariant under the translations and the cube's 48. Measurement is the meeting of the record run forward with the record read backward from a click, the one non-local element. The rule is the lattice Klein-Gordon equation to its remainder, Schr\"odinger's its slow limit, Poisson's a field's rest, light's index $1 / (1 - 2U)$. Gravity is a scalar pace slowing a Node's clock once and its Links twice, two features fitted to the bending and the perihelion; at the computed pair $[2, 3]$ the post-Newtonian observables leave Einstein's by 25 to 33 percent. Nature's fine-structure constant and highest photon put the matter pair's gap below $10^{-19}$, where the forms are Einstein's. One finding stands first against nature: a light clock between declared faces and an atom's clock shift differently. The three experiments are derivations, not runs: the two-slit row is Rule3's line stepped on the declared world; Bell's $E = \cos 2(a - b)$, so $S = 2\sqrt 2$, and GHZ's $M = -4$ follow from Rule3's linearity through the pair's root, one analyser weighting the parts at the click, the root summing the branches at the other, under one declared credit, the share as the meeting's square; every local credit gives at most $2$ by theorem. The engine's gates read all three, blind first.
\end{abstract}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The question.} The question of this paper is whether one local, reversible rule in whole numbers on a lattice can give the clicks that nature shows, and with how little state, non-locality and randomness. It starts from one statement and never contradicts it (\claimmark{assumption}): the world is the meeting of the past with the future; a click is that meeting, the one measurement there is; and an observer sees only clicks (the words are defined at the end of this section). In that sense the model is the least computation we know of that gives the two slits and Bell's correlation under the declared credit; of nature's other clicks it gives the forms of Section~\ref{sec:forms} at the computed pair, with the findings of Section~\ref{sec:open} against them. The model is a cubic lattice of Nodes on which one local rule in whole numbers acts at every Node in every interval of time. A Node holds a record's present and its past, two integer levels, and the rule makes the record's future from the two and from the six neighbouring Nodes. The rule is covariant under the lattice's translations and under the symmetry group of the cube, the $24$ rotations and, with the reflections, the $48$ signed permutations of the three axes; no boost is among them (Section~\ref{sec:objects}). The lattice holds nothing but events that split: at every Node a level splits by the rule to the six neighbours. Run forward from an emitter, the split events are the future, every path at once; read backward from a realised click by the same rule, they are the past, and through two slits both pass both gaps. A click is where the two meet at a declared detector, and which meeting is realised is the one thing the model does not determine, the one non-local element of the description (Section~\ref{sec:meeting}). What appears as a wave is the future, what appears as a particle is the present, the click, and the track is the past (Section~\ref{sec:nowaves}). Least is meant in three named counts, the state per Node, the non-local elements and the random draws, among the models we know of; no theorem of minimality is claimed, and why nature is so we do not claim to know.

\paragraph{The rule in one line.} Everything in the paper follows from one line of integer arithmetic, Rule3, together with one write and three readings. At every Node, in every interval and for every family of physical values \cite[the line; the booking; the primitives]{algebra}:
{\small\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
&\text{Rule3:} && w\,\anext + r' = \textstyle\sum_{a \in \{x, y, z\}} R_a\,(\mathrm{arr}_{+a} + \mathrm{arr}_{-a}) + S\,\anow - w\,\abefore + r, \quad 0 \le r' < w,\\
&&& w = 6\,\den\,\Gamma^2, \quad R_a = 2\,\num\,p_a^2,\\
&&& S = 12\,\den\,\Gamma^2 - 12\,(\den - \num)\,p_0^2 - 4\,\num\,(p_x^2 + p_y^2 + p_z^2);\\
&\text{the one write:} && \text{a held part at a Node gains } \Bigl(\textstyle\sum_f w_f\,q_f(\text{Node}) + r\Bigr) \ddiv E, \text{ the remainder } r \text{ kept there};\\
&\text{the readings:} && D_i = \mathrm{now}_i^2 - \mathrm{next}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i, \qquad W_i = \mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{before}} - \mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{before}},\\
&&& F_{ij} = \num\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j).
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}}
In the first line $\anow$, $\abefore$ and $\anext$ are the family's level at the Node now, one interval before and one interval later: the present, the past and the future. $\mathrm{arr}_{\pm a}$ is the arrival through the Port $\pm a$, the neighbour's level. $r$ and $r'$ are the remainder kept at the Node before and after the step, $\Gamma$ the Node clock. $[\num, \den]$ is the family's pair, and $\cos\omega_0 = \num / \den$ is its rotation per interval at wave number zero. $p_0$ is the Node's clock pace and $p_a$ its pace along the axis $a$; both are the Node clock lowered by the field levels the family reads there (Section~\ref{sec:paces}). $\ddiv$ is the floor division. The second line is the only way a field's level is ever written: the sum over the sourcing families $f$ of each family's own quantity $q_f$ at the Node, weighted by $w_f$, plus the part's one remainder, divided by the part's wall $E$. The third line is what is read from a record and never written: its form $D_i$, which sources the fields; its Wronskian $W_i$, its charge; and its current $F_{ij}$ into the Node $i$ from its neighbour $j$, which a detector reads through its boundary in quanta of $\Wc = 3\,\den\,T$, with $T$ the quantum's norm, the normalisation of the quadratic form.

\paragraph{The words.} The physical location is a \emph{Node}, and its complete local information is its \emph{NodeState}. Nodes are joined by \emph{Links} through six directional \emph{Ports} per Node, and the lattice of Nodes is the \emph{GameBoard} (Fig.~\ref{fig:lattice}). A local change is an \emph{Event}, and there is one local rule, Rule3. A \emph{family} is one kind of physical value with its pair and what it holds. Its \emph{record} is its levels over the GameBoard, two per Node with a remainder. A family of quanta has a \emph{dimension}, $1$ for one real line and $2$ for a plane of two lines; a part of a record, one level pair with its remainder, is what the implementation calls a line. A \emph{body} is the set of Nodes on which a family's record stands bound; its quanta are the record's share there, and it has no law of its own. A \emph{cluster} is bodies standing in one another's wells, bound by the same fields and clicking at one another; a stone is one. A \emph{message} is a travelling record laid by the world, such as an emitter's packet of light. A \emph{detector} is a region of Nodes declared in the file to report, and its \emph{click} is one quantum credited to it from the current it saw through its boundary; the click is the one measurement. An \emph{interval} is one step of the whole GameBoard. A family that holds a value for others to read is a \emph{field family}, or \emph{holder} \cite[the objects]{algebra}\cite[the words]{engine}.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Reversible cellular automata and digital physics.} The idea that the physical world is a discrete computation goes back to Zuse \cite{zuse}; it was developed as reversible cellular automata by Fredkin, Toffoli and Margolus \cite{fredkin}\cite{margolus}\cite{toffoli}, as a programme by Wolfram \cite{wolfram}, and as a deterministic foundation beneath quantum theory by 't Hooft \cite{thooft2013}\cite{thooft}. Quantum cellular automata put a unitary step on a lattice, from the lattice Weyl, Dirac and Maxwell equations \cite{bialynicki} and quantum lattice gases \cite{meyer} to the structure theorems of the reviews \cite{arrighi}\cite{farrelly} and the derivation of Weyl's and Dirac's equations from an automaton's symmetries \cite{dariano}; the present rule's step is an integer recurrence on two levels, not a unitary on amplitudes, and quantum behaviour is sought in the clicks and not in the state. Rule3 is a second-order cellular automaton in the sense of Toffoli and Margolus, $\anext = f(\anow) - \abefore$, reversible by construction \cite{toffoli}; what the model adds is an integer alphabet with one Euclidean division whose remainder stays at the Node, and the reading of the automaton's quadratic forms as physics. Feynman noted that a local classical computer reproduces no correlations beyond Bell's bound \cite{feynman}; the model agrees, and places the excess in the declared credit of the click rather than in the lattice (Section~\ref{sec:bell-blind}).

\paragraph{Time-symmetric accounts of measurement.} Wheeler and Feynman described radiation as an interaction between the source and the absorber, with advanced and retarded fields together \cite{wheeler}; Cramer's transactional interpretation has an offer wave forward and a confirmation wave backward, meeting at the absorber \cite{cramer}; the two-state vector formalism evolves one state forward from the preparation and one backward from the outcome \cite{abl}\cite{vaidman}; Price argued that a time-symmetric account is what Bell's theorem leaves open \cite{price}, and Wharton and Argaman showed that every locally mediated reformulation of quantum theory must let some mediating parameters depend on measurement settings in their future \cite{wharton}. The model belongs to this class: the backward reading from a realised click depends on the detector's setting, and nothing else does. It differs in that both directions are the same integer line on a lattice that is exact and reversible to the bit, that the product of the two directions is a reading and not a wave, and that the choice among the meetings is named as the one non-local element rather than derived. The record is an ontic state in the terms of \cite{harrigan}.

\paragraph{Bell's theorem and local models.} Bell's inequality bounds every local account of a pair's correlations \cite{bell}, in the form that experiments test \cite{chsh}, and a local model, a joint distribution and the inequalities are one condition \cite{fine}. The model's local credits give $S = \sqrt 2$ and $S = 2$, as they must (Section~\ref{sec:bell-blind}); its credit by the contrast reaches the quantum correlation only by discarding pairs, Pearle's road \cite{pearle}, below the efficiency bounds of Eberhard and of Garg and Mermin \cite{eberhard}\cite{gargmermin}, which the loophole-free tests of 2015 close for nature \cite{hensen}\cite{giustina}\cite{shalm}. Superdeterminism \cite{thooft}\cite{hossenfelder} the model does not take: its settings are declared.

\paragraph{Discrete gravity and its tests.} Regge calculus \cite{regge} and causal sets \cite{bombelli} discretise the geometry of space-time itself; here the lattice is never curved, and the weak-field forms are read from the coefficients (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). In the parametrised post-Newtonian framework \cite{will} the two fitted choices give $\gamma = \beta = 1$ with $\alpha_U = 0.063$ in the model's own potential and $\gamma_K = \den / \num$, $\beta_K = (\den + \num) / (2\num)$ against the potential the tests read. The universality of free fall, which the model derives for extended bodies of one family, holds in nature to $10^{-13}$ on the ground \cite{will} and to $10^{-15}$ in orbit \cite{microscope}. A lattice breaks Lorentz invariance at order one unless its terms are tuned \cite{collins}, and the bounds are reviewed in \cite{mattingly}; the paper's answer is the closing gap of Section~\ref{sec:forms}.

\section{The model}\label{sec:model}

This section states the model: the lattice, the families, the rule, the paces through which one family acts on another, the one write, the readings and the order of acts in an interval \cite[the GameBoard and the families; Rule3; the primitives]{algebra}.

\subsection{The GameBoard}\label{sec:objects}

The GameBoard is a box of $N_x \times N_y \times N_z$ Nodes. Two Nodes that differ by one step along one axis are joined by a Link, and each Node has six Links, one through each of its six Ports, $\pm x$, $\pm y$ and $\pm z$ . Each axis is declared periodic, open or folded. Beyond an open face no Node lies and a level there is read as $0$; a folded axis has one layer, and its two Ports return the Node itself. An inner face is a plane of Nodes declared closed except for declared gaps, which is how a wall with slits is made. A face may also be declared receding (\claimmark{assumption}): the box then grows by a layer of zeros whenever a nonzero level reaches the Node next to the face, so no event ever meets the face, and a run on such a box is a run on the unbounded lattice. On a receding face the fields build their rest behind the front by Rule3 itself, as Huygens' construction on the lattice, and nothing reflects; the box grows and nothing stretches, so a receding face gives no cosmological redshift \cite[the objects; the unbounded board]{algebra}\cite[the receding face]{decisions}.

\paragraph{The group and its action.} The symmetry group of the cube has $48$ elements, the $24$ rotations and, with the reflections, the signed permutations of the three axes, $O_h$; with the translations it is the space group of the simple cubic lattice. It acts on the state space by permuting the Nodes and their Ports and carrying every level pair with its Node; the time reversal exchanges a record's present and its past and carries the step to its inverse (Theorem~\ref{th:direction}); and on a plane record, whose two parts obey one line, a rotation of the two parts by an angle with a rational cosine and sine, $(p, q) / r$ with $p^2 + q^2 = r^2$, carries the rational solutions of the line without its division to solutions and leaves the Wronskian unchanged; the integer step's three shears (Section~\ref{sec:sign}) are a reversible map of the integer state and not this rotation. Rule3 commutes with the space group's action, since its line reads the six Ports symmetrically and names no axis: the law is covariant, and a world turned by an element of the group runs as the turned world. Under the group the form $D$ is a scalar, even under the time reversal; the current $F$ a vector, odd under it; the Wronskian $W$ a pseudoscalar; and the click a scalar, since it names a region and an interval and never a Node or an axis. No boost, no rotation beyond the cube's $24$ and no scaling is in the group, so Lorentz's group and the continuum's rotations are no symmetries of the lattice, and what the paper finds of the moving clock it finds as a property of the clicks (Section~\ref{sec:forms}) (\claimmark{theorem} for the covariance and the readings' kinds; \fence{GameBoard}; S.4) \cite[the direction; the four acts]{algebra}\cite[the one algebra]{decisions}.


The NodeState holds the model's own numbers and nothing else: for every family and every part, two levels and one remainder, and for every part of a field family, the remainder of its one write. A count, a current, a charge or a tension is a reading computed from these numbers when it is needed; no register, table, formula or random draw is kept at a Node \cite[the objects]{algebra}\cite[nothing at a Node but the law's numbers]{decisions}.

\subsection{Families and records}\label{sec:records} A family is declared by two keys: its pair $[\num, \den]$ and its shape. A family of quanta, such as matter or light, declares its dimension, $1$ for one real line or $2$ for a plane of two lines, and holds nothing. A field family declares its sources, the form, the form with the three tensions, or the Wronskian, which fix its parts, one real line per source, with its divisor $E_s$ and, for the massless field, its rest; a field sourced by the form or the tensions holds the content, which slows the clocks of the families that read it, and a field sourced by the Wronskian holds the sign, the charge's field. A family's record is its levels over the GameBoard, at every Node a level now and a level one interval before, with a remainder; a family of dimension $2$ lays two level pairs $(\mathrm{re}, \mathrm{im})$, two solutions of the same line, and only a plane carries a charge (Section~\ref{sec:sign}). A body is a connected set of Nodes on which a family's record stands bound, and a message is a travelling record laid by the world \cite[the objects]{algebra}.

\subsection{Rule3}\label{sec:line} At every Node, in every interval, every family steps by the first line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}. The six arrivals through the Ports, each weighted by its axis's coefficient $R_a$, the Node's own level times $S$, minus $w$ times the level before, plus the remainder the Node kept, are summed as one whole number and divided once by the wall $w$; the quotient is the next level, and the remainder, $0 \le r' < w$, stays at the Node and never travels. Nothing is rounded away, and the step back is the same division, which is why every run is exact and reversible to the bit (Theorem~\ref{th:direction}). The name comes from the three axes: the line has one coefficient per direction serving that direction's two Ports, the six Ports and the wall's factor $6$ are the three directions twice, and for light the next level is the sum of the six arrivals over $3$, minus the level before \cite[the line]{algebra}.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:vacuum}
\anext + \abefore = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a (\mathrm{arr}_{+a} + \mathrm{arr}_{-a}),
\end{equation}
whose rotation at the wave vector $\mathbf k$ is $\cos\omega = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a \cos k_a$. At wave number zero $\cos\omega_0 = \num / \den$, and for the massless pair $[1, 1]$ along one axis $\cos\omega = (2 + \cos k) / 3$, so $\omega = |k| / \sqrt 3$ as $k \to 0$ (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{GameBoard}; S.1). Light's speed at long wavelength is therefore $1 / \sqrt 3$ Links per interval, below the causal bound of one Link per interval, and its group speed falls at shorter wavelengths as $1 - 0.083\,k^2$ \cite[the lattice constants]{algebra}. In nature's names the vacuum's line is the discrete Klein-Gordon equation exactly: with $S_6 = 6 + \Delta_{\mathrm{lat}}$, $\anext - 2\anow + \abefore = c_K^2\,\Delta_{\mathrm{lat}}\anow - m^2\anow$ up to the remainder term $(r - r') / w$, with $c_K^2 = \num / (3\,\den)$ and $m^2 = 2(1 - \num / \den)$, the rest rotation as the mass ($c_K$ is the equation's coefficient; the band's speed $c_m$ of Section~\ref{sec:forms}, (e), is $\omega_0 / (3\tan\omega_0)$, the two one as the gap closes); its slow limit at small wave number is Schr\"odinger's equation with the inertia $m^* = 3\tan\omega_0$ and, in a content $U$, the potential $\omega_0 f(\omega_0)\,U$ of Section~\ref{sec:bodyclicks} (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{GameBoard}); and $m^* c_m^2 = \omega_0$ exactly for every pair, with $c_m^2 = \omega_0 / (3\tan\omega_0)$ of Section~\ref{sec:forms}, (e): the model's $E = mc^2$ at the family's own light speed, read in three click numbers, nature's its small-gap limit, the departure $1 - \omega_0^2 / 3$ (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}; S.18, S.21). At the vacuum's pace Rule3 is the leapfrog scheme of the Klein-Gordon equation, whose dispersion is the known one \cite{cfl}; what the law claims of it is five things: one rational $[\num, \den]$ fixing mass and speed together, the equation exact and not an approximation, its departures predictions with bounds (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}), the integers with the remainder, and the paces (\claimmark{derived}).

\subsection{The paces: how a field acts on a record}\label{sec:paces}

A field acts on a family by changing the family's paces at a Node, the coefficients of Rule3 there:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paces}
\begin{aligned}
p_0^2 = (\Gamma - c)^2 + c^2, \qquad p_a(i, j) = \frac{p_i\,q_{ij}}{\Gamma}, \quad p_i = \Gamma - 2c_i, \quad q_{ij} = \Gamma - t_a(i, j), \\ c = \sum_{\text{reads}} \text{weight} \times \text{the read family's time level},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $c_i$ is the content the family reads at the Node, the sum over its reads of the weight times the read field's level, read twice into the Node's pace $p_i$ and once, with its second order, into the clock $p_0$; $t_a(i, j)$ is the Link's tension, one number per Link from its two ends' parts (Section~\ref{sec:form}), read into the Link's factor $q_{ij}$ the same from both ends; the Link's pace is their product, $\Gamma - 2c_i - t_a$ to first order \cite[every row reads the content; the clock is the Node's, the tension is the Link's]{algebra}. A positive weight makes a hollow, an attraction; a negative weight makes a hill. A family with no reads steps at $p_0 = p_a = \Gamma$. The pace is the clock's slowing: a record at a Node of pace $p$ rotates and moves as a record at $\Gamma$ does, with its intervals $p / \Gamma$ as long. Every family reads every holder of the content at the weight $1$, the fields among the readers and a holder of the content its own level among it (the law's line; the implementation's build of the fields' own read is pending, Section~\ref{sec:engine}), so $x = (L_b + L_g) / \Gamma$ with $L_b$ the binding field's level and $L_g$ gravity's; a family of dimension $2$ also reads the holder of the sign plainly, and light, the holder's own record, reads the holders of the content as every family does and not the holder of the sign; and a family sources what it reads at the weight it reads with, so every pair of families that read each other is reciprocal \cite[the paces; the reads are the held row's pair's]{algebra}.

\paragraph{Two choices fixed with a known result.} The level enters the clock's pace once and each Link's pace twice. Nothing in Rule3 forces the factor two on the Link: it was chosen so that light passing a body bends by twice the clock's share, as nature's light does \cite{will} (\claimmark{assumption}, \claimmark{fitted}). The clock's pace carries the second-order term of a slowing composed unit by unit, $p_0 = \Gamma (1 - 1 / \Gamma)^c$, whose square to second order is $(\Gamma - c)^2 + c^2 = \Gamma^2 (1 - 2\Phi + 2\Phi^2)$ with $\Phi = c / \Gamma$; this term was adopted with the perihelion's advance known, the clock to first order giving $7\pi$ times the potential per orbit and to second order Einstein's $6\pi$ (\claimmark{assumption}, \claimmark{fitted}). They are inputs of the model, and Section~\ref{sec:forms} says where each is used \cite[the clock once and the Link twice; the clock's second order is the composition]{algebra}\cite[the Node clock and the ruler]{decisions}.

\paragraph{The band at a pace.} From the coefficients, the rotation of a record at a Node of clock pace $p_0$ and Link paces $p_a$ is (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{GameBoard}; S.1)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bandpace}
\cos\omega = 1 - \Bigl(1 - \frac{\num}{\den}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{p_0}{\Gamma}\Bigr)^2 - \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a \Bigl(\frac{p_a}{\Gamma}\Bigr)^2 (1 - \cos k_a),
\end{equation}
The mass term scales with the clock's square and the band term with the Link's. Two readings follow. First, the clocks' shifts: at $k = 0$ every massive band's rest rotation obeys $1 - \cos\omega_b = (p_0 / \Gamma)^2 (1 - \cos\omega_0)$, one factor $(p_0 / \Gamma)^2$ on $1 - \cos\omega$ for every family, so two bound bodies at one Node shift $1 - \cos\omega$ alike, and their rates by $f(\omega_0)\,U$, each by its own gap (Section~\ref{sec:bodyclicks}). Second, along a Link every wave's speed falls with the Link's pace, $\Gamma - 2c - t_a$ to first order, so light is delayed and bent by twice the clock's share. The line is not conformal: a light wave at a fixed wave number scales with $(p_a / \Gamma)^2$ and a bound mode at rest with $(p_0 / \Gamma)^2$. At a Node of content $x = L / \Gamma$ light's band is $[2 - 4(1 - 2x)^2, 2]$ in $2\cos\omega$, and at long wavelength $\omega = (1 - 2x)\,|k| / \sqrt 3$: light's speed is $(1 - 2x)$ times its vacuum speed, an index $n = 1 / (1 - 2x)$ with $n - 1 = 2x$ (S.2). A record whose rotation lies outside the band at a Node is evanescent there and reflected whole; light meets a total mirror where $p_a < \Gamma \sin(k / 2)$ \cite[the band at a pace; the rows against nature, (b)]{algebra}.


\paragraph{The guard.} A pace must be positive and below an edge $P$, with $P^2 = 2\,\den\,\Gamma^2 \ddiv (\den + \num)$, which is $\Gamma^2$ for light: beyond $P$ the mode at wave number $\pi$ on every axis grows without bound, and at or below $P$ it stays bounded (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}). The guard is the stability condition of Courant, Friedrichs and Lewy for the explicit second-order scheme that Rule3 is at the vacuum's pace \cite{cfl}; it is checked once, on squares, over the whole initial state, and never during a run. At $p_i = 0$, a content of $\Gamma / 2$, the Node's pace closes its six Links: that is a horizon, on Nodes; beyond it $p_i^2$ is even under $c \to \Gamma - c$, so the line reopens mirrored, with tensions present too (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{GameBoard}). The clock never stops there, since $(p_0 / \Gamma)^2 = (1 - \Phi)^2 + \Phi^2 \ge 1 / 2$ for every content: at the horizon the Links close while the clock's pace is $1 / \sqrt 2$ of the vacuum's, a massive band's rotation there $\cos\omega = 1 - g / 2$, $0.70$ of $\omega_0$ at $[2, 3]$, and a Node whose three Link paces are $0$ rotates at $2\cos\omega = 1 + \cos\omega_0$ (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{GameBoard}; S.3). Such a frozen Node is cut from its six neighbours: no current crosses its Links, its share is not a number, and it stands in no instrument's front, so the horizon is dark. The guard's bound, $|2\cos\omega| \le 2$ at the band's edges, excludes exponential growth and admits the edge itself, where a linearly growing solution exists, and it is stated for $\num \ge 0$; at that edge the vacuum's checkerboard mode, driven by the integer step's remainders, grows as $t^{3/2}$, about $0.02\,t^{3/2}$ levels per Node at $\Gamma = 6{,}000$, the uniform mode undriven and no click reading it (its form is $O(1)$ per Node), and reaches the loader's amplitude bound near $1.4 \times 10^5$ intervals, ending a vacuum run by the host's trap (\claimmark{computed}, \fence{GameBoard}), a negative numerator's lowest rotation lying at $k = 0$ (S.3). At a negative content light's zone-corner mode grows, so light refuses a content below $0$, and the vacuum's content is $0$ (Section~\ref{sec:vacuum}) \cite[the guard; the frozen Node has no share]{algebra}.

\subsection{The write, the readings and the interval}\label{sec:interval}

\paragraph{The one write.} Every write into a field's level is one act, the second line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}: at a Node in each interval a held part gains $(\sum_f w_f\,q_f + r) \ddiv E$, one division per part with one remainder $r$ carried at that Node. Here $q_f$ is the sourcing family's own quantity at the Node: its form $D_f$ for a holder of the content, its Wronskian $W_f$ for the holder of the sign, or its tension $T_{aa,f}$ for an axis part. $w_f$ is the weight with which that family reads the written field, and $E$ is the part's wall, $E_s T$ for the time part and $E_s \Wc$ for an axis part. The sources are summed in the numerator and never divided apart, so no remainder stands per sourcing family \cite[the primitives]{algebra}\cite[the one algebra]{decisions}.

\paragraph{The readings.} A reading, which we also call a booking, is a bilinear form of the levels with declared coefficients; it writes nothing on the GameBoard. The law reads three, the third line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}. The form $D_i = \mathrm{now}_i^2 - \mathrm{next}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i$, the present against the product of the future and the past, is $B^2\sin^2\omega_b$ at every interval for a standing record of peak $B$; its quotient over $T$, the well, sources the fields. The Wronskian $W_i$ is the charge (Section~\ref{sec:sign}). The current $F_{ij} = \num\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j)$ into the Node $i$ from its neighbour $j$ is antisymmetric and is the Link term of the conserved form (Theorem~\ref{th:form}); a wave travelling from $i$ to $j$ has $F_{ij} < 0$. A static record has $\mathrm{now} = \mathrm{next} = \mathrm{before}$, so its form and its current vanish: a static field carries no quanta and no detector sees it, by the arithmetic and not by a clause \cite[the booking; the conserved form]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The interval.} Every read is of the values at the interval's start; Rule3's line reads one Link per interval, and nothing in zero time; every booking of the interval, the step, the tensions and the hold's write, is read from the record as it stands at the interval's start, so a Node's NodeState after one interval depends on the Node and its six neighbours alone, the interval's dependency radius one Link (the law's line, \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the interval's dependency radius is one Link]{algebra}. The interval has four acts in order: (i) the read, each family's paces from the field levels, the reading field's own among them, and each Link's factor from its tension; (ii) Rule3 on every record; (iii) the readings, including each record's six currents at the levels the step started from, its tension, form and Wronskian, and each detector's report, the net current into its region through the region's front boundary; (iv) the write of each field from its summed sources. Light is born at (iv), when a rotating body's varying Wronskian is written into the holder of the sign (Section~\ref{sec:light}) \cite[the interval]{algebra}\cite[the interval]{engine}.


\subsection{The postulates}\label{sec:postulates}

The model rests on seven postulates \cite[the postulates]{algebra}: (1) the world is Nodes and Events, an Event a level stepped by Rule3 with its remainder, a click a detector's report, and nothing else happens; (2) a Node holds bounded local information, read from itself and its six neighbours, and nothing of the past is kept; (3) consistency is local and causal, one Link per interval for every booking of the interval (Section~\ref{sec:form}), with one exception, the credit of a click by the shares over every declared detector, the one non-local element, not derived; (4) the causal speed is one Link per interval; (5) every physical calculation is on bounded integers, with no floating-point number, no root and no random draw in the law; (6) a measurement is a detector's click, the net current into a declared region through its front boundary over its window, never a Node; (7) the GameBoard, where no one measures, is deterministic and reversible to the bit, and the detectors' clicks are the only thing claimed to represent nature.

\section{Exact properties}\label{sec:theorems}

This section proves what holds exactly on every lattice: the reversibility of the rule, the kind of function its acts can form, the conserved form with its current and its momentum flux, and the count as a reading of the record.

\subsection{Reversibility}\label{sec:direction}

Let $\sigma$ be the direction, $+1$ forward and $-1$ backward; let $\ddiv$ and $\dmod$ be the floor division and its remainder in $[0, w)$; and let $\rho$ be the remainder carried in. With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direction}
u = \sigma\Bigl(\sum_a R_a\,\mathrm{arr}_a + S\,x - w\,y\Bigr) + \rho, \qquad z = \sigma\,(u \ddiv w), \qquad \rho' = u \dmod w,
\end{equation}
the forward call with $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \abefore, r)$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\anext, r')$, and the backward call with $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \anext, r')$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\abefore, r)$.

\begin{theorem}[the direction]\label{th:direction}
The backward call returns $(\abefore, r)$ bit for bit; the reversibility of every step needs no second function.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Forward, $\sigma = 1$, the line is the definition of $\ddiv$ and $\dmod$. Backward, $\sigma = -1$: write $Y$ for the right side's first two terms, $\sum_a R_a\,\mathrm{arr}_a + S\,\anow$. Then $u = w\,\anext - Y + r'$ and $z = -\lfloor u / w \rfloor = \lceil (Y - w\,\anext - r') / w \rceil$. From the forward line, $Y - w\,\anext - r' = w\,\abefore - r$ with $0 \le r < w$, whose ceiling divided by $w$ is $\abefore$ exactly; and $\rho' = u - w\lfloor u / w \rfloor = w\,\abefore - (Y - w\,\anext - r') = r$. \cite[the direction]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

The whole run therefore goes back: $N$ intervals forward and $N$ back return every array of every family bit for bit, provided every number an act read at an interval's start is one the state after the interval still holds (\claimmark{theorem}, by construction from Theorem~\ref{th:direction}). The implementation checks this as a gate, running a world forward and back and comparing every array at every interval, \textsc{match} or the first interval and Node that differ; on the two worlds of Section~\ref{sec:slits} it reports \textsc{match}, on the two slits' world over $40$ intervals and, with its far face receding, over $130$, and on each of Bell's four worlds over $100$ intervals, every record of every family bit for bit, the writes' remainders among them (\claimmark{computed}, a run of the implementation; \fence{GameBoard}, a diagnostic and no measurement) \cite[the inverse; the objects]{algebra}\cite[how to run a world]{engine}\cite[the Node knows no family]{decisions}. The condition rules out one kind of read: a coefficient read from a Node's own state across a step cannot be recovered at the step back. This is why a record's rotation sense is never read at a Node, and why the holder of the sign is read according to the record's shape, a plane or a line, and not according to a sense (Section~\ref{sec:sign}) \cite[the interval]{algebra}\cite[the board goes back in time, as a gate; there is no sense]{decisions}.

\subsection{What the acts can form}\label{sec:acts}

Rule3's line is one act, and declared coefficients make it four: (a) the read, the line with its six arrivals; (b) the one-Node step, the line with the arrivals declared $0$, which rotates a level by Chebyshev's recurrence; (c) the division, the line with $R = S = 0$, Euclid's division with the remainder kept; and (d) the load, a declared integer in the numerator, which is how a count enters a level \cite[the four acts]{algebra}.

\begin{theorem}[what the acts can form]\label{th:acts}
Every composition of the four acts is a piecewise-linear function of the levels with integer slopes: a sum of linear forms and of floors of linear forms, composed. No such function is quadratic in the levels.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each act is a linear form of the levels or the floor of one; a floor of a linear form is piecewise linear with integer slopes; sums and compositions of such functions are of the same kind. The second difference of such a function in any one level is $0$ almost everywhere, while the second difference of a product $\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j$ in the pair $(\mathrm{now}_i, \mathrm{before}_j)$ is $1$. So nothing bilinear comes from the acts alone.
\end{proof}

So every product of two levels is a reading and never a write, and the three readings of Section~\ref{sec:interval} are the only products the law takes. A line enters the law only if it passes three tests: it is generic (one primitive with declared integers, no family's name), vector (an act on the whole state, with no root and no floating-point number) and local (its own record and the six neighbours, nothing kept at a Node beyond the law's numbers). A line that needs more is a hypothesis under its own name. No act of the interval takes a square root; the integer square root appears only when a world is laid, as the fixed point of the division act $x \leftarrow (x + n \ddiv x) \ddiv 2$ \cite[the three tests of every rule]{algebra}\cite[the six verbs; the root leaves everywhere]{decisions}.

\subsection{The conserved form and the momentum flux}\label{sec:form}

The read coefficient $R_a(i \to j) = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ carries the Node's factor and the Link's; the Node's factor is not symmetric where the paces differ, so the conserved form carries the weight $1 / p_i^2$ on its Node terms and the plain current on its Links:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:form}
E = \sum_i \frac{w\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - S_i\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i}{p_i^2} - 2\,\num\sum_i \mathrm{now}_i\,S_6(\mathrm{before})_i .
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[the conserved form]\label{th:form}
At constant paces for which a positive Node weight $d_i$ with $d_i R_a(i \to j) = d_j R_a(j \to i)$ on every Link exists, $E$ with the weights $d_i$ is exact under the line without the rounding. Under the law's read coefficient, the Node's pace squared times the Link's factor squared, $R_a(i \to j) = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ with $q_{ij} = q_{ji}$, the weight $d_i = 1 / p_i^2$ cancels the Node's factor on both sides of every Link, so $E$ is exact for any field and any tensions. With the tension booked into the Node's pace per axis instead, no such weight exists in general and the exact statement is the share identity Eq.~\eqref{eq:share-identity} with its anisotropy term, $E$ then drifting; with the rounding each Node's remainder adds at most one unit of the level per interval to the walk of $E$. Its Link term, the plain current $\num\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j)$ through the Link $ij$, is the current of Rule3's own conservation law.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Without the rounding the line is $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} + \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} = \bM\,\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}$ with $\bM$ the matrix whose diagonal is $S_i / w$ and whose entries on the six Links are $R / w$. Let $\bD$ be the diagonal matrix of the weights $1 / p_i^2$; then $\bD\bM$ is symmetric iff $d_i M_{ij} = d_j M_{ji}$ on every Link, $d_i R_a(i \to j) = d_j R_a(j \to i)$: with $R_a(i \to j) = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ the weight $1 / p_i^2$ cancels the Node's factor on both sides of every Link and the Link's factor $q_{ij}^2$ is the same from both ends, so $\bD\bM$ is symmetric for any field and any tensions; with the tension booked into the Node's pace per axis the products of the ratios $R_a(i) / R_a(j)$ around a square of Links must be $1$ for a weight to exist, which fails in general. For a symmetric $\bK = \bD\bM$ the quantity $Q(\mathbf x, \mathbf y) = \mathbf x \cdot \bD\mathbf x + \mathbf y \cdot \bD\mathbf y - \mathbf x \cdot \bK\mathbf y$ satisfies $Q(\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}) = Q(\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}})$: with $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} = \bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}$, $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} \cdot \bD\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} \cdot \bK\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} = \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} \cdot \bD\mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} \cdot \bD\bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}$, which is the other side. Multiplied by $w$ this is $E$ up to the Link term's sign convention \cite[the conserved form]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

$E$ is a diagnostic. Where the paces move, the total form $D = |\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}|^2 - \langle \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}\rangle$ changes exactly by $D(t + 1) - D(t) = \langle \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, (\bM(t) - \bM(t + 1))\,\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}\rangle$, which is $0$ at fixed uniform paces and negative where a well deepens under a standing record (\claimmark{theorem}, by substitution; \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the conserved form]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The momentum flux is the wave's own stress.} The momentum density along the axis $a$ at the Node $i$ is the current per axis, $P_a(i) = (F_{i,i+a} - F_{i,i-a}) / \num$, and the line without its division gives exactly, at constant paces, the identity below; the integer step adds the remainder term $\delta_i\,\Delta_a(\mathrm{now})_i - \mathrm{now}_i\,\Delta_a(\delta)_i$ with $\delta = r - r'$ and $\Delta_a$ the centred difference, which the implementation's test includes (\claimmark{theorem} for the line, \fence{GameBoard}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum}
\begin{aligned}
w\,[P_a(t + 1) - P_a(t)] &= \sum_b R_b\,[G_{ab}(i - b) - G_{ab}(i)],\\
G_{ab}(i) &= \mathrm{now}_i\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a+b} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a+b}) - \mathrm{now}_{i+b}\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $G_{ab}(i)$ the stress reading on the $b$-Link $(i, i + b)$, the levels now alone. The proof substitutes $w\,\mathrm{now}' = \sum_b R_b (\mathrm{now}_{i+b} + \mathrm{now}_{i-b}) + S\,\mathrm{now}_i - w\,\mathrm{before}_i$ into $w\,P_a(t + 1)$: the $S$ term cancels, the $w$ term is $w\,P_a(t)$, and the $R$ term is the difference of the fluxes. The tension on the axis $a$ at a Node, the part a field reads into the Link's pace and the source of a field's axis part, is the momentum flux averaged over the Node's two $a$-Links,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tension}
\begin{aligned}
T_{aa}(i) &= -\num\,\bigl(G_{aa}(i) + G_{aa}(i - a)\bigr) \ddiv 2,\\
G_{aa}(i) &= \mathrm{now}_i\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+2a} - \mathrm{now}_i) - \mathrm{now}_{i+a}\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and each Node sources its own part, $-\num\,h_a(i)$ with $h_a(i) = \mathrm{now}_{i-a}\,\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_i^2$, read from its two neighbours along the axis at the interval's start; the Link's tension is its two ends' parts, one number per Link read the same from both ends by the pace at the next interval. So a Node's NodeState after one interval depends on the Node and its six neighbours alone, and a change of the state two Links away leaves it bit for bit, forward and back (the law's line, \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the interval's dependency radius is one Link]{algebra}. A plane wave of amplitude $b$ at the wave number $k$ has $G_{ab} = -2\,b^2\sin k_a \sin k_b$, so the flux along the wave is positive, as a wave's pressure is (S.7); a packet of count $c$ and velocity $\mathbf v$ has $c\,v_a v_b$, the stress of dust. With the sign as written a wave's stress deepens the content around it, so light gravitates by its pressure too. The off-diagonal parts and the vector part, which Rule3 never reads, are not written \cite[the tension; the final unification]{algebra}\cite[the tensor part is the record's own stress]{decisions}.

\subsection{The count is the record's share}\label{sec:share}

A family's count of quanta is a reading of its record and no line of its own. At every Node the record's share is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:share}
e_i = 3\,\bigl[w\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - S_i\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i\bigr] \ddiv \Bigl(2\sum_a p_a^2\Bigr) - \num\,\mathrm{now}_i\,S_6(\mathrm{before})_i,
\end{equation}
the conserved form's Node term over the mean of the three Link paces squared, less the plain Link term, read in quanta over $\Wc$ as the nearest whole number. Read with the vacuum's coefficients instead of the Node's own paces, the same expression is the vacuum's share, the unit of the tables of Section~\ref{sec:bodies}. Nothing is kept at a Node beside the record and the write's remainder: no count, no remainder of a count and no sense. Over one step of Rule3 the share's rational value, the weighted Node term before its floor, changes by exactly the six currents into the Node, plus a term that vanishes where the three Link paces are equal, plus Rule3's own rounding (\claimmark{theorem} for the rational identity, \fence{GameBoard}); the integer share of Eq.~\eqref{eq:share} adds the reader's own floor, a residual strictly between $-1$ and $1$ per Node and level pair, distinct from Rule3's remainder:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:share-identity}
\begin{aligned}
e_i(\mathrm{next}, \mathrm{now}) - e_i(\mathrm{now}, \mathrm{before}) &= \sum_j F_{ij} + \num\,(\mathrm{next}_i - \mathrm{before}_i)\sum_a \frac{(3p_a^2 - P^2)\,\mathrm{arr}_a(\mathrm{now})_i}{P^2}\\
&\quad - \frac{3\,(\mathrm{next}_i - \mathrm{before}_i)(r'_i - r_i)}{2P^2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $P^2 = \sum_a p_a^2$, $\mathrm{arr}_a$ the two arrivals of the axis $a$ summed, and $r$, $r'$ Rule3's remainder before and after the step. The identity is derived in S.5 and checked by a test of the implementation up to one reader's floor per Node and level pair. So over any region whose three Link paces are equal, the total share changes only by the currents through the region's boundary, the remainder terms and the readers' floors (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the count is the record's share; the share's change is the currents]{algebra}. A formulation that moves whole quanta between Nodes by a division of the current, and the sign by a division of the Wronskian's current, would put two more numbers at every Node that no act of Rule3 reads back; the law does not carry it (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[the count's line and the sense's line leave the law]{decisions}. Where the paces move, as under a breathing body, the share read at the new paces drifts with no current crossing any Link; the drift's formula is Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift} of Section~\ref{sec:bodyclicks}, a diagnostic \cite[the paces' own change moves the share with no current]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The signed integers.} The levels are signed integers, the currents are signed by their direction and the Wronskian by its sense, and a click is one whole quantum, never negative. The total share of a closed or periodic GameBoard is non-negative for $|\num| \le \den$ (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}): with $\mathbf n$ and $\mathbf b$ the vectors of now and before and $S_6$ the six-neighbour sum, a symmetric operator whose spectrum lies in $[-6, 6]$, $\sum_i e_i = 3\,\den\,(|\mathbf n|^2 + |\mathbf b|^2) - \num\,\mathbf n \cdot S_6\mathbf b \ge 3\,(\den - |\num|)\,(|\mathbf n|^2 + |\mathbf b|^2) \ge 0$, with equality for a static uniform level of light and, on an even periodic board, for its checkerboard, $\mathrm{now} = (-1)^{x + y + z}$ with $\mathrm{before} = -\mathrm{now}$, whose shares are $0$ (S.6). \section{Measurement: the click as a meeting}\label{sec:click}

The click is the meeting of the future with the past at a declared detector, and no act of the GameBoard. This section states what is on the lattice, the meeting, the detector, what a click leaves behind, the charge and light, and what locality costs \cite[the click; the reading]{algebra}.

\subsection{Events, and no waves}\label{sec:nowaves} On the GameBoard there are events and amplitudes. An event is a level split by Rule3 to the six neighbours; an amplitude is the level Rule3 produces at a Node, the sum of the split events there; and the record is the amplitudes over the GameBoard. In our world there are clicks, the detectors' reports, and nothing else. In the two slits the emitter's events pass both gaps, every path of the future at once; the fringes are where split events from the two gaps meet at the screen with the same sign. The past is made of the same split events: read backward from the realised click by the same line, Rule3 at $-1$, they form the past's tree, which also passes both gaps back to the emitter. What stays on the GameBoard, the amplitudes no click realised, is not in our world (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[no waves on the event board]{algebra}\cite[no waves on the event board]{decisions}.

\subsection{The click is the meeting of the future with the past}\label{sec:meeting}

\begin{assumption}[the click is a meeting, the law and not a hypothesis]\label{as:meeting}
A record runs on the GameBoard in both directions by the one line: forward from the emitter, the possibilities, the future; backward from the realised click, what happened, the past, Rule3 at $-1$. A click is where the two carry the same signal at a declared detector; its share is the product of the two directions at the region, the form $D = \mathrm{now}^2 - \mathrm{next} \times \mathrm{before}$, the now against the future times the past; which meeting is realised is the draw, the one non-local place. The detector only asks whether the future and the past met at it.
\end{assumption}

Computationally, by the linearity of the line, the backward reading from a realised click equals a sum over the forward records, so the meeting adds a reading of what a click is and no mechanism of choice; which meeting is realised stays the draw by the shares, the one non-local place.

In plain words (Fig.~\ref{fig:meeting}): a click happens when the past meets the future; the click is the now; the detector only asks whether the future and the past met at it; and a click is created only at their meeting. This is a line of the model and not a hypothesis \cite[the click is the meeting of the future with the past]{decisions}.

\paragraph{The click, algebraically.} Let $X$ be the state space: at every Node, for every part of every family, one integer level pair and the write remainders, and nothing else. The step $S_t$ is Rule3 over the interval with the paces read from the fields at its start; it is a bijection of $X$, exact by the division with its remainder; the line without its division is linear in the levels at fixed paces, the linearity the backward reading uses, while the integer map is not affine, the floor of $4 / 3$ of $1$ and of $2$ not adding to that of $3$; its forward orbit is the future and its inverse orbit the past. The readings $D$, $F$ and $W$ are bilinear forms on $X$, covariant under the symmetry group of Section~\ref{sec:objects} and never written. An instrument $R$ is a declared region with its front Ports and its coefficients, which define $Q_R$: for one line a quadratic form of the levels, the inflow through its front Ports accumulated over the window; for a pair, the window's sum of the squared cross-side sum over the parts, quadratic in the joint amplitude, the product of the two sides' levels, and so quartic in the levels. A click is the pair $(R, t)$, the credit of one whole quantum to the instrument $R$ at the interval $t$, drawn with the probability $s_R / \sum_{R'} s_{R'}$, where $s_R = Q_R(x_t) / \Wc$ is the instrument's form on the forward orbit over the quantum's measure of a current. In the meeting's words, the inverse orbit from the credited state coincides with the forward orbit on the realised path, and the share is the bilinear form's value at the coincidence. The click writes nothing to $X$: it is the one map in the model that is not a bijection, from the vector of shares to a unit vector, and it is a scalar under the group, so no click names a Node. Every observable is $Q_R / \Wc$ on the forward orbit or a ratio of two such. The window is the whole passage of a packet, or the credit floors at $0$ (Section~\ref{sec:share}); a Node whose three Link paces are $0$ stands in no instrument's front and is never clicked (\claimmark{assumption} for the definition, \claimmark{derived} for the precisions; \fence{clicks}) \cite[the click's algebraic definition]{decisions}.

\paragraph{One form for every click.} At the credit the parts of a record are read with the detector's coefficients, each part paired with the same part through the common origin, summed and squared; the share is that density, and the draw is by the shares. The two slits are the case with nothing to pair: light is one real line, and the share is the record's own inflow. Bell's gate is the case through the common origin: a pair born as one event is two trees with one root, and the backward reading from the first click passes the root and continues forward on the partner's tree (Section~\ref{sec:bell}). In the implementation the joint share is computed from the forward records alone, by the linearity of the rule; no backward Rule3 is stepped, and the gate of Section~\ref{sec:direction} shows that the backward reading exists to the bit (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}).

\paragraph{No duality.} What is called the particle as a wave is the future: the record run forward by Rule3 with every possibility, which has not yet met the past. What is called the particle as a particle is the present: the click, one whole quantum credited at one region in one interval. The particle's track is the past: the chain of realised clicks, and the same record read backward through them. There are not two things but one record read in two directions, and the point where the two readings coincide is what appears as a particle. After a click no wave vanishes and nothing collapses: the record goes on, the click is a credit, and the past has grown by one click (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[the wave is the future, the click is the present, the track is the past]{decisions}.

\subsection{The detector is a declared instrument}\label{sec:detector}

\begin{assumption}[the detector's declaration]\label{as:detector}
A detector is one connected region of Nodes declared in the file, never one Node, at least half the wavelength of the family it reports across the beam ($q / p$ Links for the wave $[p, q]$, $4$ at $\lambda = 8$); a narrower region, a single Node or a region in pieces is refused by name at load, and the depth along the beam is not gated. It is opaque: backed by a receding face or by the wall's own face, so that what enters it leaves the declared board and never returns, with no act at its Nodes and no log. Its click is its report, per interval and family: the net current into the region through its front boundary Ports, the Ports leading in from the declared board, summed in integers, with the region's name and the family and never a Node; one wall $\Wc = 3\,\den\,T$ of the inflow over a passage is one quantum, so a screen saw $N$ quanta where its regions' inflows sum to $N\,\Wc$. The credit: one quantum to one detector, drawn by the shares of the regions' inflows, read outside the GameBoard over every declared detector of the world, one credit per quantum and never two. The credit is the one non-local place of the description and no line of the law.
\end{assumption}

Why a region: a record confined to one Node contains every wave number at once, $\Delta x\,\Delta k \ge 1 / 2$ on the lattice as in nature, and the finest structure a family's amplitudes carry is half its wavelength; so no click names a position finer than a region, and every per-Node number is a diagnostic \cite[the click is the detector's report; no click names a Node]{algebra}\cite[no click names a Node]{decisions}.

What the model determines and what the instrument declares are separate. What produces a click is deterministic: the meeting, the current of the amplitudes through a region's boundary. What is not known is which of the possibilities becomes the click: when one possibility is spread over several detectors, the GameBoard gives the shares, no line of the law gives which detector fires, and the instrument draws. Exclusivity, one click per quantum, and Born's rule, the credit's chance equal to the intensity's share \cite{born}, are therefore declarations of the instrument and not findings of the model, as nature's detector efficiency and dead time are \cite[what produces a click is not known]{algebra}. The uncertainty relation is not a separate rule: it is the detector's three declarations, a region of at least half a wavelength, a credit per interval and one draw per quantum (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[the uncertainty relation is not a rule of its own]{decisions}. What each declaration costs: a region of $n$ Nodes reads a fringe of period $\Lambda$ with the factor $\sin(\pi n / \Lambda) / (n\sin(\pi / \Lambda))$, $0.90$ for four rows on a fringe of sixteen; a frequency is a click rate over a window of $N$ intervals, read to $\Delta\omega \ge 2\pi / N$; and $N$ quanta drawn by the shares give each region $Np_i \pm \sqrt{Np_i(1 - p_i)}$, so a pattern is in the histogram and never in one click (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}; S.8). The credit by the shares is done by the reader of the output and never by the GameBoard \cite[the output]{engine}.

\subsection{The click ends nothing}\label{sec:nothing}

\begin{assumption}[the click ends nothing]\label{as:nothing}
The click is a detector's reading, local to its boundary; the record goes on after it and nothing ends anywhere. What a detector absorbed leaves the declared board through the face that backs it; the other possibilities go on splitting and leave through the receding faces, events that were not realised, in no click. The ending of a record at a click by the detector is decided against and is no hypothesis of the law.
\end{assumption}

This is the model's claim and not a deficiency: perhaps nature is local. No signal passes, since nothing at one detector changes the amplitudes at another. A measurement needs no collapse: the record never ends, the click is a reading, and the backward reading from the click writes nothing \cite[the click ends nothing]{decisions}. A bound body can act as an instrument in the same form: the future is light's forward record at its boundary, the past is the body raised by one quantum read backward to the source, and the meeting is the same frequency, the resonance of the body's rotation with the light's; emission is the same meeting reversed, and a body at rest and symmetric clicks nowhere (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}; Section~\ref{sec:bodyclicks}). \subsection{The sign is the rotation sense}\label{sec:sign}

A family of quanta laid as a plane carries its record as two level pairs, $(\mathrm{re}, \mathrm{im})$, stepped by one call of Rule3 with one set of coefficients, each with its own remainder; a field's parts stay real. The charge density at a Node is the reading
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wronskian}
W_i = \mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{before}} - \mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{before}},
\end{equation}
the Wronskian of the two pairs, which the line without its division conserves exactly with the conserved form's weights, under Theorem~\ref{th:form}'s condition on the paces (\claimmark{theorem}, since $\bD\bM$ is then symmetric; \fence{GameBoard}; S.9). The integer step adds to each part the remainder term $\epsilon = (r - r') / w$, $|\epsilon| < 1$, so the integer $W$ changes at a Node by $\epsilon_{\mathrm{re}}\,\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{now}} - \epsilon_{\mathrm{im}}\,\mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{now}}$ per interval, below $|\mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{now}}| + |\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{now}}|$ against a $W$ of the order of the levels' product, and not zero in general: at the vacuum's pace of $[2, 3]$ the parts $(1, 3)$ with zeros before step to $(1, 4)$ and $W$ from $0$ to $-1$ (\claimmark{computed}, \fence{GameBoard}; S.9); an exactly conserved integer charge is open (Section~\ref{sec:open}). A real record ($\mathrm{im} = 0$) has $W = 0$, so light, the real record of the holder of the sign, is neutral; a rotating record has $W$ of either sign by its sense \cite[the sign is the rotation sense; the sign's count is the record's own]{algebra}.

\subsection{Light is born by the write}\label{sec:light}

Light is the holder of the sign's own record: one record whose time part is a real level pair, stepped by Rule3 at the paces of its reads, so that light is bent by the content, and written by the one write from the Wronskian of the records that read it. A static rotating body writes a static level, Coulomb's field, and radiates nothing. \subsection{The price of locality}\label{sec:price} Three consequences of the model's locality, stated against nature's clicks \cite[what is open, item 4; the rows against nature, (g) and (h)]{algebra}\cite[the click is the meeting of the future with the past]{decisions}.

\subsection{Stability}\label{sec:stable}

Let a body of $M$ quanta have the record $\phi$ over its region, $\sum\phi_i^2 = 1$, with the counts $c_i = M\phi_i^2$. Let every field $r$ it sources be a hollow ($\sigma_r = +1$) or a hill ($\sigma_r = -1$), with the static kernel $\mathbf G_r$ of its line and the divisor $E_r$, so that the well at a Node is $\Phi_i = \sum_r \sigma_r (M / E_r) \sum_j G_{r,ij}\,\phi_j^2$. To first order in $\Phi / \Gamma$ the well raises the one-Node rotation $2\cos\omega$ by $4g\Phi / \Gamma$, $g = (\den - \num) / \den$, and the body's fixed point is a critical point on the sphere of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:functional}
F[\phi] = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_{\text{Links}} (\phi_i - \phi_j)^2 - \frac{2g}{\Gamma}\sum_r \sigma_r\,\frac{M}{E_r}\sum_{ij}\phi_i^2\,G_{r,ij}\,\phi_j^2,
\end{equation}
the band's pressure less the wells' energy, each well counted once because the record is its own source (Hartree's form). The record is the top mode of Rule3's symmetric form at the paces, and $dF / d\phi = 2\lambda\phi$ on the sphere is the fixed point's own equation with $2\cos\omega_b = 2\cos\omega_0 - \lambda$ (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}; S.13). Let $\phi_R(x) = R^{-d / 2}\phi(x / R)$ be the body dilated, with $d$ the GameBoard's dimension; the pressure is $K / R^2$ and each field's well energy is $W_r / R^{s_r}$, with $s_r$ the field's scaling power at the body's size: $s_r = d$ beyond the field's reach and $s_r = d - 2$ within it or for the massless field \cite[the functional and the dilation]{algebra}.

\begin{theorem}[the stable body, under the dilation and to first order in the well]\label{th:stable}
At a fixed point of the binding row of width $R$ the virial $2K = \sum_r \sigma_r s_r W_r$ holds, and the second variation of $F$ along the dilation is $R^2 F''(R) = \sum_r \sigma_r s_r (2 - s_r)\,W_r$. A body is stable only where this sum is positive; it is a minimum of $F$ where further the Hessian on the modes orthogonal to the dilation and the translations is positive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$F(R) = K / R^2 - \sum_r \sigma_r W_r R^{-s_r}$; $F'(1) = 0$ is $-2K + \sum_r \sigma_r s_r W_r = 0$; $F''(1) = 6K - \sum_r \sigma_r s_r (s_r + 1) W_r = \sum_r \sigma_r (3s_r - s_r^2 - s_r)\,W_r$: Derrick's scaling \cite{derrick}, Pohozaev's identity \cite{pohozaev} on the GameBoard \cite[the functional and the dilation]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

The theorem is a statement about the functional of Eq.~\eqref{eq:functional}, to the first order in the well, along the one direction of the dilation, with the dilation taken as continuous; it is not a theorem of the integer step on the full lattice, and the Hessian beyond the dilation is open (Section~\ref{sec:open}).

The factor $s(2 - s)$ is $+1$ at $s = 1$, $0$ at $s = 2$ and $-3$ at $s = 3$: a hollow holds the body where its well falls more slowly than the pressure and breaks it where it falls faster, and a hill does the opposite. Three consequences follow.
