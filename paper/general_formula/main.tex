\begin{abstract}
We take the world to be the meeting of the past and the future: that meeting is a click, and an observer sees only clicks. One local rule in whole numbers, Rule3, steps every Node of a cubic lattice each interval from its present, its past and its six neighbours, by one Euclidean division whose remainder stays at the Node, so every run is exactly reversible. The board holds only events, split to the six neighbours at every Node: forward from an emitter they are the future, every path at once; read backward from a realised click by the same rule, the past. A click is their meeting at a declared detector, a region of Nodes, and which meeting is realised is the one non-local element. A family enters as one row, its pair and what it holds, and the universe has as few free numbers as the law allows. Gravity is a level every record writes and reads, slowing a Node's clock once and its Links twice; from the coefficients come the gravitational redshift, light's bending by twice the clock's share, the perihelion's advance of $6\pi$ times the potential, Lorentz's factor at a body's own light speed, and the force between parallel moving charges as Coulomb's over that factor. Running no experiment, the paper derives what two must give: the two-slit fringe row, and Bell's correlation $E = \cos 2(a - b)$ with the Clauser-Horne-Shimony-Holt sum $2\sqrt 2$, where every local credit gives at most $2$.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{The line}\label{sec:line}

At every Node, each interval, every family steps by the first line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}: the six arrivals, each the neighbour's level through its Port ($0$ beyond an open face, the Node's own level on a folded axis), the Node's own level now, minus its level before, plus the remainder kept at the Node, divided once by the wall $w$; the quotient is the next level and the remainder stays. The remainder is the Node's own and never travels \cite[the line]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The name, and the one division.} Rule3 is named for the three axes of the lattice, the three directions of the vector the rule works with: the Link's paces $(p_x, p_y, p_z)$ are that vector, and the line has one read coefficient per direction, $R_a = 2\,\num\,p_a^2$, serving the direction's two Ports; the six Ports and the wall's $6$ are the three directions twice, so the six follows from the three; in time the rule is a three-level recurrence (before, now, next), and in light's own line the three stands in the open: the next level is the six arrivals over $3$ minus the level before (the owner's word of 2026-10-01: the name stays). At every Node, each interval, Rule3 is one division of whole numbers. The six arrivals through the Ports, each weighted by its direction's coefficient, together with the Node's own level, the level before and the remainder the Node kept, are summed as one whole number; that whole is divided by the wall $w$; the quotient is the next level, and the remainder, $0 \le r' < w$, stays at the Node and never travels. The whole is taken apart over the Ports and put together again at the neighbours: nothing is rounded away and nothing is lost, the step back is the same division, and that is why the board is exact and reversible to the bit (the owner's word of 2026-10-01, ``you take the whole and take it apart over the Ports and leave the remainders'') \cite[the line]{algebra}\cite[the paper]{decisions}.

In the engine the line is one function, \texttt{core/rule3.py}, called on whole-board arrays for every record with the direction $+1$ or $-1$, and every level that crosses a Link crosses it through a Port, \texttt{core/ports.py}; a test holds the rule's arithmetic to that one function and every integer literal of the engine to the law's own \cite[the gates]{engine}. At the vacuum's pace $p_0 = p_a = \Gamma$ the self coefficient vanishes and the line is the plain second-order wave rule of the pair,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vacuum}
\anext + \abefore = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a (\mathrm{arr}_{+a} + \mathrm{arr}_{-a}),
\end{equation}
whose rotation at the wave vector $\mathbf k$ is $\cos\omega = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a \cos k_a$: at wave number zero $\cos\omega_0 = \num / \den$, and for the massless pair $[1, 1]$ along one axis $\cos\omega = (2 + \cos k) / 3$, so that $\omega = |k| / \sqrt 3$ as $k \to 0$. Light's speed at long wavelength is therefore $1 / \sqrt 3$ Links per interval, under the causal bound of one Link per interval, the arrival's own speed; the two differ, and what in nature is one Link and one interval rests on the units of Section~\ref{sec:constants} \cite[the lattice constants]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The clock once and the Link twice.} From the coefficients, the rotation at a Node of the clock's pace $p_0$ and the Link's paces $p_a$ is (\claimmark{derived})
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bandpace}
\cos\omega = 1 - \Bigl(1 - \frac{\num}{\den}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{p_0}{\Gamma}\Bigr)^2 - \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a \Bigl(\frac{p_a}{\Gamma}\Bigr)^2 (1 - \cos k_a),
\end{equation}
the mass term scaling with the clock's square and the band term with the Link's pace. Two readings follow and no third \cite[the clock once and the Link twice]{algebra}. (i) \emph{The clocks shift alike}: at $k = 0$ every massive band's rest rotation obeys $1 - \cos\omega_b = (p_0 / \Gamma)^2 (1 - \cos\omega_0)$, one factor for every family, so two bound bodies at one Node shift their clocks alike, and a packet's fall in a pace gradient does not depend on its family beyond its band's own shape. (ii) \emph{The Link is twice}: along a Link every wave's speed falls with $p_a = \Gamma - 2c - t_a$, the held level $c$ entering the Link twice and the clock once, so light, which reads the held rows as every family does, is delayed and bent by twice the clock's share (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). The line is not conformal: a light wave at a fixed wave number scales with $(p_a / \Gamma)^2$ and a bound mode at rest with $(p_0 / \Gamma)^2$, and the two differ already at first order in $c / \Gamma$.

A held family's field steps at its row's pair with the pace $1$ and the wall $3\,\den$, at first order: for the massless row $\anext = (S_6(\anow) - 3\,\abefore + r) \ddiv 3$, $S_6$ the sum over the six neighbours, and for a row with a gap $3\,\den\,\anext + r' = \num\,S_6(\anow) - 3\,\den\,\abefore + r$, the same call at the row's own pair \cite[the vacuum's row steps at the divisor 1]{algebra}.

\subsection{The direction}\label{sec:direction}

Let $\sigma$ be the direction, $+1$ forward and $-1$ backward, let $\ddiv$ and $\dmod$ be the floor division and its remainder in $[0, w)$, and let $\rho$ be the remainder carried in. With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direction}
u = \sigma\Bigl(\sum_a R_a\,\mathrm{arr}_a + S\,x - w\,y\Bigr) + \rho, \qquad z = \sigma\,(u \ddiv w), \qquad \rho' = u \dmod w,
\end{equation}
forward $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \abefore, r)$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\anext, r')$, and backward $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \anext, r')$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\abefore, r)$.

\begin{theorem}[the stable body]\label{th:stable}

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}

\end{proof}

\paragraph{The files.} \emph{The universe file} declares the integers ($\Gamma$, $T$, the width) and the families, each its name, its pair and, for a held family, what it holds, its divisor and, on the massless row, its rest; the rule derives the rest. \emph{The world file} declares the board's shape and its faces (periodic, open or closed; the inner faces with their gaps; the receding faces with their largest size and their layers per growth), the intervals, the bodies by their family, their Nodes with their counts, the messages by their family, axis, wave number, amplitude, envelope, phase and transverse wave number, and the detectors by their regions or the body they name; a missing key is refused by name, and no default is written. \emph{The mode file} beside the world holds the generator's levels of every body and message. \emph{The expectation file} holds the blind row, every number the file's: the regions, the window, the blind counts and the draw's seed for a screen; the ports, the settings, the calibrated phases and the blind $E$ and $S$ for Bell. The two experiments' worlds are written by a builder from one design file each, every number of the worlds in it \cite[the loader and the files; how to run a world]{engine}.

\paragraph{A run.} The generator lays the bodies and the messages, a record and no count; the gate at the start checks each body's declared count against its record's share, a reading and no lay; the run is headless, one output file per world with the verdict (\textsc{lawful}, or \textsc{refused} with the reason, the guard's and the gate's at load among them), the intervals run, the end where a receding face reached its largest size, one \texttt{click} line per family and region per interval with the net current through the region's front boundary (the detector's report, the measurement), a \texttt{field} line per region with a family's density over it (a GameBoard reading), and the books (the share's total, its drift against the one the world started with, the least Link pace of the final state, diagnostics). The readers: the screen's reader takes a world's \texttt{click} lines over the window against the expectation file and gives per region the share, $N$ the total over $\Wc$, the laid count less $N$ as the part that left elsewhere, the clicks credited by the largest remainders and one draw of $N$ clicks by the shares at the declared seed, \textsc{detector}; Bell's reader takes the runs' \texttt{click} lines per port, calibrates the ports and the settings' phases, credits each side by the rule the file names (the sign, or the contrast) and gives $E$ among the coincidences, $S$, the efficiencies and the readings beside them in exact fractions; the two speeds' reader takes the \texttt{field} lines at the far region. A look runs only after the back-in-time gate says \textsc{match} on its own world over its intervals \cite[the output; how to run a world]{engine}.

\paragraph{How to use the simulator, in a few lines.} Write a world file: the board's shape and faces, the universe file it names, the bodies by their family, Nodes and counts, the messages by their family, axis, wave number, amplitude and envelope, and the detectors by their regions; every number of physics is in the files and none in the engine. Lay it: \texttt{PYTHONPATH=src python tools/pixel\_mode.py --input world.json} writes the mode file with every body's fixed point and every message's levels. Run it headless: \texttt{PYTHONPATH=src python tools/run\_inputs.py --out runs/first world.json} writes one output file with the verdict (\textsc{lawful}, or \textsc{refused} by name), the \texttt{click} lines (the measurement) and the \texttt{field} lines (a diagnostic). Check it: \texttt{PYTHONPATH=src python tools/back\_in\_time.py --intervals 40 world.json} runs the world forward and back and says \textsc{match} or \textsc{miss} by name; no look runs before \textsc{match}. Read it: \texttt{tools/click\_counts.py} credits a screen's clicks by the shares against the blind file, and \texttt{tools/look/record.py} with \texttt{tools/look/page.py} draws the board as a diagnostic. Where each chapter of this paper lives in it: the line in \texttt{core/rule3.py} and the Ports in \texttt{core/ports.py} (Section~\ref{sec:rule}); the paces and the guard in \texttt{features/signed\_read}; the readings of the form, the Wronskian, the currents and the tension in \texttt{node.py} and the share in \texttt{share.py} (Section~\ref{sec:click}); the one write in \texttt{features/hold} and \texttt{features/write} and the start in \texttt{features/start} (Sections~\ref{sec:write} and~\ref{sec:families}); the detectors' reports in \texttt{reports.py} and the receding face in \texttt{growth.py} (Section~\ref{sec:detector}); the interval and its inverse in \texttt{game\_board.py} (Section~\ref{sec:interval}); the families from the rule in \texttt{loader/derived.py} (Section~\ref{sec:families}); the generator in \texttt{tools/pixel\_mode.py} (Section~\ref{sec:generator}); and the two experiments' worlds under \texttt{examples/events/} (Section~\ref{sec:slits}) \cite[how to run a world]{engine}.

A behaviour change needs a dedicated test; a physics change starts from the law's line \cite[the gates]{engine}.

\paragraph{To test a hypothesis.} Write its line in the law's document first, under its own name, with the three verdicts; the advisor computes the blind number from the engine's own lines; the worker builds it as one pure function of arrays that calls Rule3 alone, with its test, on one short branch; the world and its blind expectation are written before the run; the run's reading is compared with that number, a difference being a defect of the engine and never a finding, and a number outside its band with no defect found naming the missing law as a hypothesis under its own name. No expectation is rewritten after a look, and no body's number is tuned to a result \cite[the blind number first]{workflow}.
