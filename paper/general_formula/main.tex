\begin{abstract}
We propose one local rule in whole numbers, Rule3, applied at every Node of a lattice each interval: a second-order step of a family's level from its two levels and its six neighbours' arrivals, with integer coefficients from the family's pair and the Node's paces, and one Euclidean division whose remainder stays at the Node. A quantum moves whole when a remainder crosses the quantum's action; that division is the click, the one non-local act and the only measurement. A detector is a set of a bound body's Nodes, so every measurable number is a click at a bound body, and the algebra is the algebra of bound bodies: their shape, their stability, their cooling, what they give and take. Exact on the lattice: reversibility bit for bit, a conserved form, one quantum per click, Born's rule, no signalling, and for a pair record the correlation $\cos 2(a - b)$ with the sum $2\sqrt 2$. The families are bands of the rule with one free number per tensor rank. From the same coefficients, by a band's geometric optics, come Lorentz's factor at the body's own light speed, Einstein's bending and delay with the level entering the Link twice, and the perihelion's $6\pi$ once the clock carries its second order; nothing is fitted. The integer engine that computes the rule, where every hypothesis enters as a line, a folder, a test and a blind expectation, is described with its use; nothing is run here and no agreement with nature is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{The objects}\label{sec:objects}

A \emph{Node} is a physical location. The \emph{GameBoard} is all the Nodes together, a box of $\mathrm{shape} = (N_x, N_y, N_z)$ Nodes; each axis is periodic (the box a torus on that axis) or open (a face beyond which no Node lies), as the world declares. Two Nodes that differ by one step along one axis are joined by a \emph{Link}; each Node has six Links, one through each of its six \emph{Ports}, named by their outward direction $+x$, $-x$, $+y$, $-y$, $+z$, $-z$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:lattice}). An axis of one layer folds: its two Ports return the Node itself. The local information of a Node is its \emph{NodeState}: the levels and remainders of the families at that Node, and nothing else. A change of a NodeState is an \emph{Event}; the rule that makes it is a \emph{LocalRule}, and there is one, Rule3.

A \emph{family} is one kind of physical value with its own declaration. A family's \emph{record} is a set of levels over the GameBoard, one integer per Node per level, with a remainder per Node; a family with two levels per Node holds a pair $(\mathrm{re}, \mathrm{im})$ at each Node. A \emph{body} is a set of Nodes on which a family's \emph{count} stands, with the values the body's writer declares (its content, its momentum, its spin, its moment); a body is one connected region.

\subsection{The line}\label{sec:line}

At every Node, each interval, for every family, with the wall $w$, the read coefficient $R_a$ on each axis $a$, the self coefficient $S$, the level $\anow$ at the interval's start, $\abefore$ one interval earlier and the remainder $r$ kept at the Node,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rule3}
w\,\anext + r' = \sum_{a} R_a\,(\mathrm{arr}_{+a} + \mathrm{arr}_{-a}) + S\,\anow - w\,\abefore + r, \qquad 0 \le r' < w,
\end{equation}
$\mathrm{arr}_{\pm a}$ the arrival through the Port $\pm a$: the neighbour's level, or its rotation by the Link's angle (Section~\ref{sec:transport}); $0$ beyond an open face; the Node's own level on a folded axis. The remainder is the Node's own and never travels. The coefficients at a Node, from its paces (Section~\ref{sec:paces}) and its family's pair, are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficients}
w = 6\,\den\,\Gamma^2, \qquad R_a = 2\,\num\,p_a^2, \qquad S = 12\,\den\,\Gamma^2 - 12\,(\den - \num)\,p_0^2 - 4\,\num\,(p_x^2 + p_y^2 + p_z^2).
\end{equation}
One floor division per Node per interval; the remainder stays at the Node. A held family's field (a real field of phase $1$) steps at its row's pair with the pace $1$ and the wall $3\,\den$, at first order \cite[the line]{algebra}.

\begin{proposition}[the vacuum's wave rule and the band]\label{prop:band}
At the vacuum's pace, $p_0 = p_a = \Gamma$, the self coefficient vanishes and Eq.~\eqref{eq:rule3} is, up to the remainder, $\anext + \abefore = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\,S_6(\anow)$ with $S_6$ the sum over the six neighbours. A plane wave of wave number $k$ along an axis rotates by $\omega$ per interval with
\[
\cos\omega = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\,(2 + \cos k), \qquad \cos\omega_0 = \frac{\num}{\den} \text{ at } k = 0,
\]
and off an axis $\cos k_x + \cos k_y + \cos k_z$ stands in place of $2 + \cos k$. The family's band of rotations is $[\num/(3\,\den), \num/\den]$ in the cosine; light, the pair $[1, 1]$, has $\cos\omega = (2 + \cos k)/3$, its group velocity at long wavelength $1/\sqrt 3$ Links per interval, and its band reaches $\cos\omega = 1$; a pair with $\den > \num$ has the gap $\cos\omega_0 = \num/\den$ below $1$, a rest rotation, a mass.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With $p_0 = p_a = \Gamma$, $S = 12\,\den\,\Gamma^2 - 12\,\den\,\Gamma^2 + 12\,\num\,\Gamma^2 - 12\,\num\,\Gamma^2 = 0$ and $R_a / w = \num / (3\,\den)$. For $\anow = A\cos(k x - \omega t)$ along the axis $x$, $S_6(\anow) = (4 + 2\cos k)\,\anow$ and $\anext + \abefore = 2\cos\omega\,\anow$, so $2\cos\omega = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}(4 + 2\cos k)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[the pace is conformal]\label{prop:conformal}
At a Node whose pace is $p$ on every axis, every family's rotation at every wave number obeys
\[
\sin^2\frac{\omega}{2} = \Bigl(\frac{p}{\Gamma}\Bigr)^2 \sin^2\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{vac}}(k)}{2},
\]
$\omega_{\mathrm{vac}}(k)$ the rotation of Proposition~\ref{prop:band}: the pace multiplies the clock of every wave and every bound mode alike.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At an isotropic pace $S = 12\,\den\,(\Gamma^2 - p^2)$ and $R_a = 2\,\num\,p^2$, so $2\cos\omega = \frac{\num\,p^2}{3\,\den\,\Gamma^2}(4 + 2\cos k) + 2\Bigl(1 - \frac{p^2}{\Gamma^2}\Bigr) = 2 - 2\Bigl(\frac{p}{\Gamma}\Bigr)^2\Bigl(1 - \frac{\num}{3\,\den}(2 + \cos k)\Bigr) = 2 - 2\Bigl(\frac{p}{\Gamma}\Bigr)^2 (1 - \cos\omega_{\mathrm{vac}})$, and $1 - \cos\omega = 2\sin^2(\omega/2)$.
\end{proof}

Two clocks at one level shift alike, $d\ln\omega / dc = 2\tan(\omega/2) / (\omega\,p)$ per unit of the held level $c$, and a packet's fall in a pace gradient does not depend on its family or its shape beyond its band's own \cite[the line]{algebra}: this is the paper's equivalence principle, a reading of the line and no postulate. The clock's slowing is this pace: a record at a Node of pace $p$ rotates and moves as a record at $\Gamma$ does, with its intervals $p / \Gamma$ as long.

\subsection{The direction}\label{sec:direction}

\begin{theorem}[the step is reversible bit for bit]\label{th:direction}
Let $\sigma$ be the direction, $+1$ forward and $-1$ backward, $\ddiv$ and $\dmod$ the floor division and its remainder in $[0, w)$, and $\rho$ the remainder carried in. With
\[
u = \sigma\Bigl(\sum_a R_a\,\mathrm{arr}_a + S\,x - w\,y\Bigr) + \rho, \qquad z = \sigma\,(u \ddiv w), \qquad \rho' = u \dmod w,
\]
forward $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \abefore, r)$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\anext, r')$, and backward $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \anext, r')$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\abefore, r)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Forward, $\sigma = 1$, the line is the definition of $\ddiv$ and $\dmod$. Backward, $\sigma = -1$: write $Y$ for the right side's first two terms, $\sum_a R_a\,\mathrm{arr}_a + S\,\anow$. Then $u = w\,\anext - Y + r'$ and $z = -\lfloor u / w \rfloor = \lceil (Y - w\,\anext - r') / w \rceil$. From the forward line, $Y - w\,\anext - r' = w\,\abefore - r$ with $0 \le r < w$, whose ceiling divided by $w$ is $\abefore$ exactly; and $\rho' = u - w\lfloor u / w \rfloor = w\,\abefore - (Y - w\,\anext - r') = r$. So the backward call returns $(\abefore, r)$ bit for bit, and the reversibility of every run needs no second function \cite[the direction]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

The whole interval is a bijection but for the click: the places of the interval (Section~\ref{sec:interval}) run backward in reverse order, each primitive by its own inverse, and the count's line by the same line with the current reversed (Section~\ref{sec:count}).

\subsection{The four acts and what they can form}\label{sec:acts}

Rule3's line is one act; declared coefficients make it four \cite[the four acts]{algebra}: (a) \emph{the read}, the line over the GameBoard with the six arrivals; (b) \emph{the one-Node step}, the line with the neighbours' reads declared $0$, $\anext + \abefore = (a / b)\,\anow$, the rotation by the angle whose doubled cosine is $a / b$ (Chebyshev's recurrence); with $a / b = 1$ and the coefficient on $\abefore$ declared $0$ it keeps a level, and with $a / b = 2$ from $(0, 1)$ it counts, $a_t = t$; (c) \emph{the division}, the line with $R = S = 0$, $w\,\anext + r' = \text{the numerator} + r$, Euclid's division with the remainder kept; (d) \emph{the load}, a declared integer in the numerator, how a count enters a level. A composition of the acts is a finite sequence of them, each with its coefficients (a pair, a weight, a wall, a load) declared for the world, applied to declared levels.

\begin{theorem}[what the acts can form]\label{th:acts}
Every composition of the four acts is a piecewise-linear function of the levels with integer slopes: a sum of linear forms and of floors of linear forms, composed. No such function is quadratic in the levels.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each act is a linear form of the levels or the floor of one; a floor of a linear form is piecewise linear with integer slopes; sums and compositions of piecewise-linear functions with integer slopes are of the same kind. The second difference of such a function in any one level is $0$ almost everywhere, while the second difference of a product $\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j$ in the pair $(\mathrm{now}_i, \mathrm{before}_j)$ is $1$.
\end{proof}

What the theorem separates: every product of two levels in the law is a \emph{booking}, a reading of a bilinear form with declared coefficients and no write (Section~\ref{sec:booking}); the one thing written from a product is the count, by the count's line, and that product is Rule3's own conserved current (Theorem~\ref{th:form}). \emph{The three tests of every rule:} a line enters the law only if it is generic (one primitive with declared integers, no family's name and no kind), vector (one of the acts on the state, no root and no float) and local (its own record and the six neighbours, nothing kept at a Node beyond the law's numbers). A hypothesis that needs more is stated under its own name, outside the law.

\subsection{The paces and the guard}\label{sec:paces}

The \emph{pace} of a Node for a family is what its reads make of the Node clock \cite[the paces]{algebra}:
\begin{align}
p_0 &= \Gamma - \sum_{\text{reads}} \bigl(\text{weight} \times \text{by} \times \text{the read family's time level}\bigr), \label{eq:pace}\\
p_a &= p_0 - \sum_{\text{reads}} \bigl(\text{the same}\bigr) - \sum_{\text{reads}} \bigl(\text{weight} \times \text{by} \times \text{its } aa \text{ component} \ddiv 2\bigr), \quad a = x, y, z, \notag
\end{align}
one division per read per axis, rounded at the read: the pace is a coefficient of the interval and no level, so no remainder is kept for it. A positive weight is a hollow (the read level slows the clock, an attraction); a negative weight is a hill; by ``$q$'' the weight is multiplied by the reading record's charge sign. A family with no reads steps at $p_0 = p_a = \Gamma$, the plain rule. The time level lowers the clock's pace $p_0$ once and each axis's pace $p_a$ once more, so a wave's speed along a Link falls by the level twice (the Link as well as the clock, as nature's light past a body bends by twice the clock's share) while a mode's rest rotation, from $S$ alone, falls by the level once.

\begin{proposition}[the guard]\label{prop:guard}
The step is stable at a Node exactly while $0 < p \le P$ with the edge
\[
P = \isqrt\bigl(2\,\den\,\Gamma^2 \ddiv (\den + \num)\bigr),
\]
$\Gamma$ for light and above $\Gamma$ where $\num < \den$; every pace at or below $\Gamma$ is inside it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The mode at wave number $\pi$ on every axis has $S_6(\anow) = -6\,\anow$, so its factor is $(S - 6R) / w = 2 - 2\,(1 + \num/\den)\,(p / \Gamma)^2$, which is $-2\,\num/\den$ at $p = \Gamma$ and falls below $-2$, the edge of Chebyshev's recurrence, once $p^2\,(\den + \num)$ exceeds $2\,\den\,\Gamma^2$; there the record grows without bound. $P$ is the largest integer pace at which $p^2\,(\den + \num) \le 2\,\den\,\Gamma^2$ holds. Below $0$ the pace is no clock.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The conserved form}\label{sec:form}

\begin{theorem}[the conserved form]\label{th:form}
Let the paces stand. The read matrix $2\,\num\,p_i^2$ is not symmetric where the paces differ, so the form carries the weight $1 / p_i^2$ on the Node terms and the plain current on the Links:
\[
E = \sum_i \frac{w\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - S_i\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i}{p_i^2} - 2\,\num \sum_i \mathrm{now}_i\,S_6(\mathrm{before})_i .
\]
$E$ is exact where the paces stand and changes by the work term where they move; with the rounding, each Node's remainder adds at most one unit of the level per interval to the walk of $E$. Its Link term, the plain current $\num\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j)$ through the Link $ij$, is the current of Rule3's own conservation law and the one product the click reads.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Without the rounding the line is $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} + \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} = \bM\,\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}$ with $\bM$ the matrix whose diagonal is $S_i / w$ and whose entries on the six Links are $R / w$. Let $\bD$ be the diagonal matrix of the weights $1 / p_i^2$; then $\bD\bM$ is symmetric, since $R_a = 2\,\num\,p_a^2$ at the reading Node and the weight $1 / p_i^2$ cancels it on both sides of every Link. For a symmetric $\bK = \bD\bM$ the quantity $Q(\mathbf x, \mathbf y) = \mathbf x \cdot \bD\mathbf x + \mathbf y \cdot \bD\mathbf y - \mathbf x \cdot \bK\mathbf y$ satisfies $Q(\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}) = Q(\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}})$: with $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} = \bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}$, $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} \cdot \bD\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} \cdot \bK\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} = (\bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}) \cdot \bD(\bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}) - (\bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}) \cdot \bD\bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} = \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} \cdot \bD\mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} \cdot \bD\bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}$, which is the other side. Multiplied by $w$ this is $E$ up to the Link term's sign convention \cite[the conserved form]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

$E$ is a GameBoard reading. Its Node term at a Node, $D_i = \mathrm{now}_i^2 - \mathrm{next}_i \times \mathrm{before}_i$, is the record's \emph{form} there, and the record's quanta at the Node are $D_i \ddiv T$: the count is the record's form, and this is how a body's well is laid (Section~\ref{sec:write}).

\subsection{The booking}\label{sec:booking}

\subsection{The count's line}\label{sec:count}

The \emph{family of clicks} is one family like every other: its level at a Node is the \emph{count} $c$, the number of quanta there; its wall is $T$, the quantum's action; its read is the current. At every Node,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
T\,c_{\mathrm{next}} + r' = T\,c_{\mathrm{now}} + \sum_j F_{ij} + r, \qquad 0 \le r' < T,
\end{equation}
the sum over the six Ports of the current into the Node over the interval, $r$ the remainder kept at the Node: Rule3 with the coefficient $1$ on each axis's inflow through its two Ports, $T$ on the count and the wall $T$. The inverse is the same line with the current reversed, exact.

\begin{theorem}[the count is conserved]\label{th:count}
Over any region of Nodes, $\sum (T c_i + r_i)$ changes in one interval only by the currents through the region's boundary Links.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Summing Eq.~\eqref{eq:count} over the region, each interior Link's current enters one Node's right side as $+F$ and its neighbour's as $-F$ and cancels; what remains is the sum over the boundary Links, a telescoping sum, exact in integers. On a periodic GameBoard with no face the sum is constant to the bit.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The velocity.} The record's wave number is conserved by the record's line, and the count's line is the continuity equation of the current in integers, so the count's centroid moves at the current's velocity, with no leak beyond the ladder's remainder; a body at rest has zero net current at every Node in the mean and its count stays in place. \subsection{The ladder}\label{sec:ladder}

At a detector the click is read by the \emph{ladder}, the same division act on a record's one-way inward flux \cite[the ladder]{algebra}. The data: the record's residue $u$ (its remainder at its giving Node), its norm $T$, its wheel $V$, hence its threshold $\theta = (2u + 1)\,T / (2V)$, fixed at the giving; the named detectors in their declared order with the face last. At every interval each Node $i$ of a detector receives the one-way inward flux $f_i(t) \ge 0$ of the record through its Ports (a Link between two Nodes of one detector is not a Port and carries no offer); the record's running total is $C(t) = C(t - 1) + \sum_i f_i(t)$. The click is at the first interval $t^*$ with $C(t^*) \ge \theta$, at the Node whose segment of that interval's increment, laid in the ladder's order, contains $\theta - C(t^* - 1)$; there the record ends, whole. The rungs are the division act, $b_k = (2 V C_k + \text{Total}) \ddiv (2\,\text{Total})$.

\begin{theorem}[one quantum, one click]\label{th:oneclick}
$\theta$ is crossed exactly once.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$C$ is non-decreasing, $\theta < T$, and the total offered with the face last is at least $T$ (what leaves offers everything at the face), so $C$ crosses $\theta$ once; the deletion of the record at once leaves nothing to cross it again.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Born's rule]\label{th:born}
Let $u$ be spread evenly over $\{0, \ldots, V - 1\}$; then the probability that the click lands at Node $i$ is $C_i(\infty) / T$, the detector's share of the record's total inward flux, whatever the time profile.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\theta$ is then spread evenly over $V$ points in $[0, T)$. The segments $[C(t - 1) + \sum_{j < i} f_j(t),\ C(t - 1) + \sum_{j \le i} f_j(t))$, one per (interval, Node), partition $[0, C(\infty))$ and have the lengths $f_i(t)$; so the probability of the click at interval $t$ at Node $i$ is $f_i(t) / T$ to the grain $1 / V$, and summing over $t$ gives the share. The counts over many records are binomial about the shares, since the residues are equidistributed and not a permutation.
\end{proof}

The residue is the rule's own remainder at the giving Node, never declared and never drawn: chance lives in the remainders, which stay at the Nodes, bounded, never reset and never erased. A detector is one connected region of Nodes with one name; separate places are separate names. A detector \emph{set} is one Node of the count's line with its Ports the set's outer Ports, the sum of its Nodes' lines with one remainder; Born's rule is the shares of the current among the set's Nodes.

\subsection{The pair record and Bell's correlation}\label{sec:pair}

\begin{proposition}[the pair's shares]\label{prop:bell}
With $\bM$ the identity at the giving, the first click lands on either of its side's two sets with the share $1/2$ at every setting; the second click lands on the set of the same index with the share $\cos^2(a - b)$ and on the other with $\sin^2(a - b)$, whichever label clicks first. So over all pairs
\[
E(a, b) = \cos^2(a - b) - \sin^2(a - b) = \cos 2(a - b),
\]
each side's marginal is $1/2$ at every setting of the other side (no signalling), and the Clauser--Horne--Shimony--Holt sum \cite{chsh1969} at the settings $0$, $\pi/4$ for one side and $\pi/8$, $3\pi/8$ for the other is $S = 2\sqrt 2 = 2.83$, Tsirelson's bound \cite{tsirelson1980}, derived from the local law with the click's one non-local act alone.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The rows of the rotation $\bR(-a)$ are two unit vectors, so each row's booking is one half of the label's norm: the first click's shares are $1/2$ and $1/2$. After the click of the row $i$ the record's level is that row, the unit vector at the angle $-a + i\,\pi/2$; the second polariser applies $\bR(-b)$, whose components on the two sets are $\cos(a - b)$ and $\pm\sin(a - b)$ up to the index's quarter turn, so the shares are their squares. $E$ is the difference of the shares of the same and the other index. Summed over the first click's index at $1/2$ each, the second side's marginal is $\frac12(\cos^2 + \sin^2) = \frac12$, whatever $a$; the first side's is $1/2$ by the first step, whatever $b$. The sum $S = E(a, b) - E(a, b') + E(a', b) + E(a', b')$ at $a = 0$, $a' = \pi/4$, $b = \pi/8$, $b' = 3\pi/8$ has $E(0, \pi/8) = E(\pi/4, \pi/8) = E(\pi/4, 3\pi/8) = \cos(\pi/4)$ and $E(0, 3\pi/8) = \cos(3\pi/4) = -\cos(\pi/4)$, which enters with the minus sign, so $S = 4\cos(\pi/4) = 2\sqrt 2$.
\end{proof}

The correlation travels in the record alone, by Rule3, one Link per interval; the click ends one label everywhere at once and carries no information, since each side's marginal does not depend on the other's setting: the law is local in its transport and non-local in the click only, as Bell's theorem requires of any account that reaches $2\sqrt 2$ \cite{bell1964}. The pairs are the host's coincidence reading, not law: the two clicks' intervals within a window declared beside the world, as Aspect's \cite{aspect1982}; the law has no coincidence set and no joint click, since no uniform time of events exists on the GameBoard. With switching polarisers, each side's angle is the one in force at the label's passage, by the polariser's own clock, the count act on the body. \section{The engine and how to use it}\label{sec:engine}

The engine, Universe24 \cite{zenodo}, is the integer simulator of the law. The whole physics is Rule3 at every Node; every primitive of the law (the hold, the source, the giving, the start, the recoil, the click, the transport, the polariser, the crystal) is one folder found by its name, with a card that declares its place in the interval, what it reads and what it writes, and one function in whole integers with its own remainders; the code holds no number, no formula, no family's name and no flag, and every physical value comes from three files.

\paragraph{The three files.} \emph{The universe file} declares the families: each a pair $[m, \Gamma]$ (the mass one integer over the Node clock), what it holds (the content or the sign, with the row's divisor), and nothing the rule fixes, since the reads, the ranks and the clicks come from the rule; and the universe's integers ($\Gamma$, the quantum's action $T$, the width of the machine's integers, the twist table). \emph{The world file} declares the GameBoard (its sides, each axis periodic or open), the universe it names, the bodies by their Nodes and counts (an emitter naming its given family, a crystal, a polariser by their keys), the detectors as sets on bodies' Nodes and the faces, and the readings; a body's standing record is written beside the world by the generator as the fixed point of its binding row. \emph{The expectation file}, written before any run, holds the DETECTOR rows (a detector's count, its first click or its mean interval, each with a band) and the GAMEBOARD rows (a body's centre, momentum, count or width at an interval, diagnostics never compared with nature).

\paragraph{A run.} A run is headless: \texttt{PYTHONPATH=src python tools/run\_inputs.py --out runs --jobs 4 <world>.json} writes one output file per world with the verdict (LAWFUL; REFUSED with the key or the Node refused; LEAK when a family moved with no source), every click with its detector, interval, record and giver, the counts per detector, and MATCH or MISS per row of the expectation. A refusal names its key; there is no silent default and no draw. The step is a bijection but for the click, and the inverse step is a tool of the same engine.

\paragraph{To test a hypothesis.} Write its line in the law's document first, under its own name; make one folder with its card, its function and its test beside it; write the world and its blind expectation; run the repository's check, under which every recorded world must come out bit for bit while the new primitive is undeclared in the files, and the gates hold (the language, the shape of the code, the genericity on drawn universes, the documents' caps, the ownership of every area); then run the world once. A run that lands outside the band is a finding that names a missing line, and no body's numbers are patched to meet it. \emph{The standing criterion} the generator reads a body by: between two whole cycles of its rotation the form $D_i$ returns to itself at every Node of the region within one quantum, the region every Node whose form is at least a hundredth of a quantum, and nothing inside it purged; a reading of the centre is a reading and no stop.

\section{Where this model stands among discrete models}\label{app:related}

Deterministic lattice models of physics are old: the lattice gases of Hardy, de Pazzis and Pomeau \cite{hpp1976} and of Frisch, Hasslacher and Pomeau \cite{fhp1986} recover hydrodynamics from Boolean collisions; the quantum cellular automata of Bialynicki-Birula \cite{bialynicki1994}, Meyer \cite{meyer1996} and Arrighi \cite{arrighi2019} step a unitary state on a lattice. This model differs from both in its state and in its measurement: the state is integer levels with a remainder per Node and no complex amplitude, and the measurement is a division of the law and no projection. It differs from the pilot-wave account \cite{bohm1952, dgz1992} in that no particle position is kept beside the wave: the count follows the record's current by the count's line, and a body's position is its count's centroid. It differs from spontaneous-collapse models \cite{grw1980} in that the click's residue is the rule's own remainder and no stochastic term. In the ontological-models language \cite{spekkens2007, hs2010} the record is the ontic state and the click its one read; the correlation $2\sqrt 2$ is reached, and Popescu and Rohrlich's $4$ \cite{pr1994} is not, since the second label's shares are a rotation's squares.
