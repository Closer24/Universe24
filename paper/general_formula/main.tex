\title[Universe24: an integer cellular automaton read through clicks]{Universe24: an integer cellular automaton read through clicks, the formulas it shares with nature and the ones it adds}
\author*{\fnm{Alon} \sur{Gonen}}\email{alon@defounder.ai}
\affil{\orgname{Independent researcher}, \city{Haifa}, \country{Israel}}

\abstract{Universe24 is computed by an integer cellular automaton: a cubic lattice of Nodes, two integer levels per field, stepped by one recurrence, Rule3, combining a Node's present, past and six neighbours through one integer division, reversibly bit for bit and covariantly under the cube's group of 48. The lattice is read only through declared counting regions, the NodeDetectors, whose clicks are the only measurements: a measurement is the meeting at a NodeDetector of the field propagated forward from its source with the transition read backward from the click. From the recurrence follow, in lattice form, the Klein-Gordon equation with Schr\"odinger's as its slow limit, Planck's relation and, under one named assumption fixed by two measurements, the exponential weak-field metric with $\gamma = \beta = 1$. A field's rational pair $[\num, \den]$, $\cos\omega_0 = \num / \den$, fixes its inertial mass $m^* = 3\tan\omega_0$ and the post-Newtonian parameters $\gamma_K = \den / \num$ and $\beta_K = (\den + \num) / (2\num)$, closing to $1$ with the gap. The lattice's own terms are predictions under their conditions, a black-hole shadow radius $2e\,m$, light's gravitational source at $E / c^2$ under the static kernel, the metric's second order. Bell's and Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) correlations follow from a declared counting rule, not from the lattice. The misses are named, the preferred-frame parameters $\alpha_1 = -8$ and, for a static source, $\alpha_2 = -1$, excluded by observation, no frame dragging and a decelerating cosmology. Universe24 is one world of the method, which can build others.}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The question and the claim}\label{sec:question}

This paper is about one method of computing physics. A cubic lattice of Nodes is stepped by one local rule in whole numbers, Rule3, which is exactly reversible, and it is read only through clicks, the reports of declared counting regions that the model calls NodeDetectors. In the words of the title: the integer cellular automaton is the lattice stepped by Rule3; Universe24 is its clicks, written by the NodeDetectors from the fields that its configuration files declare; the formulas it shares with nature are the known formulas reached from the one line in lattice form, the Klein-Gordon equation with Schr\"odinger's slow limit, Planck's relation in the band's form and, under one named assumption, the exponential weak-field metric of gravity (Section~\ref{sec:shared}); and the ones it adds are the lattice's own terms, which depart from the continuum or have no counterpart there (Section~\ref{sec:added}). The paper has one claim: that Universe24 helps compute things in nature. Its content is what the model computes of nature set against measurement, each quantity with its status and its number, the hits and the misses alike; the method is how it computes and not the claim. The paper does not claim that nature is such a lattice.

The criterion is the same throughout: a derived, click-fenced formula that disagrees with a measurement beyond its stated order refutes the model at that formula, and Section~\ref{sec:limits} lists the refutations found. Universe24 is one world built by the paper's method: one declared configuration file, one run read by its NodeDetectors (the two slits, Section~\ref{sec:slits}), and the forms of Sections~\ref{sec:builds} and~\ref{sec:formulas} derived from the law's lines and computed from no run. Other worlds closer to nature can be built by the same method, and building one needs the calculations named in Section~\ref{sec:closer}, which this work does not do.

The paper starts from one statement and never contradicts it (an assumption, Table~\ref{tab:claims}): Universe24 is the meeting of the past with the future; a click is that meeting, the one measurement there is in the model; and in it only clicks are seen. A Node holds a field's present and its past, two integer levels, and the rule makes the field's future from these two and from the six neighbouring Nodes. Run forward from the source Node of its initial data, the split events are the future, every path at once; read backward from a click that happened, by the same rule, they are the past, and through two slits both of them pass through both gaps. A click is where the two meet at a declared NodeDetector, and which meeting happens is the one thing the model does not determine, the one non-local element of the description. What looks like a wave is the future; what looks like a particle is the present, the click; the track is the past.

\subsection{What the paper adds, and its precursors}\label{sec:adds}

Three things, each with its status in Table~\ref{tab:claims}: the method, one line of integer arithmetic stepped reversibly on a lattice and read only through clicks, with measurement as the meeting, at a NodeDetector, of the future with the past, the NodeDetector being the one place of the random draw; the forms it reaches, the continuum's known formulas in lattice form from that one line, each with its inputs and its status (Table~\ref{tab:shared}), and the lattice's own, each a prediction at the measurement its row names (Table~\ref{tab:adds}); and the method of claim, every statement carrying its status and its fence, every input and every miss named, and the derivations, the files and the implementation public.

The idea that the physical world is a discrete computation goes back to Zuse \cite{zuse}; Toffoli and Margolus developed it as reversible cellular automata \cite{toffoli}, and 't Hooft as a deterministic foundation beneath quantum theory \cite{thooft}. The step of the present rule is an integer recurrence on two levels, a second-order cellular automaton in the sense of Toffoli and Margolus \cite{toffoli}, whose alphabet is the integers of the declared width with one Euclidean division whose remainder stays at the Node. Feynman noted that a local classical computer reproduces no correlations beyond Bell's bound \cite{feynman}; the model agrees and places the excess in the declared counting rule of the click, not in the lattice (Section~\ref{sec:bell}). Time-symmetric accounts of measurement, Wheeler and Feynman's absorber \cite{wheeler}, Cramer's transaction \cite{cramer}, the two-state vector formalism \cite{vaidman} and Wharton and Argaman's result on locally mediated reformulations \cite{wharton} are the precursors of the click as a meeting; here nothing on the lattice depends on a setting, both directions are one integer line exact to the bit, and the choice among the meetings is the one non-local element. The forms in the model's potential $U$ (Section~\ref{sec:paces}) are the exponential metric's, reached before by a position-dependent index on flat space \cite{yilmaz}; the Link phases of Section~\ref{sec:shared} are a lattice gauge field in the sense of \cite{wilson}; a random walk in integers on a lattice that reproduces Schr\"odinger's statistics is \cite{sciarretta}, where here the lattice is deterministic and the one draw is the click's. The formulas the title names as shared with nature are the continuum limit's, reached here from Rule3 under the one assumption; the capture radius of the shadow, $2e\,m$, is the exponential metric's own form, its photon sphere at $r = 2m$ and its critical impact parameter $2e\,m$ \cite[Section 7]{boonserm}, and the paper's own is the route to that metric from Rule3 and the comparison with the Event Horizon Telescope's shadows. At the rule's own matter pair the model does not describe the solar system's gravity (Section~\ref{sec:added}). A lattice breaks Lorentz invariance at order one unless its terms are tuned, with the bounds reviewed in \cite{mattingly}; the paper's answer is that the model has no loops and that the matter band's factors close with its gap under the Link's bound (Section~\ref{sec:shared}).

\subsection{Why the world is read in clicks, and never on the lattice}\label{sec:whyclicks}

In the model, whoever sees is itself a cluster of bound states on the lattice, a Node reads its six Ports and nothing else, and beyond the cluster's tail nothing reaches it except what a NodeDetector reports, one quantum credited at a meeting; what stands on the lattice between clicks is not a fact of the world but its possibilities, and a fact is a realised event, a quantum credited at a click. So the paper has three words and keeps them apart. The lattice is the lattice stepped by Rule3; it computes. The NodeDetector is the gate between the lattice and Universe24: it reads the lattice and writes the clicks that happened. Universe24 is those clicks, the world of the model. A lattice reading, a level, a support, a count at a Node, is a diagnostic of the computation and is labelled so, and only a click pattern is compared with a measured pattern of nature. A deterministic, reversible integer lattice read only through clicks gives the quantum statistics computed here, one quantum per click, the two slits' pattern and Bell's values, from a wave on the lattice and a declared counting rule; Born's rule is put in, and the Hilbert space beyond one quantum, spin and the many-body atom are not reached (derived for the wave, exclusivity and interference; an assumption for the counting rule, the draw and the conditional; the misses by name, Section~\ref{sec:limits}). The backward reading fixes no number that the forward counting rule does not: the joint energy is computed from the forward configurations alone (Section~\ref{sec:click}), the click is the same click read from its outcome, and the meeting of the two quanta is the model's definition of a click, not a second computation.

\section{The model and the rule}\label{sec:model}

The physical location is a \emph{Node}, and its complete local information is its NodeState: for every field and every part (a part one level pair of a field: a potential field's time and axis parts, a bound state's own levels, a pair field's two lines), two integer levels, the present and the past, and one remainder, the potential fields' levels being the potential level a Node holds for the other fields to read; nothing else is kept at a Node, and every count, current, charge or stress is a reading computed from these numbers when it is needed. Nodes are joined by \emph{Links} through six directional \emph{Ports} per Node, and the cubic array of Nodes is the lattice. A \emph{field} (the long version's family) is one kind of physical value with its rational pair and its shape, and its \emph{configuration} is its levels over the lattice. A \emph{pace} is the step factor of a Node (its clock) or of a Link (its ruler). A \emph{NodeDetector} is the model's counter, a connected region of Nodes declared in the configuration file, and its \emph{click} is one quantum credited to it, the one measurement.

\subsection{The lattice and its group of 48}\label{sec:lattice}

The lattice is a box of $N_x \times N_y \times N_z$ Nodes, each joined to its six neighbours by Links through its Ports $\pm x$, $\pm y$, $\pm z$. An axis is declared periodic, open (a level beyond an open face is read as $0$) or folded (one layer, whose two Ports return the Node itself); an inner face is a plane of Nodes declared closed except for declared gaps, a wall with slits; and a face may be declared receding (an assumption), the box growing by a layer of zeros whenever a nonzero level reaches the Node next to it, so that a run on such a box is a run on the unbounded lattice, with no cosmological redshift since nothing stretches.

The symmetry group of the cube has $48$ elements, the $24$ rotations and, with the reflections, the signed permutations of the three axes: $O_h$, the group of the octahedron of the Nodes one interval away (Fig.~\ref{fig:lattice}), with the translations the space group of the simple cubic lattice. It acts by permuting the Nodes and their Ports and carries every level pair with its Node, and Rule3 commutes with it, since its line reads the six Ports symmetrically and names no axis. Under the group the form $D$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law} is a scalar, even under the time reversal, which exchanges a field's present and its past and carries the step to its inverse; the current $F$ is a vector, odd under it; the Wronskian $W$ is a scalar under the cube's $48$, odd under the time reversal and under the plane's reflection; and the click is a scalar, naming a region and an interval, never a Node or an axis. No boost, no rotation beyond the cube's $24$ and no scaling is in the group, so Lorentz's group and the continuum's rotations are no symmetries of the lattice, and what the paper finds of the moving clock it finds as a property of the clicks (a theorem; S.4).

\subsection{Rule3 and the vacuum band}\label{sec:rule3}

\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}}
In the first line $\anow$, $\abefore$ and $\anext$ are the field's level at the Node now, one interval before and one interval later; $\mathrm{arr}_j$ is the level of the neighbour $j$, the six Ports summed; $r$ and $r'$ are the remainder kept at the Node before and after the step; $\Gamma$ is the Node clock; $[\num, \den]$ is the field's pair, $\cos\omega_0 = \num / \den$ its rotation per interval at wave number zero; $p_0$ is the Node's clock pace, $p_i$ its own pace and $p_a(i, j)$ the pace of the Link through the Port, the Node's pace times the Link's factor $q_{ij} / \Gamma$; at the vacuum's paces the two Links of an axis share one $R_a = 2\,\num\,\Gamma^2$ and $S = 0$, and $\ddiv$ is floor division. One whole number is formed and divided once by the wall $w$; the quotient is the next level, the remainder stays at the Node, nothing is rounded away, and the step back is the same division (Theorem~\ref{th:direction}). The second line is the only way a potential field's level (the long version's holder: a field that holds a value for others to read) is ever written, the sourcing fields' quantities $q_f$ at the Node, each a form $D_f$, a Wronskian $W_f$ or a stress $T_{aa,f}$, weighted by $w_f$, summed with the part's one remainder and divided by the part's wall $E$ (a divisor, not the form $E$ of Section~\ref{sec:form}), $E_s T$ for a time part and $E_s \Wc$ for an axis part. The third line is what is read from a configuration and never written: its form $D_i$, which sources the potential fields; its Wronskian $W_i$, its charge (for a plane field $\mathrm{re}$ and $\mathrm{im}$ are its two level pairs); and its current $F_{ij}$ into the Node $i$ from its neighbour $j$. A NodeDetector reads that current through its boundary in quanta of the field's unit $\Wc = 3\,\den\,T$, $T$ the quantum's norm, one quantum's inflow being $\Wc\sin\omega_0$ in the current's unit and its energy $T\sin\omega_0$ in the quantum's, and a massless field's quantum $\Wc$ itself.

At the vacuum's paces the line reads $\anext + \abefore = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a (\mathrm{arr}_{+a} + \mathrm{arr}_{-a})$, whose rotation at the wave vector $\mathbf k$ is $\cos\omega = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a \cos k_a$, $\cos\omega_0 = \num / \den$ at wave number zero. For the massless pair $[1, 1]$ along one axis $\cos\omega = (2 + \cos k) / 3$, so $\omega = |k| / \sqrt 3$ as $k \to 0$, and along the cube's diagonal $\cos\omega = \cos k$ exactly, with no dispersion (S.1): light's speed at long wavelength is $1 / \sqrt 3$ Link per interval, below the causal bound of one Link per interval, and its group speed falls at shorter wavelengths as $1 - 0.083\,k^2$. In nature's names the vacuum's line is the discrete Klein-Gordon equation, exact but for the remainder term, with $c_K^2 = \num / (3\,\den)$ and $m^2 = 2(1 - \num / \den)$, the rest rotation the mass, and its slow limit at small wave number is Schr\"odinger's equation with the inertia $m^* = 3\tan\omega_0$ under three named conditions (S.63); $m^* c_m^2 = \omega_0$ exactly for every pair, with $c_m^2 = \omega_0 / (3\tan\omega_0)$ the band's kinetic scale, the model's $E = mc^2$ read in three click numbers, nature's its small-gap limit, the departure $\omega_0 / \tan\omega_0$, $1 - \omega_0^2 / 3$ to the order $\omega_0^2$ (S.18, S.21). At the vacuum's pace Rule3 is the leapfrog scheme of the Klein-Gordon equation, exact in the variable $s = 2\sin(\omega / 2)$; what the law adds is one rational $[\num, \den]$ that fixes mass and speed together, departures that are predictions with bounds (Section~\ref{sec:shared}), the integers with the remainder, and the paces.

\subsection{The paces and the one assumption}\label{sec:paces}

A potential field enters a field's step by changing the field's paces at a Node, the coefficients of Rule3 there:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paces}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
Here $c_i$ is the potential level the field reads at the Node (the long version's content), the sum over its reads of the weight times the read field's time level, and $U = c / \Gamma$ is the same quantity in the potential's unit; the clock composes, each unit of the level slowing it by the same factor $1 - 1 / \Gamma$, with the law's integer roundings in S.34, and the Link's stress $t_a(i, j)$ (the long version's tension, the level the Link holds), one number per Link from the parts at its two ends (S.7), makes the Link's pace $\Gamma - 2c_i - t_a$ to first order. Nothing in Rule3 fixes how a level enters the paces. \section{Exact properties}\label{sec:theorems}

This section states what holds exactly on every lattice, each item proved in S.48: the reversibility of the rule, the kind of function its acts can form, the conserved form with its current, the count as a reading of the configuration (S.5, S.6), the Wronskian as the charge (S.9) and the three exact bands.

\subsection{Reversibility}\label{sec:direction}

Let $\sigma$ be the direction, $+1$ forward and $-1$ backward, $\ddiv$ and $\dmod$ the floor division and its remainder in $[0, w)$, and $\rho$ the remainder carried in. With $u = \sigma(\sum_j R_{ij}\,\mathrm{arr}_j + S\,x - w\,y) + \rho$, $z = \sigma\,(u \ddiv w)$ and $\rho' = u \dmod w$, the forward call with $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \abefore, r)$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\anext, r')$, and the backward call with $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \anext, r')$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\abefore, r)$.

\begin{theorem}[the direction]\label{th:direction}
The backward call returns $(\abefore, r)$ bit for bit; the reversibility of every step needs no second function.
\end{theorem}

A NodeDetector's write is outside the reversal, as the initial data are; the whole run goes back, $N$ intervals forward and $N$ back returning every array of every field bit for bit, provided every number an act read at an interval's start is one the state after the interval still holds. The implementation checks this as a test, a file run forward and back with every array compared at every interval; on the two slits' file it reports \textsc{match} over $40$ intervals at the commit 1fe3790a, a file whose write stays under one level and whose NodeDetectors write nothing (computed, a run read by \texttt{back\_in\_time.py}). The condition rules out one read: a coefficient read from a Node's own state across a step cannot be recovered at the step back, so a configuration's rotation sense is never read at a Node, and the charge potential is read by the configuration's shape, a plane or a line (Section~\ref{sec:sign}).

\subsection{What the acts can form}\label{sec:acts}

Rule3's line is one act, and declared coefficients make four special cases of it, which the law also calls acts: the read, the line with its six arrivals; the one-Node step, the arrivals declared $0$, which rotates a level by Chebyshev's recurrence; the division, $R = S = 0$, Euclid's division with the remainder kept; and the load, a declared integer in the numerator, which is how a count enters a level.

\begin{theorem}[what the acts can form, on the infinite lattice, for one field at fixed coefficients]\label{th:acts}
Every fixed finite composition of the line's four acts with fixed coefficients is an affine form of one field's levels plus a term bounded by a constant of the composition, since a floor is its argument less a fraction in $[0, 1)$; so no product of two of its levels is written by the step on the whole lattice. The products across fields enter through the coefficients the read turns from the held levels: the read multiplies. On a bounded domain every integer function is a finite sum of floors of affine forms, and the theorem says nothing there.
\end{theorem}

So every product of two levels is a reading and never a write by the step, and the three readings of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law} are the only products the law takes. A line enters the law only if it passes three tests, generic (one primitive with declared integers, no field's name), vector (an act on the whole state, with no root and no floating-point number) and local (its own configuration and the six neighbours); a line that needs more is a hypothesis under its own name.

\subsection{The conserved form}\label{sec:form}

The read coefficient $R_a(i \to j) = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ carries the Node's factor and the Link's; where the paces differ the Node's factor is not symmetric and the Link's is, so the conserved form carries the weight $1 / p_i^2$ on its Node terms and the Link's factor on its Link terms:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:form}
\boxed{E = \sum_i \frac{w\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - S_i\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i}{p_i^2} - 2\,\num\sum_i \sum_{j \sim i} \frac{q_{ij}^2}{\Gamma^2}\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[the conserved form]\label{th:form}
At constant paces for which a positive Node weight $d_i$ with $d_i R_a(i \to j) = d_j R_a(j \to i)$ on every Link exists, $E$ with the weights $d_i$ is exact under the line without the rounding. Under the law's read coefficient, with $q_{ij} = q_{ji}$ and $p_i = p_0(c_i)^2 / \Gamma$, the weight $d_i = 1 / p_i^2$ cancels the Node's factor on both sides of every Link. So the weighted form, with $\bM$ the matrix of Rule3's step, $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} + \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} = \bM\,\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}$, $\bD = \mathrm{diag}(1 / p_i^2)$ and $\bD\bM$ symmetric at every interval, is exact for any static potential levels and static Link stresses. Its Link term carries the Link's factor, so the current of Rule3's own conservation law through the Link $ij$ is $\num\,(q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2)\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j)$, the plain current $F_{ij}$ where no stress stands; the form with the plain current holds only where no stress stands, and with a stress it is not conserved (two witnesses, S.32).
