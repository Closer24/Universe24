\begin{abstract}
The world is the meeting of the past and the future: the meeting is a click, and an observer sees only clicks. One local rule in whole numbers, Rule3, steps every Node of a cubic lattice from its present, its past and its six neighbours by one Euclidean division whose remainder stays at the Node; every run is exactly reversible. The lattice holds only events, split to the six neighbours at every Node: run forward from an emitter they are the future, every path at once; read backward from a click, the past. A click is their meeting at a declared detector, the one non-local element: the wave is the future, the click the present, the track the past. A family enters as one row, its pair and its shape, with as few free numbers as the rule allows. Gravity is a level that slows a Node's clock once and its Links twice; two features were fitted to light's bending and the perihelion, and against the potential Kepler's orbits measure the same forms reach Einstein's only as the matter family's gap closes, which bounds that gap. Lorentz's factor at a body's own light speed and free fall's universality follow unfitted; the magnetic force between moving charges does not. Every formula is fenced as a lattice reading or a click formula. Running no experiment, we derive the two-slit fringe row and, under a declared credit rule, Bell's correlation with the sum $2\sqrt 2$, where every local credit gives at most $2$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{What our world is.} This paper starts from one statement and never contradicts it (\claimmark{assumption}): the world is the meeting of the past and the future; that meeting is a click; and an observer sees only clicks. We give the statement an exact form. The model is a cubic lattice of Nodes on which one local rule in whole numbers acts at every Node in every interval of time. A Node holds a record's present and its past, two integer levels, and the rule makes the record's future from the two and from the six neighbouring Nodes. The lattice holds nothing but events that split: at every Node a level splits by the rule to the six neighbours, and the record is the sum of the split events. Run forward from an emitter, the split events are the future, every path at once. Read backward from a realised click by the same rule, they are the past, and through two slits both pass both gaps. A click is where the two meet at a declared detector. Which meeting is realised is the one thing the model does not determine, and we name it as the one non-local element of the description (Section~\ref{sec:meeting}). What appears as a wave is the future, the record run forward with every possibility; what appears as a particle is the present, the click; and the track is the past (Section~\ref{sec:nowaves}). We hold this to be the least computation that shows nature's clicks: the least state, the least non-locality and the least randomness, in bounded integers, locally and reversibly. That is a statement of economy and not of why nature is so, which we do not claim to know.

\paragraph{The rule in one line.} Everything in the paper follows from one line of integer arithmetic, Rule3, together with one write and three readings. At every Node, in every interval and for every family of physical values \cite[the line; the booking; the primitives]{algebra}:
{\small\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
&\text{Rule3:} && w\,\anext + r' = \textstyle\sum_{a \in \{x, y, z\}} R_a\,(\mathrm{arr}_{+a} + \mathrm{arr}_{-a}) + S\,\anow - w\,\abefore + r, \quad 0 \le r' < w,\\
&&& w = 6\,\den\,\Gamma^2, \quad R_a = 2\,\num\,p_a^2,\\
&&& S = 12\,\den\,\Gamma^2 - 12\,(\den - \num)\,p_0^2 - 4\,\num\,(p_x^2 + p_y^2 + p_z^2);\\
&\text{the one write:} && \text{a held part at a Node gains } \Bigl(\textstyle\sum_f w_f\,q_f(\text{Node}) + r\Bigr) \ddiv E, \text{ the remainder } r \text{ kept there};\\
&\text{the readings:} && D_i = \mathrm{now}_i^2 - \mathrm{next}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i, \qquad W_i = \mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{before}} - \mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{before}},\\
&&& F_{ij} = \num\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j).
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}}
In the first line $\anow$, $\abefore$ and $\anext$ are the family's level at the Node now, one interval before and one interval later: the present, the past and the future. $\mathrm{arr}_{\pm a}$ is the arrival through the Port $\pm a$, the neighbour's level. $r$ and $r'$ are the remainder kept at the Node before and after the step. $\Gamma$ is the Node clock, the pace of an empty Node. $[\num, \den]$ is the family's pair, and $\cos\omega_0 = \num / \den$ is its rotation per interval at wave number zero. $p_0$ is the Node's clock pace and $p_a$ its pace along the axis $a$; both are the Node clock lowered by the field levels the family reads there (Section~\ref{sec:paces}). $\ddiv$ is the floor division. The second line is the only way a field's level is ever written: the sum over the sourcing families $f$ of each family's own quantity $q_f$ at the Node, weighted by $w_f$, plus the part's one remainder, divided by the part's wall $E$. The third line is what is read from a record and never written: its form $D_i$, which sources the fields; the Wronskian $W_i$ of its two level pairs, its charge; and its current $F_{ij}$ into the Node $i$ from its neighbour $j$, the conserved flux of Rule3, which a detector reads through its boundary in quanta of $\Wc = 3\,\den\,T$, with $T$ the quantum's action. Everything else is a number in a file.

\paragraph{What the paper contributes.} We state the rule and prove what is exact about it on the lattice: every run is reversible to the bit, the rule conserves a quadratic form whose Link term is a current, and the flux of momentum is the wave's own stress (Section~\ref{sec:theorems}). We formulate measurement as the meeting of the forward and the backward record at a declared detector, with the record never ending and the one non-local element named (Section~\ref{sec:click}). We derive the families of physical values as bands of the rule, each entered as one row of a file, and choose a universe with as few free numbers as the rule allows (Section~\ref{sec:families}). We show that bodies are fixed points of the rule and prove a stability theorem for them (Section~\ref{sec:bodies}). We derive the weak-field forms of gravity and Lorentz's factor from the rule's coefficients, mark each by what it rests on, and state them both in the model's own potential and in the potential that nature's tests of gravity read (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). We state what a detector reads of a laid record, of a pair and of a body as formulas of clicks, each with its status (Sections~\ref{sec:click} and~\ref{sec:bodies}). We state the implementation's two gates, the two slits and Bell's, as derivations of what each must give, without running either (Section~\ref{sec:slits}).

\paragraph{How to read the paper.} Every claim carries one of six marks. \claimmark{Theorem}: proved for every lattice. \claimmark{Derived}: obtained from theorems by argument. \claimmark{Computed}: a number obtained from the rule's lines before any run. \claimmark{Assumption}: a line held by decision and named where it is used. \claimmark{Hypothesis}: a line outside the model, under its own name, with what would decide it. \claimmark{Experiment}: a number the model does not fix, with the experiment named that would fix it, what is measured against which number of the model and with which instrument; the paper runs none. A result that rests on an input chosen with that result known is also marked \claimmark{fitted}; it shows consistency, not a prediction. Every formula carries, beside its mark, one of two labels, its fence. \fence{GameBoard}: a reading of the lattice's own numbers that no instrument reports, the levels, the form, the Wronskian, the currents, the paces, the bands and the tensions, valid on the lattice and a diagnostic. \fence{Clicks}: a formula of what a detector reports, the share $Q_R / \Wc$ on the forward orbit (Section~\ref{sec:meeting}) or a ratio of two such, the only kind that nature can be set beside. A GameBoard formula is a step toward a click formula and never a claim about nature. Only a detector's click is a measurement. A number read off the lattice's state is a diagnostic and is labelled so; it is never compared with nature. No number from a simulation run appears in this paper. \paragraph{The method of derivation.} Every derivation of the paper follows six steps. It starts from Rule3's line alone, the reader's band at the paces its fields give, the clock once and the Link twice; no structure is written before its band is computed. Its source is a field's static rest, the six Ports' Green's function with a body's well as the one number. Its bridge to nature is a body's clicks and nothing else: a clock is a bound body's rotation reading the content once, a source is a body's well, a detector is a body, and an orbit is a body's chain of clicks. Every observable is a formula of clicks. Every form is re-derived whenever a line changes, and what does not come out is named; two independent derivations precede any line of the law. Every result carries its status and enters the paper as a derivation, never as a run's result \cite[algebra first; the paper]{decisions}.

\paragraph{The words.} The physical location is a \emph{Node}, and its complete local information is its \emph{NodeState}. Nodes are joined by \emph{Links} through six directional \emph{Ports} per Node, and the lattice of Nodes is the \emph{GameBoard} (Fig.~\ref{fig:lattice}). A local change is an \emph{Event}, and there is one local rule, Rule3. A \emph{family} is one kind of physical value with its pair and what it holds. Its \emph{record} is its levels over the GameBoard, two per Node with a remainder. A family of quanta has a \emph{dimension}, $1$ for one real line and $2$ for a plane of two lines; a part of a record, one level pair with its remainder, is what the implementation calls a line. A \emph{body} is the set of Nodes on which a family's record stands bound; its quanta are the record's share there, and it has no law of its own. A \emph{message} is a travelling record laid by the world, such as an emitter's packet of light. A \emph{detector} is a region of Nodes declared in the file to report, and its \emph{click} is one quantum credited to it from the current it saw through its boundary; the click is the one measurement. An \emph{interval} is one step of the whole GameBoard. A family that holds a value for others to read is a \emph{field family}, or \emph{holder}: the holder of the content slows the clocks of the families that read it, and the holder of the sign carries the charge's field \cite[the objects]{algebra}\cite[the words]{engine}.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Reversible cellular automata and digital physics.} The idea that the physical world is a discrete computation goes back to Zuse \cite{zuse}. It was developed as reversible computation and reversible cellular automata by Fredkin, Toffoli and Margolus \cite{fredkin}\cite{margolus}\cite{toffoli}, as a general programme by Wolfram \cite{wolfram}, and as a deterministic foundation beneath quantum theory by 't Hooft \cite{thooft2013}\cite{thooft}. Rule3 is a second-order cellular automaton in the sense of Toffoli and Margolus: the next state depends on the present state and the previous one, as $\anext = f(\anow) - \abefore$, which is reversible by construction \cite{toffoli}. What the model adds is an integer alphabet with one Euclidean division whose remainder stays at the Node, which keeps the construction exact for rational coefficients, and the reading of the automaton's quadratic forms as physics: the form, the current and the share. Feynman noted that a local classical computer reproduces no correlations beyond Bell's bound \cite{feynman}; the model agrees, and places the excess in the declared credit of the click rather than in the lattice (Section~\ref{sec:bell-blind}).

\paragraph{Lattice schemes and lattice physics.} At the vacuum's pace Rule3 is the explicit second-order difference scheme for the wave equation on a cubic lattice, and its guard is the stability condition of Courant, Friedrichs and Lewy \cite{cfl}: a pace beyond the edge $P$ lets the mode at the zone's corner grow (Section~\ref{sec:paces}). Lattice gauge theory uses a lattice as a regulator and takes the continuum limit \cite{wilson}; here the lattice is the physics, the Link and the interval are units, and the band's dispersion is a prediction and not an artefact (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}). Quantum walks and unitary cellular automata reproduce the Weyl, Dirac and Maxwell equations in their continuum limit \cite{adz}\cite{bialynicki}\cite{meyer}. They take complex amplitudes as the state, whereas here the state is integer levels, the quantum-like features are readings of the record, and the measurement is a separate declared instrument.

\paragraph{Time-symmetric accounts of measurement.} The meeting of a forward and a backward description at the point of measurement has a history. Wheeler and Feynman described radiation as an interaction between the source and the absorber, with advanced and retarded fields together \cite{wheeler}. Cramer's transactional interpretation has an offer wave travelling forward and a confirmation wave travelling backward, meeting at the absorber \cite{cramer}. The two-state vector formalism of Aharonov, Bergmann and Lebowitz evolves one state forward from the preparation and one backward from the outcome \cite{abl}\cite{vaidman}. Price argued that a time-symmetric account is what Bell's theorem leaves open \cite{price}, and Wharton and Argaman showed that every locally mediated reformulation of quantum theory must let some mediating parameters depend on measurement settings in their future \cite{wharton}. The model belongs to this class: the backward reading from a realised click depends on the detector's setting, and nothing else does. It differs in three ways. Both directions are the same integer line on a lattice that is exact and reversible to the bit. The product of the two directions is a reading, the form $D$, and not a wave. And the choice among the meetings is named as the one non-local element rather than derived. In the terms of Harrigan and Spekkens the record is an ontic state, a real state of the lattice and not a state of knowledge \cite{harrigan}; what such a state costs in locality is confined here to the credit.

\paragraph{Bell's theorem and local models.} Bell's inequality bounds every local account of a pair's correlations \cite{bell}, in the form that experiments test \cite{chsh}, and Fine showed that a local model, a joint distribution of all the observables and the inequalities are one condition \cite{fine}. The model's local credits give $S = \sqrt 2$ and $S = 2$, as they must (Section~\ref{sec:bell-blind}). Its credit by the contrast reaches the quantum correlation at the efficiency $2 / \pi$ by discarding the pairs near the contrast's zero, which is Pearle's model of data rejection \cite{pearle}. The efficiency below which such a model is possible is $2 / 3$ for a suitably chosen pair \cite{eberhard} and $0.83$ for a maximally entangled one \cite{gargmermin}, and $2 / \pi = 0.64$ lies below both, as it must. The loophole-free tests of 2015 \cite{hensen}\cite{giustina}\cite{shalm} close this road for nature. Superdeterminism, in which the settings share a cause with the pair \cite{thooft}\cite{hossenfelder}, is a different road, which the model does not take: its settings are declared and not produced by the lattice.

\paragraph{Discrete gravity and its tests.} Regge calculus \cite{regge} and causal sets \cite{bombelli} discretise the geometry of space-time itself. Here the lattice is never curved: gravity is a level that slows the rule's clock once and its Links twice, and the weak-field forms are read from the coefficients (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). In the parametrised post-Newtonian framework in which the tests of gravity are stated \cite{will}, the two fitted choices of Section~\ref{sec:paces} are $\gamma = 1$ and $\beta = 1$. The universality of free fall, which the model derives for extended bodies of one family, holds in nature to $10^{-13}$ on the ground \cite{will} and to $10^{-15}$ in orbit \cite{microscope}.

\paragraph{Bodies as fixed points.} A body here is a self-trapped mode of the rule, the lattice form of Pekar's polaron \cite{pekar}. Its stability theorem is Derrick's scaling argument \cite{derrick} with Pohozaev's identity \cite{pohozaev} on the lattice, and the saddle between its two branches is read by the criterion of Vakhitov and Kolokolov \cite{vk}. The binding field's screened reach is Yukawa's \cite{yukawa}.
\section{The model}\label{sec:model}

This section states the model: the lattice, the families, the rule, the paces through which one family acts on another, the one write, the readings and the order of acts in an interval \cite[the GameBoard and the families; Rule3; the primitives]{algebra}.

\subsection{The GameBoard}\label{sec:objects}

The GameBoard is a box of $N_x \times N_y \times N_z$ Nodes. Two Nodes that differ by one step along one axis are joined by a Link, and each Node has six Links, one through each of its six Ports, $\pm x$, $\pm y$ and $\pm z$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:octahedron}). Each axis is declared periodic, open or folded. Beyond an open face no Node lies and a level there is read as $0$; a folded axis has one layer, and its two Ports return the Node itself. An inner face is a plane of Nodes declared closed except for declared gaps, which is how a wall with slits is made. A face may also be declared receding (\claimmark{assumption}): the box then grows by a layer of zeros whenever a nonzero level reaches the Node next to the face, so no event ever meets the face, and a run on such a box is a run on the unbounded lattice. On a receding face the fields build their rest behind the front by Rule3 itself, as Huygens' construction on the lattice, and nothing reflects; the box grows and nothing stretches, so a receding face gives no cosmological redshift \cite[the objects; the unbounded board]{algebra}\cite[the receding face]{decisions}.

The maps of the six Ports onto themselves that keep opposite Ports opposite are the $48$ signed permutations of the axes, the symmetry group of the cube. The rule is covariant under them and under the translations of the lattice. No boost is among them, so Lorentz's group is not a symmetry of the lattice; Section~\ref{sec:forms} shows how Lorentz's factor nonetheless appears in the clicks.

The NodeState holds the model's own numbers and nothing else: for every family and every part, two levels and one remainder, and for every part of a field family, the remainder of its one write. A count, a current, a charge or a tension is a reading computed from these numbers when it is needed; no register, table, formula or random draw is kept at a Node \cite[the objects]{algebra}\cite[nothing at a Node but the law's numbers]{decisions}.

\subsection{Families and records}\label{sec:records}

A family is declared by two keys: its pair $[\num, \den]$ and its shape. A family of quanta, such as matter or light, declares its dimension, $1$ for one real line or $2$ for a plane of two lines, and holds nothing. A field family declares its sources, the form, the form with the three tensions, or the Wronskian, which fix its parts, one real line per source, and with them its divisor $E_s$ and, for the massless field, its rest; a field sourced by the form or the tensions holds the content, which slows the clocks of the families that read it, and a field sourced by the Wronskian holds the sign, which carries the charge's field. A family's record is its levels over the GameBoard: at every Node a level now and a level one interval before, with a remainder. A family of dimension $1$ lays its record as one real level pair, and a family of dimension $2$ as a plane of two level pairs $(\mathrm{re}, \mathrm{im})$, two solutions of the same line stepped by one call of the rule; only a plane carries a charge, and a neutral body and a charged body of the same pair are two families, two rows of the file with the dimensions $1$ and $2$ (Section~\ref{sec:sign}). A body is a connected set of Nodes on which a family's record stands bound (Section~\ref{sec:bodies}), and a message is a travelling record laid by the world \cite[the objects]{algebra}.

\subsection{Rule3}\label{sec:line}

At every Node, in every interval, every family steps by the first line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}. The six arrivals through the Ports, each weighted by its axis's coefficient $R_a$, the Node's own level times $S$, minus $w$ times the level before, plus the remainder the Node kept, are summed as one whole number. That whole is divided once by the wall $w$. The quotient is the next level, and the remainder, $0 \le r' < w$, stays at the Node and never travels. The whole is taken apart over the Ports and put together again at the neighbours, so nothing is rounded away and nothing is lost; the step back is the same division, which is why every run is exact and reversible to the bit (Theorem~\ref{th:direction}) \cite[the line]{algebra}.

The name comes from the three axes. The Link's paces $(p_x, p_y, p_z)$ are a vector of three directions, the line has one coefficient per direction serving that direction's two Ports, and the six Ports and the wall's factor $6$ are the three directions twice. In time the rule is a three-level recurrence, before, now and next. For light the three appears plainly: the next level is the sum of the six arrivals over $3$, minus the level before.

At the vacuum's pace, $p_0 = p_a = \Gamma$, the self coefficient vanishes and the line is the second-order wave rule of the pair,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vacuum}
\anext + \abefore = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a (\mathrm{arr}_{+a} + \mathrm{arr}_{-a}),
\end{equation}
whose rotation at the wave vector $\mathbf k$ is $\cos\omega = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a \cos k_a$. At wave number zero $\cos\omega_0 = \num / \den$, and for the massless pair $[1, 1]$ along one axis $\cos\omega = (2 + \cos k) / 3$, so $\omega = |k| / \sqrt 3$ as $k \to 0$ (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{GameBoard}). Light's speed at long wavelength is therefore $1 / \sqrt 3$ Links per interval, below the causal bound of one Link per interval, and its group speed falls at shorter wavelengths as $1 - 0.083\,k^2$ \cite[the lattice constants]{algebra}. In the implementation the line is one function, \texttt{core/rule3.py}, called on whole-board arrays with the direction $+1$ or $-1$, and every level that crosses a Link crosses it through \texttt{core/ports.py} \cite[the gates]{engine}.

\subsection{The paces: how a field acts on a record}\label{sec:paces}

A field acts on a family by changing the family's paces at a Node, the coefficients of Rule3 there:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paces}
p_0^2 = (\Gamma - c)^2 + c^2, \qquad p_a = \Gamma - 2c - t_a, \qquad c = \sum_{\text{reads}} \text{weight} \times \text{the read family's time level},
\end{equation}
Here $c$ is the content the family reads at the Node, the sum over its reads of the weight times the read field's level, and $t_a$ is the axis content from the fields' tensions on the axis $a$. A positive weight makes a hollow, an attraction; a negative weight makes a hill. A family with no reads steps at $p_0 = p_a = \Gamma$. The pace is the clock's slowing: a record at a Node of pace $p$ rotates and moves as a record at $\Gamma$ does, with its intervals $p / \Gamma$ as long. Every family of quanta reads every holder of the content at the weight $1$, so $x = (L_b + L_g) / \Gamma$ with $L_b$ the binding field's level and $L_g$ gravity's; a family of dimension $2$ also reads the holder of the sign plainly, and light, the holder's own record, reads none; and a family sources what it reads at the weight it reads with, so every pair of families that read each other is reciprocal \cite[the paces; the reads are the held row's pair's]{algebra}. In the implementation the paces are read in \texttt{features/read}.

\paragraph{Two choices fixed with a known result.} The level enters the clock's pace once and each Link's pace twice. Nothing in Rule3 forces the factor two on the Link: it was chosen so that light passing a body bends by twice the clock's share, as nature's light does \cite{will} (\claimmark{assumption}, \claimmark{fitted}). The clock's pace carries the second-order term of a slowing composed unit by unit, $p_0 = \Gamma (1 - 1 / \Gamma)^c$, whose square to second order is $(\Gamma - c)^2 + c^2 = \Gamma^2 (1 - 2\Phi + 2\Phi^2)$ with $\Phi = c / \Gamma$. This term was adopted with the perihelion's advance known: the clock to first order gives $7\pi$ times the potential per orbit, and the clock to second order gives Einstein's $6\pi$ (\claimmark{assumption}, \claimmark{fitted}) \cite[the clock once and the Link twice; the clock's second order is the composition]{algebra}\cite[the Node clock and the ruler]{decisions}. In the post-Newtonian language of tests of gravity the two choices are the parameters $\gamma = 1$ and $\beta = 1$ \cite{will} (\claimmark{derived}). They are inputs of the model, and Section~\ref{sec:forms} says where each is used. Rule3's coefficients take the paces' squares as written, with no rounding and no square root.

\paragraph{The band at a pace.} From the coefficients, the rotation of a record at a Node of clock pace $p_0$ and Link paces $p_a$ is (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{GameBoard})
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bandpace}
\cos\omega = 1 - \Bigl(1 - \frac{\num}{\den}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{p_0}{\Gamma}\Bigr)^2 - \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a \Bigl(\frac{p_a}{\Gamma}\Bigr)^2 (1 - \cos k_a),
\end{equation}
The mass term scales with the clock's square and the band term with the Link's. Two readings follow. First, the clocks shift alike: at $k = 0$ every massive band's rest rotation obeys $1 - \cos\omega_b = (p_0 / \Gamma)^2 (1 - \cos\omega_0)$, one factor for every family, so two bound bodies at one Node shift their clocks alike. Second, along a Link every wave's speed falls with $p_a = \Gamma - 2c - t_a$, so light is delayed and bent by twice the clock's share. The line is not conformal: a light wave at a fixed wave number scales with $(p_a / \Gamma)^2$ and a bound mode at rest with $(p_0 / \Gamma)^2$. At a Node of content $x = L / \Gamma$ light's band is $[2 - 4(1 - 2x)^2, 2]$ in $2\cos\omega$, and at long wavelength $\omega = (1 - 2x)\,|k| / \sqrt 3$: light's speed is $(1 - 2x)$ times its vacuum speed, an index $n = 1 / (1 - 2x)$ with $n - 1 = 2x$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:band}). A record whose rotation lies outside the band at a Node is evanescent there and reflected whole; light meets a total mirror where $p_a < \Gamma \sin(k / 2)$ \cite[the band at a pace; the rows against nature, (b)]{algebra}.

\begin{figure}[!tb]\centering\includegraphics{figures/band.pdf}\caption{\label{fig:band}Light's band at a content $x = L / \Gamma$ read into the paces (\claimmark{derived}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:bandpace} at $\num = \den$; \fence{GameBoard}): the doubled cosine of the rotation along one axis against the wave number, $2\cos\omega = 2 - 2(1 - 2x)^2(1 - \cos k)$, at $x = 0$, $0.1$ and $0.25$. The band's bottom rises with the content, its top stays at $2$; at the horizon $x = 1/2$ the band closes to a point. The slope at $k = 0$ is light's speed, $(1 - 2x) / \sqrt 3$ Links per interval. Drawn from the definitions; no run.}\end{figure}

\paragraph{The guard.} A pace must be positive and below an edge $P$, with $P^2 = 2\,\den\,\Gamma^2 \ddiv (\den + \num)$, which is $\Gamma^2$ for light. Beyond $P$ the mode at wave number $\pi$ on every axis has the factor $2 - 2(1 - \num / \den)(p_0 / \Gamma)^2 - 4(\num / \den)(p_a / \Gamma)^2$ below $-2$ and grows without bound; at or below $P$ it stays at or above $-2$ (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}). The guard is checked once, on squares, over the whole initial state, and never during a run; in a run no act reads a Node in order to refuse. At $R_a = 0$, a content of $\Gamma / 2$, a Link closes: that is a horizon. The clock never stops there: $(p_0 / \Gamma)^2 = (1 - \Phi)^2 + \Phi^2 \ge 1 / 2$ for every content, with its minimum at $\Phi = 1 / 2$, so at the horizon the Links close while the clock runs at $1 / \sqrt 2$ of the vacuum's rate, a Link horizon with a running clock, and the rotation of a Node whose three Link paces are $0$ is $2\cos\omega = 1 + \cos\omega_0$ from the self coefficient alone (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{GameBoard}). Such a frozen Node is cut from its six neighbours: no current crosses its Links, its share is not a number and no number is invented for it, and it stands in no instrument's front, so the horizon is dark \cite[the frozen Node has no share]{algebra}. At a negative content light's zone-corner mode grows, so light refuses a content below $0$, and the vacuum's content is $0$ (Section~\ref{sec:vacuum}) \cite[the guard]{algebra}.

\subsection{The write, the readings and the interval}\label{sec:interval}

\paragraph{The one write.} Every write into a field's level is one act, the second line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}: at a Node in each interval a held part gains $(\sum_f w_f\,q_f + r) \ddiv E$, one division per part with one remainder $r$ carried at that Node. Here $q_f$ is the sourcing family's own quantity at the Node: its form $D_f$ for a holder of the content, its Wronskian $W_f$ for the holder of the sign, or its tension $T_{aa,f}$ for an axis part. $w_f$ is the weight with which that family reads the written field, and $E$ is the part's wall, $E_s T$ for the time part and $E_s \Wc$ for an axis part. The sources are summed in the numerator and never divided apart, so no remainder stands per sourcing family \cite[the primitives]{algebra}\cite[the one algebra]{decisions}. In the implementation this is \texttt{features/hold}.

\paragraph{The readings.} A reading, which we also call a booking, is a bilinear form of the levels with declared coefficients; it writes nothing on the GameBoard. The law reads three, the third line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}. The form $D_i = \mathrm{now}_i^2 - \mathrm{next}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i$, the present against the product of the future and the past, is $B^2\sin^2\omega_b$ at every interval for a standing record of peak $B$; its quotient over $T$, the well, sources the fields. The Wronskian $W_i$ is the charge (Section~\ref{sec:sign}). The current $F_{ij} = \num\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j)$ into the Node $i$ from its neighbour $j$ is antisymmetric and is the Link term of the conserved form (Theorem~\ref{th:form}); a wave travelling from $i$ to $j$ has $F_{ij} < 0$. A static record has $\mathrm{now} = \mathrm{next} = \mathrm{before}$, so its form and its current vanish: a static field carries no quanta and no detector sees it, by the arithmetic and not by a clause \cite[the booking; the conserved form]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The interval.} Every read is of the values at the interval's start; one Link per interval, and nothing in zero time. The interval has four acts in order: (i) the read, each family's paces from the field levels; (ii) Rule3 on every record; (iii) the readings, including each record's six currents at the levels the step started from, its tension, form and Wronskian, and each detector's report, the net current into its region through the region's front boundary; (iv) the write of each field from its summed sources. Light is born at (iv), when a rotating body's varying Wronskian is written into the holder of the sign (Section~\ref{sec:light}) \cite[the interval]{algebra}\cite[the interval]{engine}.

\paragraph{The three channels.} One family acts on another in three ways and no fourth (Fig.~\ref{fig:channels}). \emph{The read}, at (i), takes a field's level times the read's weight into the reader's paces, once for the clock and twice for the Link; it is multiplicative and is the whole of gravity, of binding and of the charge's pull. \emph{The write}, at (iv), takes the writing family's own quantity into the field's level over the field's divisor; it is additive and is the source of every field and the birth of light. \emph{The click}, at (iii), is a declared detector's reading of the current through its boundary; it is the measurement and writes nothing on the GameBoard. No product of two levels is written anywhere, and no bilinear coupling between families is in the law \cite[the coupling is the click alone; light is born by the write]{decisions}.

A family of quanta steps its record by Rule3 and books its form and its current; a field family carries a level. \section{Exact properties}\label{sec:theorems}

This section proves what holds exactly on every lattice: the reversibility of the rule, the kind of function its acts can form, the conserved form with its current and its momentum flux, and the count as a reading of the record.

\subsection{Reversibility}\label{sec:direction}

Let $\sigma$ be the direction, $+1$ forward and $-1$ backward; let $\ddiv$ and $\dmod$ be the floor division and its remainder in $[0, w)$; and let $\rho$ be the remainder carried in. With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direction}
u = \sigma\Bigl(\sum_a R_a\,\mathrm{arr}_a + S\,x - w\,y\Bigr) + \rho, \qquad z = \sigma\,(u \ddiv w), \qquad \rho' = u \dmod w,
\end{equation}
the forward call with $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \abefore, r)$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\anext, r')$, and the backward call with $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \anext, r')$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\abefore, r)$.

\begin{theorem}[the direction]\label{th:direction}
The backward call returns $(\abefore, r)$ bit for bit; the reversibility of every step needs no second function.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Forward, $\sigma = 1$, the line is the definition of $\ddiv$ and $\dmod$. Backward, $\sigma = -1$: write $Y$ for the right side's first two terms, $\sum_a R_a\,\mathrm{arr}_a + S\,\anow$. Then $u = w\,\anext - Y + r'$ and $z = -\lfloor u / w \rfloor = \lceil (Y - w\,\anext - r') / w \rceil$. From the forward line, $Y - w\,\anext - r' = w\,\abefore - r$ with $0 \le r < w$, whose ceiling divided by $w$ is $\abefore$ exactly; and $\rho' = u - w\lfloor u / w \rfloor = w\,\abefore - (Y - w\,\anext - r') = r$. Checked bit for bit on $20{,}000$ random cases against a forward and a backward function written separately \cite[the direction]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

The whole run therefore goes back: $N$ intervals forward and $N$ back return every array of every family bit for bit, provided every number an act read at an interval's start is one the state after the interval still holds (\claimmark{theorem}, by construction from Theorem~\ref{th:direction}). The implementation checks this as a gate: it runs a world forward and back and compares every array at every interval, reporting \textsc{match} or naming the first interval and Node that differ, and a world is not run for results before the gate says \textsc{match}. The condition rules out one kind of read: a coefficient read from a Node's own state across a step cannot be recovered at the step back. This is why a record's rotation sense is never read at a Node, and why the holder of the sign is read according to the record's shape, a plane or a line, and not according to a sense (Section~\ref{sec:sign}) \cite[the interval]{algebra}\cite[the board goes back in time, as a gate; there is no sense]{decisions}.

\subsection{What the acts can form}\label{sec:acts}

Rule3's line is one act, and declared coefficients make it four: (a) the read, the line with its six arrivals; (b) the one-Node step, the line with the arrivals declared $0$, which rotates a level by Chebyshev's recurrence; (c) the division, the line with $R = S = 0$, Euclid's division with the remainder kept; and (d) the load, a declared integer in the numerator, which is how a count enters a level \cite[the four acts]{algebra}.

\begin{theorem}[what the acts can form]\label{th:acts}
Every composition of the four acts is a piecewise-linear function of the levels with integer slopes: a sum of linear forms and of floors of linear forms, composed. No such function is quadratic in the levels.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each act is a linear form of the levels or the floor of one; a floor of a linear form is piecewise linear with integer slopes; sums and compositions of such functions are of the same kind. The second difference of such a function in any one level is $0$ almost everywhere, while the second difference of a product $\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j$ in the pair $(\mathrm{now}_i, \mathrm{before}_j)$ is $1$. So nothing bilinear comes from the acts alone.
\end{proof}

So every product of two levels is a reading and never a write, and the three readings of Section~\ref{sec:interval} are the only products the law takes. A line enters the law only if it passes three tests: it is generic (one primitive with declared integers, no family's name), vector (an act on the whole state, with no root and no floating-point number) and local (its own record and the six neighbours, nothing kept at a Node beyond the law's numbers). A line that needs more is a hypothesis under its own name. No act of the interval takes a square root; the integer square root appears only when a world is laid, as the fixed point of the division act $x \leftarrow (x + n \ddiv x) \ddiv 2$ \cite[the three tests of every rule]{algebra}\cite[the six verbs; the root leaves everywhere]{decisions}.

\subsection{The conserved form and the momentum flux}\label{sec:form}

The read matrix $2\,\num\,p_i^2$ is not symmetric where the paces differ, so the conserved form carries the weight $1 / p_i^2$ on its Node terms and the plain current on its Links:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:form}
E = \sum_i \frac{w\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - S_i\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i}{p_i^2} - 2\,\num\sum_i \mathrm{now}_i\,S_6(\mathrm{before})_i .
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[the conserved form]\label{th:form}
At constant paces $E$ is exact under the line without the rounding; with the rounding each Node's remainder adds at most one unit of the level per interval to the walk of $E$. Its Link term, the plain current $\num\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j)$ through the Link $ij$, is the current of Rule3's own conservation law.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Without the rounding the line is $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} + \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} = \bM\,\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}$ with $\bM$ the matrix whose diagonal is $S_i / w$ and whose entries on the six Links are $R / w$. Let $\bD$ be the diagonal matrix of the weights $1 / p_i^2$; then $\bD\bM$ is symmetric, since $R_a = 2\,\num\,p_a^2$ at the reading Node and the weight $1 / p_i^2$ cancels it on both sides of every Link. For a symmetric $\bK = \bD\bM$ the quantity $Q(\mathbf x, \mathbf y) = \mathbf x \cdot \bD\mathbf x + \mathbf y \cdot \bD\mathbf y - \mathbf x \cdot \bK\mathbf y$ satisfies $Q(\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}) = Q(\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}})$: with $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} = \bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}$, $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} \cdot \bD\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} \cdot \bK\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} = \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} \cdot \bD\mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} \cdot \bD\bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}$, which is the other side. Multiplied by $w$ this is $E$ up to the Link term's sign convention \cite[the conserved form]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

$E$ is a diagnostic. Where the paces move, the total form $D = |\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}|^2 - \langle \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}\rangle$ changes exactly by $D(t + 1) - D(t) = \langle \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, (\bM(t) - \bM(t + 1))\,\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}\rangle$, which is $0$ at fixed paces and negative where a well deepens (\claimmark{theorem}, by substitution; \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the conserved form]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The momentum flux is the wave's own stress.} The momentum density along the axis $a$ at the Node $i$ is the current per axis, $P_a(i) = (F_{i,i+a} - F_{i,i-a}) / \num$, and the line gives exactly, at constant paces (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum}
\begin{aligned}
w\,[P_a(t + 1) - P_a(t)] &= \sum_b R_b\,[G_{ab}(i - b) - G_{ab}(i)],\\
G_{ab}(i) &= \mathrm{now}_i\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a+b} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a+b}) - \mathrm{now}_{i+b}\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $G_{ab}(i)$ the stress reading on the $b$-Link $(i, i + b)$, the levels now alone. The proof substitutes $w\,\mathrm{now}' = \sum_b R_b (\mathrm{now}_{i+b} + \mathrm{now}_{i-b}) + S\,\mathrm{now}_i - w\,\mathrm{before}_i$ into $w\,P_a(t + 1)$: the $S$ term cancels, the $w$ term is $w\,P_a(t)$, and the $R$ term is the difference of the fluxes. The tension on the axis $a$ at a Node, the part a field reads into the Link's pace and the source of a field's axis part, is the momentum flux averaged over the Node's two $a$-Links,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tension}
\begin{aligned}
T_{aa}(i) &= -\num\,\bigl(G_{aa}(i) + G_{aa}(i - a)\bigr) \ddiv 2,\\
G_{aa}(i) &= \mathrm{now}_i\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+2a} - \mathrm{now}_i) - \mathrm{now}_{i+a}\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and it is local as $F$ is. A plane wave of amplitude $b$ at the wave number $k$ has $G_{ab} = -2\,b^2\sin k_a \sin k_b$, so the flux along the wave is positive, as a wave's pressure is; a packet of count $c$ and velocity $\mathbf v$ has $c\,v_a v_b$, the stress of dust. With the sign as written a wave's stress deepens the content around it, so light gravitates by its pressure too. The off-diagonal parts and the vector part, which Rule3 never reads, are not written \cite[the tension; the final unification]{algebra}\cite[the tensor part is the record's own stress]{decisions}.

\subsection{The count is the record's share}\label{sec:share}

A family's count of quanta is a reading of its record and no line of its own. At every Node the record's share is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:share}
e_i = 3\,\bigl[w\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - S_i\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i\bigr] \ddiv \Bigl(2\sum_a p_a^2\Bigr) - \num\,\mathrm{now}_i\,S_6(\mathrm{before})_i,
\end{equation}
the conserved form's Node term over the mean of the three Link paces squared, less the plain Link term, read in quanta over $\Wc$ as the nearest whole number. Read with the vacuum's coefficients instead of the Node's own paces, the same expression is the vacuum's share, the unit of the tables of Section~\ref{sec:bodies}. Nothing is kept at a Node beside the record and the write's remainder: no count, no remainder of a count and no sense. Over one step of Rule3 the share changes by exactly the six currents into the Node, plus a term that vanishes where the three Link paces are equal, plus Rule3's own rounding (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:share-identity}
\begin{aligned}
e_i(\mathrm{next}, \mathrm{now}) - e_i(\mathrm{now}, \mathrm{before}) &= \sum_j F_{ij} + \num\,(\mathrm{next}_i - \mathrm{before}_i)\sum_a \frac{(3p_a^2 - P^2)\,\mathrm{arr}_a(\mathrm{now})_i}{P^2}\\
&\quad - \frac{3\,(\mathrm{next}_i - \mathrm{before}_i)(r'_i - r_i)}{2P^2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $P^2 = \sum_a p_a^2$, $\mathrm{arr}_a$ the two arrivals of the axis $a$ summed, and $r$, $r'$ Rule3's remainder before and after the step. The identity was checked by hand and by a test of the implementation. So over any region whose three Link paces are equal, the total share changes only by the currents through the region's boundary and the remainder terms (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the count is the record's share; the share's change is the currents]{algebra}. A formulation that moves whole quanta between Nodes by a division of the current, and the sign by a division of the Wronskian's current, would put two more numbers at every Node that no act of Rule3 reads back; the law does not carry it (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[the count's line and the sense's line leave the law]{decisions}. Where the paces move, as under a breathing body, the share read at the new paces drifts with no current crossing any Link; the drift's formula is Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift} of Section~\ref{sec:bodyclicks}, a diagnostic \cite[the paces' own change moves the share with no current]{algebra}. In the implementation the share is one reading, \texttt{share.py}.

\paragraph{The signed integers.} The levels are signed integers, the currents are signed by their direction and the Wronskian by its sense, and a click is one whole quantum, never negative. The total share of a closed or periodic GameBoard is non-negative for $|\num| \le \den$ (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}): with $\mathbf n$ and $\mathbf b$ the vectors of now and before and $S_6$ the six-neighbour sum, a symmetric operator whose spectrum lies in $[-6, 6]$, $\sum_i e_i = 3\,\den\,(|\mathbf n|^2 + |\mathbf b|^2) - \num\,\mathbf n \cdot S_6\mathbf b \ge 3\,(\den - |\num|)\,(|\mathbf n|^2 + |\mathbf b|^2) \ge 0$, with equality only for a static uniform level of light, whose share is $0$; in a well the Node term carries the weight $3\Gamma^2 / \sum_a p_a^2 \ge 1$ and the Link term does not, so the bound holds there too. One Node's share can be negative, where its six neighbours' before outweigh its own by more than $6\,\den / \num$, which no plane record does and a lay's edge can; a region's share is negative at most by what stands just outside its front, half a level-square per boundary Port in the current's units. Over the whole passage of one forward packet the inflow accumulated at a region is non-negative up to the rounding, since the lay's current is forward at every Link, so the credit by the shares is well defined with no further line; a window cut inside a passage or a record returning through the front can give a negative reading, and the instrument then credits $\max(s_R, 0)$ (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}) \cite[the law's integers are signed]{decisions}.

\paragraph{The lattice constants.} The massless field's rest around a static source of $s$ quanta per interval at the divisor $1$ is $3\,G(r)\,s$, with $3\,G(0) = 0.7582$ at the Node and $0.2582$, $0.1286$, $0.0826$, $0.0608$ and $0.0483$ at one to five Links, toward $3 / (4\pi) = 0.2387$ times $1 / r$ far away (\claimmark{computed}, \fence{GameBoard}). These numbers and light's $1 / \sqrt 3$ belong to the six Ports and are not numbers of nature \cite[the lattice constants]{algebra}.
\section{Measurement: the click as a meeting}\label{sec:click}

The click is the meeting of the future with the past at a declared detector, and no act of the GameBoard. This section states what is on the lattice, the meeting, the detector, what a click leaves behind, the charge and light, and what locality costs \cite[the click; the reading]{algebra}.

\subsection{Events, and no waves}\label{sec:nowaves}

On the GameBoard there are events and amplitudes. An event is a level split by Rule3 to the six neighbours; an amplitude is the level Rule3 produces at a Node, the sum of the split events there; and the record is the amplitudes over the GameBoard. In our world there are clicks, the detectors' reports, and nothing else. What nature calls a wave is, on the lattice, an emitter's events that spread, and a detector that measures them or does not; between the two ends there are events and amplitudes and no wave. An emitter is a message declared in the file, or a body whose write varies. In the two slits the emitter's events pass both gaps, one beam that splits into two fans, every path of the future at once. The fringes are where split events from the two gaps meet at the screen with the same sign, and interference is split events meeting with opposite signs. The past is made of the same split events: read backward from the realised click by the same line, Rule3 at $-1$, they form the past's tree, which also passes both gaps back to the emitter. A gap closed by an opaque detector leaves the past one gap. What stays on the GameBoard, the amplitudes no click realised, is not in our world, and it changes a click only where it meets another event that is realised (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[no waves on the event board]{algebra}\cite[no waves on the event board]{decisions}.

\subsection{The click is the meeting of the future with the past}\label{sec:meeting}

\begin{assumption}[the click is a meeting, the law and not a hypothesis]\label{as:meeting}
A record runs on the GameBoard in both directions by the one line: forward from the emitter, the possibilities, the future; backward from the realised click, what happened, the past, Rule3 at $-1$. A click is where the two carry the same signal at a declared detector; its share is the product of the two directions at the region, the form $D = \mathrm{now}^2 - \mathrm{next} \times \mathrm{before}$, the now against the future times the past; which meeting is realised is the draw, the one non-local place. The detector only asks whether the future and the past met at it.
\end{assumption}

In plain words: a click happens when the past meets the future; the click is the now; the detector only asks whether the future and the past met at it; and a click is created only at their meeting. This is a line of the model and not a hypothesis \cite[the click is the meeting of the future with the past]{decisions}.

\paragraph{The click, algebraically.} Let $X$ be the state space: at every Node, for every part of every family, one integer level pair and the write remainders, and nothing else. The step $S_t$ is Rule3 over the interval with the paces read from the fields at its start; it is a bijection of $X$, exact by the division with its remainder, and affine in the levels at fixed paces; its forward orbit is the future and its inverse orbit the past. The steps generate a group with the lattice's translations, the $48$ signed permutations, the time reversal and the plane's rational rotation as its symmetries. The readings $D$, $F$ and $W$ are bilinear forms on $X$, covariant under the group and never written: $D$ is even under the time reversal, $F$ and $W$ odd, as a current and a sense flip. An instrument $R$ is a declared region with its front Ports and its coefficients, which define a quadratic form $Q_R$ on $X$: for one line, the inflow through its front Ports accumulated over the window; for a pair, the window's sum of the squared cross-side sum over the parts. A click is the pair $(R, t)$, the credit of one whole quantum to the instrument $R$ at the interval $t$, drawn with the probability $s_R / \sum_{R'} s_{R'}$, where $s_R = Q_R(x_t) / \Wc$ is the instrument's form on the forward orbit over the quantum's measure of a current. In the meeting's words, the inverse orbit from the credited state coincides with the forward orbit on the realised path, and the share is the bilinear form's value at the coincidence. The click writes nothing to $X$ and is no element of the group: it is the one map in the model that is not a bijection, from the vector of shares to a unit vector, and it is a scalar under the group, so no click names a Node. Every observable is $Q_R / \Wc$ on the forward orbit or a ratio of two such. Three precisions: the window is the whole passage of a packet, or the credit floors at $0$ (Section~\ref{sec:share}); the credit's interval within the window is the draw's by the inflow's time profile, which the model gives (Section~\ref{sec:slits-blind}); and a Node whose three Link paces are $0$ stands in no instrument's front and is never clicked, so the horizon is dark (\claimmark{assumption} for the definition, \claimmark{derived} for the precisions; \fence{clicks}) \cite[the click's algebraic definition]{decisions}.

\paragraph{What the backward reading is.} Along the realised path the backward reading is the same triples of levels, since the GameBoard is reversible, so it brings no new number, carries no count and no signal, and writes nothing. What it does is choose which tree is the past. The reversibility of the lattice is therefore required by the model and is not a convenience of the implementation: without the exact inverse the past could not be read back from a click, and a pair's common origin could not be reached. A Node in the now reads neither the future nor the past; the two meet only at a click, so a Node is read only through a detector.

\paragraph{One form for every click.} At the credit the parts of a record are read with the detector's coefficients, each part paired with the same part through the common origin, summed and squared; the share is that density, and the draw is by the shares. The two slits are the case with nothing to pair: light is one real line, and the share is the record's own inflow. Bell's gate is the case through the common origin: a pair born as one event is two trees with one root, the backward reading from the first click passes the root and continues forward on the partner's tree, and the partner's click meets the re-weighted record (Section~\ref{sec:bell}). Absorption by a body is the case of resonance, and emission is the same meeting with the roles reversed. In the implementation the joint share is computed from the forward records alone, by the linearity of the rule: the instrument reads each part's levels at each port, forms the sum over the parts for every pair of ports, takes its density as the joint share and draws once per pair, the first side from its marginal and the partner from the conditional. No backward Rule3 is stepped; the gate of Section~\ref{sec:direction} shows that the backward reading exists to the bit (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}).

\paragraph{How the meeting looks.} Fig.~\ref{fig:meeting} draws the statement schematically. (a) \emph{The future covers every possibility.} The emitter's events split at every Node in every interval; after the wall the record stands at every Node behind both gaps at once, and no path is chosen. One rule at one Node covers all the possibilities by the linearity of the line. (b) \emph{The past is read from the click.} At a declared region of the screen the record's current through the boundary is the share; one wall $\Wc$ of inflow is one quantum, and the instrument credits a quantum to one region, the click. From that click the same line runs backward and retraces the realised past through both gaps to the emitter. (c) \emph{Where they meet.} The two directions carry the same signal at the region that clicked and nowhere else; the share there is the product of the two, the form $D = \mathrm{now}^2 - \mathrm{next} \times \mathrm{before}$, and the current $F_{ij}$ is the same product across a Link, the present at one end with the past at the other. (d) \emph{Why two gaps give fringes and a closed gap a hump.} With both gaps open, split events through the two gaps meet at the screen with the same sign where the path difference is a whole wavelength and with opposite signs where it is half of one, so the share along the screen alternates, and the clicks credited by the shares build that pattern quantum by quantum. With an opaque detector in one gap its events leave the GameBoard through the face behind it, the backward reading meets one gap alone, and the share is one gap's envelope (Section~\ref{sec:slits-blind}). (e) \emph{The pair.} The click at A's port weights the parts by A's analyser; read back to the root and forward to B, it meets B's analyser's weighting, the root sums the two branches, and the share is the density of the sum, while each side alone reads one half at every port and nothing is signalled. (f) \emph{What an observer sees.} Only the click. Every drawing of the lattice, this one included, is ours from outside the world.

\paragraph{No duality.} What is called the particle as a wave is the future: the record run forward by Rule3 with every possibility, which has not yet met the past. What is called the particle as a particle is the present: the click, the meeting, one whole quantum credited at one region in one interval. The particle's track is the past: the chain of realised clicks, and the same record read backward through them. There are not two things but one record read in two directions, and the point where the two readings coincide is what appears as a particle. Between clicks there is no particle, only the future. After a click no wave vanishes; the past has grown by one click. The collapse is not a process but the present: nothing collapses and nothing is erased, the record goes on and the click is a credit. The wave appears as knowledge rather than as a thing because its numbers are real on the lattice and unreadable except at a meeting. A standing body is repeated clicks at one place, and a stone is a past updated every interval (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[the wave is the future, the click is the present, the track is the past]{decisions}.

\subsection{The detector is a declared instrument}\label{sec:detector}

\begin{assumption}[the detector's declaration]\label{as:detector}
A detector is one connected region of Nodes declared in the file, never one Node, at least half the wavelength of the family it reports across the beam ($q / p$ Links for the wave $[p, q]$, $4$ at $\lambda = 8$); a narrower region, a single Node or a region in pieces is refused by name at load, and the depth along the beam is not gated. It is opaque: backed by a receding face or by the wall's own face, so that what enters it leaves the declared board and never returns, with no act at its Nodes and no log. Its click is its report, per interval and family: the net current into the region through its front boundary Ports, the Ports leading in from the declared board, summed in integers, with the region's name and the family and never a Node; one wall $\Wc = 3\,\den\,T$ of the inflow over a passage is one quantum, so a screen saw $N$ quanta where its regions' inflows sum to $N\,\Wc$. The credit: one quantum to one detector, drawn by the shares of the regions' inflows, read outside the GameBoard over every declared detector of the world, one credit per quantum and never two. The credit is the one non-local place of the description and no line of the law.
\end{assumption}

Why a region: a quantum with a direction has no single Node, since a record confined to one Node contains every wave number at once, $\Delta x\,\Delta k \ge 1 / 2$ on the lattice as in nature, and the finest structure a family's amplitudes carry is half its wavelength. So no click names a position finer than a region, and every per-Node number is a diagnostic. Why the net inflow through the front: adjacent regions share transverse Links, and a density that entered one region and moved to the next inside the layer is credited where it entered. Why opaque: a region whose back faces other Nodes passes the density on and reads nothing net, while a region backed by a receding face removes what entered with no echo \cite[the click is the detector's report; no click names a Node]{algebra}\cite[no click names a Node]{decisions}.

What the model determines and what the instrument declares are separate. What produces a click is known and deterministic: the meeting, the current of the amplitudes through a region's boundary. What is not known is which of the possibilities becomes the measured click: when one possibility is spread over several detectors, the GameBoard gives the shares, no line of the law gives which detector fires, and the instrument draws. Exclusivity, one click per quantum and never two, and Born's rule, the credit's chance equal to the intensity's share \cite{born}, are therefore declarations of the instrument and not findings of the model, for nature or against it, as nature's detector efficiency and dead time are \cite[what produces a click is not known]{algebra}. The uncertainty relation is not a separate rule: it is the detector's three declarations, a region of at least half a wavelength, a credit per interval and one draw per quantum, since a meeting is a region and an interval and never a Node and an instant (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[the uncertainty relation is not a rule of its own]{decisions}. What each declaration costs, in numbers: a region of $n$ Nodes reads a fringe of period $\Lambda$ with the factor $\sin(\pi n / \Lambda) / (n\sin(\pi / \Lambda))$, $0.90$ for four rows on a fringe of sixteen and $0$ at $\Lambda = n$, so the smallest region the model allows reads every fringe the amplitudes can carry except the finest; one click carries no frequency, a frequency being a click rate over a window of $N$ intervals, read to $\Delta\omega \ge 2\pi / N$; and $N$ quanta drawn by the shares give each region $Np_i \pm \sqrt{Np_i(1 - p_i)}$, so a pattern is in the histogram and never in one click (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}). In the implementation a detector is a region declared in the world file, its report is the \texttt{click} line of the output (\texttt{reports.py}), and the credit by the shares is done by the reader of the output and never by the GameBoard \cite[the output]{engine}.

\subsection{The click ends nothing}\label{sec:nothing}

\begin{assumption}[the click ends nothing]\label{as:nothing}
The click is a detector's reading, local to its boundary; the record goes on after it and nothing ends anywhere. What a detector absorbed leaves the declared board through the face that backs it; the other possibilities go on splitting and leave through the receding faces, events that were not realised, in no click. The ending of a record at a click by the detector is decided against and is no hypothesis of the law.
\end{assumption}

This is the model's claim and not a deficiency: perhaps nature is local. No signal passes, since nothing at one detector changes the amplitudes at another. A measurement needs no collapse: the record never ends, the click is a reading, and the backward reading from the click writes nothing \cite[the click ends nothing]{decisions}. A bound body can act as an instrument in the same form: the future is light's forward record at its boundary, the past is the body raised by one quantum read backward to the source, and the meeting is the same frequency, the resonance of the body's rotation with the light's; emission is the same meeting reversed, and a body at rest and symmetric clicks nowhere (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}; Section~\ref{sec:bodyclicks}). The body's write back waits on the fixed point with the write \cite[a Node of the board is read only through a detector]{decisions}\cite[the rows against nature, (y)]{algebra}.

\subsection{The sign is the rotation sense}\label{sec:sign}

A family of quanta laid as a plane carries its record as two level pairs, $(\mathrm{re}, \mathrm{im})$, stepped by one call of Rule3 with one set of coefficients, each with its own remainder; a field's parts stay real. The charge density at a Node is the reading
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wronskian}
W_i = \mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{before}} - \mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{before}},
\end{equation}
the Wronskian of the two pairs, which Rule3 conserves exactly with the conserved form's weights $1 / p_i^2$ (\claimmark{theorem}, since $\bD\bM$ is symmetric; \fence{GameBoard}). Its local change is the Link current $\num\,(\mathrm{im}_i\,\mathrm{re}_j - \mathrm{re}_i\,\mathrm{im}_j)$, and its total over the GameBoard is invariant under any change of the paces, since the bracket $\mathbf{im}_{\mathrm{now}}^{\top}\bM\,\mathbf{re}_{\mathrm{now}} - \mathbf{re}_{\mathrm{now}}^{\top}\bM\,\mathbf{im}_{\mathrm{now}}$ vanishes for every symmetric $\bM$ (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}). A real record ($\mathrm{im} = 0$) has $W = 0$, so light, the real record of the holder of the sign, is neutral; a rotating record has $W$ of either sign by its sense \cite[the sign is the rotation sense; the sign's count is the record's own]{algebra}.

Who reads the holder of the sign is decided by the family's dimension and by one further rule, and by no name (\claimmark{assumption}, \fence{GameBoard}). A family of dimension $2$ has a Wronskian and reads the holder of the sign; a family of dimension $1$ has $W = 0$ and does not, and a neutral body and a charged body of the same pair are two families, two rows of the file with the same pair and the dimensions $1$ and $2$; the dimension is a key of the row and never a reading of a Node's state. A field is always real, one line per source. A field does not read its own level, so light does not read the holder of the sign, a static charge does not bend light, and light's own $W$ is no charge. A reader's own sense never enters its coefficients, since a coefficient read from a Node's own state would break the exact inverse (Section~\ref{sec:direction}). The holder of the sign therefore acts on its readers by a declared act: the plain read of its level into the paces, which is the law today, or a rotation of the two-part record by the angle of its level over $\Gamma$, three integer shears with no remainder, under which a record rotating at $+\omega$ rotates at $\omega + \theta$ and one at $-\omega$ at $|\omega| - \theta$. Under the plain read like and unlike charges are not distinguished; under the rotation they would be, and the rotation is Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:rotation}, not yet part of the law \cite[there is no sense; every family has a dimension]{decisions}\cite[the sign holder rotates the two-part record]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The charge is the record's.} For a record $(\mathrm{re}, \mathrm{im}) = (A\cos\omega t, e A\sin\omega t)$ the reading is $W = -e A^2\sin\omega$: the charge is the record's size times the ellipticity $e$ of its rotation, which Rule3 conserves, since the two levels obey one linear line. A circular quantum ($e = 1$) has $|W| / D = 1 / \sin\omega$, and a nearly real cloud of $1{,}836$ quanta with $e = 1 / 1{,}836$ has the charge of one circular quantum. Nothing in the law quantises $e$, so a body's charge is continuous, and the equality of the proton's and the electron's charges, which nature fixes to $10^{-21}$ \cite{bressi}, is an input and not derived (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:charge-unit}) \cite[the sign is the rotation sense]{algebra}.

\subsection{Light is born by the write}\label{sec:light}

Light is the holder of the sign's own record: one record whose time part is a real level pair, stepped by Rule3 at the paces of its reads, so that light is bent by the content, and written by the one write from the Wronskian of the records that read it. A static rotating body writes a static level, Coulomb's field, and radiates nothing. A breathing or moving body writes a varying source at its Nodes, the field carries it away at one Link per interval, and those spreading events are the light (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}). Its count is the share of that record, read like every family's. A detector reads light's current through its boundary in quanta of $\Wc$, and the travelling events of the other fields, which in gravity's case nature calls a gravitational wave, enter a detector the same way. A body's quanta that leave it stay its family, and no quantum changes family at a crossing. A body reads the holder of the sign at its own Nodes, so a breathing body reads the light it writes and its pace answers, the radiation reaction, in the same loop \cite[light is born by the write]{algebra}\cite[light is born by the write]{decisions}.

\subsection{The price of locality}\label{sec:price}

Three consequences of the model's locality, stated against nature's clicks \cite[what is open, item 4; the rows against nature, (g) and (h)]{algebra}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item[(i)] \emph{Exclusivity and Born's rule are declared.} A quantum spread over several detectors is a set of shares of the current at each; the model gives the shares and the instrument gives the click. A dilute packet, under one quantum per Node, builds its pattern click by click by the declaration.
\item[(ii)] \emph{The which-way measurement.} An opaque detector in one gap absorbs that gap's events, whether or not it is credited, and the screen sees the other gap alone, with no fringes: a closed slit by construction, in nature too. A which-way measurement that marks the path without absorbing it is beyond the instrument the model has.
\item[(iii)] \emph{Bell.} A pair is one laid record, a common cause, and its phase is a shared classical variable inside Bell's hidden variable \cite{bell}. Every local credit of that phase gives $|S| \le 2$ (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{clicks}; Section~\ref{sec:bell-blind}) \cite{chsh}. The meeting through the pair's root gives $E = \cos 2(a - b)$ and quantum theory's $S = 2\sqrt 2$ with every quantum counted, while each side alone reads one half at every port and nothing is signalled; this rests on the credit rule, the square of the meeting, which is a declaration of the model and not a derivation (\claimmark{assumption}, \claimmark{fitted}; \fence{clicks}). The loophole-free test of 2015 that measured the sum found $S = 2.42 \pm 0.20$ \cite{hensen} \cite[the click is the meeting of the future with the past]{decisions}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The postulates}\label{sec:postulates}

The model rests on seven postulates \cite[the postulates]{algebra}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item The world is Nodes and Events. Space is the GameBoard; an Event is a change of a NodeState, a level stepped by Rule3 with its remainder; a click is a detector's report of the currents; nothing else happens.
\item A Node holds bounded local information, its NodeState, fixed in size and read from itself and its six neighbours; nothing beyond them is read, no list grows with the world, and nothing of the past is kept.
\item Consistency is local and causal: an Event first changes its own Node and travels Link by Link, one Link per interval. The one exception is the production of a click, the instrument's credit of one quantum to one detector by the shares over every declared detector, which is the one non-local element and is not derived.
\item The causal speed is one Link per interval; every other speed is a rational number of Links per interval.
\item Every physical calculation is on bounded integers, with no floating-point number, no root and no random draw in the law.
\item A measurement is a detector's click: the net current into a declared region through its front boundary over one interval, reported with the region and the family and never a Node. Only a click is compared with nature.
\item The GameBoard, where no one measures, is deterministic and reversible to the bit; the detectors and their clicks are the only thing claimed to represent nature.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stability}\label{sec:stable}

Let a body of $M$ quanta have the record $\phi$ over its region, $\sum\phi_i^2 = 1$, with the counts $c_i = M\phi_i^2$. Let every field $r$ it sources be a hollow ($\sigma_r = +1$) or a hill ($\sigma_r = -1$), with the static kernel $\mathbf G_r$ of its line and the divisor $E_r$, so that the well at a Node is $\Phi_i = \sum_r \sigma_r (M / E_r) \sum_j G_{r,ij}\,\phi_j^2$. To first order in $\Phi / \Gamma$ the well raises the one-Node rotation $2\cos\omega$ by $4g\Phi / \Gamma$, $g = (\den - \num) / \den$, and the body's fixed point is a critical point on the sphere of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:functional}
F[\phi] = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_{\text{Links}} (\phi_i - \phi_j)^2 - \frac{2g}{\Gamma}\sum_r \sigma_r\,\frac{M}{E_r}\sum_{ij}\phi_i^2\,G_{r,ij}\,\phi_j^2,
\end{equation}

\subsection{Bell: what the model says it must give}\label{sec:bell-blind}

Three layers, two from the algebra of the GameBoard and the third a declaration. (1) Rule3 gives each part's amplitudes at the ports: the two parts of one beam, laid equal, are two records of one family stepped by the same line along the same path, identical bit for bit at every Node and interval (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}). (2) The meeting with the linearity of Rule3 gives the joint amplitude: the realised click at A's port weights the parts by A's analyser, is read back to the root where the two parts were one event and forward to B, where B's analyser weights them, and the root sums the two branches, $\sum_k c_k\,e_k(a)\,e_k(b) = \cos(a - b) / \sqrt 2$ for equal parts (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}). (3) That the share is the density of the meeting, its square, is Born's rule for a pair \cite{born}: nothing in Rule3 forces it, and it is the least non-local credit that reaches nature's correlations with every quantum counted (\claimmark{assumption}, \claimmark{fitted}; \fence{clicks}). From the three, with the pair's common phase $\theta$ uniform over the pairs and A's $+$ port at $a$ and B's at $b$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bell}
\begin{aligned}
&\text{the meeting:} && P(++) = \tfrac{1}{2}\cos^2(a - b), \quad E(a, b) = \cos 2(a - b), \quad S = 2\sqrt 2;\\
&\text{a local credit by the shares:} && P(++) = \tfrac{1}{4} + \tfrac{1}{8}\cos 2(a - b), \quad E = \tfrac{1}{2}\cos 2(a - b), \quad S = \sqrt 2;\\
&\text{a local credit by the sign:} && E = 1 - 4\,|a - b| / \pi \ (|a - b| \le \pi / 2), \quad S = 2;\\
&\text{the credit by the contrast:} && E = \cos 2(a - b) \text{ at the efficiency } 2 / \pi, \quad S = 2\sqrt 2,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The second line follows by averaging $\cos^2(a - \theta)\cos^2(b - \theta)$ over $\theta$, the third from the band of width $|a - b|$ in each half-period where the two signs differ, and the fourth from the integral of $\cos 2(b - \theta)$ over the half-period where $\cos 2(a - \theta) > 0$, against the mean weight $(1 / \pi)\int |\cos 2u|\,du$ (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}). So $E(a, b) = \cos 2(a - b)$ under the meeting. For the declared pairs it is exact, Lagrange's identity of the reader on equal parts: $E = ((p_A p_B + q_A q_B)^2 - (p_A q_B - q_A p_B)^2) / ((p_A^2 + q_A^2)(p_B^2 + q_B^2))$, so at the four settings $E = 119 / 169$, $-119 / 169$, $120 / 169$ and $120 / 169$ and $S = 478 / 169 = 2.8284$ exactly, with every pair credited on both sides, an efficiency of $1$ by construction and the marginal one half on each side whatever the partner's setting (\claimmark{computed}, \fence{clicks}). For parts of unequal amplitudes, with $M_k = \alpha_k\beta_k$ and $r = M_2 / M_1$, the same credit gives $E(a, b) = \cos 2a\cos 2b + \rho\sin 2a\sin 2b$ with $\rho = 2r / (1 + r^2)$, the marginal $P(A+) = (\cos^2 a + r^2\sin^2 a) / (1 + r^2)$ independent of $b$, and $S = \sqrt 2\,(1 + \rho)$ at the ideal angles and $(238 + 240\rho) / 169$ at the integer pairs: $\rho = 1$ is the cosine, $\rho = 0$ the product form $238 / 169 = 1.408$ of the local shares, and the sign alone the sawtooth with $S = 2$; a shortfall of a run reads as $1 - \rho$, a reading of the two parts' amplitudes at the ports (\claimmark{derived}; $\rho$ and $r$ \fence{GameBoard}, $E$ and $S$ \fence{clicks}). This is the two-photon correlation of a pair with the unequal weights $(1, r)$, quantum theory's own.

What the GameBoard gives without the declaration is the second and third lines of Eq.~\eqref{eq:bell}, two theorems about local credits on the same world (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{clicks}). A credit of each side by the shares of its ports gives $S = \sqrt 2$. A credit of each side by the sign of its larger port, the pair's phase being a shared classical variable, gives the sawtooth, whose sum is exactly $2$ for every set of four settings within one period, Bell's bound. A two-port detector that credits by its contrast and counts nothing where its two ports' shares are nearly equal gives $E = \cos 2(a - b)$ exactly at the efficiency $(2 / \pi)V$ for a visibility $V$: the uncounted pairs are those near the contrast's zero, which is the detection loophole of Bell tests \cite{pearle}, a local form that reaches nature's cosine only below the loophole's bound, $2 / 3$ at best \cite{eberhard}, as $2 / \pi$ does (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}). Bell's value in this model therefore sits in the credit rule, which is a declaration, and the local theorems state what the GameBoard gives without it. Nature beside the derivation: $E = \cos 2(a - b)$, and $S = 2.42 \pm 0.20$ in the loophole-free test of 2015 that measured the sum \cite{hensen}; the two photon tests of that year violated the Clauser-Horne-Eberhard form of the inequality \cite{eberhard} at detection efficiencies near $0.75$ \cite{giustina}\cite{shalm}. No signal passes between the sides: the detectors read locally, the root joins by linearity, and each side's marginal is one half. No joint credit is declared by an instrument, since two detectors are never at one Node, and no settings are chosen by the GameBoard's own past. A superdeterministic world, in which the settings are set by bodies sharing the source's history, would be a world of its own \cite{hossenfelder} and is not this one \cite[the rows against nature, (h)]{algebra}\cite[the click is the meeting of the future with the past; Bell in nature's own layout]{decisions}.

\paragraph{To test a hypothesis.} Its line is written in the law's document first, under its own name, with what would decide it. The expected number is computed from the implementation's own lines and written down before the run. The hypothesis is built as one pure function of arrays that calls Rule3 alone, with its own test. The run's reading is compared with the written number: a difference is a defect of the implementation, never a finding, and a number outside its band with no defect found names the missing law as a hypothesis. No expectation is rewritten after a run, and no body's number is tuned to a result \cite[the blind number first]{workflow}.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

One line of integer arithmetic at every Node of a cubic lattice, with one remainder kept at the Node, is reversible to the bit, conserves a quadratic form with its current, and carries the momentum flux as the wave's own stress. On that lattice there are only events that split; the future and the past are the same split events run forward from an emitter and read backward from a click, and a click is their meeting at a declared detector, with the choice among the meetings as the one non-local element. Families are bands of the rule, each one row of a file, and the universe holds as few free numbers as the rule allows. Bodies are the rule's fixed points, the extended ones behaving as nature's bodies and the compact ones as collapsed matter. Gravity is a level that slows a Node's clock once and its Links twice. In the model's own potential two of its features were fitted, one to light's bending and one to the perihelion; against the potential that Kepler's orbits measure, the same forms reach Einstein's only as the matter family's gap closes, which bounds that gap and stands against the rule's own matter pair. Bell's value rests on a credit rule chosen to reach nature's correlations. Each such result is marked as a consistency check and not a prediction. Lorentz's factor at a body's own light speed and the universality of free fall for extended bodies follow without fitting; the magnetic force between moving charges does not follow from the rule as it stands, and the paper names the hypothesis that would give it. Every formula is fenced as a reading of the lattice or as a formula of clicks, and the formulas of clicks stand with their statuses as the model's list of what it gives. The two slits and Bell are stated as the implementation's two gates, what each must give; the next step is to run them as gates, to compare a body's clicks with nature's, and to fix the model's remaining numbers by nature's clicks one at a time. We hold the whole to be the least computation that shows nature's clicks, with the least state, the least non-locality and the least randomness; why nature is so, we do not claim to know.
