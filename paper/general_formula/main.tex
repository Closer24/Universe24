\begin{abstract}
How much of physics does one local rule in whole numbers compute, nothing continuous beneath and a detector's click the only measurement? We present a method: an integer cellular automaton, a cubic lattice of Nodes stepped by one recurrence taking a Node's present, past and six neighbours through one integer division, reversibly, conserving a quadratic form exactly in the rationals, covariantly under the cube's 48, with no boost. A change crosses at most one Link per interval, light's speed $1/\sqrt 3$ of that bound, nature's $c$ setting the Link and the interval. Measurement is the meeting at a detector of the record run forward from its root with the detector's own transition read backward from the realised click, two quanta and never one; the detector draws the click, the lattice draws nothing, that draw the one non-local element. On it the wave equation, the quantum of action and the metric of gravity are three readings of one line. From the line alone, its own formulas with no number of nature: a family's pair $[p, q]$, $\cos\omega_0 = p / q$, gives its inertial mass $m^* = 3\tan\omega_0$ and its light speed $c_m^2 = \omega_0 / (3\tan\omega_0)$, with $E = m^* c_m^2$ exactly and Lorentz's factor at $c_m$ as the gap closes; the matter pair's post-Newtonian $\gamma_K = q / p$ and $\beta_K = (q + p) / (2p)$, closing to $1$ with the gap; charge universality and the forces' ratio $(m_p / m_e)^2$ across families; and $\alpha = (3\sqrt 3 / 8\pi) k / (\Gamma E_s)$ between two quanta, $k$ the file's declared weight. Newton's gravitation is reflected, $G$ a reading, and Einstein's weak field under one named assumption fitted to two clicks: the exponential metric, $\gamma = 1$ exactly and $\beta = 1$ as the gap closes, a shadow $4.6$ percent over Schwarzschild's. The paper claims the method and not nature: every result a supporting finding for the reader's test, what it does not give listed.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} integer lattice rule; reversible cellular automaton; measurement as a meeting of past and future; Bell's inequality; weak-field gravity; limiting speed on a lattice

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The question.} The object of this paper is one method of computing physics: a GameBoard of Nodes stepped by one local, reversible rule in whole numbers, read only through clicks, the one measurement there is. In the title's words: the integer cellular automaton is the GameBoard stepped by Rule3; the world it computes is a declared world's records and clicks; and the clicks of nature it approaches are the comparisons of Sections~\ref{sec:forms} to~\ref{sec:constants}, supporting findings and never the claim. On it the classical wave equation, the quantum of action and the metric of gravity are three readings of one line, Rule3: the line itself is the lattice wave equation, its count read in quanta is the click, and its paces read as a clock and a ruler are the metric, with nothing continuous beneath. The question is whether that method can give the clicks that nature shows, and with how little state, non-locality and randomness; the fifteen formulas and the three computed worlds below are examples of what the method yields, not its scope, and Section~\ref{sec:open} lists what it does not yet give. The paper's one claim is the method: a GameBoard of integers stepped by one rule and read into the world through clicks, from which the forms set beside nature's are derived in the order stated. It does not claim that nature is such a lattice; every derivation, comparison and prediction below is a supporting finding for the method, each with its status, and none is the paper's thesis. The paper computes the click patterns of declared detectors and sets them beside nature's clicks; why a click happens stands outside it as a declaration, the draw, in the open list by name, and no claim of the paper rests on it: the form is claimed, the cause is not. It starts from one statement and never contradicts it (\claimmark{assumption}): the world is the meeting of the past with the future; a click is that meeting, the one measurement there is; and an observer sees only clicks (the words are defined at the end of this section). Every physical number of the paper is a reading of one integer rule with its declared integers, the pairs, $\Gamma$, $T$, the two holders' weights, gravity's $E_g$ and the sign holder's $k$, and the vacuum content (Section~\ref{sec:choice}); of nature's other clicks it gives the forms of Section~\ref{sec:forms} at the computed pair, with the findings of Section~\ref{sec:open} against them. A Node holds a record's present and its past, two integer levels, and the rule makes the record's future from the two and from the six neighbouring Nodes. The rule is covariant under the lattice's translations and under the symmetry group of the cube, of order $48$; no boost is among them (Section~\ref{sec:objects}). The lattice holds nothing but events that split: at every Node a level splits by the rule to the six neighbours. Run forward from an emitter, the split events are the future, every path at once; read backward from a realised click by the same rule, they are the past, and through two slits both pass both gaps. A click is where the two meet at a declared detector, and which meeting is realised is the one thing the model does not determine, the one non-local element of the description (Section~\ref{sec:meeting}). What appears as a wave is the future, what appears as a particle is the present, the click, and the track is the past (Section~\ref{sec:nowaves}). No theorem of minimality is claimed.

\paragraph{The rule in one line.} Everything in the paper follows from one line of integer arithmetic, Rule3, together with one write, three readings, the declarations of Section~\ref{sec:model} and the one assumption of Section~\ref{sec:paces}. At every Node, in every interval and for every family of physical values \cite[the line; the booking; the primitives]{algebra}:
{\small\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
&\text{Rule3:} && w\,\anext + r' = \textstyle\sum_{j \sim i} R_{ij}\,\mathrm{arr}_j + S\,\anow - w\,\abefore + r, \quad 0 \le r' < w,\\
&&& w = 6\,\den\,\Gamma^2, \quad R_{ij} = 2\,\num\,p_a(i, j)^2 = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2,\\
&&& S = 12\,\den\,\Gamma^2 - 12\,(\den - \num)\,p_0^2 - \textstyle\sum_{j \sim i} R_{ij};\\
&\text{the one write:} && \text{a held part at a Node gains } \Bigl(\textstyle\sum_f w_f\,q_f(\text{Node}) + r\Bigr) \ddiv E, \text{ the remainder } r \text{ kept there};\\
&\text{the readings:} && D_i = \mathrm{now}_i^2 - \mathrm{next}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i, \qquad W_i = \mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{before}} - \mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{before}},\\
&&& F_{ij} = \num\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j).
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}}
In the first line $\anow$, $\abefore$ and $\anext$ are the family's level at the Node now, one interval before and one interval later: the present, the past and the future. $\mathrm{arr}_j$ is the arrival through the Port to the neighbour $j$, that neighbour's level, the six Ports summed. $r$ and $r'$ are the remainder kept at the Node before and after the step, $\Gamma$ the Node clock. $[\num, \den]$ is the family's pair, and $\cos\omega_0 = \num / \den$ is its rotation per interval at wave number zero. $p_0$ is the Node's clock pace, $p_i$ its own pace and $p_a(i, j)$ the pace of the Link through the Port, the Node's pace times the Link's factor $q_{ij} / \Gamma$, the factor read the same from both ends and the pace the reading Node's own. All are the Node clock lowered by the field levels the family reads there (Section~\ref{sec:paces}). At the vacuum's paces the two Links of an axis share one $R_a = 2\,\num\,\Gamma^2$ and $S = 0$. $\ddiv$ is the floor division. The second line is the only way a field's level is ever written: the sum over the sourcing families $f$ of each family's own quantity $q_f$ at the Node, weighted by $w_f$, plus the part's one remainder, divided by the part's wall $E$. The third line is what is read from a record and never written: its form $D_i$, which sources the fields; its Wronskian $W_i$, its charge; and its current $F_{ij}$ into the Node $i$ from its neighbour $j$, which a detector reads through its boundary in quanta of $\Wc = 3\,\den\,T$, with $T$ the quantum's norm, the normalisation of the quadratic form.

\paragraph{What is put in, and the three kinds of claim.} The paper's claims are of three kinds, and the text keeps them apart. \emph{Found}: what is proved or derived from the rule with no input chosen for it, the fifteen formulas above and the click formulas of Table~\ref{tab:clicks} marked derived; the findings against nature are comparisons, listed in Section~\ref{sec:open}. \emph{Assumed, chosen or declared}: the assumption and the credit below, the universe's rows, the detector's four declarations, its unit of credit among them, the electron as the free quantum of matter and the matter pair's denominator as the family's own integer (Section~\ref{sec:constants-law}). \emph{Held to be computable and not yet computed}: the bodies at the closing gap, the moving fixed point of a body and the absorbed fraction with the body's write; the explicitly named hypotheses are stated where they stand, among them light's gravity entered as a derived hypothesis (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}), the carrier that would mend the lattice's frame and the nuclear holders (Section~\ref{sec:open}). One assumption is named with the forms, that a Link is clocked twice by its reading Node's own clock, $hN = 1$, fixed by the bending and the perihelion (Section~\ref{sec:paces}); the forms against Kepler's potential reach Einstein's only as the matter family's gap closes (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). One choice was made with a known result in view, and we state it as an input, not a prediction: a click's share is the square of the meeting, a credit chosen to reach nature's correlations, so Bell's $2\sqrt 2$ follows from it and does not test it. The file's numbers are nature's and not the rule's: the vacuum content, a declaration; the vacuum write's rate, $\Lambda$, the one declared number of the hypothesis of Section~\ref{sec:clusters}; the binding range, a world's choice; gravity's weight $E_g$ and the sign holder's write weight $k$, read from $G$ and from $\alpha$; and the gaps, read from the masses (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}, Table~\ref{tab:calibration}). What is not claimed: that nature is a lattice; that any number of the computed pair $[2, 3]$ is nature's; that a comparison with nature is more than a consistency statement at the stated order; or that a formula known in the continuum is new where the paper gives its lattice form, the known form standing beside it in every row. Every derivation was checked twice, independently, within the project and, where a world exists, by the implementation's checks, not by an independent referee. Where the text says exact it means exact in the rational arithmetic before the rounding, with the integer remainders bounded as stated. And a line decided after a derivation is stated as the law with the implementation's run pending, a run that differed from its prediction being a defect of the implementation to repair and never a result. The paper computes one world of the many the method admits: every number of the files and every declared row, a family, a holder, a read, an instrument, can be changed and the same rule run again; what is brought here is one plane of them, with every derivation and every new formula shown, and the claim is only that it approaches nature's clicks in the order stated, never that nature is so.

\paragraph{The three layers of the method.} The method has three layers, each with a fixed part and a free part. The board's rules (Section~\ref{sec:model}): which families and of what dimension, which holders, who reads what and how; Rule3 with its reads is fixed, the file's rows are free. The numbers (Section~\ref{sec:families}): $\Gamma$, the pairs, the holders' weights, gravity's $E_g$ and the sign holder's $k$, $T$, the vacuum content, the ranges, all free and as few as the rule allows. The click (Sections~\ref{sec:click}, \ref{sec:detector} and~\ref{sec:bodyclicks}): how the board becomes a click, what the detector declares, what is credited and how a board mass is read as a click mass. Fixed here, common to every world, is the click's one form: the meeting, the bilinear credit, the draw by the shares, the detector's one write at the Node of the exchange and the postulate that only a click is a measurement; free, the world's, are the detector's four declarations, the unit it counts in among them.

\paragraph{How to read the paper.} Every claim carries one of seven marks. \claimmark{Theorem}: proved for every lattice. \claimmark{Derived}: obtained from theorems by argument. \claimmark{Computed}: a number obtained from the rule's lines before any run. \claimmark{Assumption}: a line held by decision and named where it is used. \claimmark{Hypothesis}: a line outside the model, under its own name. \claimmark{Experiment}: a number the model does not fix, with the experiment named that would fix it; the paper performs none. \claimmark{Fitted}: an input chosen with a known result in view. The tables' status columns use the same marks, with calibration (a number taken from nature), declaration (a world's own number) and implemented (checked by the implementation) beside them. \section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Reversible cellular automata and digital physics.} The idea that the physical world is a discrete computation goes back to Zuse \cite{zuse}; it was developed as reversible cellular automata by Fredkin, Toffoli and Margolus \cite{fredkin}\cite{margolus}\cite{toffoli}, as a programme of cellular automata by Wolfram \cite{wolfram}, and as the cellular automaton interpretation, a deterministic foundation beneath quantum theory, by 't Hooft \cite{thooft2013}\cite{thooft}. Quantum cellular automata put a unitary step on a lattice, from the lattice Weyl, Dirac and Maxwell equations \cite{bialynicki} and quantum lattice gases \cite{meyer} to the structure theorems of the reviews \cite{arrighi}\cite{farrelly} and the derivation of Weyl's and Dirac's equations from an automaton's symmetries \cite{dariano}; the present rule's step is an integer recurrence on two levels, not a unitary on amplitudes. Rule3 is a second-order cellular automaton in the sense of Toffoli and Margolus, $\anext = f(\anow) - \abefore$ \cite{toffoli}, with an integer alphabet and one Euclidean division whose remainder stays at the Node, which is what the title calls an integer cellular automaton. Feynman noted that a local classical computer reproduces no correlations beyond Bell's bound \cite{feynman}; the model agrees, and places the excess in the declared credit of the click rather than in the lattice (Section~\ref{sec:bell-blind}).

\paragraph{Time-symmetric accounts of measurement.} Wheeler and Feynman described radiation as an interaction between the source and the absorber, with advanced and retarded fields together \cite{wheeler}; Cramer's transactional interpretation has an offer wave forward and a confirmation wave backward, meeting at the absorber \cite{cramer}. The two-state vector formalism evolves one state forward from the preparation and one backward from the outcome \cite{abl}\cite{vaidman}; Price argued that a time-symmetric account is what Bell's theorem leaves open \cite{price}, and Wharton and Argaman showed that every locally mediated reformulation of quantum theory must let some mediating parameters depend on measurement settings in their future \cite{wharton}. The model belongs to this class, the backward reading from a realised click depending on the detector's setting alone; it differs in that both directions are one integer line, exact to the bit, that their product is a reading and not a wave, and that the choice among the meetings is the one non-local element; the record is an ontic state in the terms of \cite{harrigan}.

\paragraph{Bell's theorem and local models.} Bell's inequality bounds every local account of a pair's correlations \cite{bell}, in the form that experiments test \cite{chsh}, and a local model, a joint distribution and the inequalities are one condition \cite{fine}. The model's local credits give $S = \sqrt 2$ and $S = 2$, as they must (Section~\ref{sec:bell-blind}); its credit by the contrast reaches the quantum correlation only by discarding pairs, Pearle's road \cite{pearle}, below the efficiency bounds of Eberhard and of Garg and Mermin \cite{eberhard}\cite{gargmermin}, which the loophole-free tests of 2015 close for nature \cite{hensen}\cite{giustina}\cite{shalm}.

\paragraph{Discrete gravity and its tests.} Regge calculus \cite{regge} and causal sets \cite{bombelli} discretise the geometry of space-time itself; here the lattice is never curved, and the weak-field forms are read from the coefficients (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). In the parametrised post-Newtonian framework \cite{will} the one assumption gives $\gamma = 1$ exactly and $\beta = 1$ at the small gap in the model's own potential, with the matter band's factors against the potential the tests read closing with its gap. A lattice breaks Lorentz invariance at order one unless its terms are tuned \cite{collins}, and the bounds are reviewed in \cite{mattingly}; the paper's answer is no loops and the Link's bound of S.24, with the closing gap for matter (Section~\ref{sec:forms}).

\section{The model}\label{sec:model}

This section states the model: the lattice, the families, the rule, the paces through which one family acts on another, the one write, the readings and the order of acts in an interval \cite[the GameBoard and the families; Rule3; the primitives]{algebra}.

\subsection{The GameBoard}\label{sec:objects}

The GameBoard is a box of $N_x \times N_y \times N_z$ Nodes. Two Nodes that differ by one step along one axis are joined by a Link, and each Node has six Links, one through each of its six Ports, $\pm x$, $\pm y$ and $\pm z$. Each axis is declared periodic, open or folded. Beyond an open face no Node lies and a level there is read as $0$; a folded axis has one layer, and its two Ports return the Node itself. An inner face is a plane of Nodes declared closed except for declared gaps, which is how a wall with slits is made. A face may also be declared receding (\claimmark{assumption}): the box then grows by a layer of zeros whenever a nonzero level reaches the Node next to the face, so no event ever meets the face, and a run on such a box is a run on the unbounded lattice. The box grows and nothing stretches, so a receding face gives no cosmological redshift \cite[the objects; the unbounded board]{algebra}\cite[the receding face]{decisions}.

\paragraph{The group and its action.} The symmetry group of the cube has $48$ elements, the $24$ rotations and, with the reflections, the signed permutations of the three axes, $O_h$, the octahedron's group as it is the cube's (Fig.~\ref{fig:lattice}(b)); with the translations it is the space group of the simple cubic lattice. It acts by permuting the Nodes and their Ports and carrying every level pair with its Node; the time reversal exchanges a record's present and its past and carries the step to its inverse (Theorem~\ref{th:direction}). Rule3 commutes with the space group's action, since its line reads the six Ports symmetrically and names no axis. Under the group the form $D$ is a scalar, even under the time reversal; the current $F$ a vector, odd under it; the Wronskian $W$ a scalar under the cube's $48$, odd under the time reversal and under the plane's reflection; and the click a scalar, naming a region and an interval and never a Node or an axis. No boost, no rotation beyond the cube's $24$ and no scaling is in the group, so Lorentz's group and the continuum's rotations are no symmetries of the lattice, and what the paper finds of the moving clock it finds as a property of the clicks (Section~\ref{sec:forms}) (\claimmark{theorem} for the covariance and the readings' kinds; \fence{GameBoard}; S.4) \cite[the direction; the four acts]{algebra}\cite[the one algebra]{decisions}.


The NodeState holds the model's own numbers and nothing else: for every family and every part, two levels and one remainder, and for every part of a field family, the remainder of its one write. A count, a current, a charge or a tension is a reading computed from these numbers when it is needed; no register, table, formula or random draw is kept at a Node \cite[the objects]{algebra}\cite[nothing at a Node but the law's numbers]{decisions}.

\end{aligned}}

\subsection{The write, the readings and the interval}\label{sec:interval}

\paragraph{The one write.} Every write into a field's level is one act, the second line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}: at a Node in each interval a held part gains $(\sum_f w_f\,q_f + r) \ddiv E$, one division per part with one remainder $r$ carried at that Node. \section{Exact properties}\label{sec:theorems}

This section states what holds exactly on every lattice, each proved in S.48: the reversibility of the rule, the kind of function its acts can form, the conserved form with its current and its momentum flux, and the count as a reading of the record (S.5, S.6).

\subsection{Reversibility}\label{sec:direction}

Let $\sigma$ be the direction, $+1$ forward and $-1$ backward; let $\ddiv$ and $\dmod$ be the floor division and its remainder in $[0, w)$; and let $\rho$ be the remainder carried in. With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direction}
u = \sigma\Bigl(\sum_j R_{ij}\,\mathrm{arr}_j + S\,x - w\,y\Bigr) + \rho, \qquad z = \sigma\,(u \ddiv w), \qquad \rho' = u \dmod w,
\end{equation}
the forward call with $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \abefore, r)$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\anext, r')$, and the backward call with $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \anext, r')$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\abefore, r)$.

\begin{theorem}[the direction]\label{th:direction}
The backward call returns $(\abefore, r)$ bit for bit; the reversibility of every step needs no second function.
\end{theorem}

\noindent (Proved in S.48.) Theorem~\ref{th:direction} holds for the GameBoard's step at every interval; a detector's write and a body's window, click or not (Section~\ref{sec:nothing}), are the instruments' acts outside the reversal, as the lay is; the back-in-time gate is exact between clicks and runs across a click and every erasure interval with the presented face values as its boundary, read from the GameBoard-labelled lines beside the click line, the lay's acts crossed from their lines, the click line itself naming no Node (\claimmark{theorem} for the step; the write \claimmark{assumption}). The whole run therefore goes back: $N$ intervals forward and $N$ back return every array of every family bit for bit, provided every number an act read at an interval's start is one the state after the interval still holds (\claimmark{theorem} where the writes change no level, by construction from Theorem~\ref{th:direction}). The implementation checks this as a test, running a world forward and back and comparing every array at every interval; on the worlds of Section~\ref{sec:slits} it reports \textsc{match} over their whole runs, worlds whose write stays under one level (Section~\ref{sec:choice}) and whose detectors write nothing; the interval's reversal with a level-changing write is gated: the back-in-time check gives \textsc{match} across the faces, the region detectors' clicks and every erasure interval, the presented face values the boundary, and \textsc{miss} at a lay, the taking, the giving and the null window, by design, the tool's crossing of a lay from its lines not yet built (Theorem~\ref{th:direction}'s clause), on the three worlds (the two slits $128$, Bell $98$, the GHZ $58$ intervals) (\claimmark{computed}, \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the inverse; the objects]{algebra}\cite[how to run a world]{engine}\cite[the Node knows no family]{decisions}. The condition rules out one read: a coefficient read from a Node's own state across a step cannot be recovered at the step back, so a record's rotation sense is never read at a Node and the holder of the sign is read by the record's shape, a plane or a line (Section~\ref{sec:sign}) \cite[the interval]{algebra}\cite[the board goes back in time, as a gate; there is no sense]{decisions}.

\subsection{What the acts can form}\label{sec:acts}

Rule3's line is one act, and declared coefficients make it four: (a) the read, the line with its six arrivals; (b) the one-Node step, the line with the arrivals declared $0$, which rotates a level by Chebyshev's recurrence; (c) the division, the line with $R = S = 0$, Euclid's division with the remainder kept; and (d) the load, a declared integer in the numerator, which is how a count enters a level \cite[the four acts]{algebra}.

\begin{theorem}[what the acts can form]\label{th:acts}
Every composition of the four acts is a piecewise-linear function of the levels with integer slopes: a sum of linear forms and of floors of linear forms, composed. No such function is quadratic in the levels.
\end{theorem}

\noindent (Proved in S.48.) So every product of two levels is a reading and never a write, and the three readings of Section~\ref{sec:interval} are the only products the law takes. A line enters the law only if it passes three tests: it is generic (one primitive with declared integers, no family's name), vector (an act on the whole state, with no root and no floating-point number) and local (its own record and the six neighbours, nothing kept at a Node beyond the law's numbers). A line that needs more is a hypothesis under its own name. No act of the interval takes a square root; the integer square root appears only when a world is laid, as the fixed point of the division act $x \leftarrow (x + n \ddiv x) \ddiv 2$ \cite[the three tests of every rule]{algebra}\cite[the six verbs; the root leaves everywhere]{decisions}.

\subsection{The conserved form and the momentum flux}\label{sec:form}

The read coefficient $R_a(i \to j) = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ carries the Node's factor and the Link's; the Node's factor is not symmetric where the paces differ and the Link's is, so the conserved form carries the weight $1 / p_i^2$ on its Node terms and the Link's factor $q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ on its Link terms:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:form}
\boxed{E = \sum_i \frac{w\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - S_i\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i}{p_i^2} - 2\,\num\sum_i \sum_{j \sim i} \frac{q_{ij}^2}{\Gamma^2}\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[the conserved form]\label{th:form}
At constant paces for which a positive Node weight $d_i$ with $d_i R_a(i \to j) = d_j R_a(j \to i)$ on every Link exists, $E$ with the weights $d_i$ is exact under the line without the rounding. Under the law's read coefficient, the Node's pace squared times the Link's factor squared, $R_a(i \to j) = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ with $q_{ij} = q_{ji}$ and $p_i = p_0(c_i)^2 / \Gamma$ the composed Node factor, the weight $d_i = 1 / p_i^2$ cancels the Node's factor on both sides of every Link, so $E$ is exact for any static field and static tensions. Its Link term carries the Link's factor, so the current of Rule3's own conservation law through the Link $ij$ is $\num\,(q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2)\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j)$, the plain current $F_{ij}$ where no tension stands, $q_{ij} = \Gamma$; the form with the plain current holds there alone, and with a tension it is not conserved (two witnesses, S.32).
\end{theorem}

\noindent (Proved in S.48.) With the rounding each Node's remainder adds the term $(r - r')(\mathrm{next} - \mathrm{before}) / p_i^2$ to the walk of $E$ (\claimmark{derived}; S.5). $E$ is a diagnostic. Where the paces move, the total form $D = |\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}|^2 - \langle \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}\rangle$ changes exactly by $D(t + 1) - D(t) = \langle \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, (\bM(t) - \bM(t + 1))\,\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}\rangle$, which is $0$ at fixed uniform paces and negative where a well deepens under a standing record (\claimmark{theorem}, by substitution; \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the conserved form]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The momentum flux is the wave's own stress.} The momentum density along the axis $a$ at the Node $i$ is the current per axis, $P_a(i) = (F_{i,i-a} - F_{i,i+a}) / \num$, and the line without its division gives exactly, at constant paces, the identity below; the integer step adds the remainder term $\delta_i\,\Delta_a(\mathrm{now})_i - \mathrm{now}_i\,\Delta_a(\delta)_i$ with $\delta = r - r'$ and $\Delta_a$ the centred difference, which the implementation's test includes (\claimmark{theorem} for the line, \fence{GameBoard}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum}
\begin{aligned}
w\,[P_a(t + 1) - P_a(t)] &= \sum_b R_b\,[G_{ab}(i) - G_{ab}(i - b)],\\
G_{ab}(i) &= \mathrm{now}_i\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a+b} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a+b}) - \mathrm{now}_{i+b}\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $G_{ab}(i)$ the stress reading on the $b$-Link $(i, i + b)$, the levels now alone. The proof is in S.48. The tension on the axis $a$ at a Node, the part a field reads into the Link's pace and the source of a field's axis part, is the momentum flux averaged over the Node's two $a$-Links,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tension}
\begin{aligned}
T_{aa}(i) &= -\num\,\bigl(G_{aa}(i) + G_{aa}(i - a)\bigr) \ddiv 2,\\
G_{aa}(i) &= \mathrm{now}_i\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+2a} - \mathrm{now}_i) - \mathrm{now}_{i+a}\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and each Node sources its own part, $-\num\,h_a(i)$ with $h_a(i) = \mathrm{now}_{i-a}\,\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_i^2$, read from its two neighbours along the axis at the interval's start; the Link's tension is its two ends' parts, one number per Link read the same from both ends by the pace at the next interval. A plane wave of amplitude $b$ at the wave number $k$ has $G_{ab} = -2\,b^2\sin k_a \sin k_b$, so the flux along the wave, $T_{aa} = -\num\,G_{aa} \ge 0$, is positive, as a wave's pressure is (S.7); a packet of count $c$ and velocity $\mathbf v$ has $2c\,v_a v_b$, the two Links' currents summed, the stress of dust (S.7 for the plane wave). With the sign as written a wave's stress deepens the content around it, so light gravitates by its pressure too. The off-diagonal parts and the vector part, which Rule3 never reads, are not written \cite[the tension; the final unification]{algebra}\cite[the tensor part is the record's own stress]{decisions}.

\subsection{The count is the record's share}\label{sec:share}

A family's count of quanta is a reading of its record and no line of its own. At every Node the record's share is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:share}
\boxed{e_i = \Bigl[w\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - S_i\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i - \mathrm{now}_i \sum_{j \sim i} R_{ij}\,\mathrm{before}_j\Bigr] \ddiv (2p_i^2)},
\end{equation}
the conserved form's term at the Node with its weights (Theorem~\ref{th:form}), the Node's $1 / p_i^2$ and each Link's $q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ inside $R_{ij}$, read in quanta over $\Wc$ as the nearest whole number; at the vacuum's paces it is $3\,\den\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - \num\,\mathrm{now}_i\,S_6(\mathrm{before})_i$ exactly, $S_6$ the six-neighbour sum, and read with the vacuum's coefficients instead of the Node's own paces the same expression is the vacuum's share, the unit of the tables of Section~\ref{sec:bodies}. The implementation reads it so. Over one step of Rule3 the share's rational value, before its floor, changes by exactly the six currents into the Node, each weighted by its Link's factor, plus Rule3's own rounding (\claimmark{theorem} for the rational identity, \fence{GameBoard}; S.5); the integer share of Eq.~\eqref{eq:share} adds the reader's own floor, a residual strictly between $-1$ and $1$ per Node and level pair, distinct from Rule3's remainder:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:share-identity}
\begin{aligned}
e_i(\mathrm{next}, \mathrm{now}) - e_i(\mathrm{now}, \mathrm{before}) &= \sum_{j \sim i} \frac{q_{ij}^2}{\Gamma^2}\,F_{ij} - \frac{(\mathrm{next}_i - \mathrm{before}_i)(r'_i - r_i)}{2p_i^2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $F_{ij}$ at the levels the step started from and $r$, $r'$ Rule3's remainder before and after the step. So over any region the total share changes only by the weighted currents through the region's boundary, the remainder terms and the readers' floors (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the count is the record's share; the share's change is the currents]{algebra}. A formulation moving whole quanta by a division of the current would put numbers at every Node that no act of Rule3 reads back; the law does not carry it (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[the count's line and the sense's line leave the law]{decisions}. Where the paces move the share drifts with no current crossing any Link, Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift}, a diagnostic \cite[the paces' own change moves the share with no current]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The signed integers.} The levels are signed integers, the currents are signed by their direction and the Wronskian by its sense, and a click is one whole quantum, never negative. The total share of a closed or periodic GameBoard is non-negative for $|\num| \le \den$, with equality for a static uniform level of light and, on an even periodic board, for its checkerboard (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}; S.6). One Node's share can be negative at a lay's edge, and a region's by at most half a level-square per boundary Port. Over the whole passage of one forward packet the inflow accumulated at a region's front equals the region's final share plus what left through its back, each bounded below by S.6 (Eq.~\eqref{eq:share-identity} summed over the window), so it is non-negative up to the rounding and the credit by the shares is well defined; where a window is cut inside a passage the instrument credits $\max(s_R, 0)$ (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}) \cite[the law's integers are signed]{decisions}.

\section{Measurement: the click as a meeting}\label{sec:click}

The click is where the method meets the world it computes: everything Universe24 computes is read through clicks and nothing else, and the paper's supporting findings are the places where those clicks stand close to nature's; the paper claims the method, and never that nature is so. The click is the meeting of the future with the past at a declared detector, and no act of the GameBoard. A click is a fact: one whole quantum passed from a giver to a taker, written once and never undone; it is always the meeting of two quanta, so every click has its giver and its taker, the record and the body's transition, and happens never by itself (S.49 (16)). In the law's one line: a detector drives Rule3 from outside the board, as a face and a start do; it presents one value at one Port of the click's Node, in the place of what it would see from the board, and lays whole quanta there, and the Node steps Rule3 as always, knowing neither. A click is this act: one whole quantum of one record taken, Rule3 writing the hole, and whole quanta of others given, the rotations summing at the Node; the conversion, the weak force, is the same act with the family changed on the out side, and every other click is of its kind with the family kept; the draw alone chooses whether and where, which future continues; that is all the board shows of a click.
