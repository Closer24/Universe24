\begin{abstract}
Can one local integer operator on a cubic lattice, read only through detectors' clicks, confront the quantum and relativistic experiments? The whole GameBoard is one element of one integer module, and the law is one linear operator on it: at every Node a second-order step on the Node's two levels and its six neighbours, with one integer pair per family and the division's remainder kept. The lattice's symmetries are the $48$ signed permutations of the axes and their $24$ rotations. The click is the only non-local act: when a record's one-way inward flux into a detector crosses its threshold, the record ends whole, at once. The birth, the other side of a click, is the only write. Exact on the lattice: the conserved quadratic forms, the step's bijection, the local flux identity, one click per quantum, Born's rule for a detector to the grain of the record's wheel, and no signalling through the click. A product state gives the Clauser--Horne--Shimony--Holt (CHSH) sum at most $2$; an entangled pair on integer polariser axes gives $14/5$. Recovered at long wavelength: $c = 1/\sqrt3$, isotropic at second order, and a moving packet's clock slowed by its own dispersion. The paper has twenty-four experiments: eighteen measured in clicks against pins written blind, ten with their pin, seven still owed and one a diagnostic, and six algebraic. None is run yet on this engine.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} integer lattice model; the symmetry group of the cube; one linear operator; Bell correlations; discrete relativity; pre-registered simulation

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The group was found, not assumed.} The operations came first, as acts on the board, and the road is told in Section~\ref{sec:route}: a message crossed one Link per interval, messages met at a Node, a detector clicked. The group is the answer to which maps preserve what the acts work on, a Node with six Ports and its Links and a causal cone of one Link per interval: the bijections of the six Ports that keep opposite Ports opposite are the $48$ signed permutations of the axes, and the only $\Z$-linear bijections that preserve the cone are these $48$, so no boost is among them and Lorentz's form is reached Outside from the clicks, not as a symmetry of the lattice. The $24$ came from the hand, a pseudoscalar that a reflection flips and a rotation keeps, which splits the $48$ into the $24$ rotations and the $24$ reflections. Every rule is required to commute with the $48$, a requirement beyond locality. Today the law is one operator on one element, with no stepping body and no merge of messages \cite[9.19]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The whole move, in the order it is built.} Seven things, each named once in the project's terminology \cite{terminology}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] \emph{The GameBoard and its group.} The board is a cubic lattice of Nodes, six neighbours each. The symmetry group of the cube is the group of order $48$: the $24$ rotations, the group of order $24$, and the reflections. The law is the same at every Node and commutes with the $48$ and with the translations, and two things follow: the pace of light is the same in every direction at leading order, $c^2 = 1/3$ from the six reads, and the departure from it appears only in a short wave, in the fixed cubic pattern of the $48$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:dispersion}).
\item[(2)] \emph{The one element.} The state of the whole board, every Node, every family and every label together, is one element $\bv$ of one integer module: one summand per quantum, a \emph{record}, with its two levels (now and one interval before) at every Node and label and the remainder of the one division \cite[9.19 (1)]{algebra}. A \emph{family} is an index of the summands: its vacuum pair $[\num, \den]$ and its label module, and nothing else \cite[9.22 (1)]{algebra}. A world has at most three: light, the experiment's matter and the holder of the tools' bodies.
\item[(3)] \emph{The one operator.} The law is one linear map $\bS$ of that element (Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}): per family the second-order step with the family's pair at every Node, and at declared Nodes a coupling between two families and an integer label matrix \cite[9.19 (2)]{algebra}.
\item[(4)] \emph{The tools.} A tool is a region of the operator: a cube of the holder family with its material integers (a pair, a coupling, an axis), its total momentum $\mathbf K$ (the wave vector of its character, $\mathbf 0$ at rest), at most a birth, and a name \cite[9.19 (5)]{algebra}; no code belongs to a tool \cite[A]{labtools}.
\item[(5)] \emph{The detector and the click.} A detector is a named region, a cube of side at least $3$. The click is the only non-local act: a record's one-way inward flux into the detectors named on its ladder, summed over intervals, crosses the record's threshold, and the record ends whole, everywhere, at that interval; the click is the detector's and names no Node inside it \cite[9.25]{algebra}.
\item[(6)] \emph{The birth.} The only write, the other side of a click: the click of an emitter body's own excited record writes the born record's two levels once on the emitter's Nodes, the transpose $\bE^{\mathsf T}$ of the reading, and from then on only the law advances it \cite[9.13, 9.17]{algebra}.
\item[(7)] \emph{The experiment.} A list of entries on the board, each its family, how much and where, with its momentum; a generator writes the initial state once from the operator itself, the engine checks it in integers at load and runs the law, and the output is the clicks, compared with a pin written before the run (Section~\ref{sec:algebra}, ``The experiment as data''; Section~\ref{sec:gameboard}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The general formula.} The law is one step $\bS$ of the one element at every interval, and two acts on its summands:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
&\text{the step:} && \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} + \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} = \bM_F\,\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} + \mathbf c_F, \qquad \bM_F = \bD_F^{-1}\bA/3,\\
&&& \text{per family } F, \text{ with the remainder kept};\\
&\text{the click:} && 2W\,C(t^*) \ge (2u + 1)\,T, \qquad C(t) = \sum_{s \le t}\ \sum_{j \to i} \max\bigl(G_{ij}(s), 0\bigr),\\
&&& \text{the sum over the Ports } j \to i \text{ into the ladder's detectors};\\
&\text{the birth:} && (\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}) \leftarrow \bE^{\mathsf T}(\text{the born pair}) \text{ on the emitter's Nodes},\\
&&& \text{at the click's interval}.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
In the step, $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}$, $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}$ and $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}$ are a record's levels over the Nodes now, one interval before and one interval on; $\bA$ is the read matrix of the board (the six reads of every Node); $\bD_F$ is the diagonal matrix of the per-Node weights $\den_i/\num_i$ of the family's pair; and $\mathbf c_F$ is the coupling's first difference of the other family's levels at the Nodes of a coupled body, with, at the Nodes of a polar body, the integer label matrix on the record's two label rows. In integers the step is Eq.~\eqref{eq:rule}, one division by $3\,\den_i$ with the remainder kept. In the click, $G_{ij}$ is the flux into Node $i$ from Node $j$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:flux}), $T$ the record's conserved form, $u$ its residue and $W$ its wheel, both read from the law at its birth (Definition~\ref{def:click}); the click falls at the first interval $t^*$ at which the inequality holds, at the detector whose share of that interval's flux holds the threshold, and deletes the record. The birth is fired only by a click. Nothing else happens on the board: no table, no rate, no drive, no take and no declared residue. Section~\ref{sec:algebra} states each piece with its proof or its mark; every derivation of the paper names the piece it starts from.

\paragraph{Physics as the passage of information.} What the law describes is the passage of information from an emitter to a detector, and nothing between them is read. A record is born at an emitter's click with its two levels written once; it moves on the lattice by the one operator, at most one Link per interval; it leaves at a click, where the record ends whole, and the click is what is read: reality Outside is the clicks, and a number of the board is a diagnostic. Transport is local at every step; the one non-local act is the click itself, the record ending everywhere at once, and no information passes through it (Theorem~\ref{th:nosignal}). The road by which this object was found is Section~\ref{sec:route}; the engine on which it is computed is Section~\ref{sec:gameboard}.

\paragraph{The reading rule and the notation.} Only a detector's click is a measurement, and an experiment is compared with nature and with its pin through its receivers' clicks alone; the operator, the tools, conservation and the initial state are proved in the algebra and checked by the property test and the load check, never by an experiment's run \cite[9.21 (4)]{algebra}. Every physics name in this paper (Born's, Tsirelson's, Malus's, Lorentz's) names the known form that the law's result recovers. A number of the GameBoard's state is a diagnostic of the host, labelled so, never compared with nature and never pinned. Every symbol is named in English at its first use, a scalar plain, a vector in bold lowercase, a matrix or an operator in bold uppercase.

\paragraph{What this paper contributes, and what it does not claim.} New here: one explicitly stated integer operator with its click and its birth, whose conserved forms, bijection, flux identity, Born's rule and no-signalling are theorems on the lattice; experiments as short lists of entries, their initial states generated from the operator itself and checked in integers at load; and twenty-four experiments: eighteen measured, placed as tools, ten with a pin written blind before any run and seven with their pin still owed (Table~\ref{tab:list}), and six algebraic, each with how it is computed (Table~\ref{tab:algebraic}). Not claimed: a derivation of the Lorentz group, which is not among the $48$; a moving rigid rod, which the model does not yet define (it has clocks and no rods, \cite[9.24 (6)]{algebra}); or any comparison with nature before the experiments are run on this engine. Gravity is not measured in this paper: its forms under a shell average appear only as an algebraic experiment, a limit of the algebra and not a run.

\section{The algebra: one element, one operator, the click and the birth}\label{sec:algebra}

This section states the object and nothing else: the board and its group, the one element, the one operator with its conserved forms, the flux, the click, the birth, the families and the experiment as data. Each statement carries the mark of the algebra document \cite{algebra}: PROVED (a proof for every board, in the document or here), DERIVED (from proved statements, by argument), COMPUTED (a computation, not a proof), or CARRIED (a design's proof, cited).

\paragraph{The definitions, in order.} Five objects, each one line of mathematics before its words.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] The GameBoard: the Nodes of a box in $\Z^3$ whose axes are periodic or open; the six Ports of a Node, $P = \{\pm x, \pm y, \pm z\}$, with the involution $e \mapsto -e$ (the opposite Port); at most one Link per interval.
\item[(2)] The group of order $48$ and its rotations: $G_{48} = \{g : P \to P \text{ a bijection with } g(-e) = -g(e)\}$, the $48$ signed permutations of the three axes; $\det : G_{48} \to \{+1, -1\}$ a homomorphism; $G_{24} = \ker \det$, the rotations, the group of order $24$ (Theorem~\ref{th:group}). The hand, a pseudoscalar, $h \mapsto \det(g)\,h$, tells the two cosets apart.
\item[(3)] The one element: $V = \bigoplus_q V_q$, $V_q = (\Z^{\text{Nodes}} \otimes L_q)^2 \otimes (\text{the remainders})$, one summand per quantum $q$, $L_q$ its label module ($\Z^2$ per arm; $\Z^2 \otimes \Z^2$ for a record of rank $2$); the state of the world at an interval is one element $\bv \in V$ \cite[9.19 (1)]{algebra}.
\item[(4)] The one operator: $\bS : V \to V$, per summand of family $F$ the step of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}, plus the coupling between summands of coupled families and the label matrix on the axes' Nodes; linear, local and equivariant \cite[9.19 (2), (6)]{algebra}.
\item[(5)] The two acts: the click, a record's one-way inward flux $\bE$ against its form, the rung and the deletion of the summand, the only non-local act; and the birth $\bE^{\mathsf T}$, the only write, at the click's interval (Definitions~\ref{def:click} and~\ref{def:birth}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The words of this paper.} The \emph{GameBoard} is the cubic array of Nodes, each joined to six neighbours by Links through directional Ports; time is a sequence of intervals. \emph{Inside} is the board and what moves on it, where no one measures; \emph{Outside} is the game above the board, the detectors and their clicks, the only place a number is read. A \emph{record} is one summand of the element, one quantum: its two levels over the Nodes and its labels, with the remainder kept per Node on the record's own row. A \emph{family} is an index of the summands, its vacuum pair and its label module. A \emph{body} is a set of Nodes, a region of the operator where a family's pair differs from the vacuum's; a location on the GameBoard is a Node and has no other name; a \emph{tool} is a region with its integers, at most a birth and a name; a \emph{face} is the operator's zero row beyond an open border; a \emph{receiver} or \emph{detector} is a name the host gives a region, on records' ladders \cite[9.19 (5)]{algebra}. Every number carries its \emph{kind}: DETECTOR, a click or a count of clicks, the only measurement; GAMEBOARD, a number of the board's state, a diagnostic and never a result; COMPUTATION, a number computed from the algebra before any run; DECLARATION, a declared integer of an experiment; HOST, a number of the host's bookkeeping, never a reading. Figure~\ref{fig:lattice} draws the board in space.

\paragraph{The six Ports, the group and the front.} Everything geometric in the law follows from one Node and its six Ports, $\pm x$, $\pm y$, $\pm z$, and from at most one Link per interval. The maps of the six Ports onto themselves that keep opposite Ports opposite are the signed permutations of the three axes, $48$ in all, the symmetry group of the cube and of the octahedron. The determinant splits them into two halves of $24$: the rotations, which permute the cube's four body diagonals, and the improper maps, the reflections with the central inversion among them.

\begin{theorem}[the $24$ of the name]\label{th:group}
The maps of the six Ports onto themselves that send opposite Ports to opposite Ports form the group of the signed permutations of the three axes, of order $2^3 \cdot 3! = 48$. The determinant is a homomorphism of this group onto $\{+1, -1\}$; its kernel, the group of order $24$ (the rotation group of the cube), has index $2$ and is isomorphic to the symmetric group $S_4$ of the permutations of the cube's four body diagonals; the other coset, the reflections, has $24$ elements too. The hand, a pseudoscalar that a map $g$ sends to $\det(g)$ times itself, tells the $24$ rotations from the $24$ reflections, as does an axial vector.
\end{theorem}
The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\paragraph{The rule per family.} At every Node $i$ a record's row $(\anow, \abefore, r)$ is mapped once per interval by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rule}
3\,\den_i\,\anext + r' = \num_i\,S_6 - 3\,\den_i\,\abefore + r, \qquad 0 \le r' < 3\,\den_i,
\end{equation}
then $\abefore \leftarrow \anow$, $\anow \leftarrow \anext$, $r \leftarrow r'$ \cite[8.1]{algebra}. Here $S_6$ is the sum of the six reads of the record's $\anow$ (the neighbour across a Link; the Node itself on a folded axis of extent $1$; $0$ beyond an open face), $[\num_i, \den_i]$ the family's pair at the Node (the vacuum's pair everywhere, a body's pair on its Nodes) and $r$ the remainder of the division, kept on the record's row. Light is the pair $[1, 1]$; a massive family is a pair with $\den > \num$, and nothing else names the mass. The step is made of three verbs on the row: the bilinear form (B), one entry $\num_i$ on the six reads and one entry $-3\,\den_i$ on the row's past; the translation (T) of the row by that rate; and the division (D) by the wall $3\,\den_i$ with the remainder kept. The three tests hold \cite[8.1]{algebra}: generic, one primitive with one declared pair and no family name; vector, the three verbs with no root and no float; local, the record's own row and its six reads, nothing kept at a Node beyond the row and its remainder.

\paragraph{The conserved form.} Let $\bA$ be the read matrix of the board, $A_{ij}$ the number of reads of $j$ among the six reads of $i$ (the neighbour across a Link once; the Node itself once per folded direction; nothing beyond an open face). It is symmetric, because $i$ reads $j$ in the direction $d$ exactly when $j$ reads $i$ in $-d$, and every row sums to at most $6$. Let $\bD = \mathrm{diag}(D_i)$ with $D_i = \den_i/\num_i \ge 1$ and $\bM = \bD^{-1}\bA/3$. Dividing Eq.~\eqref{eq:rule} by $3\,\den_i$, the level is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rulematrix}
\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} = \bM\,\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} + \mathbf e, \qquad e_i^{\mathrm{rem}} = \frac{r_i - r'_i}{3\,\den_i},
\end{equation}
the rule without the remainder being $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} + \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} = \bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}$ ($\mathbf e = \mathbf 0$), and $\mathbf e$ the vector of the remainder terms $e_i^{\mathrm{rem}}$.
\begin{theorem}[the conserved form]\label{th:form}
Without the remainder, the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:form}
\begin{aligned}
I(\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}) &= \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}\cdot\bD\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} + \mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}\cdot\bD\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}\cdot\bD\bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}\\
&= \sum_i D_i\,(a_{\mathrm{next},i}^2 + a_{\mathrm{now},i}^2) - \tfrac13 \sum_i a_{\mathrm{next},i}\,(\bA\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}})_i
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
is conserved on every board and every extent. With the remainders kept, the change in one interval is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:remid}
I(t) - I(t - 1) = \sum_i D_i\,e_i^{\mathrm{rem}}\,(a_{\mathrm{next},i} - a_{\mathrm{before},i}) = \sum_i \frac{(a_{\mathrm{next},i} - a_{\mathrm{before},i})(r_i - r'_i)}{3\,\num_i},
\end{equation}
an identity in the state the engine holds, with no tolerance.
\end{theorem}
The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
In the engine's units, times $3\,\num$ for one pair, the form is $3\,\den\,(a_{\mathrm{next}}^2 + a_{\mathrm{now}}^2)$ summed over the Nodes less $\num\,(a_{\mathrm{next},i}a_{\mathrm{now},j} + a_{\mathrm{next},j}a_{\mathrm{now},i})$ summed over the Links, an integer, and the change of Eq.~\eqref{eq:remid} is the integer $\sum_i (a_{\mathrm{next},i} - a_{\mathrm{before},i})(r_i - r'_i)$, bounded by $3\,\den$ times the motion. A static level ($\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} = \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}$ everywhere) has $I = 0$, so it clicks nowhere; the sum of the squares would count it.

\begin{proposition}[positive definiteness]\label{prop:posdef}
$I$ is positive definite for every massive pair ($\den_i > \num_i$ at every Node) on every board, and semidefinite at light's pair.
\end{proposition}
The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\paragraph{The modes and the stability condition.} Restricted to one family without the remainder, $\bS$ is the second-order linear recurrence $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} + \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} = \bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}$. In the variables $\bD^{1/2}\mathbf a$ it reads with the symmetric $\mathbf N$, whose orthonormal eigenvectors $\mathbf q$ are the board's \emph{modes}: on a mode with the eigenvalue $\lambda = 2\cos\omega$ the recurrence $q(t + 1) + q(t - 1) = 2\cos\omega\,q(t)$ has the solutions $\cos(\omega t)$ and $\sin(\omega t)$, a rotation at the angle $\omega$ per interval, the mode's clock, while an eigenvalue $|\lambda| \ge 2$ gives $\cosh$ and $\sinh$, a growth without bound. On a torus there is one mode per Node. A mode is \emph{bound} when $2\cos\omega$ lies above the vacuum band's top and \emph{free} inside the band (Section~\ref{sec:massive}).
\begin{theorem}[the stability condition]\label{th:stability}
Every mode is a rotation, and $I$ is positive definite, exactly when the largest eigenvalue of $\mathbf N$ is below $2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:posdef}'s block form, $I$ is positive definite exactly when every $|\lambda| < 2$. The matrix $\mathbf N$ has nonnegative entries, so by Perron--Frobenius its largest eigenvalue $\lambda_{\max}$ is the largest in absolute value, and every eigenvalue lies in $[-\lambda_{\max}, \lambda_{\max}]$. So $\lambda_{\max} < 2$ puts every eigenvalue in $(-2, 2)$: the one check bounds both ends \cite[9.19 (2)]{algebra}.
\end{proof}
A pair with $\num \le \den$ at every Node is sufficient (Proposition~\ref{prop:posdef}), not necessary: a one-Node well $[8, 7]$ on the vacuum $[7, 8]$ has $\lambda_{\max} = 1.90$ on a chain and is stable, while $[800, 700]$ on $[800, 809]$ has $2.03$ and runs away (COMPUTATIONS of the algebra, 9.19 (2)). The loader's check is the spectral one: the composed operator's largest eigenvalue below $2$, the file refused otherwise. Light cannot be a body: an index for light has $D_i \ge 1$ everywhere, so $\|\mathbf N\| \le 6/3 = 2$, no eigenvalue lies above the massless band's top and no bound mode of light exists \cite[8.3]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The coupling of two families.} At a Node of a coupled body $R$ the two records' rows are coupled by one entry of verb (B) over the other record's two levels, the first difference both ways, with $g$ and $G$ the two declared coupling pairs at the body's Nodes (the capital $G$ apart from the flux $G_{ij}$ below, which always carries two Node indices; CARRIED \cite[8.5]{algebra}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coupling}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf a_{m,\mathrm{next}} + \mathbf a_{m,\mathrm{before}} &= \bM\mathbf a_{m,\mathrm{now}} + g\,\mathbf P_R(\mathbf a_{l,\mathrm{now}} - \mathbf a_{l,\mathrm{before}}),\\
\mathbf a_{l,\mathrm{next}} + \mathbf a_{l,\mathrm{before}} &= \tfrac13\bA\mathbf a_{l,\mathrm{now}} - G\,\mathbf P_R(\mathbf a_{m,\mathrm{next}} - \mathbf a_{m,\mathrm{now}}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
the massive record $m$ receiving light's backward difference and light $l$ receiving the massive record's forward difference as its source, $\mathbf P_R$ the projector onto the Nodes ($1$ on $R$, $0$ elsewhere); the massive step is taken first and light's step reads the massive level just written. The body declares one pair on its coupled Nodes, so $\mathbf P_R\bD = D_{\mathrm{in}}\mathbf P_R$, and we write $\alpha = g\,D_{\mathrm{in}}/G$, one number of the body.
\begin{proposition}[the scheme is variational]\label{prop:lagrangian}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:coupling} is the discrete Euler--Lagrange equation of the two-point Lagrangian
\[
L(t) = -\mathbf a_{m,t}\cdot\bD\mathbf a_{m,t+1} + \tfrac12\,\mathbf a_{m,t}\cdot\tfrac13\bA\mathbf a_{m,t} + \alpha\bigl(-\mathbf a_{l,t}\cdot\mathbf a_{l,t+1} + \tfrac12\,\mathbf a_{l,t}\cdot\tfrac13\bA\mathbf a_{l,t}\bigr) + g\,\mathbf a_{m,t+1}\cdot\bD\mathbf P_R(\mathbf a_{l,t+1} - \mathbf a_{l,t}).
\]
\end{proposition}
The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
Being variational and linear, the scheme is symplectic, and a symplectic linear map has an exact quadratic invariant.
\begin{theorem}[the coupled invariant]\label{th:J}
With $I_m$ the massive form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:form} and $I_l$ light's form with the weight $1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J}
J = I_m + \alpha\,I_l + g \sum_{i \in R} D_i\,(a_{m,\mathrm{next}} - a_{m,\mathrm{now}})_i\,(a_{l,\mathrm{next}} - a_{l,\mathrm{now}})_i
\end{equation}
is conserved exactly on every board and every extent, before the remainders.
\end{theorem}
The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
The amplitude form of the coupling, light's row gaining $g$ times the massive level, is withdrawn as tachyonic: its mass matrix at zero wave vector, $\begin{pmatrix}0 & -g\\ -g & \omega_0^2\end{pmatrix}$, has the determinant $-g^2 < 0$ and so one negative eigenvalue \cite[8.5]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The label matrix.} Every record carries its rows per label value of each arm. \begin{definition}[The detector and the click]\label{def:click}
A \emph{detector} is one connected named region, a cube of side at least $3$ (a smaller cube, or one name on disconnected pieces, is refused at load); separate places are separate names \cite[9.25 (7)]{algebra}; record 1899 \cite{log}. At every interval $t$ each detector $d$ of the ladder receives the increment
\begin{equation}\label{eq:increment}
f_d(t) = \sum_{\substack{i \in d,\ j \notin d\\ j \text{ read by } i}} \max\bigl(G_{ij}(t), 0\bigr),
\end{equation}
the one-way inward flux through the region's Ports from outside it (a Link between two Nodes of one detector is no Port of it), and $C(t) = C(t - 1) + \sum_d f_d(t)$. The \emph{click} falls at the first interval $t^*$ with $C(t^*) \ge \theta$, that is $2W\,C(t^*) \ge (2u + 1)\,T$, at the detector whose segment of that interval's increment, laid out in the ladder's order, contains $\theta - C(t^* - 1)$; there the record ends, whole, everywhere, at that interval. \end{definition}

The three tests hold for the click: generic, the integers $u$, $T$, $W$ and the fluxes, no family name; vector, sums and one comparison per detector, the division's comparison (D), no root; local, each detector's increment is its own Ports', and the record's two integers $\theta$ and $C(t - 1)$ travel with the record as its residue does. \section{The massive family: the band, the cone, the bound body and the moving packet}\label{sec:massive}

\paragraph{The band and the pitch.} Read Eq.~\eqref{eq:rule} without its remainder on a character $a(\mathbf x, t) = \cos(\omega t - \mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x)$ of a periodic board, with $\omega$ the frequency angle per interval and $\mathbf k$ the wave vector (angles $k_x$, $k_y$, $k_z$ per Link).
\begin{theorem}[the band]\label{th:band}
The character is a solution of the rule exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:band}
\boxed{\,\cos\omega = \cos\omega_0\,\cos\omega_l(\mathbf k), \qquad \cos\omega_0 = \num/\den, \qquad \cos\omega_l(\mathbf k) = \tfrac13(\cos k_x + \cos k_y + \cos k_z)\,},
\end{equation}
$\omega_l(\mathbf k)$ being light's band at the same wave vector and $\omega_0$ the family's pitch, its rest frequency. Every character has a real $\omega$ for every $\mathbf k$, the band lying in $[-\num/\den, \num/\den]$ in $\cos\omega$.
\end{theorem}
The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
At $\mathbf k = \mathbf 0$ the gap is the pitch, the rest period $N_0 = 2\pi/\omega_0$ intervals and the rest energy $h\,\omega_0$, $h$ the action; light has $\omega_0 = 0$, a zero mode that is a level and not a clock, so the whole difference between light and a massive family in the algebra is a gap. \begin{equation}\label{eq:bandsin}
4\sin^2\frac{\omega}{2} = 2\Bigl(1 - \frac{\num}{\den}\Bigr) + \frac{\num}{\den}\cdot\frac43\Bigl(\sin^2\frac{k_x}{2} + \sin^2\frac{k_y}{2} + \sin^2\frac{k_z}{2}\Bigr),
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The two paces and the exact cone.} Near $\mathbf k = \mathbf 0$, $\cos\omega_l = 1 - k^2/6 + O(k^4)$ with $k = |\mathbf k|$, so $\omega_l^2 = k^2/3 + O(k^4)$: light's pace is $c = 1/\sqrt3$ Links per interval. For the massive band there are two second-order readings \cite[8.1]{algebra}.

\[
c_m^2 = \cos\omega_0\,c^2, \qquad c_m = c\sqrt{\cos\omega_0} = c\bigl(1 - \omega_0^2/4 + O(\omega_0^4)\bigr),
\]

\boxed{\,\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + c_{\mathrm{eff}}^2\,k^2 + O(k^4), \qquad c_{\mathrm{eff}}^2 = \cos\omega_0\,\frac{\omega_0}{\sin\omega_0}\,c^2, \qquad \frac{c_{\mathrm{eff}}^2}{c_m^2} = \frac{\omega_0}{\sin\omega_0} = 1 + \frac{\omega_0^2}{6} + O(\omega_0^4)\,},

\begin{equation}\label{eq:vg}

\end{equation}

\subsection{Discussion and conclusions}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Positioning.} The model is a local deterministic lattice dynamics with one non-local act, the click; its correlations violate the CHSH bound \cite{bell1964,chsh1969} through that act, and its counts do not signal \cite{grw1980} (Theorem~\ref{th:nosignal}). Its transport is an exact integer lattice dynamics, as a lattice gas's is \cite{hpp1976,fhp1986}, and its light disperses at $k^2$ with the cubic pattern where the lattice automata of \cite{bialynicki1994,meyer1996,arrighi2019} disperse by their own forms. The residue read from the law does the work of quantum equilibrium \cite{bohm1952,dgz1992}; the model is $\psi$-ontic (the wave function is part of the state) \cite{spekkens2007,hs2010}; its CHSH sum on integer axes lies between the local bound $2$ and Tsirelson's $2\sqrt2$ \cite{tsirelson1980,pr1994}.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} One local integer operator on the cubic lattice, required to commute with the group of order $48$, with one non-local act, the click, and one write, the birth, is read only through detectors' clicks. Exact on the lattice are the conserved forms, the step's bijection, the flux identity, one click per quantum, Born's rule for a detector and no signalling; the CHSH sum on integer axes is $14/5$; a moving clock slows by its own dispersion. Of twenty-four experiments, eighteen are measured, ten of them with a pin written blind, seven with it still owed and one a diagnostic (Table~\ref{tab:list}), and six are algebraic (Table~\ref{tab:algebraic}); none is run yet on the new engine, and the runs decide, row by row, what the law matches and where it fails.

\section{The proofs in full}\label{app:proofs}

The proofs of the statements of the body, in the order they stand there, each from the algebra document's section cited in it.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{th:group}]
A map that keeps opposite Ports opposite is fixed by the images of $+x$, $+y$, $+z$, which must be three Ports no two of them equal or opposite: $6 \cdot 4 \cdot 2 = 48$ choices, each a signed permutation matrix, and every signed permutation matrix keeps opposite Ports opposite. The determinant of a product is the product of the determinants, so $\det$ is a homomorphism, onto since $-\mathbf 1$ has determinant $-1$; its kernel has index $2$, order $24$. A rotation permutes the four lines through opposite vertices of the cube, and only the identity fixes all four, so the kernel embeds in $S_4$, of order $24$: the two are isomorphic.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{th:form}]
Write $\mathbf x = \mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}$, $\mathbf y = \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}$ and $\mathbf y' = \bM\mathbf y - \mathbf x$ the level one interval later, with no remainder. Then
\[
I(\mathbf y', \mathbf y) - I(\mathbf y, \mathbf x) = \mathbf y'\cdot\bD\mathbf y' - \mathbf x\cdot\bD\mathbf x - \mathbf y'\cdot\bD\bM\mathbf y + \mathbf y\cdot\bD\bM\mathbf x .
\]
Since $\bD\bM\mathbf y = \bD\mathbf y' + \bD\mathbf x$, the third term is $-\mathbf y'\cdot\bD\mathbf y' - \mathbf y'\cdot\bD\mathbf x$. Since $\bD\bM = \bA/3$ is symmetric, the fourth term is $(\bD\bM\mathbf y)\cdot\mathbf x = \mathbf y'\cdot\bD\mathbf x + \mathbf x\cdot\bD\mathbf x$. The four terms sum to $0$. Nothing but the symmetry of $\bD\bM$ is used, so the identity holds on every extent and with every face. With the remainder, $\mathbf y' = \bM\mathbf y - \mathbf x + \mathbf e$, so $\bD\bM\mathbf y = \bD\mathbf y' + \bD\mathbf x - \bD\mathbf e$: the third term gains $+\mathbf y'\cdot\bD\mathbf e$ and the fourth gains $-\mathbf e\cdot\bD\mathbf x$, and the change is $\mathbf e\cdot\bD(\mathbf y' - \mathbf x)$. In the interval that $\mathbf y'$ ends, $\mathbf y'$ is $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}$ and $\mathbf x$ is $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}$, which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:remid} \cite[8.2]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:posdef}]
Let $\mathbf N = \bD^{-1/2}(\bA/3)\bD^{-1/2}$, symmetric. In the variables $(\bD^{1/2}\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, \bD^{1/2}\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}})$ the form $I$ is the quadratic form of the block matrix $\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf 1 & -\mathbf N/2\\ -\mathbf N/2 & \mathbf 1\end{pmatrix}$, $\mathbf 1$ the identity; on an eigenvector of $\mathbf N$ with the eigenvalue $\lambda$ it is $\begin{pmatrix}1 & -\lambda/2\\ -\lambda/2 & 1\end{pmatrix}$, with the eigenvalues $1 \pm \lambda/2$, positive exactly when $|\lambda| < 2$. By Gershgorin every eigenvalue of $\bA$ has $|\lambda| \le 6$ (each row at most six entries, each $1$), so $|\lambda(\mathbf N)| \le 6/(3\min_i D_i) = 2\max_i(\num_i/\den_i)$, below $2$ when every $\den_i > \num_i$ and equal to $2$ at light's pair, whose null directions are the zero mode and the checkerboard \cite[8.2]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:lagrangian}]
The derivative of $L(t - 1) + L(t)$ with respect to $\mathbf a_{m,t}$, set to zero, is $-\bD\mathbf a_{m,t-1} + g\,\bD\mathbf P_R(\mathbf a_{l,t} - \mathbf a_{l,t-1}) - \bD\mathbf a_{m,t+1} + \tfrac13\bA\mathbf a_{m,t} = 0$, which is the massive step with light's backward difference. With respect to $\mathbf a_{l,t}$ it is $-\alpha\mathbf a_{l,t-1} + g\,\mathbf P_R\bD\mathbf a_{m,t} - g\,\mathbf P_R\bD\mathbf a_{m,t+1} - \alpha\mathbf a_{l,t+1} + \alpha\tfrac13\bA\mathbf a_{l,t} = 0$, that is $\mathbf a_{l,t+1} + \mathbf a_{l,t-1} = \tfrac13\bA\mathbf a_{l,t} - (g D_{\mathrm{in}}/\alpha)\,\mathbf P_R(\mathbf a_{m,t+1} - \mathbf a_{m,t})$, light's step with $g D_{\mathrm{in}}/\alpha = G$ \cite[8.5]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{th:J}]
The massive step is Eq.~\eqref{eq:rulematrix} with $\mathbf e_m = g\,\mathbf P_R(\mathbf a_{l,\mathrm{now}} - \mathbf a_{l,\mathrm{before}})$, so by Eq.~\eqref{eq:remid} $I_m(t) - I_m(t - 1) = g\,D_{\mathrm{in}}\sum_{i\in R}(a_{l,\mathrm{now}} - a_{l,\mathrm{before}})_i(a_{m,\mathrm{next}} - a_{m,\mathrm{before}})_i$. Light's step has $\mathbf e_l = -G\,\mathbf P_R(\mathbf a_{m,\mathrm{next}} - \mathbf a_{m,\mathrm{now}})$, so $\alpha(I_l(t) - I_l(t - 1)) = -g\,D_{\mathrm{in}}\sum_{i\in R}(a_{m,\mathrm{next}} - a_{m,\mathrm{now}})_i(a_{l,\mathrm{next}} - a_{l,\mathrm{before}})_i$. Write per Node $X = a_{m,\mathrm{next}} - a_{m,\mathrm{now}}$, $Y = a_{m,\mathrm{now}} - a_{m,\mathrm{before}}$, $U = a_{l,\mathrm{next}} - a_{l,\mathrm{now}}$ and $V = a_{l,\mathrm{now}} - a_{l,\mathrm{before}}$. The cross term changes by $g\,D_{\mathrm{in}}(XU - YV)$, and the whole change of $J$ per Node is
\[
g\,D_{\mathrm{in}}\bigl[V(X + Y) - X(U + V) + XU - YV\bigr] = 0
\]
\cite[8.5]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:flux}]
Write $x_i = a_{\mathrm{next},i}$, $y_i = a_{\mathrm{now},i}$ and $z_i = a_{\mathrm{before},i}$, so $e_i(t) = D_i(x_i^2 + y_i^2) - \tfrac13 x_i(\bA\mathbf y)_i$ and $e_i(t - 1) = D_i(y_i^2 + z_i^2) - \tfrac13 y_i(\bA\mathbf z)_i$. Their difference is $D_i(x_i^2 - z_i^2) - \tfrac13\bigl[x_i(\bA\mathbf y)_i - y_i(\bA\mathbf z)_i\bigr]$. The rule gives $D_i(x_i + z_i) = \tfrac13(\bA\mathbf y)_i$, so $D_i(x_i^2 - z_i^2) = \tfrac13(x_i - z_i)(\bA\mathbf y)_i$, and the difference is
\[
\tfrac13\bigl[(x_i - z_i)(\bA\mathbf y)_i - x_i(\bA\mathbf y)_i + y_i(\bA\mathbf z)_i\bigr] = \tfrac13\bigl[y_i(\bA\mathbf z)_i - z_i(\bA\mathbf y)_i\bigr] = \sum_j \tfrac13 A_{ij}\,(y_i z_j - z_i y_j),
\]
which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:flux}; the pairs have cancelled, and $G_{ji} = -G_{ij}$ since $\bA$ is symmetric \cite[9.19 (3)]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{th:oneclick}]
Every increment $f_d(t) \ge 0$, so $C$ is non-decreasing. Since $u \le W - 1$, $\theta = (2u + 1)T/(2W) \le (2W - 1)T/(2W) < T$. With the face receiver last on the ladder, a record's whole form is eventually offered: what stays on the board enters some detector, and what leaves enters the face, so $C(t) \to C(\infty) \ge T > \theta$ and $\theta$ is crossed at a first interval $t^*$. At $t^*$ the record is deleted whole, so no later increment exists and $\theta$ cannot be crossed again: one click \cite[9.25 (2)]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{th:born}]
With $u$ uniform on $\{0, \dots, W - 1\}$ the threshold $\theta = (2u + 1)T/(2W)$ is uniform on the $W$ midpoints of a partition of $[0, T)$ into parts of length $T/W$. Lay out, for each interval $t$ and each detector $d$ of the ladder in its declared order, the segment
\[
\Bigl[\,C(t - 1) + \sum_{d' \text{ before } d} f_{d'}(t),\ \ C(t - 1) + \sum_{d' \text{ up to } d} f_{d'}(t)\Bigr)
\]
of length $f_d(t)$. These segments are disjoint and cover $[0, C(\infty))$, and $C(\infty) \ge T$ with the face last. By Definition~\ref{def:click} the click falls at $(t, d)$ exactly when $\theta$ lies in that segment, so the number of the $W$ midpoints in it is $W f_d(t)/T$ up to one at each end, and $P(t, d) = f_d(t)/T$ to the grain $1/W$. Summing over $t$, $P(d) = \sum_t f_d(t)/T = C_d(\infty)/T$. The total length of a detector's segments is $\sum_t f_d(t)$ for every order within an interval, so the distribution does not depend on that order; a single record's detector at a given $u$ does, which is why the order is the world's declared data \cite[9.25 (3), (4)]{algebra}. A detector's increment is the sum of its boundary Nodes' inward fluxes from outside, so its segment is the union of its Nodes' segments and its share is the sum of their shares \cite[9.25 (10)]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{th:nosignal}]
Let the pair's four weights be $w_{ab}$, $a$ Alice's label and $b$ Bob's, and let $\bM_A$ and $\bM_B$ be the two label matrices of the axes $(a_A, b_A)$ and $(a_B, b_B)$. The joint amplitude is\[J(o_A, o_B) = \sum_{a, b} w_{ab}\,\bM_A[o_A][a]\,\bM_B[o_B][b],\] and the weight is $R = J^2$. For a fixed $o_A$ write $(\mathbf w_{o_A})_b = \sum_a w_{ab}\,\bM_A[o_A][a]$, her arm's label state projected on her own outcome; then $J(o_A, o_B) = (\bM_B\mathbf w_{o_A})_{o_B}$ and
\[
\sum_{o_B} R(o_A, o_B) = |\bM_B\mathbf w_{o_A}|^2 = (a_B^2 + b_B^2)\,|\mathbf w_{o_A}|^2
\]
by Eq.~\eqref{eq:brahmagupta}. So her share of $+$ is $(a_B^2 + b_B^2)|\mathbf w_+|^2/\bigl((a_B^2 + b_B^2)(|\mathbf w_+|^2 + |\mathbf w_-|^2)\bigr)$, the scale cancelling exactly. On the joint ladder with $o_A$ outer, her outcome $+$ is the union of the segments $[0, \sum_b R(+, b))$, whose length is fixed by her own projection whatever Bob's axis; her click's time is her own arm's flux through her own detectors. Nothing of Bob's setting, placement or distance enters either \cite[9.25 (6)]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{th:bijection}]
Fix $S_6$ and write $d = 3\,\den$. The map $(\abefore, r) \mapsto n = \num\,S_6 - d\,\abefore + r$ from $\Z \times \{0, \dots, d - 1\}$ to $\Z$ is a bijection: every integer $n$ has exactly one representation $n - \num\,S_6 = -d\,\abefore + r$ with $0 \le r < d$, the Euclidean division of $\num\,S_6 - n$ by $d$ read with the ceiling. The map $n \mapsto (\lfloor n/d \rfloor, n \bmod d) = (\anext, r')$ is a bijection of $\Z$ onto $\Z \times \{0, \dots, d - 1\}$. Their composition is the step, and its inverse is the pair of inverses: from $(\anext, r')$, $n = d\,\anext + r'$, $m = \num\,S_6 - n = d\,\abefore - r$, so $\abefore = \lceil m/d \rceil$ and $r = d\,\abefore - m$. The reads $S_6$ are of the level now, which the inverse holds at every Node, so the inverse is computed Node by Node. For two coupled families each half-step is invertible given the other family's levels, which it leaves in place, so the pair of half-steps is inverted in the reverse order \cite[8.8]{algebra}; \cite[9.20 (A) 4]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:power}]
$\bM + 2\,\mathbf 1$ has the eigenvalues $\lambda_k + 2$ of $\bM$ shifted by $2$, all in $(0, 4)$ by Theorem~\ref{th:stability}, so they are positive and the lowest mode cannot compete in absolute value. Its entries are nonnegative, so by Perron--Frobenius its top eigenvalue is simple on a connected board with a nonnegative eigenvector, the bound mode. Expanding the start on the eigenvectors, the $k$-th component is multiplied by $((\lambda_k + 2)/(\lambda + 2))^n$ after $n$ iterations, at most $(1 - \mathrm{gap}/(\lambda + 2))^n$ for every $k$ but the top \cite[9.22 (7)]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:residual}]
Write $\mathbf p = \mathbf q + \boldsymbol\delta$ with $|\delta_i| \le 1/2$, and $a/b = \lambda + \varepsilon$ with $|\varepsilon| \le 1/(2b)$. Using the eigen-equation,
\[
b\,\num_i(S_6\mathbf p)_i - 3\,\den_i\,a\,p_i = b\,\num_i(S_6\boldsymbol\delta)_i - 3\,\den_i\,b\lambda\,\delta_i - 3\,\den_i\,b\varepsilon\,q_i - 3\,\den_i\,b\varepsilon\,\delta_i .
\]
The four terms are bounded by $6 \cdot \tfrac12\,b\,\num_i = 3b\,\num_i$, by $3\,\den_i \cdot 2 \cdot \tfrac12\,b = 3b\,\den_i$, by $3\,\den_i \cdot \tfrac12 A \le \tfrac32 b\,\den_i$, and by $\tfrac34\den_i \le \tfrac32 b\,\den_i$; their sum is at most $b(3\,\num_i + 6\,\den_i)$ \cite[9.22 (7)]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{th:band}]
By the angle-addition formula, $a(\mathbf x, t + 1) + a(\mathbf x, t - 1) = 2\cos\omega\,a(\mathbf x, t)$, and the six reads give $S_6 = \sum_{\pm, i}\cos(\omega t - \mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x \mp k_i) = 2(\cos k_x + \cos k_y + \cos k_z)\,a(\mathbf x, t)$. The rule $3\,\den(\anext + \abefore) = \num\,S_6$ holds at every Node and interval exactly when $3\,\den \cdot 2\cos\omega = 2\,\num\,(\cos k_x + \cos k_y + \cos k_z)$, which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:band}. Since $|\cos k_x + \cos k_y + \cos k_z| \le 3$, $|\cos\omega| \le \num/\den \le 1$ \cite[8.1]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:dispersion}]
At $[1, 1]$, $\cos\omega = \tfrac13\sum_i\cos(kn_i) = 1 - \tfrac{k^2}{6} + \tfrac{k^4}{72}\sum_i n_i^4 + O(k^6)$, using $\sum_i n_i^2 = 1$. Write $u = 1 - \cos\omega = \tfrac{k^2}{6} - \tfrac{k^4}{72}\sum_i n_i^4 + O(k^6)$. Inverting $u = \omega^2/2 - \omega^4/24 + O(\omega^6)$ gives $\omega^2 = 2u + u^2/3 + O(u^3)$, so
\[
\omega^2 = \frac{k^2}{3} - \frac{k^4}{36}\sum_i n_i^4 + \frac{k^4}{108} + O(k^6) = \frac{k^2}{3}\Bigl(1 - \frac{(3\sum_i n_i^4 - 1)\,k^2}{36}\Bigr) + O(k^6),
\]
$\omega = (k/\sqrt3)\bigl(1 - (3\sum_i n_i^4 - 1)k^2/72 + O(k^4)\bigr)$, and $d\omega/dk$ is Eq.~\eqref{eq:dispersion} with $c = 1/\sqrt3$ \cite{lightdispersion}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{th:noselfbound}]
Away from material the step commutes with every translation of the torus, so it commutes with the discrete Fourier transform's shifts and is diagonal on the characters $e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}$: every solution is a sum of characters, each advanced by its own clock $\omega(\mathbf k)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:band}. A stationary solution, one whose level returns to itself up to a phase, is then a single character or a sum at one frequency, spread over the whole board, and not localised; a localised mode needs an eigenvector of a composed operator that breaks the translations, a region of material. A body's own quanta cannot bind each other, since that would need a term of degree two or more in one family's amplitude, which the three tests exclude. A well carried by a record of the holder family would need a family's pair at a Node to be a function of the holder's amplitude there, a term of degree three, or a threshold read on a Node, and is refused \cite[9.24 (1), (3)]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:product}]
Then $J(o_A, o_B) = (\bM_A\mathbf x)_{o_A}(\bM_B\mathbf y)_{o_B}$ and $R$ factors, $R(o_A, o_B) = P_A(o_A)P_B(o_B)\cdot$(a positive scale), so $E(\mathbf a, \mathbf b) = e_A(\mathbf a)\,e_B(\mathbf b)$ with $|e_A|, |e_B| \le 1$. Then $S = e_A(\mathbf a)[e_B(\mathbf b) - e_B(\mathbf b')] + e_A(\mathbf a')[e_B(\mathbf b) + e_B(\mathbf b')]$, and $|S| \le |e_B(\mathbf b) - e_B(\mathbf b')| + |e_B(\mathbf b) + e_B(\mathbf b')| = 2\max(|e_B(\mathbf b)|, |e_B(\mathbf b')|) \le 2$ \cite[9.22 (6)]{algebra}.
\end{proof}
