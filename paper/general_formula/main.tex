\begin{abstract}
Can one local integer operator on a cubic lattice, read only through detectors' clicks, confront the quantum and relativistic experiments? The whole GameBoard is one element of one integer module, and the law is one linear operator on it: at every Node a second-order step on the Node's own two levels and its six neighbours, with one declared integer pair per family and the remainder of the one division kept, and at declared cells a coupling between two families and an integer label matrix. The lattice's symmetries are the $48$ signed permutations of the axes and their $24$ rotations. Two acts complete the law. The click is the only non-local act: when a record's one-way inward flux into a detector, a cube of side at least $3$, crosses the record's threshold, the record ends whole, everywhere, at once. The birth is the only write, the other side of a click. Exact on the lattice: the conserved quadratic form of every family and of two coupled families, the step's bijection, the local flux identity, one click per quantum, Born's rule as a detector's share of a record's inward flux to the grain $1/W$ of the record's wheel, and no signalling through the click. A product state gives the Clauser--Horne--Shimony--Holt (CHSH) sum at most $2$; an entangled pair read on integer polariser axes gives $14/5$. Recovered as the wave number goes to $0$: $c = 1/\sqrt3$ as the band's pace, isotropic at second order by the $48$; and a moving packet's clock slows by its own dispersion, with no rule for it. An experiment is a list of entries on the board; its initial state is generated once by the operator itself and checked in integers at load. Seventeen experiments are closed in the algebra with pins written blind; none is run yet on this engine.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} integer lattice model; the symmetry group of the cube; one linear operator; entangled pair; Bell correlations; discrete relativity; pre-registered simulation

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The group was found, not assumed.} The operations came first, as acts on the board, and the road is told in Section~\ref{sec:route}: a message crossed one Link per interval, messages met at a Node, a detector clicked. The group is the answer to which maps preserve what the acts work on, a Node with six Ports and its Links and a causal cone of one Link per interval: the bijections of the six Ports that keep opposite Ports opposite are the $48$ signed permutations of the axes, and the only $\Z$-linear bijections that preserve the cone are these $48$, so no boost is among them and Lorentz's form is reached Outside from the clicks, not as a symmetry of the lattice. The $24$ came from the hand, a pseudoscalar that a reflection flips and a rotation keeps, which splits the $48$ into the $24$ rotations and the $24$ reflections. Every rule is required to commute with the $48$, a requirement beyond locality. Today the law is one operator on one element, with no stepping body and no merge of messages \cite[9.19]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The whole move, in the order it is built.} Seven things, each named once in the project's terminology \cite{terminology}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] \emph{The GameBoard and its group.} The board is a cubic lattice of Nodes, six neighbours each. The symmetry group of the cube is the group of order $48$: the $24$ rotations, the group of order $24$, and the reflections. The law is the same at every Node and commutes with the $48$ and with the translations, and two things follow: the pace of light is the same in every direction at leading order, $c^2 = 1/3$ from the six reads, and the departure from it appears only in a short wave, in the fixed cubic pattern of the $48$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:dispersion}).
\item[(2)] \emph{The one element.} The state of the whole board, every Node, every family and every label together, is one element $\bv$ of one integer module: one summand per quantum, a \emph{record}, with its two levels (now and one interval before) at every Node and label and the remainder of the one division \cite[9.19 (1)]{algebra}. A \emph{family} is an index of the summands: its vacuum pair $[\num, \den]$ and its label module, and nothing else \cite[9.22 (1)]{algebra}. A world has at most three: light, the experiment's matter and the holder of the tools' bodies.
\item[(3)] \emph{The one operator.} The law is one linear map $\bS$ of that element (Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}): per family the second-order step with the family's pair at every Node, and at declared cells a coupling between two families and an integer label matrix \cite[9.19 (2)]{algebra}.
\item[(4)] \emph{The tools.} A tool is a region of the operator: a cube of the holder family with its material integers (a pair, a coupling, an axis), its total momentum $\mathbf K$ (the wave vector of its character, $\mathbf 0$ at rest), at most a birth, and a name \cite[9.19 (5)]{algebra}; no code belongs to a tool \cite[A]{labtools}.
\item[(5)] \emph{The detector and the click.} A detector is a named region, a cube of side at least $3$. The click is the only non-local act: a record's one-way inward flux into the detectors named on its ladder, summed over intervals, crosses the record's threshold, and the record ends whole, everywhere, at that interval; the click is the detector's and names no Node inside it \cite[9.25]{algebra}.
\item[(6)] \emph{The birth.} The only write, the other side of a click: the click of an emitter body's own excited record writes the born record's two levels once on the emitter's cells, the transpose $\bE^{\mathsf T}$ of the reading, and from then on only the law advances it \cite[9.13, 9.17]{algebra}.
\item[(7)] \emph{The experiment.} A list of entries on the board, each its family, how much and where, with its momentum; a generator writes the initial state once from the operator itself, the engine checks it in integers at load and runs the law, and the output is the clicks, compared with a pin written before the run (Section~\ref{sec:algebra}, ``The experiment as data''; Section~\ref{sec:gameboard}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The general formula.} The law is one step $\bS$ of the one element at every interval, and two acts on its summands:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
&\text{the step:} && \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} + \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} = \bM_F\,\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} + \mathbf c_F, \qquad \bM_F = \bD_F^{-1}\bA/3, \quad \text{per family } F, \text{ with the remainder kept};\\
&\text{the click:} && 2W\,C(t^*) \ge (2u + 1)\,T, \qquad C(t) = \sum_{s \le t}\ \sum_{\text{Ports } j \to i \text{ into the ladder's detectors}} \max\bigl(G_{ij}(s), 0\bigr);\\
&\text{the birth:} && (\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}) \leftarrow \bE^{\mathsf T}(\text{the born pair}) \text{ on the emitter's cells, at the click's interval}.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
In the step, $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}$, $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}$ and $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}$ are a record's levels over the Nodes now, one interval before and one interval on; $\bA$ is the read matrix of the board (the six reads of every Node); $\bD_F$ is the diagonal matrix of the per-Node weights $\den_i/\num_i$ of the family's pair; and $\mathbf c_F$ is the coupling's first difference of the other family's levels at the cells of a coupled body, with, at the cells of a polar body, the integer label matrix on the record's two label rows. In integers the step is Eq.~\eqref{eq:rule}, one division by $3\,\den_i$ with the remainder kept. In the click, $G_{ij}$ is the flux into Node $i$ from Node $j$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:flux}), $T$ the record's conserved form, $u$ its residue and $W$ its wheel, both read from the law at its birth (Definition~\ref{def:click}); the click falls at the first interval $t^*$ at which the inequality holds, at the detector whose share of that interval's flux holds the threshold, and deletes the record. The birth is fired only by a click. Nothing else happens on the board: no table, no rate, no drive, no take and no declared residue. Section~\ref{sec:algebra} states each piece with its proof or its mark; every derivation of the paper names the piece it starts from.

\paragraph{Physics as the passage of information.} What the law describes is the passage of information from an emitter to a detector, and nothing between them is read. A record is born at an emitter's click with its two levels written once; it moves on the lattice by the one operator, at most one Link per interval; it leaves at a click, where the record ends whole, and the click is what is read: reality Outside is the clicks, and a number of the board is a diagnostic. Transport is local at every step; the one non-local act is the click itself, the record ending everywhere at once, and no information passes through it (Theorem~\ref{th:nosignal}). Every physics name in this paper (Born's, Tsirelson's, Malus's, Lorentz's) names the known form that the law's result recovers. Every symbol is named in English at its first use, a scalar plain, a vector in bold lowercase, a matrix or an operator in bold uppercase.

\section{The algebra: one element, one operator, the click and the birth}\label{sec:algebra}

This section states the object and nothing else: the board and its group, the one element, the one operator with its conserved forms, the flux, the click, the birth, the families and the experiment as data. Each statement carries the mark of the algebra document \cite{algebra}: PROVED (a proof for every board, in the document or here), DERIVED (from proved statements, by argument), COMPUTED (a computation, not a proof), or CARRIED (a design's proof, cited).

\paragraph{The definitions, in order.} Five objects, each one line of mathematics before its words.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] The GameBoard: the Nodes of a box in $\Z^3$ whose axes are periodic or open; the six Ports of a Node, $P = \{\pm x, \pm y, \pm z\}$, with the involution $e \mapsto -e$ (the opposite Port); at most one Link per interval.
\item[(2)] The group of order $48$ and its rotations: $G_{48} = \{g : P \to P \text{ a bijection with } g(-e) = -g(e)\}$, the $48$ signed permutations of the three axes; $\det : G_{48} \to \{+1, -1\}$ a homomorphism; $G_{24} = \ker \det$, the rotations, the group of order $24$ (Theorem~\ref{th:group}). The hand, a pseudoscalar, $h \mapsto \det(g)\,h$, tells the two cosets apart.
\item[(3)] The one element: $V = \bigoplus_q V_q$, $V_q = (\Z^{\text{Nodes}} \otimes L_q)^2 \otimes (\text{the remainders})$, one summand per quantum $q$, $L_q$ its label module ($\Z^2$ per arm; $\Z^2 \otimes \Z^2$ for a record of rank $2$); the state of the world at an interval is one element $\bv \in V$ \cite[9.19 (1)]{algebra}.
\item[(4)] The one operator: $\bS : V \to V$, per summand of family $F$ the step of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}, plus the coupling between summands of coupled families and the label matrix on the axes' cells; linear, local and equivariant \cite[9.19 (2), (6)]{algebra}.
\item[(5)] The two acts: the click, a record's one-way inward flux $\bE$ against its form, the rung and the deletion of the summand, the only non-local act; and the birth $\bE^{\mathsf T}$, the only write, at the click's interval (Definitions~\ref{def:click} and~\ref{def:birth}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The words of this paper.} The \emph{GameBoard} is the cubic array of Nodes, each joined to six neighbours by Links through directional Ports; time is a sequence of intervals. \emph{Inside} is the board and what moves on it, where no one measures; \emph{Outside} is the game above the board, the detectors and their clicks, the only place a number is read. A \emph{record} is one summand of the element, one quantum: its two levels over the Nodes and its labels, with the remainder kept per Node on the record's own row. A \emph{family} is an index of the summands, its vacuum pair and its label module. A \emph{body} is a region of the operator where a family's pair differs from the vacuum's; a \emph{tool} is a region with its integers, at most a birth and a name; a \emph{face} is the operator's zero row beyond an open border; a \emph{receiver} or \emph{detector} is a name the host gives a region, on records' ladders \cite[9.19 (5)]{algebra}. Every number carries its \emph{kind}: DETECTOR, a click or a count of clicks, the only measurement; GAMEBOARD, a number of the board's state, a diagnostic and never a result; COMPUTATION, a number computed from the algebra before any run; DECLARATION, a declared integer of an experiment; HOST, a number of the host's bookkeeping, never a reading. Figure~\ref{fig:lattice} draws the board in space.

\paragraph{The six Ports, the group and the front.} Everything geometric in the law follows from one Node and its six Ports, $\pm x$, $\pm y$, $\pm z$, and from at most one Link per interval. The maps of the six Ports onto themselves that keep opposite Ports opposite are the signed permutations of the three axes, $48$ in all, the symmetry group of the cube and of the octahedron. The determinant splits them into two halves of $24$: the rotations, which permute the cube's four body diagonals, and the improper maps, the reflections with the central inversion among them.

\begin{theorem}[the $24$ of the name]\label{th:group}
The maps of the six Ports onto themselves that send opposite Ports to opposite Ports form the group of the signed permutations of the three axes, of order $2^3 \cdot 3! = 48$. The determinant is a homomorphism of this group onto $\{+1, -1\}$; its kernel, the group of order $24$ (the rotation group of the cube), has index $2$ and is isomorphic to the symmetric group $S_4$ of the permutations of the cube's four body diagonals; the other coset, the reflections, has $24$ elements too. The hand, a pseudoscalar that a map $g$ sends to $\det(g)$ times itself, tells the $24$ rotations from the $24$ reflections, as does an axial vector.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A map that keeps opposite Ports opposite is fixed by the images of $+x$, $+y$, $+z$, which must be three Ports no two of them equal or opposite: $6 \cdot 4 \cdot 2 = 48$ choices, each a signed permutation matrix, and every signed permutation matrix keeps opposite Ports opposite. The determinant of a product is the product of the determinants, so $\det$ is a homomorphism, onto since $-\mathbf 1$ has determinant $-1$; its kernel has index $2$, order $24$. A rotation permutes the four lines through opposite vertices of the cube, and only the identity fixes all four, so the kernel embeds in $S_4$, of order $24$: the two are isomorphic.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The rule per family.} At every Node $i$ a record's row $(\anow, \abefore, r)$ is mapped once per interval by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rule}
3\,\den_i\,\anext + r' = \num_i\,S_6 - 3\,\den_i\,\abefore + r, \qquad 0 \le r' < 3\,\den_i,
\end{equation}
then $\abefore \leftarrow \anow$, $\anow \leftarrow \anext$, $r \leftarrow r'$ \cite[8.1]{algebra}. Here $S_6$ is the sum of the six reads of the record's $\anow$ (the neighbour across a Link; the Node itself on a folded axis of extent $1$; $0$ beyond an open face), $[\num_i, \den_i]$ the family's pair at the Node (the vacuum's pair everywhere, a body's pair on its cells) and $r$ the remainder of the division, kept on the record's row. Light is the pair $[1, 1]$; a massive family is a pair with $\den > \num$, and nothing else names the mass. The step is made of three verbs on the row: the bilinear form (B), one entry $\num_i$ on the six reads and one entry $-3\,\den_i$ on the row's past; the translation (T) of the row by that rate; and the division (D) by the wall $3\,\den_i$ with the remainder kept. The three tests hold \cite[8.1]{algebra}: generic, one primitive with one declared pair and no family name; vector, the three verbs with no root and no float; local, the record's own row and its six reads, nothing kept at a Node beyond the row and its remainder.

\paragraph{The conserved form.} Let $\bA$ be the read matrix of the board ($A_{ij}$ the number of reads of $j$ among the six reads of $i$), symmetric with row sums at most $6$, $\bD = \mathrm{diag}(D_i)$ with $D_i = \den_i/\num_i$, and $\bM = \bD^{-1}\bA/3$. Without the remainder the rule is $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} + \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} = \bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}$, and the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:form}
I(\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}) = \sum_i D_i\,(a_{\mathrm{next},i}^2 + a_{\mathrm{now},i}^2) - \tfrac13 \sum_i a_{\mathrm{next},i}\,(\bA\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}})_i
\end{equation}
is conserved on every board and every extent, since $\bD\bM = \bA/3$ is symmetric; it is positive definite for every massive pair and semidefinite at light's pair; and with the remainders kept the change per interval is exactly $I(t) - I(t - 1) = \sum_i (a_{\mathrm{next},i} - a_{\mathrm{before},i})(r_i - r'_i)/(3\,\num_i)$, an integer identity with no tolerance (PROVED \cite[8.2]{algebra}). A static level has $I = 0$.

\paragraph{The modes and the stability condition.} Restricted to one family, $\bS$ is a second-order linear recurrence with the symmetric operator $\bD^{-1/2}(\bA/3)\bD^{-1/2}$, whose eigenvectors are the board's \emph{modes} and whose eigenvalues are $2\cos\omega$, the angle $\omega$ being the mode's clock, its frequency per interval; on a torus there is one mode per Node. A mode is \emph{bound} when $2\cos\omega$ lies above the vacuum band's top and \emph{free} inside the band. The form $I$ is positive definite, and every mode a rotation, exactly when the largest eigenvalue is below $2$; by Perron--Frobenius that one condition bounds both ends of the spectrum. A pair with $\num \le \den$ at every Node is sufficient, not necessary. The loader's check is the spectral one: the composed operator's largest eigenvalue below $2$, the file refused otherwise (PROVED \cite[9.19 (2)]{algebra}).

\paragraph{The coupling of two families.} At a cell of a coupled body $R$ the two records' rows are coupled by one entry of verb (B) over the other record's two levels, the first difference both ways: the massive row gains $g\,(a_{l,\mathrm{now}} - a_{l,\mathrm{before}})$, the receive, and light's row gains $-G\,(a_{m,\mathrm{next}} - a_{m,\mathrm{now}})$, the source, with $g$ and $G$ the two declared coupling pairs at the body's cells (the capital $G$ apart from the flux $G_{ij}$ below, which always carries two Node indices; CARRIED \cite[8.5]{algebra}). The scheme is the Euler--Lagrange scheme of a two-point Lagrangian, so it is symplectic and has the exact invariant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J}
J = I_m + \alpha\,I_l + g \sum_{i \in R} D_i\,(a_{m,\mathrm{next}} - a_{m,\mathrm{now}})_i\,(a_{l,\mathrm{next}} - a_{l,\mathrm{now}})_i, \qquad \alpha = g\,D_{\mathrm{in}}/G,
\end{equation}
$I_m$ and $I_l$ the two families' forms, $D_{\mathrm{in}}$ the weight on the body's cells and $\alpha$ one number of the body: conserved exactly on every board, before the remainders (PROVED \cite[8.5]{algebra}). The amplitude form of the coupling is withdrawn as tachyonic, its mass matrix at zero wave vector having a negative eigenvalue.

\paragraph{The label matrix.} Every record carries its rows per label value of each arm. A polar body with the integer axis $\mathbf u = (a, b)$ applies at its cells the integer matrix $\bM_{\mathbf u} = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ -b & a \end{pmatrix}$ to the record's pair of label rows, verb (B), with no division: the common factor $a^2 + b^2$ scales the record's form by Brahmagupta's identity $(ax + by)^2 + (-bx + ay)^2 = (a^2 + b^2)(x^2 + y^2)$, exact on integers \cite[9.16 (2)]{algebra}. The polariser's angle is this integer axis; there is no table of angles \cite[9.14 (b)]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The flux.} Let $e_i = D_i\,(a_{\mathrm{next},i}^2 + a_{\mathrm{now},i}^2) - \tfrac13\,a_{\mathrm{next},i}(\bA\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}})_i$ be the Node's share of the form, so that $I = \sum_i e_i$.
\begin{proposition}[the local flux identity]\label{prop:flux}
Between two intervals,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flux}
e_i(t) - e_i(t - 1) = \sum_{j \text{ read by } i} G_{ij}, \qquad G_{ij} = \tfrac13\,A_{ij}\,\bigl(a_{\mathrm{now},i}\,a_{\mathrm{before},j} - a_{\mathrm{before},i}\,a_{\mathrm{now},j}\bigr), \qquad G_{ij} = -G_{ji}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute the rule $D_i\,(a_{\mathrm{next},i} + a_{\mathrm{before},i}) = (\bA\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}})_i/3$ into the difference of $e_i$ between the two intervals \cite[9.19 (3)]{algebra}.
\end{proof}
$G_{ij}$ is the flux into Node $i$ from Node $j$: a bilinear form of the record's own two levels at the two ends of a Link, free of the pairs (they cancel), antisymmetric and local. For a character moving from $j$ to $i$ it is positive. The one-way inward flux into one Node, summed over intervals and Ports of $\max(G_{ij}, 0)$, equals the record's form over the passage of a packet (COMPUTED to six digits on a chain \cite[9.19 (3)]{algebra}). The rectification $\max(G, 0)$ is the click's own comparison: a record's energy is counted where it enters.

\paragraph{The residue and the wheel.} Two things are called a remainder. The rule's remainder $r$ is kept per Node on the record's row and is the law's own. A record's residue $u$ is one integer, read once: the rule's remainder at the record's birth cell at the interval of the click that births it; its wheel is $W = 3\,\den/\gcd(\num, 3)$ of that cell's pair for the clicking record's family, in lowest terms. No residue, seed, order or wheel is declared anywhere; the loader refuses them (ADOPTED, record 1872 \cite{log}; \cite[9.19 (4)]{algebra}). From $r = 0$ every remainder at a Node is a multiple of $\gcd(\num, 3)$, so at most $3\,\den/\gcd(\num, 3)$ values occur (PROVED); that they are reached and equidistributed is COMPUTED, not proved. The granularity of Born's rule on a wheel of $W$ values biases a share by at most $1/(2W)$, so every birth cell carries a pair with $W \ge 500$, checked at load \cite[9.19 (4a)]{algebra}. With the law's remainder the residues are equidistributed and not a permutation, and the counts are binomial about the shares, as nature's are; the run stays deterministic and reproducible.

\begin{definition}[The detector and the click]\label{def:click}
A \emph{detector} is one connected named region, a cube of side at least $3$ (a smaller cube, or one name on disconnected pieces, is refused at load); separate places are separate names \cite[9.25 (7)]{algebra}; record 1899 \cite{log}. A record carries a \emph{ladder}, the detectors it names in a declared order with the face receiver last. At its birth the record's threshold is fixed, $\theta = (2u + 1)\,T/(2W)$, with $T$ the record's form $I$, and its running total starts at $C = 0$. At every interval $t$ each detector $d$ of the ladder receives the increment
\begin{equation}\label{eq:increment}
f_d(t) = \sum_{\substack{i \in d,\ j \notin d\\ j \text{ read by } i}} \max\bigl(G_{ij}(t), 0\bigr),
\end{equation}
the one-way inward flux through the region's Ports from outside it (a Link between two Nodes of one detector is no Port of it), and $C(t) = C(t - 1) + \sum_d f_d(t)$. \section{Technical details}\label{app:technical}

\paragraph{The tables and their rounding.} The circle is $\Z_{\Nphi}$ with $4 \mid \Nphi$. The tables are
$C_{\Nphi}[p] = \mathrm{round}(256\cos(2\pi p/\Nphi))$ and $S_{\Nphi}[p] = C_{\Nphi}[p - \Nphi/4]$,
so $C_{\Nphi}[p + \Nphi/2] = -C_{\Nphi}[p]$ and $S_{\Nphi}[p + \Nphi/4] = C_{\Nphi}[p]$ exactly; the norm
$C_{\Nphi}^2 + S_{\Nphi}^2$ lies in $[65536 - 351,\ 65536 + 361]$ for every power of
two $\Nphi \le 65536$  \cite{checks}.   The normalisation by $C_K$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rung} makes the click's probabilities sum to one at completion, and the paper claims exact unitarity nowhere.
