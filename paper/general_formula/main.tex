\begin{abstract}
One piecewise-linear map on an integer torus is the law. The state of the universe is a vector of
bounded integers on the GameBoard; the law is one
map applied at every Node every interval, per component with a rate
and a wall: translate, count the walls it holds (capped at one for the
drive), subtract them; every wall counted is an event. Its rates are constant for a message in
transit and bilinear in the state for a body reading what arrives; the
one read-out that deletes is the click, one comparison of a birth phase
against a record's integer square. Local at every Node, the law has one
non-local operation, the click of a pair, a gather of one record from
both settings; no-signalling is proved for equal weights, open beyond.
From this law, its
tables and the declared families follow: Gauss's law exactly, Newton's and
Coulomb's inverse square, the rows' pace $c = 1/\sqrt3$
Links per interval, the click's weight a positive
quadratic form of power $2$ under the splitter's conservation, Tsirelson's bound as a limit, the
uncertainty relation on the phase circle (under a stated identification) and Poisson's equation; in form, a bound set's mass; and in the limit
Doppler and Young's spacing.
What does not follow: Newton's constant and the masses are inputs;
Einstein's field equation is not reached; the Lorentz factor is not in
the law as declared, which predicts an unslowed clock in motion; a hypothesis beside it gives it within its domain (series S). Twenty-two
dimensionless readings meet nature: five pass
(four replicated, one pinned), twelve fail (seven the law's,
four pinned, one an input), one passes under the
clock's word assumed, the age moment, and fails under the presence
word; two bound a parameter, two await a run. One prediction: $S = 181/64$ at the powers of two $512$ to $8192$, inside Poh et al. at $1.05$ standard errors; at $16384$ the rule gives $5793/2048$, a function of the grains.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\paragraph{One law.} This paper states one law for a universe of bounded
integers and reports, with one source per number and one of four labels
per claim (measured in a recorded run, proved from the definitions,
assumed, or open), what follows from it and what does not. The law is
one map applied at every Node of a cubic GameBoard at every interval:
each component of the state is an accumulator with a declared rate and a
declared wall, translated by its rate, counted in walls (capped at one
for the drive), reduced by the walls it holds, and every wall counted
is an event. Nothing else happens. Where a component's rate is a constant of the world (a
message in transit) the map has a closed form and its consequences are
computed before any run; where the rate is a function of what arrives (a
body reading the messages at its Node) the map is iterated and its
continuum limit is a differential equation. One comparison is read out,
the click, and it is the only place a quantity leaves the state.
Section~\ref{sec:law} states it once as Eq.~(\ref{eq:map}), names its
symbols, lists the six operations that make its rates and walls, and
gives its immediate special cases before any derivation; the paper
refers back to Eq.~(\ref{eq:map}) thereafter. What is new here, it
seems, is not any formula recovered but the form of the law that
recovers them, one map of bounded integers with no real number
anywhere, with its supplied structures stored as integers (the tables,
the pair's joint contraction; the energy's square is the covariant readings'), and the ledger
beside it that marks each recovery; whether
that form is nature's is what the rows of Section~\ref{sec:nature} and
the one prediction of Section~\ref{sec:differs} put to measurement
(the precedents of the great papers, read for this \cite{precedents}).

The law is one map $\bPhi$ applied at every Node of the GameBoard
at every interval, per component $s$ of the state, each with its own
rate $r$ and wall $d$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:map}
s \leftarrow s + r, \qquad e \leftarrow \operatorname{sign}(s)\,\min\!\left(\lfloor |s|/d \rfloor,\ a\right), \qquad s \leftarrow s - e\,d .
\end{equation}
Here $s$ is one accumulator of the state, a bounded integer on a circle
or a line; $r$ its declared rate, signed, a constant of the world for a
message in transit and a reading of what arrives for a body; $d$ its
declared wall, a positive integer; $a$ the component's cap, $1$ where a
cap is declared and $a = \infty$ where none is (the whole part; the
primitive's \texttt{at\_most} $= 0$); and $e$ the event count: every
unit of $e$ is an event (a Link crossed, a phase step taken, a count
completed, a birth, a push), and nothing else happens; a reversed rate
first cancels what was accumulated the other way. For a count without
cap the third step is the division toward zero with its signed remainder kept, the Euclidean division for $s \ge 0$;
under the cap the residual above the wall is kept and fires one event
at each following interval until spent. The cap $a = 1$ is the
drive's alone, one Link per interval (form B's directional drive the
same); every other count takes the whole part and can fire several
events in one interval, the turn, the owed count, the release, the
lamp, the push, so that a rate of $5$ against a wall of $2$ fires $0,
2, 5, 7, 10$ events over the intervals, as the owner's high-rate runs
read (record 340 \cite{log}). The $0/1$ form $e \leftarrow [s \ge d]$,
one event bit per interval, holds exactly where the rate never exceeds
the wall: the flight ($2S_1N_l \le 2T_D$, at most one Link per
interval), the drive by its cap and with the sign ($e \in \{-1, 0,
1\}$), the birth wheel (the rate $1$ against
$N_u$) and a clock whose rate is below its wall (the derivation's section
0 \cite{derivations}). The rates and walls of every
component are made of six operations and nothing else
\cite{highlights,lawvector}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the translation of an accumulator by its rate (the flight, the
phase, the age, every count);
\item the multiplication by a declared integer matrix or bilinear form
(a split, a rotation; a coupling, the click's Gram matrix);
\item the addition in the group ring $\Z[\Z_{\Nphi}]$ (the merge, where
opposite phases cancel);
\item the permutation of the joint state (a collision, a gate);
\item the evaluation of the record at the $\Nphi$th root of unity, $\Z[\Z_{\Nphi}]
\to \Z[\zeta_{\Nphi}]$ ($\Nphi$ the phase circle's width), and its norm (the
click's one quadratic step);
\item the division toward zero with the remainder kept (the Euclidean
division for a non-negative accumulator), and the
comparison (the carry that is the event; the cap; the threshold).
\end{enumerate}
Two places are not linear and there is no third: the carry, a bijection
$s \mapsto (e, s - ed)$ with $|s - ed| < d$ and the remainder's sign that of $s$, which loses nothing, and the click's threshold,
the one comparison read out per record, whose remainder is not read:
the one place the law deletes by design. The cap and the sign are
operation 6's comparison, $\min(\lfloor |s|/d \rfloor, 1) = [|s| \ge
d]$; the capped carry is the injection $s \mapsto (e, s - ed)$, which
loses nothing either, so there is still no third place.

\paragraph{From the Node to the group, the octahedron and the pace.}
Everything geometric in the law follows from one Node and its six
Ports, $\pm x$, $\pm y$, $\pm z$, and from one Link per interval. The maps
of the six Ports onto themselves that keep opposite Ports opposite are
the signed permutations of the three axes: $3!$ orderings of the axes
times $2^3$ choices of sign, $48$ in all, the symmetry group of the cube
and of the octahedron (Theorem~\ref{th:group}). The determinant of a
signed permutation is $\pm 1$, so it splits the $48$ into two halves of
$24$: the rotations, of determinant $+1$, which are the permutations of

\begin{theorem}[the $24$ of the name]\label{th:group}
The maps of the six Ports onto themselves that send opposite Ports to
opposite Ports form the group of the signed permutations of the three
axes, of order $2^3 \cdot 3! = 48$. The determinant is a homomorphism of
this group onto $\{+1, -1\}$; its kernel, the rotations of the cube, has
index $2$ and order $24$, and is the group of the permutations of the
cube's four body diagonals; the other coset, the reflections, has $24$
elements too. The hand, the one pseudoscalar column of a row, which a
map $g$ sends to $\det(g)$ times itself, tells the $24$ rotations from
the $24$ reflections, as does an axial vector.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A map of the six Ports that keeps opposite Ports opposite permutes the
three axes ($3!$ choices) and then fixes a sign on each ($2^3$ choices),
and every such choice is realised by one signed permutation matrix; so
the group is the hyperoctahedral group $B_3$, the symmetry group of the
cube and of the octahedron of the Ports, of order $48$. The determinant
of a signed permutation matrix is $\pm 1$ and multiplicative, and it is
$-1$ on the reflection in one coordinate plane, so it is onto $\{+1,
-1\}$; the kernel of a homomorphism onto a group of two elements has
index $2$, so there are $24$ rotations and $24$ reflections. A rotation
of the cube permutes its four body diagonals; the only signed
permutations fixing all four diagonals as lines are the identity and
the central inversion, whose determinant is $-1$, so the action of the
kernel is faithful, and both groups having order $24$, the rotations are
the symmetric group on the four diagonals. A pseudoscalar column goes to
$\det(g)$ times itself by definition; series P's parity worlds with an
axis read exactly the $24$ improper elements as those under which the
image differs and none of the $24$ proper ones, and a hand without an
axis is mirror-equal under all $48$ \cite{beamlaw,log}. That the
six operations commute with the $48$ up to the declared ties is the
statement above, not part of this theorem.
\end{proof}

\section{The click's square as a positive quadratic form forced by the splitter: a lattice Gleason}\label{sec:gleason}

\begin{theorem}[The lattice Gleason]\label{th:gleason}

\qquad \sigma_j(f) = \sum_p f_p\,e^{2\pi i j p/\Nphi},
\end{equation}
the $\sigma_j$ the Galois conjugates of $\mathrm{ev}$, $\sigma_1 =
\mathrm{ev}$. Every such $R$ is a positive quadratic form on the lattice,
homogeneous, $R(af) = a^2 R(f)$; it conserves the total at every
declared split of one row or of two recombining inputs exactly if and
only if the multiplicity factor is $A = \sum a_i^2$, and at every label
rotation with the factor $C'^2 + S'^2$, $C'$ and $S'$ the half-angle
tables of Part~III. Two rows $ax^p$ and $bx^{p+\Delta}$ at one Node,
$\Delta$ their phase difference in steps, read $R = C_\Sigma(a^2 + b^2)
+ 2ab\,\mathcal K(\Delta)$ with $C_\Sigma = \sum c_j$ and
$\mathcal K(\Delta) = \sum c_j \cos(2\pi j\Delta/\Nphi)$, $\mathcal K(0) =
C_\Sigma$, $\mathcal K(\Nphi/4) = 0$, $\mathcal K(\Nphi/2) = -C_\Sigma$ for every
admissible $R$; the Born form $|\mathrm{ev}(f)|^2$ is $c_1 = 1$ and the
other $c_j = 0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Sketch; the derivation's 6.5 \cite{derivations} gives the steps in
full under (a) to (e). (i) $g = 0$ in (b) with (a) gives $2R(f) =
A_2\,(R(f) + R(0)) = A_2 R(f)$ by (d), so $A_2 = 2$, one $f$ with $R(f)
> 0$ sufficing, and (e) supplies it. (ii) Replacing $g$ by $x^{-\Nphi/4}g$ in (b) and
using (a) and $x^{-\Nphi/2} = -1$ gives $R(f - g) + R(f + g) = 2R(f) +
2R(g)$, the parallelogram law on the abelian group $\Z[\zeta_{\Nphi}]$. (iii)
The parallelogram law alone makes $R$ a quadratic form: on a lattice the
argument of Jordan and von Neumann \cite{jordan1935} needs no
continuity, and $R(f) = B(f, f)/2$ with $B$ a $\Z$-bilinear symmetric
form, the restriction of the real form of a symmetric matrix $\mathbf
G$, the click's Gram matrix (not the fan's grain $N_\theta$ of Part~I). (iv) By (a), $\mathbf G$ commutes with the signed permutation of
the basis that multiplication by $x$ is, whose eigen-functionals are the
$\sigma_j$, $j$ odd; so $\mathbf G$ is diagonal in that basis with real
entries paired under $j \leftrightarrow -j$. (v) A negative $c_j$ would
make the form negative on an open cone containing lattice points,
against (c). (vi) The conservation at a general split and at a label
rotation follows from homogeneity, (a) and the cancellation of the
cross terms between the two outputs. (vii) Two rows: $|\sigma_j(a +
bx^\Delta)|^2 = a^2 + b^2 + 2ab\cos(2\pi j\Delta/\Nphi)$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The uncertainty relation, from the same evaluation.} A record
is the element $f$ of $\Z[\Z_{\Nphi}]$ of the amounts that ended at each
phase. The click evaluates $f$ at the $\Nphi$th roots of unity, which
is the discrete Fourier transform on the circle. That the circle is
read as position (the Node its rows ended at) and its transform as
momentum (the label whose turn per Link is $|p|\Nphi/h$, de Broglie's
$\lambda = h/|p|$ Links as the declared turn) is a stated
identification of the program, a hypothesis and not a derivation
(\cite{derivations}, section 22); on it, three statements are the
transform's mathematics on $\Z_{\Nphi}$ and nothing of the law. The support bound: a record whose
rows end on $s$ phases has an evaluation supported on at least $\Nphi/s$
roots, $|\mathrm{supp}\,f|\,|\mathrm{supp}\,\hat f| \ge \Nphi$
\cite{donoho1989}, checked by enumeration at $\Nphi = 64$ on the register's
records (one phase gives all $64$ roots, the antiphase pair $32$, a comb
of every eighth phase $8$, the equality case). The entropic bound: the
two Shannon entropies sum to at least $\log_2 \Nphi$ \cite{maassen1988},
of the same form as the identity of Section~\ref{sec:table}'s entropy
row, bits read plus bits not read of $u$ ($H(u \mid K)$ for the outcome $K$) $= \log_2 \Nphi$ per record, the outcome's coarse graining ($u$ stays in the world's list as a GameBoard diagnostic), read for the
conjugate pair. The algebra: the Weyl relation of the shift and the phase
multiplication, an identity of the group ring, the finite commutator in
place of $i\hbar$. Kennard's variance form $\Delta x\,\Delta p \ge \hbar/2$
\cite{kennard1927,robertson1929} follows, as is well known, by the Cauchy--Schwarz argument
on the Gram form of Theorem~\ref{th:gleason} in the limit $\Nphi \to
\infty$ of a record spread over many phases, with $\hbar = h\Nphi/(2\pi)$;
at finite $\Nphi$ the bound is the finite group's and the Gaussian equality
case does not exist on $\Z_{\Nphi}$. What makes it an uncertainty and not a

\section{The click model}\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{In one sentence.} The model is the law of Part~I on worlds
without bodies; the measurement is the one threshold that is read out.
The state is a vector of integers, and in the worlds of this chapter
every interval applies three of the six operations to it: it translates
the vector by a rate and takes what crossed a wall as the events (the
flight, the phase, every count and the birth), it multiplies by a
declared integer matrix (the split and the rotation, exact and
reversible), and it adds (the merge, where equal phases sum and opposite
phases cancel). Between clicks the rates are
constant, so the map has a closed form, and every count of the
measurements table is computed from it by the design's check scripts
before its run, the two slits excepted (its expectation is the
generator's reading of a one-birth reference world through the same
geometry on the engine, re-pinned once after a first run,
Section~\ref{sec:measure}); the departures of Section~\ref{sec:measure}
are what the runs added. (The engine also holds a collision table and a push of matter
by a row's share, $\mathrm{amount}^2/\mathtt m$; neither acts in these worlds. The
flight table is computed once at load.) The click is the one place with a fourth
operation: the apparatus sums a record's rows through the tables and
\emph{squares} the sum (the norm, where the Born rule is put in), and only
then applies its threshold to $u$; it is the one threshold whose crossing
is written down, and it is done with the record it read (the archived
code then releases it).

\paragraph{The GameBoard and the circle.} Nodes are the points of a box in
$\Z^3$ (an axis may be periodic); each Node has six Links. Time is the
interval $t \in \Z$. The circle is $\Z_{\Nphi}$ with $4 \mid \Nphi$. The tables are
$C_{\Nphi}[p] = \mathrm{round}(256\cos(2\pi p/\Nphi))$ and $S_{\Nphi}[p] = C_{\Nphi}[p - \Nphi/4]$,
so $C_{\Nphi}[p + \Nphi/2] = -C_{\Nphi}[p]$ and $S_{\Nphi}[p + \Nphi/4] = C_{\Nphi}[p]$ exactly; the norm
$C_{\Nphi}^2 + S_{\Nphi}^2$ lies in $[65536 - 351,\ 65536 + 361]$ for every power of
two $\Nphi \le 65536$ (the extremes $65185$ and $65897$, the latter at
$\Nphi = 4096$, $p = 503$ and $3593$: $C = 184$, $|S| = 179$; at $\Nphi = 64$ the range is
$-88$ to $+237$) \cite{checks}. The half-angle tables are $C'_{\Nphi} = C_{2\Nphi}$,
$S'_{\Nphi} = S_{2\Nphi}$. Directions $D$ are the six headings and declared
primitive integer vectors; the flight table gives, per direction $D$ and
age $\tau$, the Node offset $\mathrm{pos}_D(\tau)$ on the digital line of
$D$, at most one Link per interval, and Euclidean speed $N_l|D|/T_D$ with
$T_D = \lfloor\sqrt{3|D|^2N_l^2}\rfloor$ and the flight scale $N_l = 64$:
within $0.8$ percent of $1/\sqrt3$ for every $D$, exact on $(1,1,1)$
\cite{beamlaw}.

\paragraph{The pace of the rows, and the octahedron.} Two rules are the
whole kinematics of a row: it crosses at most one Link per interval (the
locality of the GameBoard), and it follows the digital line of its
direction, so that after one period of $S_1 = |D_x| + |D_y| + |D_z|$
Links its displacement is the vector $D$ itself (straightness). The Nodes
one interval away from a Node are the six neighbours at the ends of its
Links, the vertices of the octahedron $|x| + |y| + |z| \le 1$
(Figure~\ref{fig:octahedron}); the octahedron scaled by the age is the
causal front, inside which every row stays. A row's mean Euclidean pace
lies within $1/T_D$ of the octahedron's inscribed sphere
(Proposition~\ref{prop:pace}), so the vertex's pace, one Link per
interval along a heading, is reached by no row's mean pace: the front is
a bound and not a signal.

\begin{proposition}[The pace]\label{prop:pace}
For every primitive direction $D$, $S_1 N_l \le T_D$: the Manhattan pace
$S_1 N_l/T_D$ is at most one Link per interval on every digital line, and
$S_1 = \sqrt3\,|D|$ exactly when $|D_x| = |D_y| = |D_z|$. A pace $v$ that
is the same in every direction and at most one Link per interval on every
line satisfies $v \le |D|/S_1$ for every $D$, hence $v \le 1/\sqrt3$, the
radius of the octahedron's inscribed sphere; and the table's Euclidean
pace obeys $1/\sqrt3 \le N_l|D|/T_D < (1/\sqrt3)(1 + 1/T_D)$ for every $D$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$S_1^2 = (|D_x| + |D_y| + |D_z|)^2 \le 3(D_x^2 + D_y^2 + D_z^2)$ is the
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality against $(1, 1, 1)$, an equality exactly when
the three sizes are equal; so $S_1 N_l \le \sqrt{3|D|^2N_l^2}$, and since
$S_1 N_l$ is an integer, $S_1 N_l \le \lfloor\sqrt{3|D|^2N_l^2}\rfloor =
T_D$. A row at the Euclidean pace $v$ on the line of $D$ makes $S_1$
Links per length $|D|$, so $vS_1/|D|$ Links per interval on average, and
at most one Link per interval bounds this by $1$: $v \le |D|/S_1$. The
least of $|D|/S_1$ over directions is $1/\sqrt3$, on the cube diagonals
($1/\sqrt n$ in $n$ dimensions). $T_D \le \sqrt3\,N_l|D| < T_D + 1$ gives
the pace's interval.
\end{proof}

So $c = 1/\sqrt3$ Links per interval is the largest isotropic pace the two
rules allow, and the table sits at it; that it sits at the supremum and
not below is the flight's declared wall $T_D = \lfloor\sqrt{3|D|^2
N_l^2}\rfloor$, the design's third statement, a rule chosen among few
(P9; the derivation's 24.1, rows 7 and 9), not a consequence of the
two; given the wall the value is the flight operator's norm, so the
value rests on locality, straightness and the declared wall. Stated exactly: the two rules give
$c \le 1/\sqrt3$ (one Link per interval allows the pace $1$ on a heading
and only $1/\sqrt3$ on a cube diagonal, three Links for the displacement
$\sqrt3$), and what is derived is the largest uniform pace the GameBoard
allows under them; why the rows realise it, and what a detector in
motion reads of it, are not derived here (Section~\ref{sec:lorentz}:
a one-way count of a stream reads $1 \mp \beta$, $\beta$ (beta) the
detector's speed as a fraction of $c$). Over the $1\,780\,418$ primitive
directions with components within $64$ the bound holds on every one, with
the integer equality $S_1 N_l = T_D$ on $2072$: the $8$ cube diagonals,
and $2064$ directions near them where the floor closes a gap below
$1/N_l$ (the shortest $(22, 21, 21)$, $S_1 = 64$, $\sqrt3|D| = 64.016$);
on all of these the row crosses exactly one Link per interval
(computation, \texttt{light\_speed.txt}; the design's map counts the same
$1\,780\,418$ and $2072$ and describes the $2072$ as cube diagonals, which
is corrected here \cite{lightspeed}). The pace is $64/110 = 0.5818$ on a
heading, $0.77$ percent above $1/\sqrt3$, $0.49$ percent above on the
plane diagonal, exact on the cube diagonal; the anisotropy is the table's
rounding, below $1/T_D$ in every direction and so of order $1/(\sqrt3
N_l)$; a measured bound on the anisotropy of $c$ in the GameBoard's frame
puts $N_l$ at or above its inverse, of order $5.8 \times 10^{17}$ for
$10^{-18}$ \cite{nagel2015} (a two-arm interferometer at rest with both
arms on headings sees nothing, the two paces being equal by the $48$; the
rotating resonators of \cite{nagel2015} would see the cubic pattern to the
same order; what the law says of a laboratory in motion is
Section~\ref{sec:lorentz}). Nothing measured enters the value. The number
is, as is well known, the bound of the Courant condition of the standard
second-order scheme for the wave equation, $c\,\Delta t/\Delta x \le
1/\sqrt n$ \cite{cfl1928}, whose necessity is this same argument (the
sphere of radius $c\,\Delta t$ inside the stencil's octahedron), and the
lattice Boltzmann sound speed $c_s^2 = 1/3$, from the isotropy of the
lattice tensor \cite{qian1992}. What the paper adds is the exact integer
table sitting at the bound, its rounding's anisotropy as a bound on the
grain, and the program's intent of one operator for rows and bodies; it
claims no novelty for the number
(Section~\ref{sec:lit}). The symmetry of the octahedron and of the flight
is the group of the $48$ signed permutations of the axes ($3! \times 2^3$;
$24$ of determinant $+1$, the rotations, and $24$ of $-1$, the
reflections), the GameBoard's stand-in for the rotation group: $T_D$ and
$S_1$ are invariant under all $48$, the digital line's tie by axis order
is not (on $(1, 1, 0)$, for half of the $48$, the line of $gd$ and $g$
applied to the line of $D$ differ at the odd Links and agree at every
whole period; on $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(2, 1, 0)$ they agree everywhere
\cite{checks}), so the flight commutes with the $48$ at the periods and
not between them. No body moves in the worlds of this chapter.

\paragraph{No dispersion.} What the GameBoard does not do to the pace is
as definite as what it does.

\begin{remark}[The pace is the direction's]\label{rem:nodispersion}
By Definition~\ref{def:rules} the flight table is indexed by $(D, \tau)$,
the phase is a separate accumulator, and no rule of the interval (the
flight, the birth, the split, the rotation, the merge; the archived
collision table reads no phase) reads a phase into a direction or a
step. So a row's pace
is $N_l|D|/T_D$ at every phase rate up to the alias bound of $\Nphi/2$
steps per interval (a turn every two intervals, $1.15$ to $1.16$ Links),
and the anisotropy of Proposition~\ref{prop:pace} is the same at every
rate. This is the pure-shift case of a lattice automaton
\cite{meyer1996}: a ballistic walker with a passenger phase, free of
dispersion by construction, as a classical corpuscle is. What is not
trivial is the pair with Proposition~\ref{prop:pace}: a transport on
$\Z^3$ that is isotropic within $1/T_D$ in every direction and free of
dispersion in every direction, while the carried phase interferes at the
click. No local linear wave scheme on the GameBoard can be both: its
dispersion relation, $\omega$ the frequency and $k$ the wave vector, is
a polynomial equation in $e^{i\omega\Delta t}$ and $e^{ik_ja}$ ($a$ the
Link, $\Delta t$ the interval's length), so every branch $\omega(k)$ is
periodic in $k$ up to multiples of $2\pi/\Delta t$ while $c|k|$ is not,
and $\omega = c|k|$ holds on no open set of $k$.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Row and record]\label{def:row}
A \emph{row} is $(x, D, \tau, p, e, h, r, \ell, w, \mathtt m)$: Node, direction,
age, phase $p \in \Z_{\Nphi}$, emitter, content per unit, record $r$, branch
label $\ell$, amount $w \ge 1$, multiplicity $\mathtt m \ge 1$. Its amplitude is
\begin{equation}
z = \frac{w}{\sqrt{\mathtt m}}\,\frac{C_{\Nphi}[p] + i S_{\Nphi}[p]}{256}, \qquad |z|^2 = \frac{w^2}{m}\cdot\frac{C_{\Nphi}[p]^2 + S_{\Nphi}[p]^2}{65536}.
\end{equation}
Two rows equal in every field but $w$ are one row with the amounts added;
two rows of one record and label equal in every field but a phase
difference of $\Nphi/2$ are one row with the amounts subtracted (an equal pair
is no row). The state of record $r$ is an element $\psi_r$ of the free
$\Z$-module $M_r$ on the basis $(x, D, \tau, p \bmod \Nphi/2, e, h, \ell, \mathtt m)$
with $|p + \Nphi/2\rangle = -|p\rangle$, taken modulo the scaling
$(w, \mathtt m) \sim (kw, k^2 \mathtt m)$; the GameBoard's state is one normal form per
element of $\bigoplus_r M_r$. The norm is $\langle\psi,\psi\rangle =
\sum_{\text{rows}} w^2/\mathtt m$. The archived engine narrows the quotient at a
detector: rows of one record with different $\mathtt m$ at one set are added when
the ratio of the multiplicities is the square of a rational (the pointers
rescaled by the integer roots) and refused otherwise; at the tree of the
runs two multiplicities at one set were refused, and no reported world
has them, so every set below reads one $\mathtt m$.
\end{definition}

The host, not any Node, keeps per live record the birth phase $u \in \Z_{\Nphi}$,
the count of live units (amounts), an accumulated pointer $(X, Y)$ per
(Node, label) and a gathered flag. This is the apparatus's layer; the GameBoard never reads it. It is a memory no Node could own: on the two-slit
world the two paths of one record reach a pixel up to $18$ intervals
apart, so no Node ever holds both rows, and the coherent sum exists only
in the record's ledger \cite{log}.

\begin{definition}[The update rules of one interval]\label{def:rules}
Applied in this order to every row.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[F] Flight: $(x, D, \tau, p) \mapsto (x + \mathrm{pos}_d(\tau+1) -
\mathrm{pos}_D(\tau),\ d,\ \tau + 1,\ p + \varphi(\tau))$, where the
family's phase turn is either an integer per Link, added only when the
step is a Link, or a pair $[n, d]$ per interval of age,
$\varphi(\tau) = \lfloor (\tau+1) n/d \rfloor - \lfloor \tau n/d \rfloor$,
counted from the row's birth or last re-emission.
\item[C] Collision: at a Node holding several rows of amount $1$ the
table permutes their directions as a function of the whole set present;
$r, \ell, \mathtt m$ unchanged; a bijection of states, not a linear map. No
collision acts in any world of this paper.
\item[S] Split, at a measured event with outputs $\{(d_i, a_i, t_i)\}$,
$a_i \in \Z$: the row $(w, \mathtt m, p)$ is absorbed and re-emitted as the rows
$(w a_i,\ \mathtt m A,\ p + t_i)$ on $d_i$ at age $0$, $A = \sum_i a_i^2$. The
balanced splitter is $(1, 1)$, $A = 2$; the reflection's turn is $\Nphi/4$.
\item[R] Rotation, at a set with setting $s$ and turn $t$, on a label
pair $(0, 1)$ read into the channels $(+, -)$:
$U_{s,t} = \begin{pmatrix} C'[s] & S'[s]\,v(t) \\ -S'[s] & C'[s]\,v(t)\end{pmatrix}$
with $v(t)$ the turn by $t$ steps on the label-$1$ column; a row of
label $0$ becomes $(wC'[s], 65536\mathtt m, p)$ on $+$ and $(wS'[s], 65536\mathtt m,
p + \Nphi/2)$ on $-$, a row of label $1$ becomes $(wS'[s], 65536\mathtt m, p + t)$ on
$+$ and $(wC'[s], 65536\mathtt m, p + t)$ on $-$ (the row form exact for $t$ a
multiple of $\Nphi/4$, the only turns used here; the archived code multiplies
the column by the tables' phasor at $t$). With $t = 0$ this is $U_s$ of
Theorem~\ref{th:isometry}; the GHZ bases below are $X = (s, 0)$ and
$Y = (s, \Nphi/4)$.
\item[M] Normal form: merge and cancel as in Definition~\ref{def:row}.
\item[B] Birth: a lamp's self-creation at interval $t$ releases record $r$
with $u$ the lamp's phase at the birth, $(b - 1) \bmod \Nphi$ for a lamp
started at $0$ that turns one step per birth, $b$ its count of births
(every world here; $b - 1 = t - 1$ only while the lamp births at every
interval; the archived code writes $(b - 1) \bmod \Nphi$ directly), rows of
amount $1$ on its $k$
directions with $\mathtt m = k$, each row's phase starting at $u$ (plus the
lamp's own turn), and, for a pair, the declared labels with integer
weights on separate arms.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The click]\label{def:click}
At a set that reads the sum, the rows of record $r$ and label $\ell$ that
arrive are absorbed and the layer accumulates
\begin{equation}
X \mathrel{+}= \sum 32\,w\,C_{\Nphi}[p],\qquad Y \mathrel{+}= \sum 32\,w\,S_{\Nphi}[p],
\end{equation}
the phase $p$ as the row carries it on arrival (the turns of rule F
already in it). The pointer is kept per Node:
interference is coherent within one Node only, and the offer of a set of
Nodes (a detector, a face) and label $\ell$ is the sum over its Nodes of
$(X_x^2 + Y_x^2)/\mathtt m$, one cell of the ladder, the click landing at the Node
within the set that $u$ selects by the same rungs; all rows at a set share
$\mathtt m$. When the record's live count reaches $0$, with $C_K = \sum R$ over its
offers and the cumulative sums $C_1 \le \dots \le C_K$ (the total) in the declared
order,
\begin{equation}
\rung_k = \left\lfloor \frac{2\Nphi C_k + C_K}{2C_K} \right\rfloor \quad (\rung_0 = 0,\ \rung_K = \Nphi),
\qquad \text{the click is the } k \text{ with } \rung_{k-1} \le u < \rung_k,
\label{eq:rung}
\end{equation}
and the world's list gains one row: the record, $u$, the chosen set and
label, $R$, $C_K$, the count of combinations before and after. For a record
with arms at two rotated sets $A$ and $B$ (settings $a$, $b$) the cells
are the joint outcomes $(o_A, o_B)$ in the arms' declared order, with
the weights
\begin{equation}
R(o_A, o_B) = J(o_A, o_B)^2,\qquad J(o_A, o_B) = \sum_\ell c_\ell\,U_a[o_A][\ell]\,U_b[o_B][\ell],
\label{eq:joint}
\end{equation}
$c_\ell$ the labels' declared weights, $(1, 1)$ in every world here; and
the record is gathered once, when the rows at both arms have ended:
the world's row carries both outcomes at the completion interval, so the
near party's outcome is written at the far party's tick. Reading the
ladder in that order is the same as a coarse rung over $o_A$ followed by
a fine rung over $o_B$ within it with the same $u$, which is how
Theorem~\ref{th:marginals} states it. A row of a gathered record that
reached a set afterwards would offer nothing; no reported world has one,
since completion needs every row ended. A set that reads without
absorbing (the which-path read of the polariser, Table~\ref{tab:consequences})
stores the record's residual selector keyed by its labels and lets the
rows go on; it is a deferred offer, gathered at the record's one click
on the labels present then, not an outcome at the interval of the read.
A read, then a rotation of the labels, then a second read, taken as two
outcomes in time (a sequential-instrument test), is not modelled: the
first read's selector, keyed by the labels at the read, is applied at
the gather to the labels as the rotation left them, once (\cite{engine},
the readings by type).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[One click per record]\label{lem:one}
A record every row of which ends (at a set, a face or the border; not a
row parked at rest or on a periodic axis without a lifetime) completes
and is gathered exactly once: the live count reaches $0$; $\rung_K = \Nphi$
(with $C_K > 0$: for a record born with a nonzero row whose every end
is an offer, the offers summed over all its ends are the born row's
norm up to the tables' rounding, Theorem~\ref{th:isometry}; at $C_K = 0$
the engine gathers nowhere, the auditor's rounds 4 and 4b, record 363
\cite{log}) and $\rung_0 = 0$, so every
$u \in \Z_{\Nphi}$ lies in exactly one cell. Every
record of the worlds below is of this kind. The world's row carries the
completion interval and the last arrival's interval; the delay between
them is a delay of the record, not of the GameBoard.
\end{lemma}

Three things are put in here and not derived, and the paper claims none
of them. Definition~\ref{def:click} \emph{is} the Born rule: the weight is
the squared length of the summed pointer, and the ruler over $u$ is
inverse-transform sampling of a uniform variable, so over births with $u$
uniform on $\Z_{\Nphi}$ the count in channel $k$ is $\rung_k - \rung_{k-1}$, the
difference of two cumulative rungs, Born to the resolution $1/\Nphi$ per cell. No principle
below the square is offered. The uniformity of $u$ is an assumption about
the source: $u = (b - 1) \bmod \Nphi$ is uniform over $\Nphi$ consecutive births,
which is how every run below is made (one birth per $u$), and its
independence of the settings is a fact of a world, not of the model: $u$
is a clock and a chooser is a clock, so they are independent when the
chooser's period is coprime to $\Nphi$ and the run covers their common
period, as in this paper's choosers' world (periods $3$ and $5$ against
$\Nphi = 64$, every bin one birth per $u$, every marginal $32/64$); a chooser
whose period shares a factor with $\Nphi$ (not run) would correlate with $u$
by construction, a correlation of the kind superdeterminism posits. The earlier engine's run \cite{paper1}
is history. $u$ is also the phase every row of
the record starts from, so it enters every table read as a common offset
of the record's phasors: exactly invariant before the tables' rounding
and not after it (the record's total over $u$ takes eight values,
Section~\ref{sec:measure}); the click alone reads $u$ as a number. The
tables at $1/256$ are the third of these three, and a declared input of
the law (Part~I, ``What is put in''). What the paper derives is what
follows from these with the GameBoard's rules: the interference of one record, the exact marginals, the
$\cos^2$ correlation of the pair, GHZ's zeros, and the finite-$\Nphi$ value
of $S$. The click is the only step that removes combinations and adds a
row to the world's list; the birth is the only step that adds
combinations. At the tree of the runs reported here the law was selected by a world
key (\texttt{amplitude}) and the worlds without it read as before; the
archived code has since made the record form the only click and deleted
the key, with dated lines in its register for what its later changes
moved and the integers of series L kept as recorded \cite{beamlaw,register}.

\section{Four theorems}\label{sec:theorems}

\begin{theorem}[The interval is injective between clicks]\label{th:bijection}
For an interval without a click, an end at a face or the border, or a
collision, $M \circ R \circ S \circ F$ is an injective $\Z$-linear map from $\bigoplus_r M_r$ to
$\bigoplus_r M_r$ paired with the apparatus's record of the rows the
splitters and rotators absorbed in that interval; the GameBoard's state
at any interval between two clicks, together with that record,
determines the state at the birth. Without the record the split forgets
the arriving age. A collision, where it acts, is a bijection of the
GameBoard's states that is not linear; none acts in the worlds of this
paper.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$F$ maps a basis element to a basis element and is injective because the
age is kept whole: $(D, \tau) \mapsto (D, \tau+1)$ is injective and the
Node offset is a function of $(D, \tau)$. $S$ absorbs the arriving row,
whose age, phase and amount are written on the apparatus's click line,
and re-emits at age $0$; given that line it is injective on the
amplitudes by Theorem~\ref{th:isometry}, and without it two rows
differing only in age have the same image: the split is inverted only
because the click line records what it absorbed. $R$ is injective
because $U_{s,t} = U_s\,\mathrm{diag}(1, v(t))$, the turn a bijection and
$U_s^{\mathsf T}U_s = n_s I$. $M$ replaces a
representative by the normal form of the same element of $M_r$, the
identity on the module. A composition of injective linear maps is
injective. The collision permutes the directions of the amount-$1$ rows
at a Node as a function of the whole set present \cite{beamlaw}, a
bijection of states and not a linear map, which is why it is excluded
from the linear statement. The engine's inverse interval is the witness
on worlds without a measured event \cite{beamlaw}; with splitters the
statement is the algebraic one with the record.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The split is an isometry whose conjugate transpose inverts it]\label{th:isometry}
The split $(w, \mathtt m, p) \mapsto ((wa_i, \mathtt m A, p + t_i))_i$ preserves
$\sum w^2/\mathtt m$ for every nonzero integer vector $(a_i)$, and the conjugate
transpose of the table (the same weights with the turns reversed)
applied to its outputs, followed by the normal form, returns the input up
to the scaling $(kw, k^2\mathtt m)$ with $k = A$. The rotation satisfies
$U_s^{\mathsf T} U_s = n_s I$ exactly with $n_s = C'[s]^2 + S'[s]^2$, and
multiplies a record's norm by $n_s/65536$: an isometry only up to the
tables' rounding ($65705/65536$ at $\Nphi = 64$, $s = 1$).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\sum_i (wa_i)^2/(\mathtt m A) = w^2 \sum_i a_i^2/(\mathtt m A) = w^2/\mathtt m$. The conjugate
transpose maps the outputs $(wa_i, \mathtt m A, p + t_i)$ to $\sum_i a_i (w a_i)$
at the phase $p$ on the input direction with multiplicity $\mathtt m A \cdot A$,
that is $(Aw, A^2 \mathtt m, p) \sim (w, \mathtt m, p)$; for two inputs at one splitter
the cross terms carry phases differing by $\Nphi/2$ (a reflection's quarter
turn taken forward and its reverse taken back) and cancel in the normal
form, which the check with the pairs $(20, 21)$, $(3, 4, 5)$ and
$(119, 120, 169)$ confirms integer by integer \cite{design} (with the
turns kept instead of reversed the inputs are not returned), and the
engine's merge reproduces on its own rows \cite{beamlaw}. For $U_s$ the
diagonal of $U_s^{\mathsf T}U_s$ is $C'^2 + S'^2$ and the off-diagonal
$C'S' - S'C' = 0$ in integers.
\end{proof}

So a record's norm is conserved exactly by $S$ and by the flight, and by
$R$ and by every read through the tables only within the tables' rounding
($-351/65536$ to $+361/65536$ per row for the powers of two through
$65536$); the integer
Mach-Zehnder's total over the 64 birth phases takes eight values from
$65448/65536$ to $65773/65536$, the same eight on every one of its ten
worlds \cite{register}. The normalisation by $C_K$ in \eqref{eq:rung} makes
the click's probabilities sum to one at completion; it does not restore
the norm along the way, and the paper claims exact unitarity nowhere.

\begin{theorem}[Exact marginals; no-signalling]\label{th:marginals}
For a pair with labels $\{0, 1\}$ of equal weight and settings $a, b$, let
$J(o_A, o_B) = \sum_\ell U_a[o_A][\ell]\,U_b[o_B][\ell]$ and
$R(o_A, o_B) = J^2$. Then the first party's outcome is $+$ exactly for
$u < \Nphi/2$ and $-$ for $u \ge \Nphi/2$, for every $(a, b)$ and every $\Nphi$; and
the second party's count of $+$ is exactly $\Nphi/2$ for every $(a, b)$ unless
$2\Nphi R(+,+)/C_K$ is an odd integer (a tie of the rung), in which case it is
$\Nphi/2 + 1$. No tie occurs for any setting pair at $\Nphi = 8, 16, 24, 32, 48,
64, 96, 128, 192, 256, 512, 1024$, nor at the CHSH labels for any
multiple of $8$ up to $4096$ (computed from the rule, \cite{checks}; the
gate review found none at $64$, $256$, $1024$ and at $4096$ for
$a = 0, 1024$, \cite{log}); the
reviewer also recomputed the marginals from the rows of one registered
world at $\Nphi = 64$ for all 4096 setting pairs, $32/64$ in every pair
\cite{log}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $n_a = C'[a]^2 + S'[a]^2$. Since the rows of $U_b$ are orthogonal on
the integers, $\sum_{o_B} J(o_A, o_B)^2 = n_a n_b$ for each $o_A$, so
$C_K = 2n_a n_b$ and the coarse cumulative after the first party's $+$ is
$C_K/2$: $\rung = \lfloor (\Nphi C_K + C_K)/(2C_K)\rfloor = \lfloor (\Nphi+1)/2 \rfloor =
\Nphi/2$. For the second party, $R(o_A, o_B) = R(-o_A, -o_B)$ (the rows of
$U_s$ are each other's quarter turn), so with $x = \Nphi R(+,+)/C_K$ the counts
are $c_{++} = \lfloor x + \tfrac12\rfloor$ and $c_{-+} = \rung_3 - \Nphi/2$ with
$\rung_3 = \lfloor (2\Nphi(C_K - R(+,+)) + C_K)/(2C_K)\rfloor = \Nphi + \lfloor \tfrac12 -
x\rfloor$. Hence $c_{++} + c_{-+} = \Nphi/2 + \lfloor y\rfloor + \lfloor 1 -
y\rfloor$ with $y = x + \tfrac12$, which is $\Nphi/2$ when $y \notin \Z$ and
$\Nphi/2 + 1$ when $y \in \Z$. The tie condition is $2x \in 2\Z + 1$, that
is $2\Nphi R(+,+)/C_K$ odd. The absence of ties at the listed $\Nphi$ is the
computation over every setting pair \cite{checks}. Which party is first
is the world file's declaration of the arms' order, not a property of the GameBoard: the model has a preferred party by declaration, and
swapping the arms moves outcomes between the parties while the counts
stay \cite{log}.
\end{proof}

The strict-crossing rung $\Nphi C_k > uC_K$ gives the second party $33/64$ in
$3944$ of the $4096$ setting pairs at $\Nphi = 64$, a signalling of $1/\Nphi$ made
by the rounding \cite{design}; the nearest rung of \eqref{eq:rung} is what
Theorem~\ref{th:marginals} needs. The theorem's scope is what it states:
a pair of two labels with equal weights, the CHSH form, and the rung of
\eqref{eq:rung}; unequal weights and more than two labels are not covered,
and no-signalling for them is open. Where the two settings meet is
explicit in Definition~\ref{def:click}: $B$'s rung is computed from
$R(o_A, o_B)$ with $a$ and $o_A$ supplied by the layer. That the marginal
at $B$ is nevertheless $\Nphi/2$ for every $a$ is the content of the theorem;
the joint counts are not local, and the model does not claim they are.

\begin{theorem}[The finite-$\Nphi$ Bell value]\label{th:bell}
With the counts of Theorem~\ref{th:marginals} and off a tie,
$E_{\Nphi}(a, b) = 4c_{++}/\Nphi - 1$ and
\begin{equation}
\bigl|E_{\Nphi}(a, b) - \cos(2\pi(a-b)/\Nphi)\bigr| \le \frac{2}{\Nphi} + 4\arcsin\frac{\sqrt2}{2\varrho} < \frac{2}{\Nphi} + 0.0111,\qquad \varrho = N_t - \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},
\label{eq:ebound}
\end{equation}
so at the labels $(0, \Nphi/8, \Nphi/4, 3\Nphi/8)$, $|S(\Nphi) - 2\sqrt2| \le 8/\Nphi + 0.0444$.
$S(\Nphi)$ is an exact rational for each $\Nphi$; it lies above $2\sqrt2$ for
$252$ of the $512$ multiples of $8$ up to $4096$ (among them $\Nphi = 16, 32$
with $S = 3$ and $\Nphi = 128$ with $S = 23/8$) and equals $181/64 =
2.828125$ at every power of two from $512$ through $4096$ \cite{checks}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$c_{++} = \lfloor x + \tfrac12\rfloor$ with $x = \Nphi R(+,+)/C_K$ gives
$|c_{++} - x| \le \tfrac12$ and $E_{\Nphi} = 4c_{++}/\Nphi - 1$ from the mirror
symmetry, so $|E_{\Nphi} - (4R(+,+)/C_K - 1)| \le 2/\Nphi$. The vector
$(C'[s], S'[s])$ differs from $256(\cos\theta_s, \sin\theta_s)$,
$\theta_s = \pi s/\Nphi$, by at most $\tfrac12$ per component, so its
direction differs from $\theta_s$ by at most $\delta = \arcsin(\sqrt2/(2\varrho))$
with $\varrho = N_t - \sqrt2/2 = 255.29$, below every table vector's length
(each component within $\tfrac12$ of $256\cos$ or $256\sin$), so
$4\delta = 0.01108 < 0.0111$ and $16\delta = 0.04432 < 0.0444$;
$J(+,+)/\sqrt{n_a n_b}$ is the cosine of the angle between the two
directions, $\cos(\theta_a - \theta_b + \epsilon)$ with $|\epsilon| \le
2\delta$, and $4R(+,+)/C_K - 1 = 2\cos^2(\cdot) - 1 = \cos(2(\theta_a -
\theta_b) + 2\epsilon)$, within $4\delta$ of $\cos(2\pi(a-b)/\Nphi)$. The
four settings add the bounds. The values of $S(\Nphi)$ are the computation
from these formulas with the repository's tables \cite{checks}, which
reproduces the design's $176/64$, $720/256$ and $2896/1024$ at $\Nphi = 64,
256, 1024$ and the engine's three values below.
\end{proof}

What Theorem~\ref{th:bell} does not say: that $S(\Nphi) \le 2\sqrt2$, which is
false for about half the $\Nphi$; nor that the deficit is $O(1/\Nphi)$ from below,
which holds at the three $\Nphi$ the engine ran and not in general. The
tables' term in \eqref{eq:ebound} does not shrink with $\Nphi$; it is the
resolution $1/256$ of the circle's tables. At the CHSH labels the tables'
entries are the same at every $\Nphi$ ($237, 98$ and $181, 181$), so
$E(0, \Nphi/8) = 46565/65773$ and $E(\Nphi/4, \Nphi/8) = 46452/65773$ exactly at every
$\Nphi$ before the rounding of the cells; the rounding gives $181/64$ at
the powers of two from $512$ through $8192$ and $5793/2048 = 2.828613$
at $16384$ and $32768$, where the second pair of settings rounds to
$2893/4096$ (\cite{checks}, \texttt{s\_powers\_of\_two}; the
tables' bound $2\Nphi \le 65536$ stops the tables' computation there).
Beyond it the rungs on the fixed correlations are a closed form,
$S(\Nphi) = 8(c_1 + c_1')/\Nphi - 4$ with $c_1$ and $c_1'$ the first
cell's counts, the rungs of $(1 + E)/4$ at the two pairs of settings
(\cite{checks}; the derivation's 24.4, \texttt{bell\_plateau.py}, the
same values): $11585/4096 = 2.828369$ at $65536$,
$46341/16384 = 2.828430$ at $131072$ and $370727/131072 = 2.828423$
at $2^{20}$, two-sided about the bound; with the
tables fixed the limit in $\Nphi$ is $2(46565 + 46452)/65773 =
186034/65773 = 2.828425$, $2.1 \times 10^{-6}$ below $2\sqrt2$, and

\section{The Bell value at finite $\Nphi$ and the experiments}\label{sec:bell}

$S(\Nphi)$ is a property of the model that nothing in the model fixes: $\Nphi$ is
a declared width of the world. The model gives a prediction only once $\Nphi$
is chosen, and what a measurement of nature does is constrain $\Nphi$, under
three assumptions the paper states and does not defend: that nature's
pairs are this model's pairs at some $\Nphi$, that the experiment's
fair-sampling assumption holds, and that the experiment's four settings
are the model's labels $(0, \Nphi/8, \Nphi/4, 3\Nphi/8)$. The third matters because
$E$ depends on $(a, b)$ beyond $a - b$: at $\Nphi = 256$ the maximum of $S$
over all setting quadruples is $91/32 = 2.84375$ and $181{,}152$
quadruples lie within three standard deviations of the measured value,
while at $\Nphi = 64$ the maximum over all quadruples is $11/4$, so that
exclusion stands whatever the settings \cite{checks}. Under the three,
the photon-pair value $S = 2.82759 \pm 0.00051$ \cite{poh2015} is
incompatible with $\Nphi = 64$ (152 standard deviations) and $\Nphi = 256$ ($30$ standard errors, $29.6$; the derivation's 24.3 row 1 and 24.4 agree),
compatible with the powers of two from 512 through 8192 ($1.05$
standard deviations above; $16384$ and $32768$ at $2.0$, $65536$ and
beyond at $1.5$ to $1.65$, Section~\ref{sec:theorems}), admits its first $\Nphi$ at 184, and excludes 136 of the 512 values
up to 4096 at five standard deviations, the largest 3016 \cite{checks};
the archived engine declares $\Nphi$ a power of two, so the other multiples
of $8$ are the formula's, not runnable worlds. Of the loophole-free
experiments, \cite{hensen2015} reports a CHSH value, $S = 2.42 \pm
0.20$, which excludes no $\Nphi$ at three standard deviations;
\cite{giustina2015,shalm2015} report a CH-Eberhard quantity whose

\cite{pr1994}; at $\Nphi = 16$ and $32$ the model gives $S = 3$ with the

validates nothing beyond that; a comparison with noise and detector
