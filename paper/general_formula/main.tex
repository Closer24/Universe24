\begin{abstract}
We state one law and report what follows from it. The state of the
universe is a vector of bounded integers on the GameBoard of Nodes and
six Links; the law is one map applied at every Node at every
interval, per component with a rate and a wall: translate, compare with
the wall, subtract on crossing; every crossing is an event. Its rates
are constant for a message in transit and bilinear in
the state for a body reading what arrives; the one read-out that
deletes is the click, one comparison of a birth phase against a
record's squared integer sum. From this law, its tables
and the declared families follow, in the continuum limit and
against recorded runs: Gauss's law exactly, Newton's and Coulomb's
inverse square in the shell mean, the largest uniform pace $c =
1/\sqrt3$ Links per interval, the click's weight forced to a positive
quadratic form of power $2$ and Tsirelson's bound as a limit, Poisson's
equation for the delay field; and, in the limit only, Doppler, Young's
spacing and Bohr's condition. What does not follow is stated with the same care: Newton's
constant carries the world's declared width and the masses are inputs;
Einstein's field equation is not reached; the
Lorentz factor is not in the law as declared, which predicts an
unslowed clock in motion as a row that can fail, while the hypothesis
built beside it, the covariant readings, gives the factor and $E =
mc^2$ on paper. Seventeen dimensionless readings meet nature under one
parameter set: three pass, nine fail, two bound a parameter,
three await a run. The measurement chapter defines the click model
exactly, proves four theorems (the CHSH value at $N$ phase steps within
$8/N + 0.045$ of $2\sqrt2$, on both sides of it) and reports the runs
recorded before the text, from the Mach-Zehnder to the pair at $S =
2.75$, GHZ and a CNOT gate. Every
number has one source; every claim is measured, proved, assumed or
open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\paragraph{One law.} This paper states one law for a universe of bounded
integers and reports, with one source per number and one of four labels
per claim (measured in a recorded run, proved from the definitions,
assumed, or open), what follows from it and what does not. The law is
one map applied at every Node of a cubic GameBoard at every interval:
each component of the state is an accumulator with a declared rate and a
declared wall, translated by its rate, compared with its wall, reduced
by the wall when it crosses, and every crossing is an event. Nothing
else happens. Where a component's rate is a constant of the world (a
message in transit) the map has a closed form and its consequences are
computed before any run; where the rate is a function of what arrives (a
body reading the messages at its Node) the map is iterated and its
continuum limit is a differential equation. One comparison is read out,
the click, and it is the only place a quantity leaves the state.

\paragraph{What is derived and what is put in.} Part~I states the law:
the state, the map, its two blocks, the read-out, and the inputs (the
grain of the computation, the table of families with the world's width,
the initial state). Part~II is one table: every formula of known physics
that the law reaches in the limit and confirms in a recorded run, every
constant it takes as an input, every formula it does not reach, and
every place where it gives a different law, stated as a row that a
measurement can refute; each entry names the section of the derivation
that carries it and the run or the pin that checks it, and in this
version the table stands on those sections and the register, its rows
to be written out one by one as each passes review. Part~III is the
measurement chapter: the click model exactly, the pace of its rows and
its rounding, four theorems, and the runs of one quantum, a pair, three
quanta and a gate, recorded before the text. Part~IV states the
continuum limits that return and those that do not, the decision taken
on Lorentz, and the literature.

\paragraph{Method.} Equations first, runs as confirmation. Every formula
is derived from the definitions in a derivation document of the archive
\cite{derivations}, cited here by section; every run is cited from the
experiments register \cite{register} with its world, its expectation
written before the run and its source fingerprint; every number of the
manuscript is mapped to its source in a table beside it \cite{checks}.
A formula whose confirming run has not been made is stated as a pin, not
as a result. The archive will be cited at a version \cite{zenodo}.

\part{The formula}\label{part:formula}

\section{The law}\label{sec:law}

\paragraph{The state.} The GameBoard is a box of Nodes in $\Z^3$, each
joined to six neighbours by Links through six Ports; an axis may be
periodic. Time is the interval $t \in \Z$. The state at an interval is a
vector $\mathbf s$ of bounded integers on a product of circles and lines,
and it is the multiset of the messages in transit (the rows) and the
bodies (the measured events), and nothing else: a Node holds no
register, remainder or draw. A row carries its Node, its direction (a
primitive integer vector on whose digital line it walks), its age, its
phase on the circle $\Z_N$, its number (its family), its amount, its
content per unit, and under a record its identity, label, multiplicity
and birth phase. A body carries its Node, its content $M$, its momentum
vector $\mathbf p$ and its table of counts, one accumulator per count.

\paragraph{The map.} The law is one map $\mathbf F$ applied at every Node
at every interval, per component $s$ of the state with its own rate $r$
and wall $b$ (the ladder's rungs $\rung_k$ of Part~III are walls of this
kind):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:map}
s \leftarrow s + r, \qquad e \leftarrow [s \ge b], \qquad s \leftarrow s - e\,b,
\end{equation}
and every wall crossing $e = 1$ is an event: a Link crossed, a phase
step taken, a count completed, a birth, a push. The components and their
$(r, b)$ \cite{derivations}: a row's Manhattan count (the rate $2S_1Q_f$,
the wall $2T_d$, started at $T_d$; $d$ the direction vector, $S_1$ its
Manhattan length, $T_d = \lfloor\sqrt{3|d|^2Q_f^2}\rfloor$ its
resolution, $Q_f$ the flight's grain, written $Q$ in the register), with three axis deficits choosing
the axis of each Link; a row's phase (an integer per Link, or a declared
rational $\nu$ of steps per interval, written $n/d$ in Part~III, its
whole part read modulo $N$); a row's age (the rate $1$, no wall); a
body's drive on each axis (the rate $|p_a|$, the wall $Q_f S M +
|p_a|$, $M$ the content and $S$ the world's width, not the CHSH sum $S$
of Part~III), whose speed $p/(Q_f S M + p)$ saturates at one Link per
interval, above the rows' pace (the drive as the flight operator at a
fraction, capped at $c$, is decided and not built); a body's counts (the
turn, the owed count, the release, the lamp, the push per column, each a
rate against a wall); a record's birth phase (the rate $1$ per birth,
the wall $N$; a wheel of width $W = 4096$ is decided and not built); and
the click's ladder, one comparison read once. The operations that make
these components are six \cite{highlights}, or a rounding declared at
load: the translation of an accumulator by its rate; the multiplication
by a declared integer matrix (a split, a rotation) and the bilinear form
(a coupling, the click's Gram matrix); the addition in the group ring
$\Z[\Z_N]$ (the merge, where opposite phases cancel); the permutation (a
collision, a gate); the evaluation of the tables at the root of unity;
and the Euclidean division whose remainder is kept (the carry that is
the event, and the threshold). Two places are not linear and there is no
third \cite{highlights}: the carry, a bijection that loses nothing, and
the click's threshold, the one read-out that deletes.

\paragraph{The two blocks.} $\mathbf F$ is linear where its rates are
constants of the world and feeds back where a rate is a function of what
arrives. The linear block is the rows in flight: the flight, the phase
and the age at constant rates, the merge, the integer matrices of a
split, a rotation or a gate, and the evaluation at the click. Every
component of it has the closed form $s(t) = \lfloor s_0 + rt \rfloor$, so
a row's state at any interval is a formula of its birth and its age, and
the limits of Part~II are limits of that formula. The feedback block is
the bodies: the push, whose rate is the reader's content times the flow
of the arriving rows (bilinear in the state); the owed count, whose rate
is what the body reads (the presence, or the age moment); the turn and
the release, whose rate is the content; the drive, whose rate is the
momentum the push changes. These have no closed form; they are iterated,
and their continuum limit is the differential equation $d\mathbf s/dt =
\mathbf r(\mathbf s)$. The exact word for the whole map is therefore
piecewise-linear in the rows with bilinear rates in the bodies
\cite{derivations}.

\paragraph{The read-out.} The click reads one comparison and nothing else
leaves the rows (a detector's other readings, the counts, the flow and
the moments of the rows at its Node, read and delete nothing): for a
record whose rows have all ended, the apparatus
sums the rows' integer phasors per cell through the tables, squares the
sums (the one quadratic step of the law, where the Born rule is put in),
lays the cells on a ladder by those squared sums, and compares the
record's birth phase $u$ with the ladder's rungs; the cell whose rung $u$
falls under is the click, and the record is done. Between a birth and a
click every step of the linear block is injective given the apparatus's
record (Theorem~\ref{th:bijection}); the click is the one deletion.

\paragraph{The law in five statements.} (I1) A message is a row: created
at exactly one event, a self-creation of a body, ended at exactly one
event, a read that absorbs it, an escape or the border of its lifetime;
between them in transit, and nothing else exists. (I2) A Node transmits
per interval the rows that step out of it, one Port and one Link each at
the flight's pace, holding nothing back and copying nothing. (I3) A body
receives the rows of other numbers that reach it and emits only at its
self-creations. (I4) A clock's rate is the reciprocal of one plus what
it reads: a body's clock counts its self-creations and owes after each
an interval count proportional to the messages present, so it is slowed
by messages and by nothing else. (I5) The balance: every message
released is on exactly one line of the books at every interval,
released equals in transit plus absorbed plus escaped plus cancelled,
per family, in amount and in content, exact at every interval; a paid
message carries its label out of its emitter at birth and into its
reader at its end, so for paid messages momentum is conserved and the
third law holds message by message, while a free message (a field's row)
takes no recoil at birth and pushes its reader by its label times the
reader's content, so for free messages the third law is a symmetry
between two readers, exact only while both read the same number of each
other's messages \cite{derivations}.

\paragraph{What is put in.} Three kinds of input \cite{highlights}. The
grain of the computation, constants of the law and not of the world: $N$
the circle of phases, $Q_f$ the pace's grain, $P$ the sphere of
directions, $W$ the birth wheel (decided), $G$ the fan's grain, the
clock's pair, and the roundings declared at load (the tables at $1/256$,
$T_d$ by the integer square root, the rounding of the wheel's phase). The table of families, the physical inputs: per
family the content (the mass number), the cost $h$ per phase step, the
charge per unit of content, the strong column, the lifetime, the phase
rate and the hand; and the world's width $S$, the push per unit of
content per unit of flow, which carries what physics calls Newton's
constant. And the state and the apparatus: the box, the bodies with
their momenta and contents, the lamps, the detectors, the splitters, the
gates. In the program's words: ``if the law is right, the input to the
real world is the family table, the initial state and one grain; $c$,
$h$ and $G$ are units there as here'' \cite{highlights}.

\paragraph{The symmetries.} The law is invariant under the translations
of the box and of the interval, and under the $48$ signed permutations
of the axes, the group of the octahedron of the six Ports and of the
cube ($3! \times 2^3$; the $24$ of determinant $+1$ its rotations, the
$24$ of $-1$ its reflections, told apart by the hand, a pseudoscalar of
the group), up to three ties the derivation names (the collision's Port
order, the apportioning's, the axes' order of the digital line; this
paper meets the last, Section~\ref{sec:model}); under the circle $\Z_N$
exactly on the turns between events and at the click only up to the
tables' rounding; and under the permutations of the families, each with
its columns of the family table carried along. A boost, which mixes a
step in space with a step in time, is not among the $48$: Lorentz's
symmetry is not a symmetry of the lattice but of the limit of its linear
block, the wave equation at $c$ (Section~\ref{sec:lorentz}). The $48$
are the lattice's stand-in for the rotation group; angular momentum is
conserved only up to the lattice, and no Noether theorem for the map is
claimed \cite{log}.

\section{Newton and Coulomb from the bilinear coupling}\label{sec:newton}

The first rows of Table~\ref{tab:consequences} are written out here from
the derivation's section 3 \cite{derivations} and the register's series
C \cite{register}: a plane of $121 \times 121$ Nodes with the third
axis periodic, one source of content $2^{24}$ holding a free family and
releasing on the six headings at the declared release rate $\eta = n/d
= 1/128$ units per self-creation per unit of content per direction (two
sources, of $2^{22}$ and $2^{20}$, in the third-law world); the two rows
released on the periodic axis land on the source's own Node at their
first step, are home, and are released again in six shares at the next
self-creation, so that at the fixed point the flux $q = 6 \times 2^{17}$
units enters the plane per interval in four in-plane beams of $3 \times
2^{16}$; probes of content $1$ at the radii $4$ to $40$, and of content
$1$, $4$ and $16$ at $r = 12$; twenty-one worlds, every reading pinned
before the run; the integers below are those of the run at the source
fingerprint \texttt{703f9427} in label units (a later re-read along the
unit vector multiplies every push and momentum by $Q_f = 64$ exactly,
the ratios unchanged). No number below is recomputed by this paper.

\paragraph{The operations.} Six operations, of three of Part~I's kinds
(the translation, the bilinear form, the Euclidean division), make the
whole of gravitation and electricity in the law. The free release (a
count against a wall): a body of content $M_B$ holding a free family
releases at each self-creation the carry of the count of rate $M_B\eta$
against the wall $1$ (the whole part of $M_B\eta$ per self-creation,
$M_B\eta$ in the mean) on every declared direction, so a fan of $K$
directions releases $q = K M_B\eta$ per interval while the source's
clock owes nothing. The walk (a translation): each unit walks its
digital line at the pace of Proposition~\ref{prop:pace}. The reading (a
bilinear form): the label flow $\mathbf V = \sum \mathrm{amount} \times
\mathbf u_d$ of the rows arriving at the reader's Node, $\mathbf u_d$
the label per unit along the direction $d$, the integer unit vector at
the scale $Q_f$, $|\mathbf u_d| = Q_f$ within $1.35$ percent on every
direction. The coupling (a bilinear form with a declared integer
matrix): per axis $\mathrm{push}_A = M_A (\rho_A\rho_B - 1)\,\mathbf V$,
the gravity column $-M_A \mathbf V$ and the charge column
$+\rho_A\rho_B M_A \mathbf V$, $\rho$ the declared charge per unit of
content of each family. The momentum (a translation): $\mathbf p_A
\leftarrow \mathbf p_A + \mathrm{push}_A$. The step (the Euclidean
division): one Link per $b/|p_a|$ self-creations on the axis $a$, the
wall $b = Q_f S M_A + |p_a|$, $S$ the world's width; per interval, for a
body whose clock owes nothing, the speed on the axis is $|p_a|/(Q_f S
M_A + |p_a|)$.

\paragraph{Gauss's law, exact.} The walk is a translation: a free unit
released inside a closed surface that no periodic axis crosses, with no
absorbing reader inside it and within the unit's lifetime, crosses the
surface once and never returns (no collision acts on a fan or on lone
beams), so once the front has passed, the amount crossing the surface
per interval equals the release inside it, exactly. Registered, on the
plane where the periodic axis's two rows are home and re-released as
said above: the flux through a square of half-width $h$ around the
source, over $q$, is $1.0000$ at $h = 4, 8, 12, 20, 40$, and the escape
through the faces $1.0000\,q$ (series C, item 5). Proved from the
definitions under those conditions; measured.

\paragraph{The inverse square as a shell mean.} On a beam the flow per
Node is the same at every Node of the digital line: along a beam the
field falls as $1/r^0$, and off every beam it is $0$ (registered: the
axial count $q/4$ per interval constant with $r$; a probe off both axes
reads nothing). The inverse square is the density of beams over a shell.
A shell at distance $r$ holds $\mathcal N(r)$ Nodes and the $K$ beams
cross it at $K$ of them, so the mean over the shell of what a body reads
is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shell}
\langle V\rangle(r) = \frac{q\,Q_f}{\mathcal N(r)} \to \frac{q\,Q_f}{2\pi r}
\ \text{on the plane},\qquad \frac{q\,Q_f}{4\pi r^2}\ \text{in space},
\end{equation}
as $\mathcal N(r) \to 2\pi r$ and $4\pi r^2$; the departure at finite $r$
is the lattice's count of Nodes on a shell, Gauss's circle problem,
$\mathcal N(r) = 2\pi r + O(r^\theta)$ with the exponent $\theta \le
131/208$ \cite{huxley2003}, so the shell mean's relative ripple vanishes
as $r^{\theta - 1}$. Registered (series C, item 5, nine radii; the
register's flow is the label flow over $Q_f$, in amount units): the
count times $r$ over $q$ and the flow times $2\pi r$ over $q$ are

\part{The click model: the measurement chapter}\label{part:click}

\section{The click model}\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{The model in plain words.} The GameBoard (the lattice, where a
shorter word serves) is a mail board.
Cells (Nodes), each wired to six neighbours; a wire carries one packet one
step per tick, and nothing travels farther or faster. A packet carries
four numbers that matter here, how much (the amount), where to (the
direction), a rotating counter (the phase, advanced a fixed step at every
wire it crosses) and a birth number, and its bookkeeping (a label and a
multiplicity, Definition~\ref{def:row}). A cell has no memory: what is in it now is exactly the
packets in it now. The rules are local and few. Transfer: a packet moves
to the neighbour its direction names. Duplication at a splitter: a packet
becomes several packets in several directions with declared shares, the
birth number the same on all. Merging: identical packets add; identical
packets with opposite counters cancel and leave nothing. Deflection
beside a mass: a packet's direction is turned by a table, and the turn
can be undone (no world of this chapter has a mass). The collector, the
apparatus, keeps per birth number and per cell the running sum of the
packets' arrows. When every packet of a birth has ended, the collector
lays the cells on a ruler in proportion to the squared lengths of those
sums; a secret number born with the birth, the counter every packet of
the birth started from and which no rule reads as a number, picks the
point on the ruler; the whole birth is recorded at that cell. For a birth
that reaches two collectors with two settings, the collectors' record is
one object: the choice is made once, when the packets at both have
ended, from the sums at both with both settings, and the near
collector's row is written at the far collector's tick. That one choice
from both settings is the only action at a distance in the model;
nothing else crosses a wire faster than a packet. Births happen only at
sources, on their own clocks; deaths only at clicks. Everything between
a birth and its click can be run backwards, given the collectors' record
of what they absorbed.

\paragraph{What was asked, and what came before.} The question of this
chapter is the one a previous paper on the same simulator asked of four
candidate mechanisms \cite{paper1}: which resource does an integer,
local, causal model need to reproduce the correlations of one quantum and
of a pair. There, every candidate whose information travelled only on
Links stopped at the Bell bound, $S = 2$ exactly, also when the settings
were chosen by events of the GameBoard itself (no superdeterministic
correlation was found), and only a declared shared object, a registry
holding a configured cosine law, reached $2.83$, as that paper reported
(its runs are in its own archive, not in the current register). That
framework is the ``before'' of this paper and is not repeated; its engine
of the time was replaced and none of its tables is reproduced. Here the
registry and the
configured law are gone. What remains is the lattice, one record with
rows, and one measurement rule, the click of Definition~\ref{def:click}.
This chapter defines the model in standard terms (the words collapse
and superposition appear first in Section~\ref{sec:lit}, each as another
author's name for one of these things), proves what can be proved of it
(Section~\ref{sec:theorems}), reports what the engine measured, from
runs recorded before this text was written (Section~\ref{sec:measure}),
compares the finite-$N$ Bell value with the experiments
(Section~\ref{sec:bell}), places the click in the causal anatomy of
Bell's theorem (Section~\ref{sec:causal}), and says what is new in it,
what it does not claim and what the program hypothesised beside it at
the time of its runs (Sections~\ref{sec:new} to \ref{sec:hyp}). All
code, worlds and recorded runs are archived \cite{zenodo}.


\paragraph{In one sentence.} The model is the law of Part~I on worlds
without bodies; the measurement is the one threshold that is read out.
The state is a vector of integers, and in the worlds of this chapter
every interval applies three of the six operations to it: it translates
the vector by a rate and takes what crossed a wall as the events (the
flight, the phase, every count and the birth), it multiplies by a
declared integer matrix (the split and the rotation, exact and
reversible), and it adds (the merge, where equal phases sum and opposite
phases cancel). Between clicks the rates are
constant, so the map has a closed form, and every count of the
measurements table is computed from it by the design's check scripts
before its run, the two slits excepted (its expectation is the
generator's reading of a one-birth reference world through the same
geometry on the engine, re-pinned once after a first run,
Section~\ref{sec:measure}); the departures of Section~\ref{sec:measure}
are what the runs added. (The engine also holds a collision table and a push of matter
by a row's share, $\mathrm{amount}^2/m$; neither acts in these worlds. The
flight table is computed once at load.) The click is the one place with a fourth
operation: the apparatus sums a record's rows through the tables and
\emph{squares} the sum (the norm, where the Born rule is put in), and only
then applies its threshold to $u$; it is the one threshold whose crossing
is written down, and it is done with the record it read (the archived
code then releases it).

\paragraph{The GameBoard and the circle.} Nodes are the points of a box in
$\Z^3$ (an axis may be periodic); each Node has six Links. Time is the
interval $t \in \Z$. The circle is $\Z_N$ with $4 \mid N$. The tables are
$C_N[p] = \mathrm{round}(256\cos(2\pi p/N))$ and $S_N[p] = C_N[p - N/4]$,
so $C_N[p + N/2] = -C_N[p]$ and $S_N[p + N/4] = C_N[p]$ exactly; the norm
$C_N^2 + S_N^2$ lies in $[65536 - 351,\ 65536 + 361]$ for every power of
two $N \le 65536$ (the extremes $65185$ and $65897$, the latter at
$N = 4096$, $p = 503$ and $3593$: $C = 184$, $|S| = 179$; at $N = 64$ the range is
$-88$ to $+237$) \cite{checks}. The half-angle tables are $C'_N = C_{2N}$,
$S'_N = S_{2N}$. Directions $D$ are the six headings and declared
primitive integer vectors; the flight table gives, per direction $d$ and
age $\tau$, the Node offset $\mathrm{pos}_d(\tau)$ on the digital line of
$d$, at most one Link per interval, and Euclidean speed $Q_f|d|/T_d$ with
$T_d = \lfloor\sqrt{3|d|^2Q_f^2}\rfloor$ and the flight scale $Q_f = 64$:
within $0.8$ percent of $1/\sqrt3$ for every $d$, exact on $(1,1,1)$
\cite{beamlaw}.

\paragraph{The pace of the rows, and the octahedron.} Two rules are the
whole kinematics of a row: it crosses at most one Link per interval (the
locality of the GameBoard), and it follows the digital line of its
direction, so that after one period of $S_1 = |d_x| + |d_y| + |d_z|$
Links its displacement is the vector $d$ itself (straightness). The Nodes
one interval away from a Node are the six neighbours at the ends of its
Links, the vertices of the octahedron $|x| + |y| + |z| \le 1$
(Figure~\ref{fig:octahedron}); the octahedron scaled by the age is the
causal front, inside which every row stays. A row's mean Euclidean pace
lies within $1/T_d$ of the octahedron's inscribed sphere
(Proposition~\ref{prop:pace}), so the vertex's pace, one Link per
interval along a heading, is reached by no row's mean pace: the front is
a bound and not a signal.

\begin{proposition}[The pace]\label{prop:pace}
For every primitive direction $d$, $S_1 Q_f \le T_d$: the Manhattan pace
$S_1 Q_f/T_d$ is at most one Link per interval on every digital line, and
$S_1 = \sqrt3\,|d|$ exactly when $|d_x| = |d_y| = |d_z|$. A pace $v$ that
is the same in every direction and at most one Link per interval on every
line satisfies $v \le |d|/S_1$ for every $d$, hence $v \le 1/\sqrt3$, the
radius of the octahedron's inscribed sphere; and the table's Euclidean
pace obeys $1/\sqrt3 \le Q_f|d|/T_d < (1/\sqrt3)(1 + 1/T_d)$ for every $d$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$S_1^2 = (|d_x| + |d_y| + |d_z|)^2 \le 3(d_x^2 + d_y^2 + d_z^2)$ is the
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality against $(1, 1, 1)$, an equality exactly when
the three sizes are equal; so $S_1 Q_f \le \sqrt{3|d|^2Q_f^2}$, and since
$S_1 Q_f$ is an integer, $S_1 Q_f \le \lfloor\sqrt{3|d|^2Q_f^2}\rfloor =
T_d$. A row at the Euclidean pace $v$ on the line of $d$ makes $S_1$
Links per length $|d|$, so $vS_1/|d|$ Links per interval on average, and
at most one Link per interval bounds this by $1$: $v \le |d|/S_1$. The
least of $|d|/S_1$ over directions is $1/\sqrt3$, on the cube diagonals
($1/\sqrt n$ in $n$ dimensions). $T_d \le \sqrt3\,Q_f|d| < T_d + 1$ gives
the pace's interval.
\end{proof}

So $c = 1/\sqrt3$ Links per interval is the largest isotropic pace the two
rules allow, and the table sits at it; that it sits at the supremum and
not below is a third statement of the design (assumed), not a consequence
of the two, so the value rests on three statements: locality,
straightness and the supremum. Stated exactly: the two rules give
$c \le 1/\sqrt3$ (one Link per interval allows the pace $1$ on a heading
and only $1/\sqrt3$ on a cube diagonal, three Links for the displacement
$\sqrt3$), and what is derived is the largest uniform pace the GameBoard
allows under them; why the rows realise it, and what a detector in
motion reads of it, are not derived here (Section~\ref{sec:lorentz}:
a one-way count of a stream reads $1 \mp \beta$, $\beta$ (beta) the
detector's speed as a fraction of $c$). Over the $1\,780\,418$ primitive
directions with components within $64$ the bound holds on every one, with
the integer equality $S_1 Q_f = T_d$ on $2072$: the $8$ cube diagonals,
and $2064$ directions near them where the floor closes a gap below
$1/Q_f$ (the shortest $(22, 21, 21)$, $S_1 = 64$, $\sqrt3|d| = 64.016$);
on all of these the row crosses exactly one Link per interval
(computation, \texttt{light\_speed.txt}; the design's map counts the same
$1\,780\,418$ and $2072$ and describes the $2072$ as cube diagonals, which
is corrected here \cite{lightspeed}). The pace is $64/110 = 0.5818$ on a
heading, $0.77$ percent above $1/\sqrt3$, $0.49$ percent above on the
plane diagonal, exact on the cube diagonal; the anisotropy is the table's
rounding, below $1/T_d$ in every direction and so of order $1/(\sqrt3
Q_f)$; a measured bound on the anisotropy of $c$ in the lattice's frame
puts $Q_f$ at or above its inverse, of order $5.8 \times 10^{17}$ for
$10^{-18}$ \cite{nagel2015} (a two-arm interferometer at rest with both
arms on headings sees nothing, the two paces being equal by the $48$; the
rotating resonators of \cite{nagel2015} would see the cubic pattern to the
same order; what the law says of a laboratory in motion is
Section~\ref{sec:lorentz}). Nothing measured enters the value. The number
is known on the cubic lattice: the Courant condition of the standard
second-order scheme for the wave equation, $c\,\Delta t/\Delta x \le
1/\sqrt n$ \cite{cfl1928}, whose necessity is this same argument (the
sphere of radius $c\,\Delta t$ inside the stencil's octahedron), and the
lattice Boltzmann sound speed $c_s^2 = 1/3$, from the isotropy of the
lattice tensor \cite{qian1992}. What the paper adds is the exact integer
table sitting at the bound, its rounding's anisotropy as a bound on the
grain, and the program's intent of one operator for rows and bodies (a
body's drive as the same operator at a fraction: decided and in build,
not run \cite{log}); it claims no novelty for the number
(Section~\ref{sec:lit}). The symmetry of the octahedron and of the flight
is the group of the $48$ signed permutations of the axes ($3! \times 2^3$;
$24$ of determinant $+1$, the rotations, and $24$ of $-1$, the
reflections), the lattice's stand-in for the rotation group: $T_d$ and
$S_1$ are invariant under all $48$, the digital line's tie by axis order
is not (on $(1, 1, 0)$, for half of the $48$, the line of $gd$ and $g$
applied to the line of $d$ differ at the odd Links and agree at every
whole period; on $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(2, 1, 0)$ they agree everywhere
\cite{checks}), so the flight commutes with the $48$ at the periods and
not between them. No body moves in the worlds of this chapter.

\paragraph{No dispersion.} What the lattice does not do to the pace is
as definite as what it does.

\begin{remark}[The pace is the direction's]\label{rem:nodispersion}
By Definition~\ref{def:rules} the flight table is indexed by $(d, \tau)$,
the phase is a separate accumulator, and no rule of the interval (the
flight, the birth, the split, the rotation, the merge; the archived
collision table reads no phase) reads a phase into a direction or a
step. So a row's pace
is $Q_f|d|/T_d$ at every phase rate up to the alias bound of $N/2$
steps per interval (a turn every two intervals, $1.15$ to $1.16$ Links),
and the anisotropy of Proposition~\ref{prop:pace} is the same at every
rate. This is the pure-shift case of a lattice automaton
\cite{meyer1996}: a ballistic walker with a passenger phase, free of
dispersion by construction, as a classical corpuscle is. What is not
trivial is the pair with Proposition~\ref{prop:pace}: a transport on
$\Z^3$ that is isotropic within $1/T_d$ in every direction and free of
dispersion in every direction, while the carried phase interferes at the
click. No local linear wave scheme on the lattice can be both: its
dispersion relation, $\omega$ the frequency and $k$ the wave vector, is
a polynomial equation in $e^{i\omega\Delta t}$ and $e^{ik_ja}$ ($a$ the
Link, $\Delta t$ the interval's length), so every branch $\omega(k)$ is
periodic in $k$ up to multiples of $2\pi/\Delta t$ while $c|k|$ is not,
and $\omega = c|k|$ holds on no open set of $k$.
\end{remark}

The price is stated with it: the model carries no wave on the lattice
(the interference is summed in the record, Definition~\ref{def:click}),
and no row carries an energy, so a photon enters only by a reading:
a photon of energy $E$ is read as a family whose phase turns the
fraction $E\,\Delta t/h$ of the circle per interval, $N E\,\Delta t/h$
steps of $\Z_N$, $h$ Planck's constant (assumed); the alias bound is then
$E\,\Delta t/h < 1/2$.
Under that reading the model's light has one speed at every energy, the
null of continuum physics, which distinguishes it among lattice
transports only; one measured energy dependence of $c$ refutes the
flight as defined. The numbers (computation, \texttt{light\_speed.txt}):
the Link is bounded by the shortest wavelength seen, a bound every
lattice model shares, wave or walker: the $1.4$ PeV photon of
\cite{lhaaso2021}, of wavelength $\Lambda = 8.9 \times 10^{-22}$ m,
gives $a \le \Lambda\sqrt3/2 = 7.7 \times 10^{-22}$ m under the pair
form of the phase and $a \le \Lambda/\sqrt2 = 6.3 \times 10^{-22}$ m
under the integer form (two Links spanning $\sqrt2\,a$ on a cube
diagonal's line); the bound moves with the record. The time-of-flight
tests bound nothing here and bound a lattice wave by its scheme and its
direction: the standard second-order scheme at the Courant number
$1/\sqrt3$ has, on an axis, the group velocity $v_g = c\,(1 - (ka)^2/12
+ \dots)$ and $v_g = 0$ at the band edge $ka = \pi$, and is exactly
dispersionless on the cube diagonal; one photon of $31$ GeV arriving
within $0.83$ s of the trigger of a burst at the redshift $z = 0.903$
\cite{abdo2009},
over the comoving distance $9.5 \times 10^{25}$ m of that paper's
cosmology without the cosmological weighting (conservative), needs
$|v_g - c|/c < 2.6 \times 10^{-18}$, which puts that scheme's Link on an
axis below $3.6 \times 10^{-26}$ m and a fourth-order scheme's, taking
$(ka)^4/100$ for its leading term (an order of magnitude, not a scheme's
coefficient), below $8 \times 10^{-22}$ m. The two bounds come from two
instruments, and a lattice wave obeys both.

\begin{definition}[Row and record]\label{def:row}
A \emph{row} is $(x, d, \tau, p, e, h, r, \ell, w, m)$: Node, direction,
age, phase $p \in \Z_N$, emitter, content per unit, record $r$, branch
label $\ell$, amount $w \ge 1$, multiplicity $m \ge 1$. Its amplitude is
\begin{equation}
z = \frac{w}{\sqrt m}\,\frac{C_N[p] + i S_N[p]}{256}, \qquad |z|^2 = \frac{w^2}{m}\cdot\frac{C_N[p]^2 + S_N[p]^2}{65536}.
\end{equation}
Two rows equal in every field but $w$ are one row with the amounts added;
two rows of one record and label equal in every field but a phase
difference of $N/2$ are one row with the amounts subtracted (an equal pair
is no row). The state of record $r$ is an element $\psi_r$ of the free
$\Z$-module $M_r$ on the basis $(x, d, \tau, p \bmod N/2, e, h, \ell, m)$
with $|p + N/2\rangle = -|p\rangle$, taken modulo the scaling
$(w, m) \sim (kw, k^2 m)$; the GameBoard's state is one normal form per
element of $\bigoplus_r M_r$. The norm is $\langle\psi,\psi\rangle =
\sum_{\text{rows}} w^2/m$. The archived engine narrows the quotient at a
detector: rows of one record with different $m$ at one set are added when
the ratio of the multiplicities is the square of a rational (the pointers
rescaled by the integer roots) and refused otherwise; at the tree of the
runs two multiplicities at one set were refused, and no reported world
has them, so every set below reads one $m$.
\end{definition}

The host, not any Node, keeps per live record the birth phase $u \in \Z_N$,
the count of live units (amounts), an accumulated pointer $(X, Y)$ per
(Node, label) and a gathered flag. This is the apparatus's layer; the
lattice never reads it. It is a memory no Node could own: on the two-slit
world the two paths of one record reach a pixel up to $18$ intervals
apart, so no Node ever holds both rows, and the coherent sum exists only
in the record's ledger \cite{log}.

\begin{definition}[The update rules of one interval]\label{def:rules}
Applied in this order to every row.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[F] Flight: $(x, d, \tau, p) \mapsto (x + \mathrm{pos}_d(\tau+1) -
\mathrm{pos}_d(\tau),\ d,\ \tau + 1,\ p + \varphi(\tau))$, where the
family's phase turn is either an integer per Link, added only when the
step is a Link, or a pair $[n, d]$ per interval of age,
$\varphi(\tau) = \lfloor (\tau+1) n/d \rfloor - \lfloor \tau n/d \rfloor$,
counted from the row's birth or last re-emission.
\item[C] Collision: at a Node holding several rows of amount $1$ the
table permutes their directions as a function of the whole set present;
$r, \ell, m$ unchanged; a bijection of states, not a linear map. No
collision acts in any world of this paper.
\item[S] Split, at a measured event with outputs $\{(d_i, a_i, t_i)\}$,
$a_i \in \Z$: the row $(w, m, p)$ is absorbed and re-emitted as the rows
$(w a_i,\ m A,\ p + t_i)$ on $d_i$ at age $0$, $A = \sum_i a_i^2$. The
balanced splitter is $(1, 1)$, $A = 2$; the reflection's turn is $N/4$.
\item[R] Rotation, at a set with setting $s$ and turn $t$, on a label
pair $(0, 1)$ read into the channels $(+, -)$:
$U_{s,t} = \begin{pmatrix} C'[s] & S'[s]\,v(t) \\ -S'[s] & C'[s]\,v(t)\end{pmatrix}$
with $v(t)$ the turn by $t$ steps on the label-$1$ column; a row of
label $0$ becomes $(wC'[s], 65536m, p)$ on $+$ and $(wS'[s], 65536m,
p + N/2)$ on $-$, a row of label $1$ becomes $(wS'[s], 65536m, p + t)$ on
$+$ and $(wC'[s], 65536m, p + t)$ on $-$ (the row form exact for $t$ a
multiple of $N/4$, the only turns used here; the archived code multiplies
the column by the tables' phasor at $t$). With $t = 0$ this is $U_s$ of
Theorem~\ref{th:isometry}; the GHZ bases below are $X = (s, 0)$ and
$Y = (s, N/4)$.
\item[M] Normal form: merge and cancel as in Definition~\ref{def:row}.
\item[B] Birth: a lamp's self-creation at interval $t$ releases record $r$
with $u$ the lamp's phase at the birth, $(b - 1) \bmod N$ for a lamp
started at $0$ that turns one step per birth, $b$ its count of births
(every world here; $b - 1 = t - 1$ only while the lamp births at every
interval; the archived code writes $(b - 1) \bmod N$ directly), rows of
amount $1$ on its $k$
directions with $m = k$, each row's phase starting at $u$ (plus the
lamp's own turn), and, for a pair, the declared labels with integer
weights on separate arms.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The click]\label{def:click}
At a set that reads the sum, the rows of record $r$ and label $\ell$ that
arrive are absorbed and the layer accumulates
\begin{equation}
X \mathrel{+}= \sum 32\,w\,C_N[p],\qquad Y \mathrel{+}= \sum 32\,w\,S_N[p],
\end{equation}
the phase $p$ as the row carries it on arrival (the turns of rule F
already in it). The pointer is kept per Node:
interference is coherent within one Node only, and the offer of a set of
Nodes (a detector, a face) and label $\ell$ is the sum over its Nodes of
$(X_x^2 + Y_x^2)/m$, one cell of the ladder, the click landing at the Node
within the set that $u$ selects by the same rungs; all rows at a set share
$m$. When the record's live count reaches $0$, with $T = \sum W$ over its
offers and the cumulative sums $C_1 \le \dots \le C_K = T$ in the declared
order,
\begin{equation}
\rung_k = \left\lfloor \frac{2N C_k + T}{2T} \right\rfloor \quad (\rung_0 = 0,\ \rung_K = N),
\qquad \text{the click is the } k \text{ with } \rung_{k-1} \le u < \rung_k,
\label{eq:rung}
\end{equation}
and the world's list gains one row: the record, $u$, the chosen set and
label, $W$, $T$, the count of combinations before and after. For a record
with arms at two rotated sets $A$ and $B$ (settings $a$, $b$) the cells
are the joint outcomes $(o_A, o_B)$ in the arms' declared order, with
the weights
\begin{equation}
W(o_A, o_B) = J(o_A, o_B)^2,\qquad J(o_A, o_B) = \sum_\ell c_\ell\,U_a[o_A][\ell]\,U_b[o_B][\ell],
\label{eq:joint}
\end{equation}
$c_\ell$ the labels' declared weights, $(1, 1)$ in every world here; and
the record is gathered once, when the rows at both arms have ended:
the world's row carries both outcomes at the completion interval, so the
near party's outcome is written at the far party's tick. Reading the
ladder in that order is the same as a coarse rung over $o_A$ followed by
a fine rung over $o_B$ within it with the same $u$, which is how
Theorem~\ref{th:marginals} states it. A row of a gathered record that
reached a set afterwards would offer nothing; no reported world has one,
since completion needs every row ended.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[One click per record]\label{lem:one}
A record every row of which ends (at a set, a face or the border; not a
row parked at rest or on a periodic axis without a lifetime) completes
and is gathered exactly once: the live count reaches $0$; $\rung_K = N$
and $\rung_0 = 0$, so every $u \in \Z_N$ lies in exactly one cell. Every
record of the worlds below is of this kind. The world's row carries the
completion interval and the last arrival's interval; the delay between
them is a delay of the record, not of the lattice.
\end{lemma}

Three things are put in here and not derived, and the paper claims none
of them. Definition~\ref{def:click} \emph{is} the Born rule: the weight is
the squared length of the summed pointer, and the ruler over $u$ is
inverse-transform sampling of a uniform variable, so over births with $u$
uniform on $\Z_N$ the count in channel $k$ is $\rung_k - \rung_{k-1}$, the
nearest integer to $N W_k/T$, Born to the resolution $1/N$. No principle
below the square is offered. The uniformity of $u$ is an assumption about
the source: $u = (b - 1) \bmod N$ is uniform over $N$ consecutive births,
which is how every run below is made (one birth per $u$), and its
independence of the settings is a fact of a world, not of the model: $u$
is a clock and a chooser is a clock, so they are independent when the
chooser's period is coprime to $N$ and the run covers their common
period, as in this paper's choosers' world (periods $3$ and $5$ against
$N = 64$, every bin one birth per $u$, every marginal $32/64$); a chooser
whose period shares a factor with $N$ (not run) would correlate with $u$
by construction, a correlation of the kind superdeterminism posits. The earlier engine's run \cite{paper1}
is history. $u$ is also the phase every row of
the record starts from, so it enters every table read as a common offset
of the record's phasors: exactly invariant before the tables' rounding
and not after it (the record's total over $u$ takes eight values,
Section~\ref{sec:measure}); the click alone reads $u$ as a number. The
tables at $1/256$ are the third input. What the paper derives is what
follows from these with the
lattice's rules: the interference of one record, the exact marginals, the
$\cos^2$ correlation of the pair, GHZ's zeros, and the finite-$N$ value
of $S$. The click is the only step that removes combinations and adds a
row to the world's list; the birth is the only step that adds
combinations. At the tree of the runs reported here the law was selected by a world
key (\texttt{amplitude}) and the worlds without it read as before; the
archived code has since made the record form the only click and deleted
the key, with dated lines in its register for what its later changes
moved and the integers of series L kept as recorded \cite{beamlaw,register}.

\section{Four theorems}\label{sec:theorems}

\begin{theorem}[The interval is injective between clicks]\label{th:bijection}
For an interval without a click, an end at a face or the border, or a
collision, $M \circ R \circ S \circ F$ is an injective $\Z$-linear map from $\bigoplus_r M_r$ to
$\bigoplus_r M_r$ paired with the apparatus's record of the rows the
splitters and rotators absorbed in that interval; the GameBoard's state
at any interval between two clicks, together with that record,
determines the state at the birth. Without the record the split forgets
the arriving age. A collision, where it acts, is a bijection of the
GameBoard's states that is not linear; none acts in the worlds of this
paper.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$F$ maps a basis element to a basis element and is injective because the
age is kept whole: $(d, \tau) \mapsto (d, \tau+1)$ is injective and the
Node offset is a function of $(d, \tau)$. $S$ absorbs the arriving row,
whose age, phase and amount are written on the apparatus's click line,
and re-emits at age $0$; given that line it is injective on the
amplitudes by Theorem~\ref{th:isometry}, and without it two rows
differing only in age have the same image: the split is inverted only
because the click line records what it absorbed. $R$ is injective
because $U_{s,t} = U_s\,\mathrm{diag}(1, v(t))$, the turn a bijection and
$U_s^{\mathsf T}U_s = n_s I$. $M$ replaces a
representative by the normal form of the same element of $M_r$, the
identity on the module. A composition of injective linear maps is
injective. The collision permutes the directions of the amount-$1$ rows
at a Node as a function of the whole set present \cite{beamlaw}, a
bijection of states and not a linear map, which is why it is excluded
from the linear statement. The engine's inverse interval is the witness
on worlds without a measured event \cite{beamlaw}; with splitters the
statement is the algebraic one with the record.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The split is an isometry whose conjugate transpose inverts it]\label{th:isometry}
The split $(w, m, p) \mapsto ((wa_i, mA, p + t_i))_i$ preserves
$\sum w^2/m$ for every integer vector $(a_i)$, and the conjugate
transpose of the table (the same weights with the turns reversed)
applied to its outputs, followed by the normal form, returns the input up
to the scaling $(kw, k^2m)$ with $k = A$. The rotation satisfies
$U_s^{\mathsf T} U_s = n_s I$ exactly with $n_s = C'[s]^2 + S'[s]^2$, and
multiplies a record's norm by $n_s/65536$: an isometry only up to the
tables' rounding ($65705/65536$ at $N = 64$, $s = 1$).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\sum_i (wa_i)^2/(mA) = w^2 \sum_i a_i^2/(mA) = w^2/m$. The conjugate
transpose maps the outputs $(wa_i, mA, p + t_i)$ to $\sum_i a_i (w a_i)$
at the phase $p$ on the input direction with multiplicity $mA \cdot A$,
that is $(Aw, A^2 m, p) \sim (w, m, p)$; for two inputs at one splitter
the cross terms carry phases differing by $N/2$ (a reflection's quarter
turn taken forward and its reverse taken back) and cancel in the normal
form, which the check with the pairs $(20, 21)$, $(3, 4, 5)$ and
$(119, 120, 169)$ confirms integer by integer \cite{design} (with the
turns kept instead of reversed the inputs are not returned), and the
engine's merge reproduces on its own rows \cite{beamlaw}. For $U_s$ the
diagonal of $U_s^{\mathsf T}U_s$ is $C'^2 + S'^2$ and the off-diagonal
$C'S' - S'C' = 0$ in integers.
\end{proof}

So a record's norm is conserved exactly by $S$ and by the flight, and by
$R$ and by every read through the tables only within the tables' rounding
($-351/65536$ to $+361/65536$ per row for the powers of two through
$65536$); the integer
Mach-Zehnder's total over the 64 birth phases takes eight values from
$65448/65536$ to $65773/65536$, the same eight on every one of its ten
worlds \cite{register}. The normalisation by $T$ in \eqref{eq:rung} makes
the click's probabilities sum to one at completion; it does not restore
the norm along the way, and the paper claims exact unitarity nowhere.

\begin{theorem}[Exact marginals; no-signalling]\label{th:marginals}
For a pair with labels $\{0, 1\}$ of equal weight and settings $a, b$, let
$J(o_A, o_B) = \sum_\ell U_a[o_A][\ell]\,U_b[o_B][\ell]$ and
$W(o_A, o_B) = J^2$. Then the first party's outcome is $+$ exactly for
$u < N/2$ and $-$ for $u \ge N/2$, for every $(a, b)$ and every $N$; and
the second party's count of $+$ is exactly $N/2$ for every $(a, b)$ unless
$2N W(+,+)/T$ is an odd integer (a tie of the rung), in which case it is
$N/2 + 1$. No tie occurs for any setting pair at $N = 8, 16, 24, 32, 48,
64, 96, 128, 192, 256, 512, 1024$, nor at the CHSH labels for any
multiple of $8$ up to $4096$ (computed from the rule, \cite{checks}; the
gate review found none at $64$, $256$, $1024$ and at $4096$ for
$a = 0, 1024$, \cite{log}); the
reviewer also recomputed the marginals from the rows of one registered
world at $N = 64$ for all 4096 setting pairs, $32/64$ in every pair
\cite{log}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $n_a = C'[a]^2 + S'[a]^2$. Since the rows of $U_b$ are orthogonal on
the integers, $\sum_{o_B} J(o_A, o_B)^2 = n_a n_b$ for each $o_A$, so
$T = 2n_a n_b$ and the coarse cumulative after the first party's $+$ is
$T/2$: $\rung = \lfloor (NT + T)/(2T)\rfloor = \lfloor (N+1)/2 \rfloor =
N/2$. For the second party, $W(o_A, o_B) = W(-o_A, -o_B)$ (the rows of
$U_s$ are each other's quarter turn), so with $x = N W(+,+)/T$ the counts
are $c_{++} = \lfloor x + \tfrac12\rfloor$ and $c_{-+} = \rung_3 - N/2$ with
$\rung_3 = \lfloor (2N(T - W(+,+)) + T)/(2T)\rfloor = N + \lfloor \tfrac12 -
x\rfloor$. Hence $c_{++} + c_{-+} = N/2 + \lfloor y\rfloor + \lfloor 1 -
y\rfloor$ with $y = x + \tfrac12$, which is $N/2$ when $y \notin \Z$ and
$N/2 + 1$ when $y \in \Z$. The tie condition is $2x \in 2\Z + 1$, that
is $2NW(+,+)/T$ odd. The absence of ties at the listed $N$ is the
computation over every setting pair \cite{checks}. Which party is first
is the world file's declaration of the arms' order, not a property of
the lattice: the model has a preferred party by declaration, and
swapping the arms moves outcomes between the parties while the counts
stay \cite{log}.
\end{proof}

The strict-crossing rung $NC_k > uT$ gives the second party $33/64$ in
$3944$ of the $4096$ setting pairs at $N = 64$, a signalling of $1/N$ made
by the rounding \cite{design}; the nearest rung of \eqref{eq:rung} is what
Theorem~\ref{th:marginals} needs. The theorem's scope is what it states:
a pair of two labels with equal weights, the CHSH form, and the rung of
\eqref{eq:rung}; unequal weights and more than two labels are not covered,
and no-signalling for them is open. Where the two settings meet is
explicit in Definition~\ref{def:click}: $B$'s rung is computed from
$W(o_A, o_B)$ with $a$ and $o_A$ supplied by the layer. That the marginal
at $B$ is nevertheless $N/2$ for every $a$ is the content of the theorem;
the joint counts are not local, and the model does not claim they are.

\begin{theorem}[The finite-$N$ Bell value]\label{th:bell}
With the counts of Theorem~\ref{th:marginals} and off a tie,
$E_N(a, b) = 4c_{++}/N - 1$ and
\begin{equation}
\bigl|E_N(a, b) - \cos(2\pi(a-b)/N)\bigr| \le \frac{2}{N} + 4\arcsin\frac{\sqrt2}{2\rho} < \frac{2}{N} + 0.0111,\qquad \rho = 256 - \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},
\label{eq:ebound}
\end{equation}
so at the labels $(0, N/8, N/4, 3N/8)$, $|S(N) - 2\sqrt2| \le 8/N + 0.0444$.
$S(N)$ is an exact rational for each $N$; it lies above $2\sqrt2$ for
$252$ of the $512$ multiples of $8$ up to $4096$ (among them $N = 16, 32$
with $S = 3$ and $N = 128$ with $S = 23/8$) and equals $181/64 =
2.828125$ at every power of two from $512$ through $4096$ \cite{checks}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$c_{++} = \lfloor x + \tfrac12\rfloor$ with $x = NW(+,+)/T$ gives
$|c_{++} - x| \le \tfrac12$ and $E_N = 4c_{++}/N - 1$ from the mirror
symmetry, so $|E_N - (4W(+,+)/T - 1)| \le 2/N$. The vector
$(C'[s], S'[s])$ differs from $256(\cos\theta_s, \sin\theta_s)$,
$\theta_s = \pi s/N$, by at most $\tfrac12$ per component, so its
direction differs from $\theta_s$ by at most $\delta = \arcsin(\sqrt2/(2\rho))$
with $\rho = 256 - \sqrt2/2 = 255.29$, below every table vector's length
(each component within $\tfrac12$ of $256\cos$ or $256\sin$), so
$4\delta = 0.01108 < 0.0111$ and $16\delta = 0.04432 < 0.0444$;
$J(+,+)/\sqrt{n_a n_b}$ is the cosine of the angle between the two
directions, $\cos(\theta_a - \theta_b + \epsilon)$ with $|\epsilon| \le
2\delta$, and $4W(+,+)/T - 1 = 2\cos^2(\cdot) - 1 = \cos(2(\theta_a -
\theta_b) + 2\epsilon)$, within $4\delta$ of $\cos(2\pi(a-b)/N)$. The
four settings add the bounds. The values of $S(N)$ are the computation
from these formulas with the repository's tables \cite{checks}, which
reproduces the design's $176/64$, $720/256$ and $2896/1024$ at $N = 64,
256, 1024$ and the engine's three values below.
\end{proof}

What Theorem~\ref{th:bell} does not say: that $S(N) \le 2\sqrt2$, which is
false for about half the $N$; nor that the deficit is $O(1/N)$ from below,
which holds at the three $N$ the engine ran and not in general. The
tables' term in \eqref{eq:ebound} does not shrink with $N$; it is the
resolution $1/256$ of the circle's tables. At the CHSH labels the tables'
entries are the same at every $N$ ($237, 98$ and $181, 181$), so
$E(0, N/8) = 46565/65773$ and $E(N/4, N/8) = 46452/65773$ exactly at every
$N$ before the rounding of the cells, which is why the powers of two from
512 all give $181/64$ \cite{checks,register}. At a tie $c_{--} = c_{++} - 1$ and
$E_N$ drops by $2/N$; no tie occurs at the CHSH labels up to $4096$.

\section{The Bell value at finite $N$ and the experiments}\label{sec:bell}

$S(N)$ is a property of the model that nothing in the model fixes: $N$ is
a declared width of the world. The model gives a prediction only once $N$
is chosen, and what a measurement of nature does is constrain $N$, under
three assumptions the paper states and does not defend: that nature's
pairs are this model's pairs at some $N$, that the experiment's
fair-sampling assumption holds, and that the experiment's four settings
are the model's labels $(0, N/8, N/4, 3N/8)$. The third matters because
$E$ depends on $(a, b)$ beyond $a - b$: at $N = 256$ the maximum of $S$
over all setting quadruples is $91/32 = 2.84375$ and $181{,}152$
quadruples lie within three standard deviations of the measured value,

exclusion stands whatever the settings \cite{checks}. Under the three,
the photon-pair value $S = 2.82759 \pm 0.00051$ \cite{poh2015} is
incompatible with $N = 64$ (152 standard deviations) and $N = 256$ (30),
compatible with the powers of two from 512 (one standard deviation
above), admits its first $N$ at 184, and excludes 136 of the 512 values
up to 4096 at five standard deviations, the largest 3016 \cite{checks};
the archived engine declares $N$ a power of two, so the other multiples
of $8$ are the formula's, not runnable worlds. Of the loophole-free
experiments, \cite{hensen2015} reports a CHSH value, $S = 2.42 \pm
0.20$, which excludes no $N$ at three standard deviations;

\section{Continuum limits: what returns and what does not}\label{sec:limits}

slows by $\gamma^2$ along and $\gamma$ across, the classical ether's
light clock with nothing to contract, so a laboratory moving through
the lattice would show a two-way anisotropy of order $\beta^2/2$, which
the isotropy measurements refute unless the laboratory is at rest in
the lattice's frame (Table~\ref{tab:nature}, row 5b). Against these
stand the measurements: the muon's lifetime at $\gamma = 29.33$
\cite{bailey1977}, time dilation on stored ions to $2 \times
10^{-9}$ \cite{botermann2014}, the isotropy of $c$ to $10^{-18}$
\cite{nagel2015}; the electron's speed against its kinetic energy
\cite{bertozzi1964} the law does not reach, having no energy of motion.

\paragraph{The covariant readings.} For each reading its covariant form
exists within the six operations and without a root: the count per turn
of the body's own clock in place of the count per interval; the gradient
of the age moment across the six Ports in place of the retarded flux;
the energy $E$ as an accumulator of the work done by the push, with
the drive $\mathbf v = \mathbf p c^2/E$; and the turn per proper time,
$E_0/E$ per interval, $E_0$ the rest energy. Under them $E_0 = mc^2$, $m$
the body's content in the drive's units, is forced by the Newtonian
limit; the invariant $E^2 - p^2c^2$ is kept to $10^{-12}$ in a host
integration (whether the remainder closes it exactly is open); the
muon's $64$th turn falls at $70.9$ and $125.2$ at $0.43c$ and $0.86c$;
the field of a moving charge is Heaviside's with the magnetic term; the
contraction and the bond's period are Lorentz's by Lorentz's 1904
argument on that field, not integrated; and the identity $h\nu = Q_f S
c^2$ ties the cost $h$ of a phase step, the turn rate $\nu$, the width
and the grain to the pace; what stays outside is every general formula
beyond first order, which needs the rows to read the crowd
\cite{derivations}. The four rules are named one hypothesis,
covariant-readings-v1, passing the program's three tests on paper (no
family name, one of the six operations, the record and its six
neighbours); not built, not run; every moving body's register moves
under them.

\paragraph{The decision.} The law stays the six operations and states
its prediction as declared, an unslowed counter in motion, as a row that
can fail against the muon in flight (Table~\ref{tab:nature}, rows 4a and
4b). The hypothesis built beside it for comparison was first the seventh
operation, an integer root at a declared grain slowing the counter by
$\gamma$ and contracting its bond by declaration, and is now the
covariant readings above, decided in its place on the day the theorem
was written \cite{log}. The derivation's grounds for naming them: they
need no operation beyond the six and give $E = mc^2$ and the field where
the root gives the clock and the contraction by declaration and no
energy of motion. Both the law and the hypothesis are to be run against
the same pins (the derivation's 18.1: the muon's $64$th turn at $71$ and
$125$ within one interval, the pair in motion with the same round trip
along and across, the coasting star at $z = 0.315 \pm 0.003$), the
paper to state both readings when they exist. A third route of the same day, a slowing by the crowd a
moving body reads, is closed: the crossing count of a mover through an
isotropic crowd has the sphere mean exactly $1$ \cite{derivations}.
Whatever the runs show, the results of Part~III are unchanged, since no
row of its worlds carries a mass or a clock in motion.

\section{Literature and positioning}\label{sec:lit}

The click is a non-local step, forced by Bell's theorem because the
marginals are exact and $S > 2$ \cite{bell1964,chsh1969}; it is
parameter-dependent at fixed $u$ in the sense of the causal anatomy of
\cite{jarrett1984,shimony1986} as \cite{paper1} used it; no-signalling is
Theorem~\ref{th:marginals}, as it is a theorem of quantum mechanics
\cite{grw1980}.

't Hooft's cellular automaton interpretation \cite{thooft2016} keeps
determinism and meets Bell by superdeterminism; here the model is
deterministic given $u$, the settings are GameBoard clocks whose
periods, coprime to $N$ in the registered choosers' world, leave them
uncorrelated with $u$ (a period sharing a factor with $N$ would not, and
is not run), and the Bell value is met by an
explicit non-local click. Wolfram's hypergraph rewriting and Gorard's
multiway quantum mechanics \cite{wolfram2020,gorard2020} take the
branches of a multiway system as the superposition and a completion as
the collapse; here one lattice, one record with rows, one click with an
exact integer rule and finite tables whose consequences are computed.
Lattice gases \cite{hpp1976,fhp1986} are exact integer dynamics with the
continuum law in the limit; the GameBoard is of that kind, with the
click's reading of one record added. Its pace $1/\sqrt3$ is the cubic
lattice's known number, the Courant bound of the discretised wave
equation \cite{cfl1928}, reached in Proposition~\ref{prop:pace} by the
Courant condition's own domain-of-dependence argument, and the sound
speed of the lattice Boltzmann models \cite{qian1992}, which stream every
population with one operator as the flight does; what the proposition
adds is the exact integer table at the bound, whose rounding's
anisotropy is a bound on the grain of the kind every lattice model of
light owes to the isotropy measurements \cite{nagel2015}. Where the
GameBoard parts from a discretised wave equation is dispersion: a wave
on the lattice has a group velocity that falls with the wave number and
a band edge, and the three-dimensional automata of
\cite{bialynicki1994,arrighi2019} are
dispersive with $v_g \to c$ only as $k \to 0$, while a row here is the
pure-shift case, a walker on a digital line with one pace at every
phase rate (Remark~\ref{rem:nodispersion}), so the time-of-flight tests
\cite{abdo2009} bound a wave scheme's Link by its order and its
direction and this one's not at all. Quantum cellular automata and
quantum lattice gases \cite{bialynicki1994,meyer1996,arrighi2019} are
local unitary rules on complex amplitudes; here no complex number is on
the lattice, the split is an exact integer isometry, and the tables'
unitarity holds within $361/65536$ and is computed, not assumed. Bohm's
theory and quantum equilibrium \cite{bohm1952,dgz1992} take the hidden
position distributed as $|\psi|^2$ (from typicality); here a uniform $u$
and the ruler do the same work by inverse-transform sampling, a
different distribution, one integer per record with a table for the
guidance, and, like Bohm's, non-local at the pair. Nelson's stochastic
mechanics \cite{nelson1966} is stochastic where nothing here is, once
$u$ is fixed. Objective collapse \cite{grw1986,bassi2013} has real
discrete events with a free rate and length; the click is triggered by
the record's completion at the apparatus and has neither, and nothing
collapses spontaneously (a pair stays entangled without maintenance). In
the classification of ontological models \cite{spekkens2007,hs2010} the
rows' amplitudes and $u$ are ontic, so the model is $\psi$-ontic; its
non-locality is Bell's (Section~\ref{sec:causal}). On Tsirelson's bound
\cite{tsirelson1980,pr1994}, its finite-precision form \cite{bhz2005}
and finite precision in foundations \cite{meyer1999,kent1999}: Theorem~\ref{th:bell} puts the model's
$S(N)$ on both sides of $2\sqrt2$ with the marginals exact, a
no-signalling box of the Popescu--Rohrlich kind at finite $N$, of a size
a Bell measurement of precision $0.0005$ already constrains
(Section~\ref{sec:bell}). The single-quantum build-up of fringes
\cite{tonomura1989,grangier1986}, GHZ \cite{ghsz1990,mermin1990,pan2000}
and interaction-free measurement \cite{ev1993} are the experiments the
measurements of Section~\ref{sec:measure} are named for; none of them is
reproduced in its own geometry, and the two-slit pattern here is a sparse
fan's spots.
