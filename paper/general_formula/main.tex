\begin{abstract}
How much of physics can one local rule in whole numbers compute, with nothing continuous beneath it and with a detector's click as the only measurement? We present a method: an integer cellular automaton, a cubic lattice of Nodes stepped by one recurrence, which takes a Node's present, its past and its six neighbours through one integer division. The step is reversible, conserves a quadratic form exactly in the rationals, and is covariant under the 48 symmetries of the cube; no boost is among them. A change crosses at most one Link per interval. Light moves at $1/\sqrt 3$ of that bound, and nature's $c$ sets the length of a Link and of an interval. Measurement is a meeting at a detector: the record run forward from its root meets the detector's own transition read backward from the click that happened, two quanta and never one. The detector draws the click; the lattice draws nothing. That draw is the one non-local element. On this basis, the wave equation, the quantum of action and the metric of gravity are three readings of one line. The line gives its own formulas with no number taken from nature. A family's pair $[p, q]$, with $\cos\omega_0 = p / q$, gives the family's inertial mass $m^* = 3\tan\omega_0$ and its light speed $c_m^2 = \omega_0 / (3\tan\omega_0)$; $E = m^* c_m^2$ holds exactly, and Lorentz's factor holds at $c_m$ as the gap closes. The matter pair gives the post-Newtonian $\gamma_K = q / p$ and $\beta_K = (q + p) / (2p)$, which go to $1$ as the gap closes. It also gives charge universality, the ratio $(m_p / m_e)^2$ between the two forces across families, and $\alpha = (3\sqrt 3 / 8\pi) k / (\Gamma E_s)$ between two quanta, $k$ a weight declared in the file. Newton's gravitation comes out, $G$ a reading, and Einstein's weak field follows under one named assumption fitted to two clicks: the metric is exponential, $\gamma = 1$ exactly, $\beta = 1$ as the gap closes, and the shadow is $4.6$ percent larger than Schwarzschild's. The paper claims the method and not nature. Every result is a supporting finding for the reader to test, and what the method does not give is listed.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} integer cellular automaton; reversible lattice dynamics; measurement as a meeting of past and future; lattice dispersion and inertial mass; weak-field gravity on a lattice; limiting speed on a lattice

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The question.} This paper is about one method of computing physics. A GameBoard of Nodes is stepped by one local, reversible rule in whole numbers, and it is read only through clicks, the one kind of measurement there is. In the words of the title: the integer cellular automaton is the GameBoard stepped by Rule3; the world it computes is the records and clicks of a declared world; and the clicks of nature it approaches are the comparisons of Sections~\ref{sec:forms} to~\ref{sec:constants}. Those comparisons are supporting findings, never the claim. On this GameBoard the classical wave equation, the quantum of action and the metric of gravity are three readings of one line, Rule3. The line itself is the lattice wave equation. Its count, read in quanta, is the click. Its paces, read as a clock and a ruler, are the metric. Nothing continuous lies beneath. The question is whether this method can give the clicks that nature shows, and how little state, non-locality and randomness it needs. The fifteen formulas, the one computed world set beside its blind row, and the two gates below are examples of what the method yields. They are not its scope, and Section~\ref{sec:open} lists what it does not yet give. The paper's one claim is the method: a GameBoard of integers, stepped by one rule and read into the world through clicks, from which the forms set beside nature's are derived in the order stated. The paper does not claim that nature is such a lattice. Every derivation, comparison and prediction below is a supporting finding for the method, each with its status, and none is the paper's thesis. The paper computes the click patterns of declared detectors and sets them beside nature's clicks. Why a click happens stands outside the paper: it is a declaration, the draw, and it is in the open list. No claim of the paper rests on it. The form is claimed; the cause is not. The paper starts from one statement and never contradicts it (\claimmark{assumption}): the world is the meeting of the past with the future; a click is that meeting, the one measurement there is; and an observer sees only clicks. (These words are defined at the end of this section.) Every physical number in the paper is a reading of one integer rule with its declared integers: the pairs, $\Gamma$, $T$, the two holders' weights, gravity's $E_g$, the sign holder's $k$, and the vacuum content (Section~\ref{sec:choice}). For nature's other clicks it gives the forms of Section~\ref{sec:forms} at the computed pair, with the findings of Section~\ref{sec:open} set against them. A Node holds a record's present and its past, two integer levels. The rule makes the record's future from these two and from the six neighbouring Nodes. The rule is covariant under the translations of the lattice and under the symmetry group of the cube, which has order $48$; no boost is among these symmetries (Section~\ref{sec:objects}). The lattice holds nothing but events that split: at every Node, a level splits by the rule to the six neighbours. Run forward from an emitter, the split events are the future, every path at once. Read backward from a click that happened, by the same rule, they are the past, and through two slits both of them pass through both gaps. A click is where the two meet at a declared detector. Which meeting happens is the one thing the model does not determine, and it is the one non-local element of the description (Section~\ref{sec:meeting}). What looks like a wave is the future. What looks like a particle is the present, the click. The track is the past (Section~\ref{sec:nowaves}). No theorem of minimality is claimed.

\paragraph{The rule in one line.} Everything in the paper follows from one line of integer arithmetic, Rule3, together with one write, three readings, the declarations of Section~\ref{sec:model} and the one assumption of Section~\ref{sec:paces}. The line holds at every Node, in every interval and for every family of physical values \cite[the line; the booking; the primitives]{algebra}:
{\small\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
&\text{Rule3:} && w\,\anext + r' = \textstyle\sum_{j \sim i} R_{ij}\,\mathrm{arr}_j + S\,\anow - w\,\abefore + r, \quad 0 \le r' < w,\\
&&& w = 6\,\den\,\Gamma^2, \quad R_{ij} = 2\,\num\,p_a(i, j)^2 = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2,\\
&&& S = 12\,\den\,\Gamma^2 - 12\,(\den - \num)\,p_0^2 - \textstyle\sum_{j \sim i} R_{ij};\\
&\text{the one write:} && \text{a held part at a Node gains } \Bigl(\textstyle\sum_f w_f\,q_f(\text{Node}) + r\Bigr) \ddiv E, \text{ the remainder } r \text{ kept there};\\
&\text{the readings:} && D_i = \mathrm{now}_i^2 - \mathrm{next}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i, \qquad W_i = \mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{before}} - \mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{before}},\\
&&& F_{ij} = \num\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j).
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}}
In the first line, $\anow$, $\abefore$ and $\anext$ are the family's level at the Node now, one interval before and one interval later: the present, the past and the future. $\mathrm{arr}_j$ is the arrival through the Port to the neighbour $j$, which is that neighbour's level; the six Ports are summed. $r$ and $r'$ are the remainder kept at the Node before and after the step, and $\Gamma$ is the Node clock. $[\num, \den]$ is the family's pair, and $\cos\omega_0 = \num / \den$ is its rotation per interval at wave number zero. $p_0$ is the Node's clock pace, $p_i$ its own pace, and $p_a(i, j)$ is the pace of the Link through the Port: the Node's pace times the Link's factor $q_{ij} / \Gamma$. The factor reads the same from both ends; the pace is the reading Node's own. All of these are the Node clock, lowered by the field levels that the family reads there (Section~\ref{sec:paces}). At the vacuum's paces, the two Links of an axis share one $R_a = 2\,\num\,\Gamma^2$, and $S = 0$. $\ddiv$ is floor division. The second line is the only way a field's level is ever written. It sums, over the sourcing families $f$, each family's own quantity $q_f$ at the Node, weighted by $w_f$; adds the part's one remainder; and divides by the part's wall $E$. The third line is what is read from a record and never written: its form $D_i$, which sources the fields; its Wronskian $W_i$, which is its charge; and its current $F_{ij}$ into the Node $i$ from its neighbour $j$. A detector reads that current through its boundary in quanta of $\Wc = 3\,\den\,T$, where $T$ is the quantum's norm, the normalisation of the quadratic form.

\paragraph{The three layers of the method.} The method has three layers, and each has a fixed part and a free part (Fig.~\ref{fig:method}). The first layer is the board's rules (Section~\ref{sec:model}): which families exist and of what dimension, which holders, who reads what and how. Here Rule3 with its reads is fixed, and the rows of the file are free. The second layer is the numbers (Section~\ref{sec:families}): $\Gamma$, the pairs, the holders' weights, gravity's $E_g$ and the sign holder's $k$, $T$, the vacuum content and the ranges. All of these are free, and there are as few of them as the rule allows. The third layer is the click (Sections~\ref{sec:click}, \ref{sec:detector} and~\ref{sec:bodyclicks}): how the board becomes a click, what the detector declares, what is credited, and how a mass on the board is read as a mass in clicks. Fixed here, and common to every world, is the one form of the click: the meeting, the bilinear credit, the draw by the shares, the detector's one write at the Node of the exchange, and the postulate that only a click is a measurement. Free, and belonging to the world, are the detector's four declarations, among them the unit it counts in.

Light's band, its dispersion and its index in a well (Sections~\ref{sec:line} and~\ref{sec:paces}). The lattice Klein-Gordon equation and its Schr\"odinger limit (Section~\ref{sec:line}). The conserved form and the share identity (Sections~\ref{sec:form} and~\ref{sec:share}). The Wronskian as the charge, and the rests of the holders (Sections~\ref{sec:sign} and~\ref{sec:reach}). The functional of a bound body and its stability (Section~\ref{sec:stable}). The adiabatic invariant and the drift of the count (Section~\ref{sec:bodyclicks}). The rate of a clock, the bending of light, Shapiro's delay, the perihelion and the capture radius of the shadow, the moving clock, de Broglie's and Planck's relations and $E = mc^2$ in the band's form, the index of the content for every family, and the fall as refraction (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). Bell's $E$ and $S$, Tsirelson's bound and GHZ's $M$ under the declared credit (Section~\ref{sec:bell-blind}). The fine-structure constant as a coefficient of the file, the hierarchy of the two forces between families, the units and the highest photon energy, and Newton's $G$ as a reading (Section~\ref{sec:constants-law}). The formulas of Tables~\ref{tab:results} and~\ref{tab:clicks}, several with their companions, cover five fields, and every one is an algebraic consequence of the line and of the inputs named beside it.

\paragraph{What the paper contributes.} We state the rule and what is exact about it, with proofs in the supplement: reversibility to the bit, the conserved form with its current, and the momentum flux as the wave's own stress (Section~\ref{sec:theorems}). We formulate measurement as the meeting of the forward record and the backward record at a declared detector, and we name the one non-local element (Section~\ref{sec:click}). We obtain the families as bands of the rule, and we choose a universe with as few free numbers as the rule allows (Section~\ref{sec:families}). We show that bodies are fixed points, prove their stability under dilation, and state how they cluster (Section~\ref{sec:bodies}). We compute the weak-field forms of gravity and the factor of the moving clock, each marked by what it rests on, both in the model's own potential and in the potential that nature's tests read (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). And we state the two slits and Bell as derivations of what each must give; neither experiment is performed here (Section~\ref{sec:slits}).

\paragraph{What stands, and on what.} Five things, each with its mark. (i) The core. Rule3 and the boxed lines after it, the paces, the conserved form, the share, the Wronskian, Kepler's potential and $G$ as a reading are theorems of the line, or are derived from it with no number of nature in them, and the step is reversible to the bit (Section~\ref{sec:theorems}). What the checks at this paper's closing corrected lies beside them, in secondary derivations, marks and digits, and not in the line. (ii) One rule in integers reaches Einstein's weak field. From the composed paces alone, under the one named assumption of Section~\ref{sec:paces}, come the exponential metric, the bending $4U_b$, Shapiro's delay, the redshift and the push $E(1 + \beta^2)\nabla U$, with $\gamma = 1$ exactly in the clock's potential and $\beta = 1$ as the gap closes. The assumption is fitted by two of those clicks, and the others are its predictions. The forms against Kepler's potential close on them as the gap closes (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). (iii) The click is the one measurement, and the non-locality is accounted for in one place. Bell's $478 / 169$ and the GHZ's $-4$ follow from the declared credit; the two slits' $278$ stands against the blind $273$; the one non-local element is the credit's one draw through the root. The meeting of the past with the future at the detector is one picture (Section~\ref{sec:click}). (iv) Every claim carries its mark and its fence, and what fails is named with its number: $\alpha_1 = -8$, hydrogen's $1s$ at $-0.043$ of Rydberg's unit under the self-read (S.46), and the telegraph of the shelved ion (Section~\ref{sec:open}). Nothing is claimed beyond its mark. (v) Everything can be recomputed. The files, the engine, the scripts and the documents are public (Section~\ref{sec:engine}). Every number that stands in the paper was recomputed from them: twice and independently where its status says two derivations, once where it says one. All of it is derived from one chosen set of rules, of which the engine fixes only the form of the recurrence. Other families, primitives and integers can be taken, and other things built (Section~\ref{sec:engine}).

\paragraph{What is put in, and the three kinds of claim.} The paper's claims are of three kinds, and the text keeps them apart. \emph{Found}: what is proved or derived from the rule with no input chosen for it. These are the fifteen formulas above and the click formulas of Table~\ref{tab:clicks} marked derived. The findings against nature are comparisons, listed in Section~\ref{sec:open}. \emph{Assumed, chosen or declared}: the assumption and the credit below, the rows of the universe, the detector's four declarations, among them its unit of credit, the electron as the free quantum of matter, and the denominator of the matter pair as the family's own integer (Section~\ref{sec:constants-law}). \emph{Held to be computable and not yet computed}: the bodies at the closing gap, the moving fixed point of a body, and the absorbed fraction with the body's write. The explicitly named hypotheses are stated where they stand; among them are light's gravity, entered as a derived hypothesis (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}), the carrier that would mend the lattice's frame, and the nuclear holders (Section~\ref{sec:open}). One assumption is named with the forms: a Link is clocked twice by the clock of the Node that reads it, $hN = 1$, fixed by the bending and the perihelion (Section~\ref{sec:paces}). The forms against Kepler's potential reach Einstein's only as the gap of the matter family closes (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). One choice was made with a known result in view, and we state it as an input, not a prediction: a click's share is the square of the meeting. This credit was chosen to reach nature's correlations, so Bell's $2\sqrt 2$ follows from it and does not test it. The numbers of the file are nature's and not the rule's: the vacuum content, a declaration; the rate of the vacuum write, $\Lambda$, the one declared number of the hypothesis of Section~\ref{sec:clusters}; the binding range, a world's choice; gravity's weight $E_g$ and the write weight $k$ of the sign holder, read from $G$ and from $\alpha$; and the gaps, read from the masses (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}, Table~\ref{tab:calibration}). Table~\ref{tab:results} lists every result of the paper with its kind, its fence and its inputs. What is not claimed: that nature is a lattice; that any number of the computed pair $[2, 3]$ is nature's; that a comparison with nature is more than a consistency statement at the stated order; or that a formula known in the continuum is new where the paper gives its lattice form. The known form stands beside the lattice form in every row. Every derivation was checked twice and independently within the project, and, where a world exists, by the checks of the implementation. No independent referee has checked them. Where the text says exact, it means exact in the rational arithmetic before the rounding, with the integer remainders bounded as stated. A line decided after a derivation is stated as the law, with the run of the implementation pending; a run that differed from its prediction would be a defect of the implementation to repair, never a result. The paper computes one world of the many the method admits. Every number of the files and every declared row, a family, a holder, a read, an instrument, can be changed and the same rule run again. What is brought here is one plane of them, with every derivation and every new formula shown. The claim is only that it approaches nature's clicks in the order stated, never that nature is so.

\paragraph{How a world is built and tested.} This is the method's statement, with its pointers. A world file declares the following, and nothing else: a family's pair and dimension; a holder's sources, divisor and rest; the bodies and the messages; a detector's four declarations, which are its region, window, floor and whole, and its seed; and the faces. The engine holds no number, no formula, no family name and no flag, and every primitive is one folder found by its name \cite[how to run a world; how to add a family]{engine}. One change, in numbers the paper keeps, shows what this means: replacing the matter pair $[2, 3]$ by $[999, 1000]$ moves $c_m / c$ from $0.867$ to $1$, and moves the computed pair's $\gamma_K$ and $\beta_K$ from $1.5$ and $1.25$ to $1$ (Section~\ref{sec:forms}); a binding holder's range $R$ turns a declared body from unbound to bound (Section~\ref{sec:reach}). The procedure, with the two slits as the example, is this. The blind row is written from the law's lines before the run. The run is made. The back-in-time gate reports \textsc{match} or \textsc{miss} on its reversibility. The reader prints the row of the run beside the blind row, with its deviation and its extrema (Sections~\ref{sec:engine} and~\ref{sec:slits}). Three worlds are run and gated in this paper: the two slits, Bell's gate and the GHZ gate. Every other experiment the paper sets against nature is derived from the law's lines and not run, each with its mark and fence where it stands. These are Einstein's weak-field forms, the bending, Shapiro's delay, the redshift as the clock at two heights and the perihelion at the small gap (S.15, S.19, S.20); the shadow (S.30); the push on a moving record (S.58); the two slits' own blind row, written before the run (Section~\ref{sec:slits-blind}); the moving clock (S.21, S.22); the magnetism of moving charges (S.33); the E\"otv\"os ratio (S.47); the one-quantum image of the lens (Eq.~\eqref{eq:lens}); the deceleration of the clusters (S.29); the atom (S.46); Rabi's absorption line (S.43); the gravity of light (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}); the anticoincidence (S.57); the Zeno effect (S.59); the preferred frame's $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ against Gravity Probe B and the Sun's spin (S.35), derived and failing; and, derived under a declaration, the telegraph of the shelved ion and the giving of an excited body (the rate declared; Section~\ref{sec:open}) and the nuclear binding (S.45, the weight declared). Their engine worlds are read beside their blinds in Section~\ref{sec:open}, as named findings and not as a gate. Rule3 is fixed and no rule is added. A new line of physics enters as a hypothesis under its own name, after the three tests, generic, vector and local, which is how every family and holder of this paper entered (Section~\ref{sec:postulates}).

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Reversible cellular automata and digital physics.} The idea that the physical world is a discrete computation goes back to Zuse \cite{zuse}. Fredkin, Toffoli and Margolus developed it as reversible cellular automata \cite{fredkin}\cite{margolus}\cite{toffoli}. Wolfram developed it as a programme of cellular automata \cite{wolfram}, and 't Hooft as the cellular automaton interpretation, a deterministic foundation beneath quantum theory \cite{thooft2013}\cite{thooft}. Quantum cellular automata put a unitary step on a lattice. They range from the lattice Weyl, Dirac and Maxwell equations \cite{bialynicki} and quantum lattice gases \cite{meyer} to the structure theorems of the reviews \cite{arrighi}\cite{farrelly} and the derivation of Weyl's and Dirac's equations from an automaton's symmetries \cite{dariano}. The step of the present rule is an integer recurrence on two levels, not a unitary on amplitudes. Rule3 is a second-order cellular automaton in the sense of Toffoli and Margolus, $\anext = f(\anow) - \abefore$ \cite{toffoli}. Its alphabet is the integers, and it has one Euclidean division whose remainder stays at the Node. This is what the title calls an integer cellular automaton. Feynman noted that a local classical computer reproduces no correlations beyond Bell's bound \cite{feynman}. The model agrees, and it places the excess in the declared credit of the click, not in the lattice (Section~\ref{sec:bell-blind}).

\paragraph{Time-symmetric accounts of measurement.} Wheeler and Feynman described radiation as an interaction between the source and the absorber, with advanced and retarded fields together \cite{wheeler}. Cramer's transactional interpretation has an offer wave forward and a confirmation wave backward, and they meet at the absorber \cite{cramer}. The two-state vector formalism evolves one state forward from the preparation and one backward from the outcome \cite{abl}\cite{vaidman}. Price argued that a time-symmetric account is what Bell's theorem leaves open \cite{price}. Wharton and Argaman showed that every locally mediated reformulation of quantum theory must let some mediating parameters depend on measurement settings in their future \cite{wharton}. The model belongs to this class: the backward reading from a realised click depends on the detector's setting alone. It differs in three respects. Both directions are one integer line, exact to the bit; their product is a reading and not a wave; and the choice among the meetings is the one non-local element. The record is an ontic state in the terms of \cite{harrigan}.

\paragraph{Bell's theorem and local models.} Bell's inequality bounds every local account of a pair's correlations \cite{bell}, in the form that experiments test \cite{chsh}. A local model, a joint distribution and the inequalities are one condition \cite{fine}. The model's local credits give $S = \sqrt 2$ and $S = 2$, as they must (Section~\ref{sec:bell-blind}). Its credit by the contrast reaches the quantum correlation only by discarding pairs. This is Pearle's road \cite{pearle}, and it runs below the efficiency bounds of Eberhard and of Garg and Mermin \cite{eberhard}\cite{gargmermin}. The loophole-free tests of 2015 close that road for nature \cite{hensen}\cite{giustina}\cite{shalm}.

\paragraph{Discrete gravity and its tests.} Regge calculus \cite{regge} and causal sets \cite{bombelli} discretise the geometry of space-time itself. Here the lattice is never curved, and the weak-field forms are read from the coefficients (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). In the parametrised post-Newtonian framework \cite{will}, the one assumption gives $\gamma = 1$ exactly and $\beta = 1$ at the small gap, in the model's own potential. Against the potential that the tests read, the matter band's factors close as its gap closes. A lattice breaks Lorentz invariance at order one unless its terms are tuned \cite{collins}; the bounds are reviewed in \cite{mattingly}. The paper's answer is no loops and the Link's bound of S.24, with the closing gap for matter (Section~\ref{sec:forms}).

\section{The model}\label{sec:model}

This section states the model: the lattice, the families, the rule, the paces by which one family acts on another, the one write, the readings and the order of acts in an interval \cite[the GameBoard and the families; Rule3; the primitives]{algebra}.

\subsection{The GameBoard}\label{sec:objects}

The GameBoard is a box of $N_x \times N_y \times N_z$ Nodes. Two Nodes one step apart along one axis are joined by a Link. Each Node has six Links, one through each of its six Ports, $\pm x$, $\pm y$ and $\pm z$. Each axis is declared periodic, open or folded. Beyond an open face there is no Node, and a level there is read as $0$. A folded axis has one layer, and its two Ports return the Node itself. An inner face is a plane of Nodes declared closed except for declared gaps; this is how a wall with slits is made. A face may also be declared receding (\claimmark{assumption}). The box then grows by a layer of zeros whenever a nonzero level reaches the Node next to the face. So no event ever meets the face, and a run on such a box is a run on the unbounded lattice. The box grows and nothing stretches, so a receding face gives no cosmological redshift \cite[the objects; the unbounded board]{algebra}\cite[the receding face]{decisions}.

\paragraph{The group and its action.} The symmetry group of the cube has $48$ elements: the $24$ rotations and, with the reflections, the signed permutations of the three axes. It is $O_h$, the group of the octahedron, which is also the group of the cube (Fig.~\ref{fig:lattice}(b)). With the translations it is the space group of the simple cubic lattice. It acts by permuting the Nodes and their Ports, and it carries every level pair with its Node. The time reversal exchanges a record's present and its past and carries the step to its inverse (Theorem~\ref{th:direction}). Rule3 commutes with the action of the space group, since its line reads the six Ports symmetrically and names no axis. Under the group, the form $D$ is a scalar, even under the time reversal. The current $F$ is a vector, odd under it. The Wronskian $W$ is a scalar under the cube's $48$, odd under the time reversal and under the plane's reflection. The click is a scalar: it names a region and an interval, never a Node or an axis. No boost, no rotation beyond the cube's $24$ and no scaling is in the group. So Lorentz's group and the continuum's rotations are no symmetries of the lattice, and what the paper finds of the moving clock it finds as a property of the clicks (Section~\ref{sec:forms}) (\claimmark{theorem} for the covariance and the kinds of the readings; \fence{GameBoard}; S.4) \cite[the direction; the four acts]{algebra}\cite[the one algebra]{decisions}.


The NodeState holds the model's own numbers and nothing else: for every family and every part, two levels and one remainder; and for every part of a field family, the remainder of its one write. A count, a current, a charge or a tension is a reading, computed from these numbers when it is needed. No register, table, formula or random draw is kept at a Node \cite[the objects]{algebra}\cite[nothing at a Node but the law's numbers]{decisions}.

\subsection{The paces: how a field acts on a record}\label{sec:paces}

p_0 = \Gamma\,(1 - 1 / \Gamma)^{c}, \qquad p_i = \frac{p_0(c_i)^2}{\Gamma}, \qquad p_a(i, j) = \frac{p_i\,q_{ij}}{\Gamma}, \quad q_{ij} = \Gamma - t_a(i, j), \\ c = \sum_{\text{reads}} \text{weight} \times \text{the read family's time level},
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}

Every family reads the holders of the content that its declaration names, at the weights it names. Gravity's holder and the binding field's are read at the weight $1$ by every family. \subsection{The write, the readings and the interval}\label{sec:interval}

\paragraph{The one write.} Every write into a field's level is one act, the second line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}. \paragraph{The readings.} A reading is a bilinear form of the levels with declared coefficients. It writes nothing on the GameBoard. The law reads three of them, the third line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}. The form $D_i = \mathrm{now}_i^2 - \mathrm{next}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i$ sets the present against the product of the future and the past. \section{Exact properties}\label{sec:theorems}

This section states what holds exactly on every lattice, each item proved in S.48: the reversibility of the rule, the kind of function its acts can form, the conserved form with its current and its momentum flux, and the count as a reading of the record (S.5, S.6).

\subsection{Reversibility}\label{sec:direction}

Let $\sigma$ be the direction, $+1$ forward and $-1$ backward; let $\ddiv$ and $\dmod$ be the floor division and its remainder in $[0, w)$; and let $\rho$ be the remainder carried in. With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direction}
u = \sigma\Bigl(\sum_j R_{ij}\,\mathrm{arr}_j + S\,x - w\,y\Bigr) + \rho, \qquad z = \sigma\,(u \ddiv w), \qquad \rho' = u \dmod w,
\end{equation}
the forward call with $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \abefore, r)$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\anext, r')$, and the backward call with $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \anext, r')$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\abefore, r)$.

\begin{theorem}[the direction]\label{th:direction}
The backward call returns $(\abefore, r)$ bit for bit; the reversibility of every step needs no second function.
\end{theorem}

\noindent (Proved in S.48.) Theorem~\ref{th:direction} holds for the GameBoard's step at every interval. A detector's write and a body's window, click or not (Section~\ref{sec:nothing}), are the instruments' acts outside the reversal, as the lay is. The back-in-time gate is exact between clicks, and runs across a click and every erasure interval with the presented face values as its boundary. Those face values are read from the GameBoard-labelled lines beside the click line, with the lay's acts crossed from their lines; the click line itself names no Node (\claimmark{theorem} for the step; the write \claimmark{assumption}). The whole run therefore goes back: $N$ intervals forward and $N$ back return every array of every family bit for bit, provided every number an act read at an interval's start is one the state after the interval still holds (\claimmark{theorem} where the writes change no level, by construction from Theorem~\ref{th:direction}). The implementation checks this as a test: it runs a world forward and back and compares every array at every interval. On the worlds of Section~\ref{sec:slits} it reports \textsc{match} over their whole runs; these are worlds whose write stays under one level (Section~\ref{sec:choice}) and whose detectors write nothing. The interval's reversal with a level-changing write is gated. The back-in-time check gives \textsc{match} across the faces, the region detectors' clicks and every erasure interval, with the presented face values as the boundary, and \textsc{miss} at a lay, the taking, the giving and the null window, by design, since the tool's crossing of a lay from its lines is not yet built (Theorem~\ref{th:direction}'s clause), on the three worlds (the two slits $128$, Bell $98$, the GHZ $58$ intervals) (\claimmark{computed}, \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the inverse; the objects]{algebra}\cite[how to run a world]{engine}\cite[the Node knows no family]{decisions}. The condition rules out one read: a coefficient read from a Node's own state across a step cannot be recovered at the step back. So a record's rotation sense is never read at a Node, and the holder of the sign is read by the record's shape, a plane or a line (Section~\ref{sec:sign}) \cite[the interval]{algebra}\cite[the board goes back in time, as a gate; there is no sense]{decisions}.

\subsection{What the acts can form}\label{sec:acts}

Rule3's line is one act, and declared coefficients make four acts of it: (a) the read, the line with its six arrivals; (b) the one-Node step, the line with the arrivals declared $0$, which rotates a level by Chebyshev's recurrence; (c) the division, the line with $R = S = 0$, Euclid's division with the remainder kept; and (d) the load, a declared integer in the numerator, which is how a count enters a level \cite[the four acts]{algebra}.

\begin{theorem}[what the acts can form]\label{th:acts}
Every composition of the four acts is a piecewise-linear function of the levels with integer slopes: a sum of linear forms and of floors of linear forms, composed. No such function is quadratic in the levels.
\end{theorem}

\noindent (Proved in S.48.) So every product of two levels is a reading and never a write, and the three readings of Section~\ref{sec:interval} are the only products the law takes. A line enters the law only if it passes three tests. It must be generic (one primitive with declared integers, no family's name), vector (an act on the whole state, with no root and no floating-point number) and local (its own record and the six neighbours, with nothing kept at a Node beyond the law's numbers). A line that needs more is a hypothesis under its own name. No act of the interval takes a square root. The integer square root appears only when a world is laid, as the fixed point of the division act $x \leftarrow (x + n \ddiv x) \ddiv 2$ \cite[the three tests of every rule]{algebra}\cite[the six verbs; the root leaves everywhere]{decisions}.

\subsection{The conserved form and the momentum flux}\label{sec:form}

The read coefficient $R_a(i \to j) = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ carries the Node's factor and the Link's. Where the paces differ, the Node's factor is not symmetric and the Link's is. So the conserved form carries the weight $1 / p_i^2$ on its Node terms and the Link's factor $q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ on its Link terms:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:form}
\boxed{E = \sum_i \frac{w\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - S_i\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i}{p_i^2} - 2\,\num\sum_i \sum_{j \sim i} \frac{q_{ij}^2}{\Gamma^2}\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[the conserved form]\label{th:form}
At constant paces for which a positive Node weight $d_i$ with $d_i R_a(i \to j) = d_j R_a(j \to i)$ on every Link exists, $E$ with the weights $d_i$ is exact under the line without the rounding. Under the law's read coefficient, the Node's pace squared times the Link's factor squared, $R_a(i \to j) = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ with $q_{ij} = q_{ji}$ and $p_i = p_0(c_i)^2 / \Gamma$ the composed Node factor, the weight $d_i = 1 / p_i^2$ cancels the Node's factor on both sides of every Link. So $E$ is exact for any static field and static tensions. Its Link term carries the Link's factor, so the current of Rule3's own conservation law through the Link $ij$ is $\num\,(q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2)\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j)$, which is the plain current $F_{ij}$ where no tension stands, $q_{ij} = \Gamma$. The form with the plain current holds there alone; with a tension it is not conserved (two witnesses, S.32).
\end{theorem}

\noindent (Proved in S.48.) With the rounding, each Node's remainder adds the term $(r - r')(\mathrm{next} - \mathrm{before}) / p_i^2$ to the walk of $E$ (\claimmark{derived}; S.5). $E$ is a diagnostic. Where the paces move, the total form $D = |\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}|^2 - \langle \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}\rangle$ changes exactly by $D(t + 1) - D(t) = \langle \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, (\bM(t) - \bM(t + 1))\,\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}\rangle$. This change is $0$ at fixed uniform paces, and negative where a well deepens under a standing record (\claimmark{theorem}, by substitution; \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the conserved form]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The momentum flux is the wave's own stress.} The momentum density along the axis $a$ at the Node $i$ is the current per axis, $P_a(i) = (F_{i,i-a} - F_{i,i+a}) / \num$. At constant paces, the line without its division gives the identity below exactly. The integer step adds the remainder term $\delta_i\,\Delta_a(\mathrm{now})_i - \mathrm{now}_i\,\Delta_a(\delta)_i$, with $\delta = r - r'$ and $\Delta_a$ the centred difference, which the implementation's test includes (\claimmark{theorem} for the line, \fence{GameBoard}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum}
\begin{aligned}
w\,[P_a(t + 1) - P_a(t)] &= \sum_b R_b\,[G_{ab}(i) - G_{ab}(i - b)],\\
G_{ab}(i) &= \mathrm{now}_i\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a+b} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a+b}) - \mathrm{now}_{i+b}\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $G_{ab}(i)$ the stress reading on the $b$-Link $(i, i + b)$, from the levels now alone. The proof is in S.48. The tension on the axis $a$ at a Node is the part a field reads into the Link's pace, and the source of a field's axis part. It is the momentum flux averaged over the Node's two $a$-Links,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tension}
\begin{aligned}
T_{aa}(i) &= -\num\,\bigl(G_{aa}(i) + G_{aa}(i - a)\bigr) \ddiv 2,\\
G_{aa}(i) &= \mathrm{now}_i\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+2a} - \mathrm{now}_i) - \mathrm{now}_{i+a}\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and each Node sources its own part, $-\num\,h_a(i)$ with $h_a(i) = \mathrm{now}_{i-a}\,\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_i^2$, read from its two neighbours along the axis at the interval's start. The Link's tension is the parts of its two ends: one number per Link, read the same from both ends by the pace at the next interval. A plane wave of amplitude $b$ at the wave number $k$ has $G_{ab} = -2\,b^2\sin k_a \sin k_b$, so the flux along the wave, $T_{aa} = -\num\,G_{aa} \ge 0$, is positive, as a wave's pressure is (S.7). A packet of count $c$ and velocity $\mathbf v$ has $2c\,v_a v_b$, the two Links' currents summed, which is the stress of dust (S.7 for the plane wave). With the sign as written, a wave's stress deepens the content around it, so light gravitates by its pressure too. The off-diagonal parts and the vector part, which Rule3 never reads, are not written \cite[the tension; the final unification]{algebra}\cite[the tensor part is the record's own stress]{decisions}.

\subsection{The count is the record's share}\label{sec:share}

A family's count of quanta is a reading of its record and no line of its own. At every Node the record's share is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:share}
\boxed{e_i = \Bigl[w\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - S_i\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i - \mathrm{now}_i \sum_{j \sim i} R_{ij}\,\mathrm{before}_j\Bigr] \ddiv (2p_i^2)},
\end{equation}
the conserved form's term at the Node with its weights (Theorem~\ref{th:form}), the Node's $1 / p_i^2$ and each Link's $q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ inside $R_{ij}$, read in quanta over $\Wc$ as the nearest whole number. At the vacuum's paces it is exactly $3\,\den\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - \num\,\mathrm{now}_i\,S_6(\mathrm{before})_i$, with $S_6$ the six-neighbour sum. Read with the vacuum's coefficients instead of the Node's own paces, the same expression is the vacuum's share, the unit of the tables of Section~\ref{sec:bodies}. The implementation reads it so. Over one step of Rule3 the share's rational value, before its floor, changes by exactly the six currents into the Node, each weighted by its Link's factor, plus Rule3's own rounding (\claimmark{theorem} for the rational identity, \fence{GameBoard}; S.5). The integer share of Eq.~\eqref{eq:share} adds the reader's own floor, a residual strictly between $-1$ and $1$ per Node and level pair, distinct from Rule3's remainder:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:share-identity}
\begin{aligned}
e_i(\mathrm{next}, \mathrm{now}) - e_i(\mathrm{now}, \mathrm{before}) &= \sum_{j \sim i} \frac{q_{ij}^2}{\Gamma^2}\,F_{ij} - \frac{(\mathrm{next}_i - \mathrm{before}_i)(r'_i - r_i)}{2p_i^2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $F_{ij}$ at the levels the step started from, and $r$, $r'$ Rule3's remainder before and after the step. So over any region the total share changes only by the weighted currents through the region's boundary, the remainder terms and the readers' floors (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the count is the record's share; the share's change is the currents]{algebra}. A formulation that moved whole quanta by a division of the current would put numbers at every Node that no act of Rule3 reads back. The law does not carry it (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[the count's line and the sense's line leave the law]{decisions}. Where the paces move, the share drifts with no current crossing any Link, Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift}; this is a diagnostic \cite[the paces' own change moves the share with no current]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The signed integers.} The levels are signed integers. The currents are signed by their direction and the Wronskian by its sense, and a click is one whole quantum, never negative. The total share of a closed or periodic GameBoard is non-negative for $|\num| \le \den$, with equality for a static uniform level of light and, on an even periodic board, for its checkerboard (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}; S.6). One Node's share can be negative at a lay's edge, and a region's by at most half a level-square per boundary Port. Over the whole passage of one forward packet, the inflow accumulated at a region's front equals the region's final share plus what left through its back, and each of these is bounded below by S.6 (Eq.~\eqref{eq:share-identity} summed over the window). So the inflow is non-negative up to the rounding, and the credit by the shares is well defined. Where a window is cut inside a passage, the instrument credits $\max(s_R, 0)$ (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}) \cite[the law's integers are signed]{decisions}.

\section{Measurement: the click as a meeting}\label{sec:click}

The click is where the method meets the world it computes. Everything Universe24 computes is read through clicks and through nothing else, and the paper's supporting findings are the places where those clicks stand close to nature's. In the law's one line, a detector drives Rule3 from outside the board, as a face and a start do. It presents one value at one Port of the click's Node, in the place of what the Node would see from the board, and lays whole quanta there; the Node steps Rule3 as always and knows neither. A click is this act: one whole quantum of one record is taken, Rule3 writes the hole, and whole quanta of others are given, the rotations summing at the Node. The conversion, the weak force, is the same act with the family changed on the out side; every other click is of its kind with the family kept. The shares that entered a detector over its window, with the remainder the books carry, are divided by one quantum $\Wc$; the quotient is the clicks and the remainder is kept. The engine writes a NodeState by Rule3, by the lay and by the face, and by nothing else; it writes the hole by the face value at the click's Port (the implementation of 2026-10-03; the three gate worlds' numbers hold bit for bit under it). The credit's window it rounds half up and drops its remainder. The carried remainder is the law's form, stated under its own name with its own blind on $N$; it is the top storey's ``how many'' and never its ``where''. The board's remainders cannot stand where the draw stands: a local carry per detector gives no shot noise, and Rule3's remainders used as pick numbers put every click of the two slits on two of twelve regions (a recorded check; Section~\ref{sec:open}). \subsection{Events, and no waves}\label{sec:nowaves} On the GameBoard there are events and amplitudes. An event is a level split by Rule3 to the six neighbours. On the board, the record that gave is erased from the click's Node outward at the causal bound, one Link distance per interval, inside the click's light cone; beyond the front its levels remain, an empty wave that still sources the holders it writes to, until the front arrives (Sections~\ref{sec:price}, \ref{sec:nothing}) (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[no waves on the event board]{algebra}\cite[no waves on the event board]{decisions}.

\subsection{The click is the meeting of the future with the past}\label{sec:meeting}

\begin{assumption}[the click is a meeting, the law and not a hypothesis]\label{as:meeting}

\subsection{Stability}\label{sec:stable}

Let a body of $M$ quanta have the record $\phi$ over its region, with $\sum\phi_i^2 = 1$ and the counts $c_i = M\phi_i^2$. Let every field $r$ it sources be a hollow ($\sigma_r = +1$) or a hill ($\sigma_r = -1$), with the static kernel $\mathbf G_r$ of its line and the divisor $E_r$. The well at a Node is then $\Phi_i = \sum_r \sigma_r (M / E_r) \sum_j G_{r,ij}\,\phi_j^2$. To first order in $\Phi / \Gamma$ the well raises the one-Node rotation $2\cos\omega$ by $4g\Phi / \Gamma$, where $g$ is the gap of Section~\ref{sec:exact}. The body's fixed point is then a critical point on the sphere of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:functional}
F[\phi] = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_{\text{Links}} (\phi_i - \phi_j)^2 - \frac{2g}{\Gamma}\sum_r \sigma_r\,\frac{M}{E_r}\sum_{ij}\phi_i^2\,G_{r,ij}\,\phi_j^2,
\end{equation}
the band's pressure less the wells' energy, each well counted once because the record is its own source (Hartree's form). The record is the top mode of Rule3's symmetric form at the paces, and $dF / d\phi = 2\lambda\phi$ on the sphere is the fixed point's own equation, with $2\cos\omega_b = 2\cos\omega_0 - \lambda$ (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}; S.13). Let $\phi_R(x) = R^{-d / 2}\phi(x / R)$ be the body dilated, with $d$ the GameBoard's dimension. The pressure is then $K / R^2$, and each field's well energy is $W_r / R^{s_r}$, where $s_r$ is the field's scaling power at the body's size: $s_r = d$ beyond the field's reach, and $s_r = d - 2$ within it or for the massless field \cite[the functional and the dilation]{algebra}.

\begin{theorem}[the stable body, under the dilation and to first order in the well]\label{th:stable}
At a fixed point of the binding row of width $R$, the virial $2K = \sum_r \sigma_r s_r W_r$ holds, and the second variation of $F$ along the dilation is $R^2 F''(R) = \sum_r \sigma_r s_r (2 - s_r)\,W_r$. A body is stable only where this sum is positive. It is a minimum of $F$ where, in addition, the Hessian on the modes orthogonal to the dilation and the translations is positive.
\end{theorem}

\noindent (Proved in S.48, by Derrick's scaling \cite{derrick} and Pohozaev's identity \cite{pohozaev} on the GameBoard.) The paper gives the direction and not the whole, so that others can take the method, with this set of rules or another, and continue.
