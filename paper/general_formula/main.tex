\begin{abstract}
We present one method of computing physics: a GameBoard of Nodes in whole numbers, read through clicks, on which the classical wave equation, the quantum of action and the metric of gravity are three readings of one line, with nothing continuous beneath; every formula below is an example of its yield, Section 10.3 listing what it does not yet give. Rule3 steps every Node from its present, past and six neighbours by one division, reversibly, conserving a quadratic form, covariant under the translations and the cube's 48. Measurement is the meeting of the record run forward with the one read backward. Schr\"odinger's equation is its slow limit, light's index in a well $e^{2U}$. Newton's gravitation is derived: Poisson's equation a field's rest, the pull a packet's fall, $G$ a reading. Of Einstein the weak field is derived in the clock's potential under one named assumption, the exponential metric in the lattice's frame, a Link clocked twice by its Node, $\beta = \gamma = 1$ exactly, a shadow $4.6$ percent above Schwarzschild's, and the relativistic part only as the gap closes; the structure is a scalar pace, not the tensor equations, and against Kepler's potential the computed pair departs by 25 to 33 percent, a gap below $10^{-18}$. The three experiments are derivations, not runs: the two-slit row is the line stepped on its world, and Bell's $S = 2\sqrt 2$ and GHZ's $M = -4$ follow from the line's linearity through the pair's root under one declared credit, every local credit giving at most $2$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The question.} The object of this paper is one method of computing physics: a GameBoard of Nodes stepped by one local, reversible rule in whole numbers, read only through clicks, the one measurement there is. On it the classical wave equation, the quantum of action and the metric of gravity are three readings of one line, Rule3: the line itself is the lattice wave equation, its count read in quanta is the click, and its paces read as a clock and a ruler are the metric, with nothing continuous beneath. The question is whether that method can give the clicks that nature shows, and with how little state, non-locality and randomness; the fifteen formulas and the three experiments below are examples of what the method yields, not its scope, and Section~\ref{sec:open} lists what it does not yet give. Of Newton the paper derives gravitation whole: Poisson's equation as a field's rest on the lattice, the inverse-square pull as a free packet's fall in the field's gradient at the band's inertia, the mass as the count, Kepler's orbits, and $G$ as a reading of the law's constants. Of Einstein it derives the weak field in the clock's potential, the redshift, the bending, Shapiro's delay, the perihelion and the geodetic precession, with $\gamma = \beta = 1$ exactly under one named assumption, a Link clocked twice by its reading Node's own clock, and the special-relativistic part only as the matter family's gap closes, the moving clock's factor reaching Lorentz's as the family's limiting speed reaches light's; the structure is a scalar pace and not the tensor field equations, so it departs from Einstein's at the second order and in the strong field, has no frame dragging and not the tensor's polarisations, and against the Newtonian potential the orbits measure the computed pair's departures are the band's gap, closing with it (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). It starts from one statement and never contradicts it (\claimmark{assumption}): the world is the meeting of the past with the future; a click is that meeting, the one measurement there is; and an observer sees only clicks (the words are defined at the end of this section). In that sense the model is the least computation we know of that gives the two slits and Bell's correlation under the declared credit; of nature's other clicks it gives the forms of Section~\ref{sec:forms} at the computed pair, with the findings of Section~\ref{sec:open} against them. A Node holds a record's present and its past, two integer levels, and the rule makes the record's future from the two and from the six neighbouring Nodes. The rule is covariant under the lattice's translations and under the symmetry group of the cube, the $24$ rotations and, with the reflections, the $48$ signed permutations of the three axes; no boost is among them (Section~\ref{sec:objects}). The lattice holds nothing but events that split: at every Node a level splits by the rule to the six neighbours. Run forward from an emitter, the split events are the future, every path at once; read backward from a realised click by the same rule, they are the past, and through two slits both pass both gaps. A click is where the two meet at a declared detector, and which meeting is realised is the one thing the model does not determine, the one non-local element of the description (Section~\ref{sec:meeting}). What appears as a wave is the future, what appears as a particle is the present, the click, and the track is the past (Section~\ref{sec:nowaves}). Least is meant in three named counts, the state per Node, the non-local elements and the random draws, among the models we know of; no theorem of minimality is claimed, and why nature is so we do not claim to know.

\paragraph{The rule in one line.} Everything in the paper follows from one line of integer arithmetic, Rule3, together with one write and three readings. At every Node, in every interval and for every family of physical values \cite[the line; the booking; the primitives]{algebra}:
{\small\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
&\text{Rule3:} && w\,\anext + r' = \textstyle\sum_{a \in \{x, y, z\}} R_a\,(\mathrm{arr}_{+a} + \mathrm{arr}_{-a}) + S\,\anow - w\,\abefore + r, \quad 0 \le r' < w,\\
&&& w = 6\,\den\,\Gamma^2, \quad R_a = 2\,\num\,p_a^2,\\
&&& S = 12\,\den\,\Gamma^2 - 12\,(\den - \num)\,p_0^2 - 4\,\num\,(p_x^2 + p_y^2 + p_z^2);\\
&\text{the one write:} && \text{a held part at a Node gains } \Bigl(\textstyle\sum_f w_f\,q_f(\text{Node}) + r\Bigr) \ddiv E, \text{ the remainder } r \text{ kept there};\\
&\text{the readings:} && D_i = \mathrm{now}_i^2 - \mathrm{next}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i, \qquad W_i = \mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{before}} - \mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{before}},\\
&&& F_{ij} = \num\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j).
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}}
In the first line $\anow$, $\abefore$ and $\anext$ are the family's level at the Node now, one interval before and one interval later: the present, the past and the future. $\mathrm{arr}_{\pm a}$ is the arrival through the Port $\pm a$, the neighbour's level. $r$ and $r'$ are the remainder kept at the Node before and after the step, $\Gamma$ the Node clock. $[\num, \den]$ is the family's pair, and $\cos\omega_0 = \num / \den$ is its rotation per interval at wave number zero. $p_0$ is the Node's clock pace and $p_a$ its pace along the axis $a$; both are the Node clock lowered by the field levels the family reads there (Section~\ref{sec:paces}). $\ddiv$ is the floor division. The second line is the only way a field's level is ever written: the sum over the sourcing families $f$ of each family's own quantity $q_f$ at the Node, weighted by $w_f$, plus the part's one remainder, divided by the part's wall $E$. The third line is what is read from a record and never written: its form $D_i$, which sources the fields; its Wronskian $W_i$, its charge; and its current $F_{ij}$ into the Node $i$ from its neighbour $j$, which a detector reads through its boundary in quanta of $\Wc = 3\,\den\,T$, with $T$ the quantum's norm, the normalisation of the quadratic form.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Reversible cellular automata and digital physics.} The idea that the physical world is a discrete computation goes back to Zuse \cite{zuse}; it was developed as reversible cellular automata by Fredkin, Toffoli and Margolus \cite{fredkin}\cite{margolus}\cite{toffoli}, as a programme by Wolfram \cite{wolfram}, and as a deterministic foundation beneath quantum theory by 't Hooft \cite{thooft2013}\cite{thooft}. Quantum cellular automata put a unitary step on a lattice, from the lattice Weyl, Dirac and Maxwell equations \cite{bialynicki} and quantum lattice gases \cite{meyer} to the structure theorems of the reviews \cite{arrighi}\cite{farrelly} and the derivation of Weyl's and Dirac's equations from an automaton's symmetries \cite{dariano}; the present rule's step is an integer recurrence on two levels, not a unitary on amplitudes. Rule3 is a second-order cellular automaton in the sense of Toffoli and Margolus, $\anext = f(\anow) - \abefore$ \cite{toffoli}, with an integer alphabet, one Euclidean division whose remainder stays at the Node, and the automaton's quadratic forms read as physics. Feynman noted that a local classical computer reproduces no correlations beyond Bell's bound \cite{feynman}; the model agrees, and places the excess in the declared credit of the click rather than in the lattice (Section~\ref{sec:bell-blind}).

\paragraph{Time-symmetric accounts of measurement.} Wheeler and Feynman described radiation as an interaction between the source and the absorber, with advanced and retarded fields together \cite{wheeler}; Cramer's transactional interpretation has an offer wave forward and a confirmation wave backward, meeting at the absorber \cite{cramer}; the two-state vector formalism evolves one state forward from the preparation and one backward from the outcome \cite{abl}\cite{vaidman}; Price argued that a time-symmetric account is what Bell's theorem leaves open \cite{price}, and Wharton and Argaman showed that every locally mediated reformulation of quantum theory must let some mediating parameters depend on measurement settings in their future \cite{wharton}. The model belongs to this class, the backward reading from a realised click depending on the detector's setting and nothing else; it differs in that both directions are one integer line, exact and reversible to the bit, that their product is a reading and not a wave, and that the choice among the meetings is named as the one non-local element; the record is an ontic state in the terms of \cite{harrigan}.

\paragraph{Bell's theorem and local models.} Bell's inequality bounds every local account of a pair's correlations \cite{bell}, in the form that experiments test \cite{chsh}, and a local model, a joint distribution and the inequalities are one condition \cite{fine}. The model's local credits give $S = \sqrt 2$ and $S = 2$, as they must (Section~\ref{sec:bell-blind}); its credit by the contrast reaches the quantum correlation only by discarding pairs, Pearle's road \cite{pearle}, below the efficiency bounds of Eberhard and of Garg and Mermin \cite{eberhard}\cite{gargmermin}, which the loophole-free tests of 2015 close for nature \cite{hensen}\cite{giustina}\cite{shalm}.

\paragraph{Discrete gravity and its tests.} Regge calculus \cite{regge} and causal sets \cite{bombelli} discretise the geometry of space-time itself; here the lattice is never curved, and the weak-field forms are read from the coefficients (Section~\ref{sec:forms}). In the parametrised post-Newtonian framework \cite{will} the one assumption gives $\gamma = \beta = 1$ exactly in the model's own potential, with the matter band's factors against the potential the tests read closing with its gap. A lattice breaks Lorentz invariance at order one unless its terms are tuned \cite{collins}, and the bounds are reviewed in \cite{mattingly}; the paper's answer is the closing gap of Section~\ref{sec:forms}.

\section{The model}\label{sec:model}

This section states the model: the lattice, the families, the rule, the paces through which one family acts on another, the one write, the readings and the order of acts in an interval \cite[the GameBoard and the families; Rule3; the primitives]{algebra}.

\subsection{The GameBoard}\label{sec:objects}

The GameBoard is a box of $N_x \times N_y \times N_z$ Nodes. Two Nodes that differ by one step along one axis are joined by a Link, and each Node has six Links, one through each of its six Ports, $\pm x$, $\pm y$ and $\pm z$ . Each axis is declared periodic, open or folded. Beyond an open face no Node lies and a level there is read as $0$; a folded axis has one layer, and its two Ports return the Node itself. An inner face is a plane of Nodes declared closed except for declared gaps, which is how a wall with slits is made. A face may also be declared receding (\claimmark{assumption}): the box then grows by a layer of zeros whenever a nonzero level reaches the Node next to the face, so no event ever meets the face, and a run on such a box is a run on the unbounded lattice. The box grows and nothing stretches, so a receding face gives no cosmological redshift \cite[the objects; the unbounded board]{algebra}\cite[the receding face]{decisions}.

\paragraph{The group and its action.} The symmetry group of the cube has $48$ elements, the $24$ rotations and, with the reflections, the signed permutations of the three axes, $O_h$; with the translations it is the space group of the simple cubic lattice. \subsection{Families and records}\label{sec:records} A family is declared by two keys: its pair $[\num, \den]$ and its shape. \subsection{Rule3}\label{sec:line} At every Node, in every interval, every family steps by the first line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}. The six arrivals through the Ports, each weighted by its axis's coefficient $R_a$, the Node's own level times $S$, minus $w$ times the level before, plus the remainder the Node kept, are summed as one whole number and divided once by the wall $w$; the quotient is the next level, and the remainder, $0 \le r' < w$, stays at the Node and never travels. Nothing is rounded away, and the step back is the same division, which is why every run is exact and reversible to the bit (Theorem~\ref{th:direction}). The name comes from the three axes: the line has one coefficient per direction serving that direction's two Ports, the six Ports and the wall's factor $6$ are the three directions twice, and for light the next level is the sum of the six arrivals over $3$, minus the level before \cite[the line]{algebra}.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:vacuum}
\anext + \abefore = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a (\mathrm{arr}_{+a} + \mathrm{arr}_{-a}),
\end{equation}
whose rotation at the wave vector $\mathbf k$ is $\cos\omega = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a \cos k_a$. \subsection{The paces: how a field acts on a record}\label{sec:paces}

A field acts on a family by changing the family's paces at a Node, the coefficients of Rule3 there:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paces}
\begin{aligned}
p_0 = \Gamma\,(1 - 1 / \Gamma)^{c}, \qquad p_i = \frac{p_0(c_i)^2}{\Gamma}, \qquad p_a(i, j) = \frac{p_i\,q_{ij}}{\Gamma}, \quad q_{ij} = \Gamma - t_a(i, j), \\ c = \sum_{\text{reads}} \text{weight} \times \text{the read family's time level},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $c_i$ is the content the family reads at the Node, the sum over its reads of the weight times the read field's level; the clock $p_0$ composes, each unit of the level slowing it by the same factor $1 - 1 / \Gamma$, to the unit; the Node's pace $p_i$ is the clock twice, $p_0^2 / \Gamma$, so that the ruler goes with the clock; $t_a(i, j)$ is the Link's tension, one number per Link from its two ends' parts (Section~\ref{sec:form}), read into the Link's factor $q_{ij}$ the same from both ends; and the Link's pace is the product, $\Gamma - 2c_i - t_a$ to first order \cite[every row reads the content; the clock is the Node's, the tension is the Link's]{algebra}. A positive weight makes a hollow, an attraction; a negative weight makes a hill. The pace is the clock's slowing: a record at a Node of pace $p$ rotates and moves as at $\Gamma$, with its intervals $p / \Gamma$ as long. Every family reads every holder of the content at the weight $1$, the fields among the readers and a holder of the content its own level among it (the law's line, built; Section~\ref{sec:engine}), so $x = (L_b + L_g) / \Gamma$ with $L_b$ the binding field's level and $L_g$ gravity's; a family of dimension $2$ also reads the holder of the sign plainly, and light, the holder's own record, reads the holders of the content as every family does and not the holder of the sign; and a family sources what it reads at the weight it reads with, so every pair of families that read each other is reciprocal \cite[the paces; the reads are the held row's pair's]{algebra}.

\cos\omega = 1 - \Bigl(1 - \frac{\num}{\den}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{p_0}{\Gamma}\Bigr)^2 - \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_a \Bigl(\frac{p_a}{\Gamma}\Bigr)^2 (1 - \cos k_a),

\subsection{The write, the readings and the interval}\label{sec:interval}

\section{Exact properties}\label{sec:theorems}

This section proves what holds exactly on every lattice: the reversibility of the rule, the kind of function its acts can form, the conserved form with its current and its momentum flux, and the count as a reading of the record.

\subsection{Reversibility}\label{sec:direction}

Let $\sigma$ be the direction, $+1$ forward and $-1$ backward; let $\ddiv$ and $\dmod$ be the floor division and its remainder in $[0, w)$; and let $\rho$ be the remainder carried in. With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direction}
u = \sigma\Bigl(\sum_a R_a\,\mathrm{arr}_a + S\,x - w\,y\Bigr) + \rho, \qquad z = \sigma\,(u \ddiv w), \qquad \rho' = u \dmod w,
\end{equation}
the forward call with $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \abefore, r)$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\anext, r')$, and the backward call with $(x, y, \rho) = (\anow, \anext, r')$ gives $(z, \rho') = (\abefore, r)$.

\begin{theorem}[the direction]\label{th:direction}
The backward call returns $(\abefore, r)$ bit for bit; the reversibility of every step needs no second function.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Forward, $\sigma = 1$, the line is the definition of $\ddiv$ and $\dmod$. Backward, $\sigma = -1$: write $Y$ for the right side's first two terms, $\sum_a R_a\,\mathrm{arr}_a + S\,\anow$. Then $u = w\,\anext - Y + r'$ and $z = -\lfloor u / w \rfloor = \lceil (Y - w\,\anext - r') / w \rceil$. From the forward line, $Y - w\,\anext - r' = w\,\abefore - r$ with $0 \le r < w$, whose ceiling divided by $w$ is $\abefore$ exactly; and $\rho' = u - w\lfloor u / w \rfloor = w\,\abefore - (Y - w\,\anext - r') = r$. \cite[the direction]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

The whole run therefore goes back: $N$ intervals forward and $N$ back return every array of every family bit for bit, provided every number an act read at an interval's start is one the state after the interval still holds (\claimmark{theorem}, by construction from Theorem~\ref{th:direction}). The implementation checks this as a gate, running a world forward and back and comparing every array at every interval, \textsc{match} or the first interval and Node that differ; on the worlds of Section~\ref{sec:slits} it reports \textsc{match} over their whole runs, every record of every family bit for bit, the writes' remainders among them (\claimmark{computed}, a run of the implementation; \fence{GameBoard}, a diagnostic and no measurement) \cite[the inverse; the objects]{algebra}\cite[how to run a world]{engine}\cite[the Node knows no family]{decisions}. The condition rules out one kind of read: a coefficient read from a Node's own state across a step cannot be recovered at the step back. This is why a record's rotation sense is never read at a Node, and why the holder of the sign is read according to the record's shape, a plane or a line, and not according to a sense (Section~\ref{sec:sign}) \cite[the interval]{algebra}\cite[the board goes back in time, as a gate; there is no sense]{decisions}.

\subsection{What the acts can form}\label{sec:acts}

Rule3's line is one act, and declared coefficients make it four: (a) the read, the line with its six arrivals; (b) the one-Node step, the line with the arrivals declared $0$, which rotates a level by Chebyshev's recurrence; (c) the division, the line with $R = S = 0$, Euclid's division with the remainder kept; and (d) the load, a declared integer in the numerator, which is how a count enters a level \cite[the four acts]{algebra}.

\begin{theorem}[what the acts can form]\label{th:acts}
Every composition of the four acts is a piecewise-linear function of the levels with integer slopes: a sum of linear forms and of floors of linear forms, composed. No such function is quadratic in the levels.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each act is a linear form of the levels or the floor of one; a floor of a linear form is piecewise linear with integer slopes; sums and compositions of such functions are of the same kind. The second difference of such a function in any one level is $0$ almost everywhere, while the second difference of a product $\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j$ in the pair $(\mathrm{now}_i, \mathrm{before}_j)$ is $1$. So nothing bilinear comes from the acts alone.
\end{proof}

So every product of two levels is a reading and never a write, and the three readings of Section~\ref{sec:interval} are the only products the law takes. A line enters the law only if it passes three tests: it is generic (one primitive with declared integers, no family's name), vector (an act on the whole state, with no root and no floating-point number) and local (its own record and the six neighbours, nothing kept at a Node beyond the law's numbers). A line that needs more is a hypothesis under its own name. No act of the interval takes a square root; the integer square root appears only when a world is laid, as the fixed point of the division act $x \leftarrow (x + n \ddiv x) \ddiv 2$ \cite[the three tests of every rule]{algebra}\cite[the six verbs; the root leaves everywhere]{decisions}.

\subsection{The conserved form and the momentum flux}\label{sec:form}

The read coefficient $R_a(i \to j) = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ carries the Node's factor and the Link's; the Node's factor is not symmetric where the paces differ and the Link's is, so the conserved form carries the weight $1 / p_i^2$ on its Node terms and the Link's factor $q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ on its Link terms:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:form}
E = \sum_i \frac{w\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - S_i\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i}{p_i^2} - 2\,\num\sum_i \sum_{j \sim i} \frac{q_{ij}^2}{\Gamma^2}\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j .
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[the conserved form]\label{th:form}
At constant paces for which a positive Node weight $d_i$ with $d_i R_a(i \to j) = d_j R_a(j \to i)$ on every Link exists, $E$ with the weights $d_i$ is exact under the line without the rounding. Under the law's read coefficient, the Node's pace squared times the Link's factor squared, $R_a(i \to j) = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ with $q_{ij} = q_{ji}$ and $p_i = p_0(c_i)^2 / \Gamma$ the composed Node factor, the weight $d_i = 1 / p_i^2$ cancels the Node's factor on both sides of every Link, so $E$ is exact for any field and any tensions. With the rounding each Node's remainder adds at most one unit of the level per interval to the walk of $E$. Its Link term carries the Link's factor, so the current of Rule3's own conservation law through the Link $ij$ is $\num\,(q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2)\,(\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_j - \mathrm{before}_i\,\mathrm{now}_j)$, the plain current $F_{ij}$ where no tension stands, $q_{ij} = \Gamma$; the form with the plain current holds there alone, and with a tension it is not conserved (two witnesses, S.32).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Without the rounding the line is $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} + \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} = \bM\,\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}$ with $\bM$ the matrix whose diagonal is $S_i / w$ and whose entries on the six Links are $R / w$. Let $\bD$ be the diagonal matrix of the weights $1 / p_i^2$; then $\bD\bM$ is symmetric iff $d_i M_{ij} = d_j M_{ji}$ on every Link, $d_i R_a(i \to j) = d_j R_a(j \to i)$: with $R_a(i \to j) = 2\,\num\,p_i^2 q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2$ the weight $1 / p_i^2$ cancels the Node's factor on both sides of every Link and the Link's factor $q_{ij}^2$ is the same from both ends, so $\bD\bM$ is symmetric for any field and any tensions. For a symmetric $\bK = \bD\bM$ the quantity $Q(\mathbf x, \mathbf y) = \mathbf x \cdot \bD\mathbf x + \mathbf y \cdot \bD\mathbf y - \mathbf x \cdot \bK\mathbf y$ satisfies $Q(\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}) = Q(\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}})$: with $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} = \bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}$, $\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} \cdot \bD\mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}} \cdot \bK\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}} = \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} \cdot \bD\mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} - \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}} \cdot \bD\bM\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}$, which is the other side. Multiplied by $w$ this is $E$ up to the Link term's sign convention \cite[the conserved form]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

$E$ is a diagnostic. Where the paces move, the total form $D = |\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}|^2 - \langle \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, \mathbf a_{\mathrm{before}}\rangle$ changes exactly by $D(t + 1) - D(t) = \langle \mathbf a_{\mathrm{next}}, (\bM(t) - \bM(t + 1))\,\mathbf a_{\mathrm{now}}\rangle$, which is $0$ at fixed uniform paces and negative where a well deepens under a standing record (\claimmark{theorem}, by substitution; \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the conserved form]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The momentum flux is the wave's own stress.} The momentum density along the axis $a$ at the Node $i$ is the current per axis, $P_a(i) = (F_{i,i+a} - F_{i,i-a}) / \num$, and the line without its division gives exactly, at constant paces, the identity below; the integer step adds the remainder term $\delta_i\,\Delta_a(\mathrm{now})_i - \mathrm{now}_i\,\Delta_a(\delta)_i$ with $\delta = r - r'$ and $\Delta_a$ the centred difference, which the implementation's test includes (\claimmark{theorem} for the line, \fence{GameBoard}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentum}
\begin{aligned}
w\,[P_a(t + 1) - P_a(t)] &= \sum_b R_b\,[G_{ab}(i - b) - G_{ab}(i)],\\
G_{ab}(i) &= \mathrm{now}_i\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a+b} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a+b}) - \mathrm{now}_{i+b}\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $G_{ab}(i)$ the stress reading on the $b$-Link $(i, i + b)$, the levels now alone. The proof substitutes the line into $w\,P_a(t + 1)$: the $S$ term cancels, the $w$ term is $w\,P_a(t)$ and the $R$ term is the difference of the fluxes. The tension on the axis $a$ at a Node, the part a field reads into the Link's pace and the source of a field's axis part, is the momentum flux averaged over the Node's two $a$-Links,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tension}
\begin{aligned}
T_{aa}(i) &= -\num\,\bigl(G_{aa}(i) + G_{aa}(i - a)\bigr) \ddiv 2,\\
G_{aa}(i) &= \mathrm{now}_i\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+2a} - \mathrm{now}_i) - \mathrm{now}_{i+a}\,(\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_{i-a}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and each Node sources its own part, $-\num\,h_a(i)$ with $h_a(i) = \mathrm{now}_{i-a}\,\mathrm{now}_{i+a} - \mathrm{now}_i^2$, read from its two neighbours along the axis at the interval's start; the Link's tension is its two ends' parts, one number per Link read the same from both ends by the pace at the next interval. A plane wave of amplitude $b$ at the wave number $k$ has $G_{ab} = -2\,b^2\sin k_a \sin k_b$, so the flux along the wave is positive, as a wave's pressure is (S.7); a packet of count $c$ and velocity $\mathbf v$ has $c\,v_a v_b$, the stress of dust. With the sign as written a wave's stress deepens the content around it, so light gravitates by its pressure too. The off-diagonal parts and the vector part, which Rule3 never reads, are not written \cite[the tension; the final unification]{algebra}\cite[the tensor part is the record's own stress]{decisions}.

\subsection{The count is the record's share}\label{sec:share}

A family's count of quanta is a reading of its record and no line of its own. At every Node the record's share is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:share}
e_i = 3\,\bigl[w\,(\mathrm{now}_i^2 + \mathrm{before}_i^2) - S_i\,\mathrm{now}_i\,\mathrm{before}_i\bigr] \ddiv \Bigl(2\sum_a p_a^2\Bigr) - \num\,\mathrm{now}_i\,S_6(\mathrm{before})_i,
\end{equation}
the conserved form's Node term over the mean of the three Link paces squared, less the plain Link term, read in quanta over $\Wc$ as the nearest whole number. Read with the vacuum's coefficients instead of the Node's own paces, the same expression is the vacuum's share, the unit of the tables of Section~\ref{sec:bodies}. Nothing is kept at a Node beside the record and the write's remainder: no count, no remainder of a count and no sense. Over one step of Rule3 the share's rational value, the weighted Node term before its floor, changes by exactly the six currents into the Node, plus a term that vanishes where the three Link paces are equal, plus Rule3's own rounding (\claimmark{theorem} for the rational identity, \fence{GameBoard}); the integer share of Eq.~\eqref{eq:share} adds the reader's own floor, a residual strictly between $-1$ and $1$ per Node and level pair, distinct from Rule3's remainder:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:share-identity}
\begin{aligned}
e_i(\mathrm{next}, \mathrm{now}) - e_i(\mathrm{now}, \mathrm{before}) &= \sum_j F_{ij} + \num\,(\mathrm{next}_i - \mathrm{before}_i)\sum_a \frac{(3p_a^2 - P^2)\,\mathrm{arr}_a(\mathrm{now})_i}{P^2}\\
&\quad - \frac{3\,(\mathrm{next}_i - \mathrm{before}_i)(r'_i - r_i)}{2P^2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $P^2 = \sum_a p_a^2$, $\mathrm{arr}_a$ the two arrivals of the axis $a$ summed, and $r$, $r'$ Rule3's remainder before and after the step. The identity is derived in S.5 and checked by a test of the implementation up to one reader's floor per Node and level pair. So over any region whose three Link paces are equal, the total share changes only by the currents through the region's boundary, the remainder terms and the readers' floors (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[the count is the record's share; the share's change is the currents]{algebra}. A formulation that moves whole quanta between Nodes by a division of the current, and the sign by a division of the Wronskian's current, would put two more numbers at every Node that no act of Rule3 reads back; the law does not carry it (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[the count's line and the sense's line leave the law]{decisions}. Where the paces move the share drifts with no current crossing any Link, Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift}, a diagnostic \cite[the paces' own change moves the share with no current]{algebra}.

\paragraph{The signed integers.} The levels are signed integers, the currents are signed by their direction and the Wronskian by its sense, and a click is one whole quantum, never negative. The total share of a closed or periodic GameBoard is non-negative for $|\num| \le \den$, with equality for a static uniform level of light and, on an even periodic board, for its checkerboard (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}; S.6). One Node's share can be negative at a lay's edge, and a region's by at most half a level-square per boundary Port. Over the whole passage of one forward packet the inflow accumulated at a region is non-negative up to the rounding, since the lay's current is forward at every Link, so the credit by the shares is well defined; where a window is cut inside a passage the instrument credits $\max(s_R, 0)$ (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}) \cite[the law's integers are signed]{decisions}.

\section{Measurement: the click as a meeting}\label{sec:click}

The click is the meeting of the future with the past at a declared detector, and no act of the GameBoard. This section states what is on the lattice, the meeting, the detector, what a click leaves behind, the charge and light, and what locality costs \cite[the click; the reading]{algebra}.

\subsection{Events, and no waves}\label{sec:nowaves} On the GameBoard there are events and amplitudes. An event is a level split by Rule3 to the six neighbours; an amplitude is the level Rule3 produces at a Node, the sum of the split events there; and the record is the amplitudes over the GameBoard. In our world there are clicks, the detectors' reports, and nothing else. In the two slits the emitter's events pass both gaps, every path of the future at once; the fringes are where split events from the two gaps meet at the screen with the same sign. The past is made of the same split events: read backward from the realised click by the same line, Rule3 at $-1$, they form the past's tree, which also passes both gaps back to the emitter. What stays on the GameBoard, the amplitudes no click realised, is not in our world (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[no waves on the event board]{algebra}\cite[no waves on the event board]{decisions}.

\subsection{The click is the meeting of the future with the past}\label{sec:meeting}

\begin{assumption}[the click is a meeting, the law and not a hypothesis]\label{as:meeting}
A record runs on the GameBoard in both directions by the one line: forward from the emitter, the possibilities, the future; backward from the realised click, what happened, the past, Rule3 at $-1$. A click is where the two carry the same signal at a declared detector; its share is the product of the two directions at the region, the form $D = \mathrm{now}^2 - \mathrm{next} \times \mathrm{before}$, the now against the future times the past; which meeting is realised is the draw, the one non-local place. The detector only asks whether the future and the past met at it.
\end{assumption}

Computationally, by the linearity of the line, the backward reading from a realised click equals a sum over the forward records, so the meeting adds a reading of what a click is and no mechanism of choice; which meeting is realised stays the draw by the shares, the one non-local place.

In plain words (Fig.~\ref{fig:meeting}): a click happens when the past meets the future; the click is the now; the detector only asks whether the future and the past met at it; and a click is created only at their meeting. This is a line of the model and not a hypothesis \cite[the click is the meeting of the future with the past]{decisions}.

\paragraph{The click, algebraically.} Let $X$ be the state space: at every Node, for every part of every family, one integer level pair and the write remainders, and nothing else. The step $S_t$ is Rule3 over the interval with the paces read from the fields at its start; it is a bijection of $X$, exact by the division with its remainder; the line without its division is linear in the levels at fixed paces, the linearity the backward reading uses, while the integer map is not affine, the floor of $4 / 3$ of $1$ and of $2$ not adding to that of $3$; its forward orbit is the future and its inverse orbit the past. The readings $D$, $F$ and $W$ are bilinear forms on $X$, covariant under the symmetry group of Section~\ref{sec:objects} and never written. An instrument $R$ is a declared region with its front Ports and its coefficients, which define $Q_R$: for one line a quadratic form of the levels, the inflow through its front Ports accumulated over the window; for a pair, the window's sum of the squared cross-side sum over the parts, quadratic in the joint amplitude, the product of the two sides' levels, and so quartic in the levels. A click is the pair $(R, t)$, the credit of one whole quantum to the instrument $R$ at the interval $t$, drawn with the probability $s_R / \sum_{R'} s_{R'}$, where $s_R = Q_R(x_t) / \Wc$ is the instrument's form on the forward orbit over the quantum's measure of a current. In the meeting's words, the inverse orbit from the credited state coincides with the forward orbit on the realised path, and the share is the bilinear form's value at the coincidence. The click writes nothing to $X$: it is the one map in the model that is not a bijection, from the vector of shares to a unit vector, and it is a scalar under the group, so no click names a Node. Every observable is $Q_R / \Wc$ on the forward orbit or a ratio of two such. The window is the whole passage of a packet, or the credit floors at $0$ (Section~\ref{sec:share}); a Node whose three Link paces are $0$ stands in no instrument's front and is never clicked (\claimmark{assumption} for the definition, \claimmark{derived} for the precisions; \fence{clicks}) \cite[the click's algebraic definition]{decisions}.

\paragraph{One form for every click.} At the credit the parts of a record are read with the detector's coefficients, each part paired with the same part through the common origin, summed and squared; the share is that density, and the draw is by the shares. The two slits are the case with nothing to pair: light is one real line, and the share is the record's own inflow. Bell's gate is the case through the common origin: a pair born as one event is two trees with one root, and the backward reading from the first click passes the root and continues forward on the partner's tree (Section~\ref{sec:bell}). In the implementation the joint share is computed from the forward records alone, by the linearity of the rule; no backward Rule3 is stepped, and the gate of Section~\ref{sec:direction} shows that the backward reading exists to the bit (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}).

\paragraph{No duality.} What is called the particle as a wave is the future: the record run forward by Rule3 with every possibility, which has not yet met the past. What is called the particle as a particle is the present: the click, one whole quantum credited at one region in one interval. The particle's track is the past: the chain of realised clicks, and the same record read backward through them. There are not two things but one record read in two directions, and the point where the two readings coincide is what appears as a particle. After a click no wave vanishes and nothing collapses: the record goes on, the click is a credit, and the past has grown by one click (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[the wave is the future, the click is the present, the track is the past]{decisions}.

\subsection{The detector is a declared instrument}\label{sec:detector}

\begin{assumption}[the detector's declaration]\label{as:detector}
A detector is one connected region of Nodes declared in the file, never one Node, at least half the wavelength of the family it reports across the beam ($q / p$ Links for the wave $[p, q]$, $4$ at $\lambda = 8$); a narrower region, a single Node or a region in pieces is refused by name at load, and the depth along the beam is not gated. It is opaque: backed by a receding face or by the wall's own face, so that what enters it leaves the declared board and never returns, with no act at its Nodes and no log. Its click is its report, per interval and family: the net current into the region through its front boundary Ports, the Ports leading in from the declared board, summed in integers, with the region's name and the family and never a Node; one wall $\Wc = 3\,\den\,T$ of the inflow over a passage is one quantum, so a screen saw $N$ quanta where its regions' inflows sum to $N\,\Wc$. The credit: one quantum to one detector, drawn by the shares of the regions' inflows, read outside the GameBoard over every declared detector of the world, one credit per quantum and never two. The credit is the one non-local place of the description and no line of the law.
\end{assumption}

Why a region: a record confined to one Node contains every wave number at once, $\Delta x\,\Delta k \ge 1 / 2$ on the lattice as in nature, and the finest structure a family's amplitudes carry is half its wavelength; so no click names a position finer than a region, and every per-Node number is a diagnostic \cite[the click is the detector's report; no click names a Node]{algebra}\cite[no click names a Node]{decisions}.

What the model determines and what the instrument declares are separate. What produces a click is deterministic: the meeting, the current of the amplitudes through a region's boundary. What is not known is which of the possibilities becomes the click: when one possibility is spread over several detectors, the GameBoard gives the shares, no line of the law gives which detector fires, and the instrument draws. Exclusivity, one click per quantum, and Born's rule, the credit's chance equal to the intensity's share \cite{born}, are therefore declarations of the instrument and not findings of the model, as nature's detector efficiency and dead time are \cite[what produces a click is not known]{algebra}. The uncertainty relation is not a separate rule: it is the detector's three declarations, a region of at least half a wavelength, a credit per interval and one draw per quantum (\claimmark{assumption}) \cite[the uncertainty relation is not a rule of its own]{decisions}. What each declaration costs: a region of $n$ Nodes reads a fringe of period $\Lambda$ with the factor $\sin(\pi n / \Lambda) / (n\sin(\pi / \Lambda))$, $0.90$ for four rows on a fringe of sixteen; a frequency is a click rate over a window of $N$ intervals, read to $\Delta\omega \ge 2\pi / N$; and $N$ quanta drawn by the shares give each region $Np_i \pm \sqrt{Np_i(1 - p_i)}$, so a pattern is in the histogram and never in one click (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}; S.8). The credit by the shares is done by the reader of the output and never by the GameBoard \cite[the output]{engine}.

\subsection{The click ends nothing}\label{sec:nothing}

\begin{assumption}[the click ends nothing]\label{as:nothing}
The click is a detector's reading, local to its boundary; the record goes on after it and nothing ends anywhere. What a detector absorbed leaves the declared board through the face that backs it; the other possibilities go on splitting and leave through the receding faces, events that were not realised, in no click. The ending of a record at a click by the detector is decided against and is no hypothesis of the law.
\end{assumption}

This is the model's claim and not a deficiency: perhaps nature is local. No signal passes, since nothing at one detector changes the amplitudes at another. A measurement needs no collapse: the record never ends, the click is a reading, and the backward reading from the click writes nothing \cite[the click ends nothing]{decisions}. A bound body can act as an instrument in the same form: the future is light's forward record at its boundary, the past is the body raised by one quantum read backward to the source, and the meeting is the same frequency, the resonance of the body's rotation with the light's; emission is the same meeting reversed, and a body at rest and symmetric clicks nowhere (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}; Section~\ref{sec:bodyclicks}). The body's write back waits on the fixed point with the write \cite[a Node of the board is read only through a detector]{decisions}\cite[the rows against nature, (y)]{algebra}.

\subsection{The sign is the rotation sense}\label{sec:sign}

A family of quanta laid as a plane carries its record as two level pairs, $(\mathrm{re}, \mathrm{im})$, stepped by one call of Rule3 with one set of coefficients, each with its own remainder; a field's parts stay real. The charge density at a Node is the reading
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wronskian}
W_i = \mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{before}} - \mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{now}}\,\mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{before}},
\end{equation}
the Wronskian of the two pairs, which the line without its division conserves exactly with the conserved form's weights, under Theorem~\ref{th:form}'s condition on the paces (\claimmark{theorem}, since $\bD\bM$ is then symmetric; \fence{GameBoard}; S.9). The integer step adds to each part the remainder term $\epsilon = (r - r') / w$, $|\epsilon| < 1$, so the integer $W$ changes at a Node by $\epsilon_{\mathrm{re}}\,\mathrm{im}_{\mathrm{now}} - \epsilon_{\mathrm{im}}\,\mathrm{re}_{\mathrm{now}}$ per interval, not zero in general: at the vacuum's pace of $[2, 3]$ the parts $(1, 3)$ with zeros before step to $(1, 4)$ and $W$ from $0$ to $-1$ (\claimmark{computed}, \fence{GameBoard}; S.9); an exactly conserved integer charge is open (Section~\ref{sec:open}). Its local change is the Link current $\num\,(q_{ij}^2 / \Gamma^2)\,(\mathrm{im}_i\,\mathrm{re}_j - \mathrm{re}_i\,\mathrm{im}_j)$, and its total over the GameBoard is invariant under any change of the paces that keeps them uniform at each Node, since the bracket $\mathbf{im}_{\mathrm{now}}^{\top}\bM\,\mathbf{re}_{\mathrm{now}} - \mathbf{re}_{\mathrm{now}}^{\top}\bM\,\mathbf{im}_{\mathrm{now}}$ vanishes for every symmetric $\bM$ (\claimmark{theorem}, \fence{GameBoard}). A real record ($\mathrm{im} = 0$) has $W = 0$, so light, the real record of the holder of the sign, is neutral; a rotating record has $W$ of either sign by its sense \cite[the sign is the rotation sense; the sign's count is the record's own]{algebra}.

Who reads the holder of the sign is decided by the family's dimension and by no name (\claimmark{assumption}, \fence{GameBoard}): a family of dimension $2$ has a Wronskian and reads it, a family of dimension $1$ has $W = 0$ and does not, and a neutral body and a charged body of the same pair are two rows of the file with the dimensions $1$ and $2$. The holder of the sign does not read its own level, so light does not read it and a static charge does not bend light. A reader's own sense never enters its coefficients, since a coefficient read from a Node's own state would break the exact inverse (Section~\ref{sec:direction}); so the holder of the sign acts on its readers by the act its row declares: the plain read of its level into the paces, \texttt{pace}, under which like and unlike senses are not distinguished, or \texttt{rotation}, the holder's level over $\Gamma$ as the angle $\theta$ turning the two-part record in its own plane, $e^{-i\theta} z_{\mathrm{next}} + e^{i\theta} z_{\mathrm{before}} = (S z_{\mathrm{now}} + \sum_a R_a (z_{+a} + z_{-a})) / w$, three exact integer shears inverted bit for bit with $\theta$ read at now alone, under which like and unlike senses are told apart by the record's own sense; which sense moves apart is one bit the model does not fix, declared by nature's click that like charges repel, with the engine's convention, the opposite sign of $\theta$, absorbed by the declared bit, the shears' exact angle $2\arctan(L / 2\Gamma)$, $L / \Gamma$ at small angles, and the magnetic force follows as the Link's phase, Peierls' coupling (Section~\ref{sec:forms}) (\claimmark{assumption} for the declared bit, \fence{GameBoard}) \cite[there is no sense; every family has a dimension; the rotation round, built]{decisions}\cite[the sign holder rotates the two-part record; the rows against nature, (f)]{algebra}.

\subsection{Light is born by the write}\label{sec:light}

Light is the holder of the sign's own record: one record whose time part is a real level pair, stepped by Rule3 at the paces of its reads, so that light is bent by the content, and written by the one write from the Wronskian of the records that read it. A static rotating body writes a static level, Coulomb's field, and radiates nothing. A breathing or moving body writes a varying source at its Nodes, the field carries it away at one Link per interval, and those spreading events are the light (\claimmark{derived}, \fence{clicks}). Its count is the share of that record, read like every family's. A detector reads light's current through its boundary in quanta of $\Wc$, and the travelling events of the other fields, which in gravity's case nature calls a gravitational wave, enter a detector the same way. A body's quanta that leave it stay its family, and no quantum changes family at a crossing. A body reads the holder of the sign at its own Nodes, so a breathing body reads the light it writes and its pace answers, the radiation reaction, in the same loop \cite[light is born by the write]{algebra}\cite[light is born by the write]{decisions}.

\subsection{Stability}\label{sec:stable}

Let a body of $M$ quanta have the record $\phi$ over its region, $\sum\phi_i^2 = 1$, with the counts $c_i = M\phi_i^2$. F[\phi] = \frac{\num}{3\,\den}\sum_{\text{Links}} (\phi_i - \phi_j)^2 - \frac{2g}{\Gamma}\sum_r \sigma_r\,\frac{M}{E_r}\sum_{ij}\phi_i^2\,G_{r,ij}\,\phi_j^2,

\begin{theorem}[the stable body, under the dilation and to first order in the well]\label{th:stable}
At a fixed point of the binding row of width $R$ the virial $2K = \sum_r \sigma_r s_r W_r$ holds, and the second variation of $F$ along the dilation is $R^2 F''(R) = \sum_r \sigma_r s_r (2 - s_r)\,W_r$. A body is stable only where this sum is positive; it is a minimum of $F$ where further the Hessian on the modes orthogonal to the dilation and the translations is positive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$F(R) = K / R^2 - \sum_r \sigma_r W_r R^{-s_r}$; $F'(1) = 0$ is $-2K + \sum_r \sigma_r s_r W_r = 0$; $F''(1) = 6K - \sum_r \sigma_r s_r (s_r + 1) W_r = \sum_r \sigma_r (3s_r - s_r^2 - s_r)\,W_r$: Derrick's scaling \cite{derrick}, Pohozaev's identity \cite{pohozaev} on the GameBoard \cite[the functional and the dilation]{algebra}.
\end{proof}

The theorem is a statement about the functional of Eq.~\eqref{eq:functional}, to the first order in the well, along the one direction of the dilation, with the dilation taken as continuous; it is not a theorem of the integer step on the full lattice, and the Hessian beyond the dilation is open (Section~\ref{sec:open}).
