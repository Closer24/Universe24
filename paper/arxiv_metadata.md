# arXiv submissions

## Paper 1 (quant-ph, cross-list physics.comp-ph), file main.tex with figures

Title: A finite-integer causal-GameBoard testbed: which resources reproduce interference, causal collapse and Bell correlations on one GameBoard
Authors: Alon Gonen (ORCID 0009-0008-2701-4599)
License: CC BY 4.0
Comments: 12 pages, 7 figures. Code, configurations and recorded runs: doi:10.5281/zenodo.22738746
Abstract (1892 characters):
Universe24 is a simulator in which space is a three-dimensional GameBoard of Nodes joined by six Links, every evolving quantity is a bounded integer and every rule is local. We use it as a testbed: candidate mechanisms run under identical conditions and are asked which resource each needs for a given correlation. The mechanisms are a classical phased-ray field, a finite quantum owner, a causal source candidate coupling the two locally, and a bonded pair whose joint outcome is answered by one shared registry. The laws each obeys are configured inputs; the measurements verify that the integer implementation realizes them exactly and record what finite causal delay does to them: exact interference weights, which-path decoherence, collapse reaching remote sources only after Link transit, a local null-notice rule that restores conditional Born weights, and the emission-side classical limit. The CHSH form of Bell's test is then run on every candidate. Candidates whose information travels only on Links give S = 1.40 (exact), 1.38 and exactly 2; the two with a shared object give S = 2.84 +- 0.02 (the bonded pair, 4096 pairs per correlation; expectation 724/256) and 14/5 (the quantum owner). For each candidate we measure, at fixed hidden variable, which assumption of Bell's theorem it breaks: the bonded pair is deterministic, measurement-independent and parameter-dependent; the quantum owner breaks outcome independence; the local candidates break none. The number source is biased half by half: a bias in the coin is a signal, a bias in the agreement reaches the Popescu-Rohrlich value S = 4 with even marginals, so no-signalling bounds the coin and not the correlation. Nothing here derives quantum mechanics or explains the Bell value locally; the contribution is a reproducible integer framework in which the causal assumptions behind Bell's theorem appear as measured tables.

## Paper 2 (astro-ph.CO, cross-list physics.comp-ph), file redshift/main.tex with redshift/figures

Title: Redshift from delay growth on a closed integer GameBoard: the law, its kinematic correspondence to a scale factor, and the supernova test
Authors: Alon Gonen (ORCID 0009-0008-2701-4599)
License: CC BY 4.0
Comments: 11 pages, 2 figures. Code and recorded runs: doi:10.5281/zenodo.22738746; supernova table: the public Pantheon+ release
Abstract (1918 characters):
On a closed GameBoard whose computation load grows with age, a configured rule delays the transport of light while local cycles keep their bare rate. Rays emitted k_e ticks apart then reach an eye k_o ticks apart on the eye's own clock, 1 + z = k_o/k_e, with durations stretched by the same ratio; the law follows from the rule in the continuum, and this paper measures its discrete realisation on the Universe24 simulator. A train of twelve rays across closed rows of 16 to 96 Nodes gives the ratio within a tick per gap and 1 + z = exp(alpha D) with alpha read off the hop schedule; a single source emitting on its own clock gives the same law (z = 1.07); a phased-ray train's frequency follows the arrival gaps by construction, so the wave sector is not an independent stretch. The asymmetry between transport and clocks is put in, not derived: no clock built from the model's own processes has been tested. With D = int dt/k the hop time plays the part of a flat Friedmann scale factor for the Hubble diagram and the time dilation; the luminosity distance adds a 1/D^2 dilution and an energy that follows the frequency, both assumed. Against the Pantheon+ Hubble-flow sample (1580 light curves, full covariance, one free offset) the two load histories the model supplies on its own are behind flat LambdaCDM by delta chi^2 = 106 and 64, the best power law (k ~ t^1.4) by 9 with one fitted parameter each, and the reading in which only the arrival rate is redshifted by at least 81 at every exponent, as preferences between models rather than goodness-of-fit failures. The Tolman surface-brightness exponent, (1+z)^-2 against (1+z)^-4, is where the GameBoard and expansion part; the published exponents lie above the GameBoard's, an indication against it pending a reduction of the data under the GameBoard's own distance relations. Nothing here removes dark energy: the acceleration becomes a load history not yet derived.
