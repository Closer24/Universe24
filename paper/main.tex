\begin{abstract}
We report exact, reproducible measurements on Universe24, a simulator in which
space is a three-dimensional lattice of Nodes joined by six Links, every
evolving quantity is a bounded integer, and every rule is local. Ordinary
matter and fields are configured disturbances; a quantum excitation is a finite
joint state over at most thirty lattice registers, evolved by number-preserving
Gaussian-integer unitaries and read by local Kraus instruments. The two are
joined by a causal source candidate: each participating Node keeps a bounded
complex envelope of the wave and emits the classical field at full strength
times its local squared magnitude. On the canonical runner we measure
two-path interference with exact rational capture weights, which-path
decoherence by an actual local instrument, a collapse that reaches remote
sources only after Link transit, a local null-notice rule that restores the
exact conditional Born weights after one Link and corrects crossing nulls, an
Aharonov--Bohm-like phase from an external classical field, an emission-side
energy closure, and the emission-side classical limit. We then run the CHSH
form of Bell's test five times on one lattice with the same settings. Three
candidates whose information travels only on Links give $S = 1.40$ (exact),
$1.48$, and exactly $2$; two candidates with one shared object that no Link
carries give $S = 2.89$ (a bounded registry answering a bonded pair from one
number) and $S = 14/5$ (the finite quantum owner). We state a postulate under
which every uncertain interaction consumes one bounded integer, derive that
the integer sequence is the only entry for information outside the world and
is bound by no-signalling, and measure that bound: a uniform outside source is
invisible, a biased one is a signal. We list what is not claimed: no derivation
of the classical limit or of the configured laws, no continuum limit, and no
prediction yet distinguishable from quantum mechanics with a classical field.
The framework is called Reality Theory (Universe24); every number is
reproducible from the archived code.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Discrete, local, integer models of physics have a long history: conservative
logic and cellular automata \cite{fredkin1982,thooft2016}, lattice gases
\cite{fhp1986,succi2001}, quantum cellular automata and quantum lattice gases
\cite{bialynicki1994,meyer1996,arrighi2019}, hybrid quantum--classical
dynamics \cite{rosenfeld1963,diosi1984,oppenheim2023}, collapse and
decoherence \cite{grw1986,bassi2013,zurek2003}, and histories and causal sets
\cite{griffiths1984,bombelli1987}. This paper does not claim any of these ideas
as new. It reports a specific combination, realized exactly in bounded
integers, together with a set of measurements that the combination makes
possible because all of its parts run on one lattice under one runner.

The combination is this. Space is a lattice; every Node holds bounded local
information; influence moves at most one Link per tick; all arithmetic is
integer. Classical matter and fields are configured disturbances. A quantum
excitation is not a second world but a finite joint state over lattice
registers, evolved by explicit integer unitaries and read by explicit local
instruments. The join between them is a local envelope rule at each Node. On
this footing we can ask a question that is usually asked of separate models:
when the same lattice carries a classical phased wave and a finite quantum
state, what separates them experimentally? The answer we measure is Bell's
test, and it separates them by one thing only: whether all information
travels on Links.

Section~\ref{sec:model} states the model and its postulates, including the
postulate on randomness. Section~\ref{sec:coupling} reports the coupling
experiments. Section~\ref{sec:bell} reports the five Bell candidates.
Section~\ref{sec:outside} reports the outside-source measurement.
Section~\ref{sec:notclaimed} lists what is not claimed and what would make
the work a physics result. All code, configurations and recorded runs are
archived \cite{zenodo}.

\section{The model}\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{Lattice and locality.} Nodes lie on a three-dimensional integer
lattice with open or periodic boundary; each Node has six Links. One tick
moves a packet across one Link. A Node's state is a fixed set of bounded
integer registers; there is no global variable that a physical rule reads.
Postulate 4 states the causal bound: energy, momentum, matter and every
message a record can control move at most one Node per step.

\paragraph{Integers.} Every physical quantity, intermediate and law table is
a bounded integer or a ratio of bounded integers; there is no floating point
in any physical module, and an audit test enforces it. Trigonometric tables
for phased rays are prepared once in fixed point with a bounded series.

\paragraph{Computation cost and delay.} Every local cycle has a cost in
configured operation prices, and a cycle whose cost exceeds the normal budget
$B$ delays its Node by $k = \lceil C/B \rceil$ intervals; Link transit stays
one tick and no debt accumulates. A configured conserved field may be named the
computation field: its local value adds to the cost of every cycle at the Node,
so a mass that emits it slows the carriers, and optionally the rays, that pass
it. With directional delay each departure waits for the load on its own
channel, and with least-delay routing every carrier and every outward split
takes the cheapest eligible port first while keeping its exact directional
ratios: everything flows first toward the neighbor where computation is free.
These are declared timing rules, not a gravitational law; their measured
consequences are a light pulse that does not cross a loaded region within the
run, an interferometer fringe that shifts by the waits beside a mass, and a
distance-proportional redshift on a closed lattice whose load grows with age,
without any recession term. Host scheduling that skips empty Nodes (local
Focus) changes no physical clock and is checked against the ordinary
scheduler event by event.

\paragraph{Classical disturbances and fields.} Records with configured fields
move and interact by local rules. Spatial fields are transported by an octant
rule (outward splitting with carried allocation phases) or by straight rays
that keep one integer line, one Link per tick, and are counted whole by the
ledger. Ray emission may be funded from the emitter's stock, with recoil;
absorption adds the ray's amount and momentum to the absorber. A local
conservation audit closes energy and momentum per event.

\paragraph{Phased rays (the Kerengonen candidate).} A ray field may carry a
phase step per ray, advanced once per Link; rays meeting at a Node combine by
a fixed-point cosine of their phase differences, and the coherence
$|\sum a e^{i\phi}|^2 / (\sum |a|)^2$ gates what a reader samples and what an
absorber takes. Capture is a run-time choice: the coherent share, a whole-ray
lottery drawn from a record-row ticket, a deterministic threshold, or, for
bonded pairs, an answer of the bond registry.

\paragraph{The finite quantum owner.} A domain of at most thirty registers
holds a joint state with Gaussian-integer amplitudes. Gates are local
unitaries $U$ with $U^\dagger U = s I$ for an integer scale $s$; instruments
are Kraus pairs $(sI + O, sI - O)$; decisions carry exact rational Born
weights, and a ticket selects an outcome only when more than one weight is
nonzero.

\paragraph{The causal source candidate.} At an actual local contact a record
becomes a unit source envelope: a bounded complex amplitude at that Node. Two-mode
gates between neighboring Nodes exchange frozen amplitudes through Links and
commit after the neighbor's packet arrives. Each Node emits the ordinary
classical field at the configured full strength times its local squared
magnitude. A capture localizes the excitation into an ordinary record and sends
a terminal notice through Links; remote envelopes stop when it arrives. A null
result zeros only the local envelope; with the opt-in null notice the deciding
Node sends its own factor $1/(1-p)$ through Links, and a Node whose own null
was decided before an earlier-ordered notice arrived answers with the exact
correction $(1 - w s)/(1 - w s g)$ from its own record.

\paragraph{Bonded pairs.} Two rays emitted together carry the code of their
birth Node and tick. A detector meeting a bonded ray asks the bond registry,
one bounded object for the world (at most 4096 open pairs, each released at its
second answer, repeated questions idempotent). The pair's one number is a fixed
function of the registry seed and the birth code; its upper half answers the
first end evenly, its lower half, against the difference of the two settings,
decides whether the second end agrees with probability $(1 - \cos)/2$. The
answer carries no energy, momentum or message: each end alone sees an even
coin. This is the one declared exception to the causal bound.

\paragraph{Postulate 22: one integer per interaction.} Every interaction whose
outcome is not certain consumes exactly one bounded integer from a configured
sequence: the record's ticket, the quantum owner's ticket, or the registry's
number. The world's history is fixed by the rules, the initial state and this
sequence. Three consequences follow without further assumption: the sequence
is the only place where information not in the world's state enters; the
model is indifferent to where the sequence comes from; and whatever supplies
it is bound by no-signalling, since a biased supply moves one end's marginal
with the other end's setting. Section~\ref{sec:outside} measures the third.

\section{Bell's test five times on one lattice}\label{sec:bell}

We run the CHSH form with the same four settings, Alice $Z$ or $X$ and Bob
$(3Z \pm 4X)/5$, read as phases $0^\circ, 90^\circ, 53^\circ, 307^\circ$ on the
ray candidates, and the same combination
$S = E(a_0 b_0) + E(a_0 b_1) + E(a_1 b_0) - E(a_1 b_1)$.

The share rule equals the local model $E = \tfrac12\cos(a-b)$ exactly, one
half of the quantum owner's $\cos(a-b)$ in every correlation: Malus's law, not
a loss of visibility. The plain field sits exactly at the bound. The bonded
ray and the quantum owner both exceed it through one shared object that no
Link carries; both are non-signalling in the measured sense (each end's plus
rate does not move with the other end's setting). The difference between them
is bookkeeping, not locality: the registry answers a correlation from one
number and carries no inventory; the quantum owner also carries the conserved
carrier, the conditional collapse and the retarded classical emission. Bell's
theorem is here a table rather than a claim.

\section{A number source outside the world}\label{sec:outside}

The registry may take each pair's number from a configured stream instead of
its own sequence. With 16 seeds per hidden-phase slot and 1024 pairs per
correlation: a uniform outside stream gives $S = 2.79$ and plus
rates that move by less than $0.1$ with the other end's setting; a biased
stream, whose upper halves always answer $+1$, keeps $S = 2.78$
and moves Bob's plus rate by $0.69$ with Alice's setting
(from $0.85$ to $0.15$ at $b'$), the singlet's $(1-\cos(a-b'))/2$ with Alice's
coin fixed. An outside source may choose every single outcome without a
trace; it cannot do so with a bias without producing a signal faster than the
causal speed, and that signal is measured. Whether any real system is such a
source is outside the model.

\section{What is not claimed}\label{sec:notclaimed}

\begin{itemize}
\item Not a derivation of the classical limit or of the configured laws: the
mixers, instruments, phase advance, the $|p|/D$ rule, the cosine table and the
singlet law in the registry are data, measured to hold.
\item Born-rule consistency after null results is closed for one excitation
only; two carriers in one domain are outside the candidate by construction.
\item Back-action is a phase only; energy closure holds on the emission side
only; the field returns nothing to the wave.
\item Not a continuum or asymptotic result; every table is a finite lattice at
a finite number of ticks.
\item Not quantum electrodynamics, gravity, spin, statistics or many-body
dynamics.
\item The bonded ray is not a local explanation of the Bell value.
\end{itemize}

What would make this a physics result: a local integer rule for several
excitations in one domain; the return path from field to wave; a dynamical
classical-limit experiment; a prediction that differs from quantum mechanics
with a classical field at a checkable scale. Beyond these, the framework
raises questions it cannot yet decide (the size of the universe, flat rotation
curves from a compressed closed dimension, redshift without recession, what
the integer sequence is); they are kept on a hypotheses page apart from the
results \cite{zenodo}.

\section*{Reproducibility}
Every table is produced by a checked-in harness that writes a summary with the
runtime source fingerprint; the archived version is \cite{zenodo}. Python 3.14,
no floating point in any physical module.
