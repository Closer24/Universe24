\begin{abstract}
We define an integer model of one quantum in transit and one measurement
rule and report what it gives. Space is a GameBoard of Nodes
joined by six Links; every quantity is a bounded integer; every rule
between births and clicks is local and, given the apparatus's record,
injective. An event in transit is
a record of rows, each an amount, an integer phase on a circle of $N$
steps, a multiplicity and the record's number; a splitter multiplies
a row into rows by integer weights, an exact isometry. The measurement
rule is the click: the apparatus keeps, per record and Node, the running
sum of the rows' integer phasors; when every row of a record has ended it
lays the cells on a ruler by the squared sums, and a phase fixed at the
birth, which no rule reads as a number, picks the point where the record
is gathered. That rule is the Born rule, put in once; nothing
below it is derived. From it and the lattice we prove four
theorems (injectivity between clicks given the apparatus's record; the
split an isometry; a pair's marginals exactly $1/2$ off a rung tie,
no-signalling; the CHSH value at $N$ steps an exact rational within $8/N + 0.045$ of
$2\sqrt2$, on both sides of it) and measure on the
engine: the Mach-Zehnder always at one port, Elitzur-Vaidman, two slits one
quantum at a time (a sparse fan, no fringes), the pair at $S = 2.75$ for $N = 64$ and $181/64$ for
$N = 1024$ and $4096$ with every marginal exactly $1/2$, GHZ's exact
zeros, which-path and a CNOT gate. The click is non-local: a
pair is gathered once, when both arms have ended, from both settings'
joint weights. $S(N)$ lies above $2\sqrt2$ for about half the $N$,
so a Bell measurement constrains $N$ under stated assumptions: at the
CHSH labels the photon-pair value $2.82759 \pm 0.00051$ excludes
$N = 64$ and $256$ and admits the powers of two from 512. Every other
statement of the program is a hypothesis, claimed nowhere.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\paragraph{The model in plain words.} The GameBoard (the lattice, where a
shorter word serves) is a mail board.
Cells (Nodes), each wired to six neighbours; a wire carries one packet one
step per tick, and nothing travels farther or faster. A packet carries
four numbers that matter here, how much (the amount), where to (the
direction), a rotating counter (the phase, advanced a fixed step at every
wire it crosses) and a birth number, and its bookkeeping (a label and a
multiplicity, Definition~\ref{def:row}). A cell has no memory: what is in it now is exactly the
packets in it now. The rules are local and few. Transfer: a packet moves
to the neighbour its direction names. Duplication at a splitter: a packet
becomes several packets in several directions with declared shares, the
birth number the same on all. Merging: identical packets add; identical
packets with opposite counters cancel and leave nothing. Deflection
beside a mass: a packet's direction is turned by a table, and the turn
can be undone (no world of this paper has a mass). The collector, the
apparatus, keeps per birth number and per cell the running sum of the
packets' arrows. When every packet of a birth has ended, the collector
lays the cells on a ruler in proportion to the squared lengths of those
sums; a secret number born with the birth, the counter every packet of
the birth started from and which no rule reads as a number, picks the
point on the ruler; the whole birth is recorded at that cell. For a birth
that reaches two collectors with two settings, the collectors' record is
one object: the choice is made once, when the packets at both have
ended, from the sums at both with both settings, and the near
collector's row is written at the far collector's tick. That one choice
from both settings is the only action at a distance in the model;
nothing else crosses a wire faster than a packet. Births happen only at
sources, on their own clocks; deaths only at clicks. Everything between
a birth and its click can be run backwards, given the collectors' record
of what they absorbed.

\paragraph{What was asked, and what came before.} The question of this
paper is the one a previous paper on the same simulator asked of four
candidate mechanisms \cite{paper1}: which resource does an integer,
local, causal model need to reproduce the correlations of one quantum and
of a pair. There, every candidate whose information travelled only on
Links stopped at the Bell bound, $S = 2$ exactly, also when the settings
were chosen by events of the GameBoard itself (no superdeterministic
correlation was found), and only a declared shared object, a registry
holding a configured cosine law, reached $2.83$, as that paper reported
(its runs are in its own archive, not in the current register). That
framework is the ``before'' of this paper and is not repeated; its engine
of the time was replaced and none of its tables is reproduced. Here the
registry and the
configured law are gone. What remains is the lattice, one record with
rows, and one measurement rule, the click of Definition~\ref{def:click}.
Section~\ref{sec:model} defines the model in standard terms; the words
collapse and superposition appear first in Section~\ref{sec:lit},
each as another author's name for one of these things.
Section~\ref{sec:theorems} proves what can be proved of it;
Section~\ref{sec:measure} reports what the engine measured, from runs
recorded before this text was written; Section~\ref{sec:bell} compares
the finite-$N$ Bell value with the experiments; Section~\ref{sec:causal}
places the click in the causal anatomy of Bell's theorem;
Sections~\ref{sec:new} to \ref{sec:hyp} say what is new, what is not
claimed, and what the program hypothesises beside this result;
Section~\ref{sec:limits} states what returns in the continuum limit and
what does not. All code,
worlds and recorded runs are archived \cite{zenodo}.

\section{The model}\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{In one sentence.} The model is one piecewise-linear map on an
integer torus; the measurement is the one threshold that is read out. The
state is a vector of integers, and in the worlds of this paper every
interval does three things to it: it translates the vector by a rate and takes what
crossed a threshold as the events (the flight, the phase, every count and
the birth), it multiplies by a declared integer matrix (the split and the
rotation, exact and reversible), and it adds (the merge, where equal
phases sum and opposite phases cancel). Between clicks the rates are
constant, so the map has a closed form, and every count of the
measurements table is computed from it by the design's check scripts
before its run, the two slits excepted (its expectation is the
generator's reading of a one-birth reference world through the same
geometry on the engine, re-pinned once after a first run,
Section~\ref{sec:measure}); the departures of Section~\ref{sec:measure}
are what the runs added. (The engine also holds a collision table and a push of matter
by a row's share, $\mathrm{amount}^2/m$; neither acts in these worlds. The
flight table is computed once at load.) The click is the one place with a fourth
operation: the apparatus sums a record's rows through the tables and
\emph{squares} the sum (the norm, where the Born rule is put in), and only
then applies its threshold to $u$; it is the one threshold whose crossing
is written down, and it is done with the record it read (the archived
code then releases it).

\paragraph{The GameBoard and the circle.} Nodes are the points of a box in
$\Z^3$ (an axis may be periodic); each Node has six Links. Time is the
interval $t \in \Z$. The circle is $\Z_N$ with $4 \mid N$. The tables are
$C_N[p] = \mathrm{round}(256\cos(2\pi p/N))$ and $S_N[p] = C_N[p - N/4]$,
so $C_N[p + N/2] = -C_N[p]$ and $S_N[p + N/4] = C_N[p]$ exactly; the norm
$C_N^2 + S_N^2$ lies in $[65536 - 351,\ 65536 + 361]$ for every power of
two $N \le 65536$ (the extremes $65185$ and $65897$, the latter at
$N = 4096$, $p = 503$ and $3593$: $C = 184$, $|S| = 179$; at $N = 64$ the range is
$-88$ to $+237$) \cite{checks}. The half-angle tables are $C'_N = C_{2N}$,
$S'_N = S_{2N}$. Directions $D$ are the six headings and declared
primitive integer vectors; the flight table gives, per direction $d$ and
age $\tau$, the Node offset $\mathrm{pos}_d(\tau)$ on the digital line of
$d$, at most one Link per interval, and Euclidean speed $Q_f|d|/T_d$ with
$T_d = \lfloor\sqrt{3|d|^2Q_f^2}\rfloor$ and the flight scale $Q_f = 64$:
within $0.8$ percent of $1/\sqrt3$ for every $d$, exact on $(1,1,1)$
\cite{beamlaw}.

\begin{definition}[Row and record]\label{def:row}
A \emph{row} is $(x, d, \tau, p, e, h, r, \ell, w, m)$: Node, direction,
age, phase $p \in \Z_N$, emitter, content per unit, record $r$, branch
label $\ell$, amount $w \ge 1$, multiplicity $m \ge 1$. Its amplitude is
\begin{equation}
z = \frac{w}{\sqrt m}\,\frac{C_N[p] + i S_N[p]}{256}, \qquad |z|^2 = \frac{w^2}{m}\cdot\frac{C_N[p]^2 + S_N[p]^2}{65536}.
\end{equation}
Two rows equal in every field but $w$ are one row with the amounts added;
two rows of one record and label equal in every field but a phase
difference of $N/2$ are one row with the amounts subtracted (an equal pair
is no row). The state of record $r$ is an element $\psi_r$ of the free
$\Z$-module $M_r$ on the basis $(x, d, \tau, p \bmod N/2, e, h, \ell, m)$
with $|p + N/2\rangle = -|p\rangle$, taken modulo the scaling
$(w, m) \sim (kw, k^2 m)$; the GameBoard's state is one normal form per
element of $\bigoplus_r M_r$. The norm is $\langle\psi,\psi\rangle =
\sum_{\text{rows}} w^2/m$. The archived engine narrows the quotient at a
detector: rows of one record with different $m$ at one set are added when
the ratio of the multiplicities is the square of a rational (the pointers
rescaled by the integer roots) and refused otherwise; at the tree of the
runs two multiplicities at one set were refused, and no reported world
has them, so every set below reads one $m$.
\end{definition}

The host, not any Node, keeps per live record the birth phase $u \in \Z_N$,
the count of live units (amounts), an accumulated pointer $(X, Y)$ per
(Node, label) and a gathered flag. This is the apparatus's layer; the
lattice never reads it. It is a memory no Node could own: on the two-slit
world the two paths of one record reach a pixel up to $18$ intervals
apart, so no Node ever holds both rows, and the coherent sum exists only
in the record's ledger \cite{log}.

\begin{definition}[The update rules of one interval]\label{def:rules}
Applied in this order to every row.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[F] Flight: $(x, d, \tau, p) \mapsto (x + \mathrm{pos}_d(\tau+1) -
\mathrm{pos}_d(\tau),\ d,\ \tau + 1,\ p + \varphi(\tau))$, where the
family's phase turn is either an integer per Link, added only when the
step is a Link, or a pair $[n, d]$ per interval of age,
$\varphi(\tau) = \lfloor (\tau+1) n/d \rfloor - \lfloor \tau n/d \rfloor$,
counted from the row's birth or last re-emission.
\item[C] Collision: at a Node holding several rows of amount $1$ the
table permutes their directions as a function of the whole set present;
$r, \ell, m$ unchanged; a bijection of states, not a linear map. No
collision acts in any world of this paper.
\item[S] Split, at a measured event with outputs $\{(d_i, a_i, t_i)\}$,
$a_i \in \Z$: the row $(w, m, p)$ is absorbed and re-emitted as the rows
$(w a_i,\ m A,\ p + t_i)$ on $d_i$ at age $0$, $A = \sum_i a_i^2$. The
balanced splitter is $(1, 1)$, $A = 2$; the reflection's turn is $N/4$.
\item[R] Rotation, at a set with setting $s$ and turn $t$, on a label
pair $(0, 1)$ read into the channels $(+, -)$:
$U_{s,t} = \begin{pmatrix} C'[s] & S'[s]\,v(t) \\ -S'[s] & C'[s]\,v(t)\end{pmatrix}$
with $v(t)$ the turn by $t$ steps on the label-$1$ column; a row of
label $0$ becomes $(wC'[s], 65536m, p)$ on $+$ and $(wS'[s], 65536m,
p + N/2)$ on $-$, a row of label $1$ becomes $(wS'[s], 65536m, p + t)$ on
$+$ and $(wC'[s], 65536m, p + t)$ on $-$ (the row form exact for $t$ a
multiple of $N/4$, the only turns used here; the archived code multiplies
the column by the tables' phasor at $t$). With $t = 0$ this is $U_s$ of
Theorem~\ref{th:isometry}; the GHZ bases below are $X = (s, 0)$ and
$Y = (s, N/4)$.
\item[M] Normal form: merge and cancel as in Definition~\ref{def:row}.
\item[B] Birth: a lamp's self-creation at interval $t$ releases record $r$
with $u$ the lamp's phase at the birth, $(b - 1) \bmod N$ for a lamp
started at $0$ that turns one step per birth, $b$ its count of births
(every world here; $b - 1 = t - 1$ only while the lamp births at every
interval; the archived code writes $(b - 1) \bmod N$ directly), rows of
amount $1$ on its $k$
directions with $m = k$, each row's phase starting at $u$ (plus the
lamp's own turn), and, for a pair, the declared labels with integer
weights on separate arms.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The click]\label{def:click}
At a set that reads the sum, the rows of record $r$ and label $\ell$ that
arrive are absorbed and the layer accumulates
\begin{equation}
X \mathrel{+}= \sum 32\,w\,C_N[p],\qquad Y \mathrel{+}= \sum 32\,w\,S_N[p],
\end{equation}
the phase $p$ as the row carries it on arrival (the turns of rule F
already in it). The pointer is kept per Node:
interference is coherent within one Node only, and the offer of a set of
Nodes (a detector, a face) and label $\ell$ is the sum over its Nodes of
$(X_x^2 + Y_x^2)/m$, one cell of the ladder, the click landing at the Node
within the set that $u$ selects by the same rungs; all rows at a set share
$m$. When the record's live count reaches $0$, with $T = \sum W$ over its
offers and the cumulative sums $C_1 \le \dots \le C_K = T$ in the declared
order,
\begin{equation}
\rung_k = \left\lfloor \frac{2N C_k + T}{2T} \right\rfloor \quad (\rung_0 = 0,\ \rung_K = N),
\qquad \text{the click is the } k \text{ with } \rung_{k-1} \le u < \rung_k,
\label{eq:rung}
\end{equation}
and the world's list gains one row: the record, $u$, the chosen set and
label, $W$, $T$, the count of combinations before and after. For a record
with arms at two rotated sets $A$ and $B$ (settings $a$, $b$) the cells
are the joint outcomes $(o_A, o_B)$ in the arms' declared order, with
the weights
\begin{equation}
W(o_A, o_B) = J(o_A, o_B)^2,\qquad J(o_A, o_B) = \sum_\ell c_\ell\,U_a[o_A][\ell]\,U_b[o_B][\ell],
\label{eq:joint}
\end{equation}
$c_\ell$ the labels' declared weights, $(1, 1)$ in every world here; and
the record is gathered once, when the rows at both arms have ended:
the world's row carries both outcomes at the completion interval, so the
near party's outcome is written at the far party's tick. Reading the
ladder in that order is the same as a coarse rung over $o_A$ followed by
a fine rung over $o_B$ within it with the same $u$, which is how
Theorem~\ref{th:marginals} states it. A row of a gathered record that
reached a set afterwards would offer nothing; no reported world has one,
since completion needs every row ended.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[One click per record]\label{lem:one}
A record every row of which ends (at a set, a face or the border; not a
row parked at rest or on a periodic axis without a lifetime) completes
and is gathered exactly once: the live count reaches $0$; $\rung_K = N$
and $\rung_0 = 0$, so every $u \in \Z_N$ lies in exactly one cell. Every
record of the worlds below is of this kind. The world's row carries the
completion interval and the last arrival's interval; the delay between
them is a delay of the record, not of the lattice.
\end{lemma}

Three things are put in here and not derived, and the paper claims none
of them. Definition~\ref{def:click} \emph{is} the Born rule: the weight is
the squared length of the summed pointer, and the ruler over $u$ is
inverse-transform sampling of a uniform variable, so over births with $u$
uniform on $\Z_N$ the count in channel $k$ is $\rung_k - \rung_{k-1}$, the
nearest integer to $N W_k/T$, Born to the resolution $1/N$. No principle
below the square is offered. The uniformity of $u$ is an assumption about
the source: $u = (b - 1) \bmod N$ is uniform over $N$ consecutive births,
which is how every run below is made (one birth per $u$), and its
independence of the settings is a fact of a world, not of the model: $u$
is a clock and a chooser is a clock, so they are independent when the
chooser's period is coprime to $N$ and the run covers their common
period, as in this paper's choosers' world (periods $3$ and $5$ against
$N = 64$, every bin one birth per $u$, every marginal $32/64$); a chooser
whose period shares a factor with $N$ (not run) would correlate with $u$
by construction, a correlation of the kind superdeterminism posits. The earlier engine's run \cite{paper1}
is history. $u$ is also the phase every row of
the record starts from, so it enters every table read as a common offset
of the record's phasors: exactly invariant before the tables' rounding
and not after it (the record's total over $u$ takes eight values,
Section~\ref{sec:measure}); the click alone reads $u$ as a number. The
tables at $1/256$ are the third input. What the paper derives is what
follows from these with the
lattice's rules: the interference of one record, the exact marginals, the
$\cos^2$ correlation of the pair, GHZ's zeros, and the finite-$N$ value
of $S$. The click is the only step that removes combinations and adds a
row to the world's list; the birth is the only step that adds
combinations. At the tree of the runs reported here the law was selected by a world
key (\texttt{amplitude}) and the worlds without it read as before; the
archived code has since made the record form the only click and deleted
the key, with dated lines in its register for what its later changes
moved and the integers of series L kept as recorded \cite{beamlaw,register}.

\section{Four theorems}\label{sec:theorems}

\begin{theorem}[The interval is injective between clicks]\label{th:bijection}
For an interval without a click, an end at a face or the border, or a
collision, $M \circ R \circ S \circ F$ is an injective $\Z$-linear map from $\bigoplus_r M_r$ to
$\bigoplus_r M_r$ paired with the apparatus's record of the rows the
splitters and rotators absorbed in that interval; the GameBoard's state
at any interval between two clicks, together with that record,
determines the state at the birth. Without the record the split forgets
the arriving age. A collision, where it acts, is a bijection of the
GameBoard's states that is not linear; none acts in the worlds of this
paper.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$F$ maps a basis element to a basis element and is injective because the
age is kept whole: $(d, \tau) \mapsto (d, \tau+1)$ is injective and the
Node offset is a function of $(d, \tau)$. $S$ absorbs the arriving row,
whose age, phase and amount are written on the apparatus's click line,
and re-emits at age $0$; given that line it is injective on the
amplitudes by Theorem~\ref{th:isometry}, and without it two rows
differing only in age have the same image: the split is inverted only
because the click line records what it absorbed. $R$ is injective
because $U_{s,t} = U_s\,\mathrm{diag}(1, v(t))$, the turn a bijection and
$U_s^{\mathsf T}U_s = n_s I$. $M$ replaces a
representative by the normal form of the same element of $M_r$, the
identity on the module. A composition of injective linear maps is
injective. The collision permutes the directions of the amount-$1$ rows
at a Node as a function of the whole set present \cite{beamlaw}, a
bijection of states and not a linear map, which is why it is excluded
from the linear statement. The engine's inverse interval is the witness
on worlds without a measured event \cite{beamlaw}; with splitters the
statement is the algebraic one with the record.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The split is an isometry whose conjugate transpose inverts it]\label{th:isometry}
The split $(w, m, p) \mapsto ((wa_i, mA, p + t_i))_i$ preserves
$\sum w^2/m$ for every integer vector $(a_i)$, and the conjugate
transpose of the table (the same weights with the turns reversed)
applied to its outputs, followed by the normal form, returns the input up
to the scaling $(kw, k^2m)$ with $k = A$. The rotation satisfies
$U_s^{\mathsf T} U_s = n_s I$ exactly with $n_s = C'[s]^2 + S'[s]^2$, and
multiplies a record's norm by $n_s/65536$: an isometry only up to the
tables' rounding ($65705/65536$ at $N = 64$, $s = 1$).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\sum_i (wa_i)^2/(mA) = w^2 \sum_i a_i^2/(mA) = w^2/m$. The conjugate
transpose maps the outputs $(wa_i, mA, p + t_i)$ to $\sum_i a_i (w a_i)$
at the phase $p$ on the input direction with multiplicity $mA \cdot A$,
that is $(Aw, A^2 m, p) \sim (w, m, p)$; for two inputs at one splitter
the cross terms carry phases differing by $N/2$ (a reflection's quarter
turn taken forward and its reverse taken back) and cancel in the normal
form, which the check with the pairs $(20, 21)$, $(3, 4, 5)$ and
$(119, 120, 169)$ confirms integer by integer \cite{design} (with the
turns kept instead of reversed the inputs are not returned), and the
engine's merge reproduces on its own rows \cite{beamlaw}. For $U_s$ the
diagonal of $U_s^{\mathsf T}U_s$ is $C'^2 + S'^2$ and the off-diagonal
$C'S' - S'C' = 0$ in integers.
\end{proof}

So a record's norm is conserved exactly by $S$ and by the flight, and by
$R$ and by every read through the tables only within the tables' rounding
($-351/65536$ to $+361/65536$ per row for the powers of two through
$65536$); the integer
Mach-Zehnder's total over the 64 birth phases takes eight values from
$65448/65536$ to $65773/65536$, the same eight on every one of its ten
worlds \cite{register}. The normalisation by $T$ in \eqref{eq:rung} makes
the click's probabilities sum to one at completion; it does not restore
the norm along the way, and the paper claims exact unitarity nowhere.

\begin{theorem}[Exact marginals; no-signalling]\label{th:marginals}
For a pair with labels $\{0, 1\}$ of equal weight and settings $a, b$, let
$J(o_A, o_B) = \sum_\ell U_a[o_A][\ell]\,U_b[o_B][\ell]$ and
$W(o_A, o_B) = J^2$. Then the first party's outcome is $+$ exactly for
$u < N/2$ and $-$ for $u \ge N/2$, for every $(a, b)$ and every $N$; and
the second party's count of $+$ is exactly $N/2$ for every $(a, b)$ unless
$2N W(+,+)/T$ is an odd integer (a tie of the rung), in which case it is
$N/2 + 1$. No tie occurs for any setting pair at $N = 8, 16, 24, 32, 48,
64, 96, 128, 192, 256, 512, 1024$, nor at the CHSH labels for any
multiple of $8$ up to $4096$ (computed from the rule, \cite{checks}; the
gate review found none at $64$, $256$, $1024$ and at $4096$ for
$a = 0, 1024$, \cite{log}); the
reviewer also recomputed the marginals from the rows of one registered
world at $N = 64$ for all 4096 setting pairs, $32/64$ in every pair
\cite{log}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $n_a = C'[a]^2 + S'[a]^2$. Since the rows of $U_b$ are orthogonal on
the integers, $\sum_{o_B} J(o_A, o_B)^2 = n_a n_b$ for each $o_A$, so
$T = 2n_a n_b$ and the coarse cumulative after the first party's $+$ is
$T/2$: $\rung = \lfloor (NT + T)/(2T)\rfloor = \lfloor (N+1)/2 \rfloor =
N/2$. For the second party, $W(o_A, o_B) = W(-o_A, -o_B)$ (the rows of
$U_s$ are each other's quarter turn), so with $x = N W(+,+)/T$ the counts
are $c_{++} = \lfloor x + \tfrac12\rfloor$ and $c_{-+} = \rung_3 - N/2$ with
$\rung_3 = \lfloor (2N(T - W(+,+)) + T)/(2T)\rfloor = N + \lfloor \tfrac12 -
x\rfloor$. Hence $c_{++} + c_{-+} = N/2 + \lfloor y\rfloor + \lfloor 1 -
y\rfloor$ with $y = x + \tfrac12$, which is $N/2$ when $y \notin \Z$ and
$N/2 + 1$ when $y \in \Z$. The tie condition is $2x \in 2\Z + 1$, that
is $2NW(+,+)/T$ odd. The absence of ties at the listed $N$ is the
computation over every setting pair \cite{checks}. Which party is first
is the world file's declaration of the arms' order, not a property of
the lattice: the model has a preferred party by declaration, and
swapping the arms moves outcomes between the parties while the counts
stay \cite{log}.
\end{proof}

The strict-crossing rung $NC_k > uT$ gives the second party $33/64$ in
$3944$ of the $4096$ setting pairs at $N = 64$, a signalling of $1/N$ made
by the rounding \cite{design}; the nearest rung of \eqref{eq:rung} is what
Theorem~\ref{th:marginals} needs. The theorem's scope is what it states:
a pair of two labels with equal weights, the CHSH form, and the rung of
\eqref{eq:rung}; unequal weights and more than two labels are not covered,
and no-signalling for them is open. Where the two settings meet is
explicit in Definition~\ref{def:click}: $B$'s rung is computed from
$W(o_A, o_B)$ with $a$ and $o_A$ supplied by the layer. That the marginal
at $B$ is nevertheless $N/2$ for every $a$ is the content of the theorem;
the joint counts are not local, and the model does not claim they are.

\begin{theorem}[The finite-$N$ Bell value]\label{th:bell}
With the counts of Theorem~\ref{th:marginals} and off a tie,
$E_N(a, b) = 4c_{++}/N - 1$ and
\begin{equation}
\bigl|E_N(a, b) - \cos(2\pi(a-b)/N)\bigr| \le \frac{2}{N} + 4\arcsin\frac{\sqrt2}{2\rho} < \frac{2}{N} + 0.0111,\qquad \rho = 256 - \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},
\label{eq:ebound}
\end{equation}
so at the labels $(0, N/8, N/4, 3N/8)$, $|S(N) - 2\sqrt2| \le 8/N + 0.0444$.
$S(N)$ is an exact rational for each $N$; it lies above $2\sqrt2$ for
$252$ of the $512$ multiples of $8$ up to $4096$ (among them $N = 16, 32$
with $S = 3$ and $N = 128$ with $S = 23/8$) and equals $181/64 =
2.828125$ at every power of two from $512$ through $4096$ \cite{checks}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$c_{++} = \lfloor x + \tfrac12\rfloor$ with $x = NW(+,+)/T$ gives
$|c_{++} - x| \le \tfrac12$ and $E_N = 4c_{++}/N - 1$ from the mirror
symmetry, so $|E_N - (4W(+,+)/T - 1)| \le 2/N$. The vector
$(C'[s], S'[s])$ differs from $256(\cos\theta_s, \sin\theta_s)$,
$\theta_s = \pi s/N$, by at most $\tfrac12$ per component, so its
direction differs from $\theta_s$ by at most $\delta = \arcsin(\sqrt2/(2\rho))$
with $\rho = 256 - \sqrt2/2 = 255.29$, below every table vector's length
(each component within $\tfrac12$ of $256\cos$ or $256\sin$), so
$4\delta = 0.01108 < 0.0111$ and $16\delta = 0.04432 < 0.0444$;
$J(+,+)/\sqrt{n_a n_b}$ is the cosine of the angle between the two
directions, $\cos(\theta_a - \theta_b + \epsilon)$ with $|\epsilon| \le
2\delta$, and $4W(+,+)/T - 1 = 2\cos^2(\cdot) - 1 = \cos(2(\theta_a -
\theta_b) + 2\epsilon)$, within $4\delta$ of $\cos(2\pi(a-b)/N)$. The
four settings add the bounds. The values of $S(N)$ are the computation
from these formulas with the repository's tables \cite{checks}, which
reproduces the design's $176/64$, $720/256$ and $2896/1024$ at $N = 64,
256, 1024$ and the engine's three values below.
\end{proof}

What Theorem~\ref{th:bell} does not say: that $S(N) \le 2\sqrt2$, which is
false for about half the $N$; nor that the deficit is $O(1/N)$ from below,
which holds at the three $N$ the engine ran and not in general. The
tables' term in \eqref{eq:ebound} does not shrink with $N$; it is the
resolution $1/256$ of the circle's tables. At the CHSH labels the tables'
entries are the same at every $N$ ($237, 98$ and $181, 181$), so
$E(0, N/8) = 46565/65773$ and $E(N/4, N/8) = 46452/65773$ exactly at every
$N$ before the rounding of the cells, which is why the powers of two from
512 all give $181/64$ \cite{checks,register}. At a tie $c_{--} = c_{++} - 1$ and
$E_N$ drops by $2/N$; no tie occurs at the CHSH labels up to $4096$.

\section{The Bell value at finite $N$ and the experiments}\label{sec:bell}

$S(N)$ is a property of the model that nothing in the model fixes: $N$ is
a declared width of the world. The model gives a prediction only once $N$
is chosen, and what a measurement of nature does is constrain $N$, under
three assumptions the paper states and does not defend: that nature's
pairs are this model's pairs at some $N$, that the experiment's
fair-sampling assumption holds, and that the experiment's four settings
are the model's labels $(0, N/8, N/4, 3N/8)$. The third matters because
$E$ depends on $(a, b)$ beyond $a - b$: at $N = 256$ the maximum of $S$
over all setting quadruples is $91/32 = 2.84375$ and $181{,}152$
quadruples lie within three standard deviations of the measured value,
while at $N = 64$ the maximum over all quadruples is $11/4$, so that
exclusion stands whatever the settings \cite{checks}. Under the three,
the photon-pair value $S = 2.82759 \pm 0.00051$ \cite{poh2015} is
incompatible with $N = 64$ (152 standard deviations) and $N = 256$ (30),
compatible with the powers of two from 512 (one standard deviation
above), admits its first $N$ at 184, and excludes 136 of the 512 values
up to 4096 at five standard deviations, the largest 3016 \cite{checks};
the archived engine declares $N$ a power of two, so the other multiples
of $8$ are the formula's, not runnable worlds. Of the loophole-free
experiments, \cite{hensen2015} reports a CHSH value, $S = 2.42 \pm
0.20$, which excludes no $N$ at three standard deviations;
\cite{giustina2015,shalm2015} report a CH-Eberhard quantity whose
comparison needs the model's detector efficiency, which it lacks
(Section~\ref{sec:notclaimed}). A
no-signalling value above $2\sqrt2$ is not forbidden by no-signalling
\cite{pr1994}; at $N = 16$ and $32$ the model gives $S = 3$ with the
marginals exactly $1/2$. Agreement of one value with one experiment
validates nothing beyond that; a comparison with noise and detector
inefficiency in the model's own terms has not been made, and the model
has no rule that would give it.

\section{The click in the causal anatomy of Bell's theorem}\label{sec:causal}

Write $\lambda$ for what is fixed before the settings, $a, b$ for the
settings, $A, B$ for the outcomes. In the terms of \cite{jarrett1984,shimony1986}
as \cite{paper1} used them: $\lambda$ is $u$ with the record, chosen at
the birth before any setting exists and read by no rule that sets a
setting (measurement independence holds in the registered choosers'
world by its coprime periods, Section~\ref{sec:model}, not by
construction); the joint
outcome is one function of $(u, a, b)$, computed once at completion;
$A$, the first party in the declared order of the arms, is $+$ for
$u < N/2$, independent of both settings (parameter independence holds
at $A$, and $A$'s marginal is $1/2$); $B$ is a function of $(u, a, b)$:
parameter dependence at $B$, exactly where the bonded pair of
\cite{paper1} had it, and outcome independence in the trivial sense that
$B$ is deterministic given $\lambda$ and both settings. Which party is
$A$ is the world file's declaration, a preferred party by declaration
and not by the lattice. The model is
deterministic and parameter-dependent, and measurement-independent in the
registered choosers' world, the class
Bell's theorem requires of a deterministic model above the bound
\cite{bell1964,chsh1969}; Theorem~\ref{th:marginals} is what keeps the
dependence from being a signal, as \cite{grw1980} showed of quantum
mechanics. What is new against \cite{paper1} is where the dependence
lives: not in a configured cosine of the two settings held by a registry,
but in one gather reading one record's rows at both arms, whose products
of the two rotations' entries give $\cos^2$ by the same arithmetic that
gives one record's fringe. The law is the tables' and the rule's; it was
not written into the pair.

\section{What is new}\label{sec:new}

\begin{itemize}
\item One measurement rule, exact in integers, from which interference,
the pair's correlation with exact marginals, GHZ and the gate follow on
one lattice without a configured correlation law, with the rule's
non-locality stated as one gather of one record from both settings.
\item Four theorems about that rule, with the tie, the tables' rounding
and the apparatus's record in their statements, and one exact table

\item Runs recorded before the text, the first $N$ births one per $u$, replayed

\end{itemize}

\section{What is not claimed}\label{sec:notclaimed}

\begin{itemize}
\item Not a derivation of the Born rule: the square and the uniform birth
phase are the rule (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
\item Not a local model of the pair: the record is gathered once from
both settings, and the near party's row is written at the far party's
tick; no-signalling is proved for the equal-weight pair and open beyond
it; the first party is the world file's choice.
\item Not exact unitarity on the lattice: the tables' norm is within
$-351$ to $+361$ of $65536$ per row for the powers of two through
$65536$, the rotation is an isometry only up
to that, and the record's total over $u$ takes eight values.
\item Not a fixed $N$, hence not a prediction of nature's $S$: a
constraint on $N$ under stated assumptions.
\item Not a continuum limit of the model as a whole (Section~\ref{sec:limits}
states the computed limits), not spin or polarization (the labels are
abstract), not bosons or fermions, not Lorentz invariance (one speed for
every direction, no boost), not the classical limit as click density
(proposed, not run), not a treatment of noise or detector inefficiency.
\item Not the tree of the archive's latest version: the runs are the
tree named in Section~\ref{sec:measure}; the later code made the record
form the only click, put $u$ on the row as the record's own field (read
by no rule of the GameBoard) and re-pinned the ticks of every lamp world.
\end{itemize}

\section{Continuum limits: what returns and what does not}\label{sec:limits}

A model in integers is judged also by what it returns as its resolution
grows, as a Fourier sum returns $\pi$. Two limits follow from the
definitions and are computed in \cite{checks} (Tsirelson in
\texttt{limits.txt}, the cone in \texttt{information\_transfer.txt}), a
third (Young) is partial, two conservations hold exactly, and two limits
do not follow and are stated as such.

\paragraph{Tsirelson's value as a limit.} The bound of
Theorem~\ref{th:bell} has two terms, $2/N$ from the ladder's rungs and
$4\arcsin(\sqrt2/(2(Q - \sqrt2/2)))$ from the tables' scale $Q$ ($256$ in
every run of this paper). As $N$ and $Q$ grow both vanish: $S(N, Q) \to 2\sqrt2$. The
computed values are $S = 2.75$, $2.8125$ and $2.828125$ at $N = 64$, $256$
and $1024$, unchanged by $Q$ from $256$ to $2^{20}$ at these $N$ (the rungs
decide), with the bound $0.125$, $0.031$ and $0.0078$ at $Q = 2^{20}$
($0.169$, $0.076$ and $0.052$ at $Q = 256$). The
finite-$N$ departures of Section~\ref{sec:bell} are the terms of this
limit, not a second law.

\paragraph{The light cone and the wavelength.} The flight table moves
every direction at $1/\sqrt3$ Links per interval within $1/T_d$: $0.8$
percent fast on an axis, $0.5$ on the plane diagonal, exact on the body
diagonal (the register's L7: a row of $17$ Links on an axis and a row of
$24$ Links on the plane diagonal, at the same Euclidean distance to one
percent, reach their counters at the same age $29$). That anisotropy is
set by the flight scale $Q_f = 64$ and does not shrink with the Link or
the age; every declared direction's speed reaches $1/\sqrt3$ only as $Q_f$
grows, a second parameter beside the tables' $Q$ (the front's shape is
the fan's, a third), and then $c = a/(\tau\sqrt3)$. A family whose
phase turns $n/d$ steps per interval gives two paths of one record a
phase difference of $n/d$ times their difference in age, so a record's
interference wavelength is $cNd/n$ Links along the flight; a plane wave
of a stream of records is not computed here (a stream's phase at a fixed
interval depends on the lamp's rate as much as on the family's turn).

\paragraph{Young's fringes and the fan.} The two-slit world of L2
correlates $0.368$ with the cosine pattern and $0.744$ with the incoherent
sum, and its clicks fall on $14$ pixels. Three things hide the fringes,
in this order \cite{log}. First the wheel: $u = (b-1) \bmod 64$ repeats
with the births, so the $19$ cells landed by the first $64$ births are the
cells of every birth after them and no number of births adds a pixel to
Figure~\ref{fig:slits}; a mathematician's map that reproduces the register's $64$
clicks exactly shows it, and proposes a wheel of $2^{12}$ births beside
$N$. Second the fan: each opening re-emits along $91$ directions, and only
$27$ of the $75$ pixels reached receive rows from both openings; where one
opening alone reaches a pixel there is nothing to interfere. A computation
with $K$ directions per opening spaced equally in angle (the angular
measure) gives the correlation with the cosine $0.45$ at $K = 91$ (the
register's $0.37$ in its own geometry), $0.84$ at $361$ and $0.90$ from
$721$ on, every pixel seeing both openings from $721$; the lattice's own
fan of primitive directions with equal weights has not converged by the
width $64$ ($0.38$, $0.57$, $0.65$ at the widths $12$, $32$, $64$, a comb) and does with each direction weighted by the
angle it covers ($0.90$) \cite{log}. Third the flight's rounding to whole
intervals: $0.07$ in an idealised two-path computation at this
wavelength, $0.02$ on the world itself. The limit of every direction with
its angular weight returns Young's pattern up to a plateau at $0.90$ (a
plateau whose cause is not computed here); the same map predicts, for a
wheel, the screen's fan (one direction per pixel from each opening, equal
multiplicity) and the exact phase, fringes at the Euclidean spacing with
visibility $0.96$ over $4096$ births, a prediction pinned before its run;
for the angular fan alone it pins fringes in the weights and none in the
clicks until the wheel \cite{log}; the registered run is far from it, and the
paper does not claim the fringes.

\paragraph{Conservation, not yet a continuity equation.} Two things are
conserved exactly on the lattice: the content (measured, in transit and
escaped sum to a constant at every interval, the books) and, through
every split, $\sum w^2/m$ (Theorem~\ref{th:isometry}). The offered norm
is not: the tables' rounding moves a record's total over eight values
(Section~\ref{sec:measure}), and where paths of one record meet at one
Node their cross terms enter, the two-slit record offering $847181/745472
= 1.136$ of its birth norm. A current on the Links is not defined in this
paper, so $\partial_t \rho + \nabla \cdot j = 0$ is the derivation's task,
not a result here; what the limit inherits is the two exact conservations.

\paragraph{What does not return, and why.} The lattice and its interval
are a rest frame. The front is isotropic to $0.8$ percent in that frame; nothing in the
definitions says that a moving body reads the same $c$, so Lorentz
invariance is not a limit of this model but a property it must show or
lack, on worlds with moving clocks (the program's, not this paper's). The
field equations of gravitation are not reachable from a model without
mass: the program's rule that held content delays the flight would give,
in the limit, an optical metric and its geodesics, not the equation the
delay field obeys; that derivation is open and listed in
Section~\ref{sec:hyp}. The paper claims the two limits, the partial third
up to its plateau, and the two conservations above, and neither of these
two.

\section{Literature and positioning}\label{sec:lit}

The click is a non-local step, forced by Bell's theorem because the
marginals are exact and $S > 2$ \cite{bell1964,chsh1969}; it is
parameter-dependent at fixed $u$ in the sense of the causal anatomy of
\cite{jarrett1984,shimony1986} as \cite{paper1} used it; no-signalling is
Theorem~\ref{th:marginals}, as it is a theorem of quantum mechanics
\cite{grw1980}.

't Hooft's cellular automaton interpretation \cite{thooft2016} keeps
determinism and meets Bell by superdeterminism; here the model is
deterministic given $u$, the settings are GameBoard clocks whose
periods, coprime to $N$ in the registered choosers' world, leave them
uncorrelated with $u$ (a period sharing a factor with $N$ would not, and
is not run), and the Bell value is met by an
explicit non-local click. Wolfram's hypergraph rewriting and Gorard's
multiway quantum mechanics \cite{wolfram2020,gorard2020} take the
branches of a multiway system as the superposition and a completion as
the collapse; here one lattice, one record with rows, one click with an
exact integer rule and finite tables whose consequences are computed.
Lattice gases \cite{hpp1976,fhp1986} are exact integer dynamics with the
continuum law in the limit; the GameBoard is of that kind, with the
click's reading of one record added. Quantum cellular automata and
quantum lattice gases \cite{bialynicki1994,meyer1996,arrighi2019} are
local unitary rules on complex amplitudes; here no complex number is on
the lattice, the split is an exact integer isometry, and the tables'
unitarity holds within $361/65536$ and is computed, not assumed. Bohm's
theory and quantum equilibrium \cite{bohm1952,dgz1992} take the hidden
position distributed as $|\psi|^2$ (from typicality); here a uniform $u$
and the ruler do the same work by inverse-transform sampling, a
different distribution, one integer per record with a table for the
guidance, and, like Bohm's, non-local at the pair. Nelson's stochastic
mechanics \cite{nelson1966} is stochastic where nothing here is, once
$u$ is fixed. Objective collapse \cite{grw1986,bassi2013} has real
discrete events with a free rate and length; the click is triggered by
the record's completion at the apparatus and has neither, and nothing
collapses spontaneously (a pair stays entangled without maintenance). In
the classification of ontological models \cite{spekkens2007,hs2010} the
rows' amplitudes and $u$ are ontic, so the model is $\psi$-ontic; its
non-locality is Bell's (Section~\ref{sec:causal}). On Tsirelson's bound
\cite{tsirelson1980,pr1994}, its finite-precision form \cite{bhz2005}
and finite precision in foundations \cite{meyer1999,kent1999}: Theorem~\ref{th:bell} puts the model's
$S(N)$ on both sides of $2\sqrt2$ with the marginals exact, a
no-signalling box of the Popescu--Rohrlich kind at finite $N$, of a size
a Bell measurement of precision $0.0005$ already constrains
(Section~\ref{sec:bell}). The single-quantum build-up of fringes
\cite{tonomura1989,grangier1986}, GHZ \cite{ghsz1990,mermin1990,pan2000}
and interaction-free measurement \cite{ev1993} are the experiments the
measurements of Section~\ref{sec:measure} are named for; none of them is
reproduced in its own geometry, and the two-slit pattern here is a sparse
fan's spots.
