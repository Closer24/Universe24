## 2. The solution: what a world may hold, and what a law must give

This is the rule the writers follow from now on.
- A world's initialization may hold as given:
  - the three files' integers: the piece's shape, the setting's ticks, and the trains by name, kind and part;
  - den and width, the same in every world;
  - the laid counts, each gear's now, before and tooth, laid in whole numbers by the act itself (no mode, no root, no
    angle aimed);
  - the units only where they scale the whole action and move no motion (ONELAW3's 4A), once the 3+1 gears are built.
- Every strength the act multiplies a term by comes from a family's law, never from a run. It is either its law's unit
  (hold 1; push 1, term 5's own) or the product its law names (the tie, one product of two hands' squares; the held
  hand's spring from the hat on the pair's square; the key, the world's one quantum unit). No strength is chosen, and
  none is set by what a run shows: not by a world's clicks, nor its room, nor the visibility of its well.
- Until a family's law gives a strength, the world ships it at its law's unit, or at 0 with its term off, its
  NOT_CLOSED row named in the universe file's reason. It never ships at a value found by a run. A phenomenon that
  needs a picked value does not ship; the world ships as it falls, its record or its runaway named.
- The solution for each OPEN strength is a derivation of its family's law, brought by its deriver and passed by both
  hostile readers, then built (BUILD.md):
  - the key, the world's quantum unit;
  - the notch's h from term 9's own law;
  - the tie, from the product of the two hands' squares;
  - the held spring, from the hat (Big Pic, 6538018966);
  - the mesh, from the gradient vertex's law;
  - Gamma, from the units of the 3+1 gears.
