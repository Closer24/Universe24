# NC29: the split at the rest, built in the passing, measured on writer D's world (node-live 36fbfbbde)

## Verdict first

The knot does NOT close on D's world. The split at the rest closes the passing's books exactly (by construction,
SUM alpha = 1), and on the pair world the click books come out SHOWN at 1095 of 1095 places. But on the click world
the books open again at round 465, site 0, and stay open at 324 of 11,664 places by round 500. The meetings are
NOT SHOWN on both worlds, the held train still clicks several quanta a round, and the click rate is not steady.

- Where the books open: round 465, site 0, the sum of q is 1. Every open place is at round 465 or later. Round 465 is
  the first round the taker clicks (756 taker lines, all at rounds 465 to 500). 297 of the 324 open places hold
  lines from all three trains.
- Why the books open: three trains pass at one Node-clock. These are the held train, the giver (through the tie
  groups) and the taker (through the hands' two-leg tie). Each train's S is carried by its own click tooth at one
  key, and K SUM q = SUM r - SUM r+. With two passing trains the teeth are born summing to K - 1 and stay
  complementary, so q_g = -q_t and the books hold. With three passing trains the sum of the teeth is not kept, so
  the sum of q moves even though SUM S = 0 exactly. The split mends S, but the open item is now the tooth: one carry
  per train cannot keep a three-way passing at one place.
- Why the meetings fail: the split's held-to-giver passing is many quanta a round (|q| up to 10). A meeting needs one
  give and one take of one quantum, so the held train still clicks.
