## 2. The clock layer

**The release kind, and why it pumps.** In the escapement a Node-clock's gears move only when its own tooth turns. A
turn is one whole proper step. Where two neighbours run at different rates, their steps fall on different rounds, and
each holds its count while the other moves. A Mesh between them then couples a moving gear to a held one. That
switching cannot be removed by any rule for the Mesh (Lemma D). On a board that loses nothing, a switching repeated at a
slow rhythm drives the modes whose frequencies sum to one of its harmonics (Theorem 6).

**The lead.** Each end, when it releases, pays half of the Mesh's pull, with the half's remainder kept on the Mesh.
Where both ends release together the Mesh pays the whole pull. The Mesh adds no rhythm of its own, but its ends' rhythm
is still there.

**The carried release (the cure).** Nothing is released on some rounds and held on others. Every round, every gear
steps by its own clock: the step is carried at the dial L, the clock's own count. Every round, every Mesh pays its pull
into both ends at once, carried at the rate of its two ends together, n rest over L_i + L_j, with the remainder kept on
the Mesh. A slow clock then takes small steps every round instead of whole steps on some rounds. The rate enters twice,
once in the step and once in the pull, so the beat's square comes out by itself.

**What it gives up.** A gear that steps a fraction of a proper step every round is a slightly different clock from one
that steps a whole proper step on some rounds. Its beat is slowed by its own depth only to the calibre: the faster its
beat against the round, the more it departs (section 6.1). Where the clock moves, a remainder kept at last round's dial
must be read at this round's, and on a falling clock it may not fit (section 6.3). EDGE2's edge dial, which reads both
of the edge's dials, removes that misfit.

**What the escapement still does.** It keeps the moving clock's law by itself at one Node-clock, and the slowing of
every beat alike, because its gears always step in whole proper steps. Its tooth is also the clock's own whole count of
proper time: a counter that can tell a detector how long it waited, gating nothing.

## 3. The algebra

### 3.1 The release kind: Lemma D and Theorem 6

**Lemma D (no steady impulse at both ends).** Let a Mesh (x, y) pay whole payments, each into both its ends at one
round with opposite signs. Let x release every round and y at a rate whose intervals are k or k + 1 rounds, both
occurring. If x's impulse per own step is steady, the payment is the same p every round. Then y's impulse per own step
is k p or (k + 1) p, which is unsteady unless p = 0.

In general, steady impulses at both ends need the payment per proper step at x and at y in the ratio of the two ends'
rates at every step. That means a whole payment divided, at every step, at a ratio set by the clocks (Theorem 1's (H)).
A pull divided at the clock's dial every round does not do it: an end's step still holds k or k + 1 rounds of payments.
Status: proved where one end releases every round; for general rates it is the same count, in outline.
