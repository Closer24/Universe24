## 1. In clocks

- **What the passing counts.** Each round a train's click tooth carries the train's two-round energy change (an energy
  meaning read on the board), read on the window before, now and next; summed over the trains the passings are 0 (the
  books), so over a run the clicks only move from one train to another, up to what the teeth hold.
- **Why four legs break it.** A spring between two gears trades energy as a seesaw: what one gains the other loses,
  read off the two gears alone. A term of four legs is a seesaw whose pivot moves with the other legs; the act's passing
  reads each leg against the other three at the other slot, and the four readings no longer balance.
- **The work each gear takes.** A gear's push at the now slot times the count it moves from before to next is the work
  the term does on it over the round. The act's two-leg passing is the work on one gear less the work on the other.
- **Three ways to share a term.** A term of four legs belongs to no one gear. The middle form gives each train the share
  of its legs. The background form gives it all to the train that is not held and treats the held hand as a stage that
  trades only when its size moves. The split at the rest gives the tied train the tie's value at the hand's rest, its
  own two-leg term on the board, and shares only the tie's swing about that value: two parts to the tied train's two
  legs, one to the hand.

  stands at its rest the tie's swing is 0; where the hand also stands still over the window, or turns exactly a quarter

## 2. In the algebra

### 2.1 The residual

- For a monomial f PROD x_(l_i) over its positions i (a gear may hold several), J_g(y) = -f SUM over g's positions of
  PROD over the other positions of y. So SUM_g x_g J_g(y) = -f e1(x; y), e1 the sum over positions of one leg at x and
  the rest at y, and SUM_g X_g(x, y) = -f (e1(x; y) - e1(y; x)) = -f (e1 - e3), e3 = e1(y; x), three legs at x.
