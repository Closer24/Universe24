- **The escapement** (Big Pic, 6526070842). Between two ticks a clock's hand sits between two teeth. The tick is whole

- **The pointer** (Big Pic's candidate, 6528531569). The Node-clock reads where every clock's hand at it and at its six

  the round takes it in and as it gives it out, and sums the squares. Carried once at the teeth, that sum is the
  pointer. A square has no sign: the pointer reads how far each hand sits from the middle, not which way it leans.

  its sign. The squares stand, so the pointer stands where it stood, with no fold: the backward act parts the same

- moving a tooth by T/4 moves c by T/2, and (c + T/2)^2 = c^2 + c T + T^2/4, which is c^2 mod T when 4 divides T;
- moving it by T/8 moves c by T/4, and (c + T/4)^2 = c^2 + T/2 mod T when 16 divides T and c is odd;
- a tooth whose every move is a multiple of W/m, m dividing W, moves c by 2 j W/m, and then
  c'^2 - c^2 = (4 W/m) (j c + j^2 W/m), a multiple of 4 W/m. Mod W the multiples of 4 W/m are those of
  gcd(4 W/m, W) = (W/m) gcd(m, 4), so its square takes at most m div gcd(m, 4) residues mod W: m where m is odd, m/2
  where m is twice an odd number, m/4 where 4 divides m;
- the bound is not always reached (the end mathematician's M5, his g5r_algebra section 3). Where 3 divides m, the
  move's part mod 3 is a quadratic in j, which takes 2 of 3 values: at W/6 the most is 2 at every tooth, against the
  bound 3, and W/48 gives 8 against 12. At W/128, where W's last factor 2 is reached, 16 against 32;

  print them (the end readers' M1 and M6): the fourth reading's g5v4_algebra found them at 1,009 of 1,009 teeth of a
