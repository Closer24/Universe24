## In the great machine

1. **A click of a whole:** the great clock counts a whole quantum at one clock-node, and nothing is chosen.
2. **A click of a split:** the great clock chooses where a shared quantum lands, by the split's draw. This is the one choice, and the detector's click is of this kind.
3. **Local:** every act reads and writes one clock-node and its six meshes in one tick, so nothing reaches farther than one mesh a tick.

## The carriage

1. **The birth marks its two clock-nodes** with the pair, the end, the spoke toward the partner, and the twins open (equal).
2. **The first click reads its own clock-node alone.** Its open twins split half and half by the split's own draw, and it sends the closure (its quaternion and its answer) into the spoke toward the partner.
3. **The closure travels one mesh a tick,** back to the birth's clock-node and on to the partner's. In the bang the children click where they were laid, one mesh apart, so the path is the birth's own mesh, crossed in one tick.
4. **When it arrives, it closes the partner's twins** to the opposite twin, turned by the first quaternion, written on the partner's clock-node.
5. **The partner's click reads its own clock-node alone:** it turns its twins back by its detector's conjugate and splits them.
6. **Two clicks of one pair in one strike are one act on their one mesh,** the - end first by its role. The closure crosses that mesh inside the strike, just as a fitting reads and writes both ends of its mesh.
7. **The rewind:** each strike keeps the carriage it found, and the strike at -1 restores it.
