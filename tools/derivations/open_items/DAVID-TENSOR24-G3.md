## 1. In clocks

- **The held hand.** At every Node-clock the coupling family keeps one hand of rows, the train the frame finds by its
  character, (A1, A2) at part 1. The frame turned it a quarter a round with an own spring of twice its teeth. Here it
  turns not at all.
- **The hat.** Each Node-clock's hand is pulled toward a ring of rests: for two rows a circle, for four a sphere. If the
  hand strays in, the hat pushes it out; if it strays out, the hat pushes it in. On the ring itself no push lands, so a
  hand laid there stands still: no turn, no beat, no weight.
- **The size and the angle.** Moved in or out, the hand swings back and forth across the ring, a beat of its own, the
  size's. Moved along the ring, nothing pushes it back. The size's swing is felt along the ring too: the hat pulls the
  hand in or out along its own line, so a hand laid a little off its line swings in and out with the size.
  - With Meshes, neighbours pull the angle and a turn travels from Node-clock to Node-clock as a wave; a size swinging
    a little feeds those waves where the board holds one whose beat is near a quarter of the size's (half its
    frequency; a board too small, or grips too weak, holds none), and the waves feed the size back. A larger swing
    feeds waves near the size's own beat too.
  - Mesh-less, each Node-clock's hand moves along its own line and keeps its turn. On the act the counts are whole, a
    line through the middle meets few whole points, and a hand laid off the line wanders off it while the size swings.
- **The tie.** A tied train's spring is twice the tie times the square of the hand's size, so the tied train beats at a
  rate the hand's rest sets. The tied wave presses the hand in, a push of one sign that the hand's tooth gathers; until
  the tooth steps the hand's count, the tied train beats as a unit spring would. After that step the hand answers the
  tied wave.
