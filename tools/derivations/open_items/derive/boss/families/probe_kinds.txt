The families' pairings on the engine's own 24, kinds and tables (probe_kinds.py)
1. The 24 and the kinds, as the engine makes them from the star of six Spokes.
   candidates 216, the 24 kept by Rule3's weight: 24
   products of two of the 24 that are among the 24: 576 of 576
   the kinds' rows: {'A1': 1, 'A2': 1, 'E': 2, 'T1': 3, 'T2': 3}; the square's rows: 10

2. The Frobenius-Schur sum of every kind: SUM over the 24 of chi(g g), and its quotient by 24.
   +1 is a kind whose own pairing is symmetric (a whole basis, a real kind); -1 would be a spinor's, whose
   pairing is antisymmetric (Kramers); 0 a complex kind's, with no pairing of its own.
   A1     : SUM chi(g g) =   24, over 24: +1
   A2     : SUM chi(g g) =   24, over 24: +1
   E      : SUM chi(g g) =   24, over 24: +1
   T1     : SUM chi(g g) =   24, over 24: +1
   T2     : SUM chi(g g) =   24, over 24: +1
   square : SUM chi(g g) =  120, over 24: +5  (the square: five kinds, each +1)

3. The swap sign of every channel K x K -> m of the engine's tables (load.tables), the two inputs exchanged.
   The A1 channel is the pairing that stacks two hands of one kind into one count; its sign is the exchange
   sign of the family. The other channels say what two hands of one kind make together, and with which sign.
   A1 x A1 -> A1 (+)
   A2 x A2 -> A1 (+)
   E x E -> A1 (+), A2 (-), E (+)
   T1 x T1 -> A1 (+), E (+), T1 (-), T2 (+)
   T2 x T2 -> A1 (+), E (+), T1 (-), T2 (+)
   the A1 pairing's sign by kind: {'A1': '+', 'A2': '+', 'E': '+', 'T1': '+', 'T2': '+'}
   antisymmetric channels, all of them: [('E', 'A2'), ('T1', 'T1'), ('T2', 'T1')]; none lands in A1.
   the A1 pairing's swap sign equals the Frobenius-Schur sign on 5 of 5 kinds: True

4. The symmetric and antisymmetric squares of every kind, by characters (each multiplicity whole).
   A1: Sym^2 = A1;  Alt^2 = 0
   A2: Sym^2 = A1;  Alt^2 = 0
   E: Sym^2 = A1 + E;  Alt^2 = A2
   T1: Sym^2 = A1 + E + T2;  Alt^2 = T1
   T2: Sym^2 = A1 + E + T2;  Alt^2 = T1
   the four-vector A1 + T1: Sym^2 = 2 A1 + E + T1 + T2 (10 rows);  Alt^2 = 2 T1 (6 rows)
   the loader's square character (kinds.square, the flag of runner/load.trains_of) is the four-vector's Sym^2 on all 24 turns: True
   so the one symmetry the files flag today, the metric family's, is a symmetry of the indices (the symmetric
   square of the four-vector), not of the quanta; the antisymmetric square T1 + T1 (a field strength's six slots)
   the files would accept as a train of kinds [T1, T1] and flag nothing.

5. The one odd character of the 24: A2, the sign of the axes' permutation.
   A2 = +1 on 12 turns and -1 on 12; the +1 half closed under products: True (the tetrahedron's 12)
   the kinds of one row (the 24's characters into +-1): ['A1', 'A2']; so the 24 have one odd character and no other.
   A2 x A1 = A2
   A2 x A2 = A1
   A2 x E = E
   A2 x T1 = T2
   A2 x T2 = T1
   Under the 24 alone no kind carries a parity label (SYM24): A2 is odd under the quarter turns and the edge
   half turns of the cube itself, not under a reflection; it is the antisymmetric square of E (item 4).

6. The Gaussian hand (part 2): the 24 turn the kind's rows and leave the plane (re, im) alone.
   Pairings of two hands of a part-2 family that every turn keeps, counted by characters, then written out.
   A1 at part 2 (2 rows): pairings the 24 keep, by characters: 4 (3 symmetric, 1 antisymmetric)
      written out and checked on all 24 turns: ['dot x I (sym)', 'dot x sx (sym)', 'dot x sz (sym)', 'dot x J (anti)']
      kept also by the hand's own quarter turn (re, im) -> (-im, re): ['dot x I (sym)', 'dot x J (anti)']
      their sign under the exchange of the two hands: {'dot x I (sym)': '+', 'dot x sx (sym)': '+', 'dot x sz (sym)': '+', 'dot x J (anti)': '-'}
      their sign under the conjugation of the hands (im -> -im): {'dot x I (sym)': '+', 'dot x sx (sym)': '-', 'dot x sz (sym)': '+', 'dot x J (anti)': '-'}
   T1 at part 2 (6 rows): pairings the 24 keep, by characters: 4 (3 symmetric, 1 antisymmetric)
      written out and checked on all 24 turns: ['dot x I (sym)', 'dot x sx (sym)', 'dot x sz (sym)', 'dot x J (anti)']
      kept also by the hand's own quarter turn (re, im) -> (-im, re): ['dot x I (sym)', 'dot x J (anti)']
      their sign under the exchange of the two hands: {'dot x I (sym)': '+', 'dot x sx (sym)': '+', 'dot x sz (sym)': '+', 'dot x J (anti)': '-'}
      their sign under the conjugation of the hands (im -> -im): {'dot x I (sym)': '+', 'dot x sx (sym)': '-', 'dot x sz (sym)': '+', 'dot x J (anti)': '-'}
   So a Gaussian hand of any kind has exactly two pairings that its own turn keeps: the dot (symmetric, two
   hands stacking in step) and the wedge (antisymmetric, the turn of one hand against the other). The wedge
   of a hand with its kick is term 11's area moved, tau, and tau_M = -tau_L is the books (TERM11, Algebra,
   the common turn): the antisymmetric pairing is the click's give and take, and it is odd under the
   conjugation too, the sense of the turn (a quantum against its antiquantum), not an exchange sign of the family.

7. A vector hand at part 2 (re and im each a T1): the two whole readings its two parts and a gradient p afford.
   The winding, the plane's wedge summed through the dot, w = SUM_i (re_i p'_im,i - im_i p'_re,i) (term 11's area);
   the helicity, the epsilon table on the two parts then the dot with p, h = (re x im) . p (Big Pic's handed click).
   under the 24 turns: {'w kept': 24, 'h kept': 24, 'h negated': 0}; under the 24 reflections (every part polar, the g lift): {'w kept': 24, 'h kept': 0, 'h negated': 24}
   at the lay re = [3, -1, 2], im = [1, 4, -2]: the turning axis re x im = [-6, 8, 13]; it is 0 wherever re and im
   are parallel (a hand swinging on a line or turning in one component alone, as probe_act's lays do).
   So the winding is even under every reflection and the helicity odd under every one: a click that reads the
   winding (term 11) is achiral; a click reading one helicity would be handed on an achiral board. Which sign the
   reflection gives depends on the lift (polar or axial) that no kind carries (SYM24): the plan's to fix at G3.
