## 1. Clock (the simplest layer for this question; step 4, forward)

**The held hand.** The coupling family is a hand of two parts at every Node-clock that turns a quarter of a turn each
round: (re, im) -> (-im, re), the Gaussian unit. It is held not by standing but by turning: a count that stood would be
pushed off its place by the matter it ties (the matter's push on it is one-signed), while a turning hand's square
re^2 + im^2 stands still as its parts swing. Its stored winding D = now^2 - next x before is one: one quantum per
Node-clock. It never clicks: the click counts where two runs part, D given or taken, never D held (the owner's word of
15:3x on line 35 (4)'s carry).

**The anchor.** A whole-speed hand (a gear stepping at second order, now and before) tied to the held hand's square
is pulled back toward 0 by 2 g f |k|^2 times its count: it swings, and its beat is its mass. Light, untied, passes the
held hand and keeps no beat. Half-speed (spinor) clocks, two hands turning left and right, would be rocked by the
held hand through a linear tie: that gear is another agent's piece; its tie is named in section 2.6.

**The room.** The matter's push on the held hand is absorbed in the held hand's fine teeth as long as it stays below
half the teeth; one matter quantum per Node-clock fills that room exactly. Past it the turn bends (|k|^2 moves off 1,
the masses move with it: the vertex couples the trains); further still the windings passing into the held hand drive
its square so high that the tied hands' springs leave the step's window, and everything runs away (measured from
about four fifths of Gamma at one held quantum, with a boundary that is not sharp).

## 2. Algebra (step 3: from the big things; step 10: unify)

### 2.1 The theorem of the turn (whole numbers)

A gear with its own spring K and no other push steps a+ = 2a - b + floor((r - K a) / W). The step is a linear map of
(a, b) with whole coefficients, independent of the fine tooth, iff K is a multiple of W: K = c W gives a+ = (2 - c) a - b
with r untouched. The map is bounded iff |2 - c| <= 2; it turns (is periodic, neither still nor marginal) iff 2 - c is
-1, 0 or 1, periods 3, 4 and 6. Two parts stand in exact quadrature (the im part the re part delayed a quarter period)
only where the quarter period is a whole number of rounds: period 4, c = 2, K = 2 W. There (re, im) -> (-im, re), and
re^2 + im^2 and D = a^2 - (-b) b = a^2 + b^2 are constant to the bit. So **the held hand's own spring is twice its teeth:
the monomial (rest; rho, rho, 1, 1) on each of its two rows**, which pushes rho by -2 rest rho (the leg stands twice).
Probe 6 (i) tries the other factors: W/2 gives period 6 with |k|^2 moving 1, 2; 3W/2 period 3 with 1, 2; 2W the
marginal linear growth; 0 the free drift. Only W turns it with its square still.

The factor is the teeth themselves, 6 den Gamma^2, the loader's own number (load.geared: "the teeth at rest
T = 6 den Gamma^2"): no new number enters.

### 2.3 The tie (whole-speed hands)

For a hand X of kind K (rows x_y) and the held hand's rows k_rho, the plan lays, from the 24's tables (load.tables: the
row C of K x K -> A1, 1 for A1 and A2, the dot product 1, 1, 1 for T1 and T2, (2, -1, -1, 2) for E, which is twice the
Eisenstein norm; and the row 1 of A1 x A1 -> A1 and A2 x A2 -> A1 for the held hand):

    V = g f sum_yz C_yz x_y x_z sum_rho k_rho^2, the monomials (g f C_yz; y, z, rho, rho).

By contract.pushes each monomial pushes each leg by minus its factor times the other three, so

    J_{x_y} = -2 g f |k|^2 (C x)_y,    J_{k_rho} = -2 g f (x^T C x) k_rho.

Nothing else is laid: no leg of a gradient or a force (a plan's leg must be a value row, since in contract.pushes a leg's
index is both the reading gathered and the row its push lands on), nothing on the square (its rows are the clock's own
teeth and grips, read by load.geared through the square flag, and the time gear's spring is the owner's at G2), nothing
on light (section 2.6), no monomial linear in a hand (no tadpole), no term of the hands among themselves (allowed by

The linear tie g f (x.x) k_re, the one a standing held count would have carried, is laid in probe 5 (b) only to see what
the act does with it: its spring runs +, 0, -, 0 with the turn, a mirror well two rounds in four.

### 2.5 The room, in whole numbers

The matter pushes each part of the held hand by -2 g f (x.x) k_rho. The turn stays exact iff
floor((r - 2 g f (x.x) k_rho) / W) = -2 k_rho + 2 k_rho, i.e. the fine tooth stays within [0, W) under the pushes between
two reversals of the part's sign: born at the mid tooth, with k = (1, 0) each part is pushed once between reversals,
and the room is 2 g f (x.x) < W / 2, which is

    g (x.x) < Gamma / 4   at the Node-clock, summed over every tied row (probe 6 (iv): the tooth walks by exactly
    one round's push, 2 g A^2 / Gamma of W; probe 6 (v): two trains share the room; probe 2 F: exact at g A^2 = 196,
    off from round 1 at 256).

With k = (q, 0) the push is q times larger and the room is Gamma / (4 q); with k = (1, 1) each part keeps its sign two
rounds running (the turn goes + + - -) and the room halves to Gamma / 8 (probe 7: off at g A^2 = 144 for (1, 1), (2, 0)
and (3, 0)). Near the edge the tooth also walks slowly with the matter's swing and may reach it after hundreds of
rounds (probe 2 B: at A^2 = Gamma / 8, off at rounds 341, 1,281 and 4,961 for Gamma 800, 3,200 and 12,800).

Past the room the turn bends: |k|^2 moves among 0, 1, 2 (the held hand gives and takes windings, which is the vertex
coupling the trains) and the tied hands' beats move with it; the motion stays bounded to about g (x.x) = 0.6 Gamma
at |k|^2 = 1 (probe 2 F: bounded over 3,000 rounds at 256 to 576), and from about 0.78 Gamma the energy passing into
the held hand drives |k|^2 past 2 Gamma, the tied hand's spring past 4 W, and everything runs away (probe 2 F: 784 and
above). The boundary is not sharp (probe 7: with (1, 1) a runaway at 256 and a bounded run at 400 within 3,000 rounds).
A tied hand laid moving 2 counts a round carries about four quanta of energy and runs away at once (probe 2 E).

One matter quantum sits at the room's edge: a tied hand holding D_x = A^2 sin^2 omega = 1 has A^2 = Gamma / (2 g |k|^2),
its mean x.x is half that, and its push on the held hand's largest part averages g f A^2 max|k_rho| = W max|k_rho| / (2 |k|^2):
at k = (1, 0) exactly W / 2, the room's edge, whatever g and f are. So **the held hand at one quantum holds about one
quantum of tied matter per Node-clock, and at (q, 0) about q**; the ratio is the act's, with no strength in it. Light,
untied, pushes nothing: a light hand at 1,000 counts moving 50 a round leaves |k|^2 at 1 (probe 7).

### 2.6 Which hands tie, and the half-speed tie, named

families tie to k follows with no row apart: those whose two hands differ in a turn the held hand carries. Light's
turn is not among them." With the held hand (A1, A2), the turn it carries is the A2 sign (its sense flips under the odd

    V = g f Re(conj(L) k R) = g f [ k_re (L_re R_re + L_im R_im) + k_im (L_im R_re - L_re R_im) ],
    the monomials (g f; L_re, R_re, k_re, 1), (g f; L_im, R_im, k_re, 1), (g f; L_im, R_re, k_im, 1), (-g f; L_re, R_im, k_im, 1),

A1 part of conj(L) R tie to k_re and its A2 part to k_im, which is the pair rule above. Its stepping at first order
