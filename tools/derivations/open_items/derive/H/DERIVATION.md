## 1. In the clocks

**The gear and its going.** A gear is a coarse wheel (the notch) and a fine wheel (the fine tooth r of w teeth). Its stride is read from a home on the fine wheel, the origin: P = w (now - before) + r - origin. A dial of w teeth has its middle at w / 2. At an odd w the middle is a tooth, (w - 1) / 2; at an even w it falls between two teeth, w / 2 - 1 and w / 2, and the gear must stand its origin on one of the two. The going names which: forward (b = 0) the upper, w div 2, the head's mid tooth; reversed (b = 1) the lower, (w - 1) div 2. The going is a dial of two teeth, the ratchet's: each reversal of the gear (time's, at the turnover, and its own sign's, under a turn of the 24 that negates it) advances it by one tooth, and its whole turn (two reversals) leaves as a carry, the ratchet's count.

**The turnover.** At a Node-clock that turns over, every gear's two notches are exchanged and every fine tooth is reflected to w - 1 - r, the going advanced. The reflection w - 1 - r is the head's own going's flip (acts.flip, the carry at -1 conjugated by it): it is the one reflection that runs Rule3's carry back exactly (P1). With the origin moved to the other middle tooth, the stride reads exactly -P, so a gear at rest stays at rest in its new going. A tie of the carry (the stride exactly on a half turn of the wheel, at an even count) rounds toward the going: up forward, down reversed, so that the reversed run rounds back exactly what the forward run rounded.

**The trigger.** The time gear's three notches (the notch before, the notch now, the step's output) are read in either order alike: the top (the middle at or above both, above one) and the bottom (the middle at or below both, below one) are each the same read backward as forward. So the turnover's whether is reversal-symmetric, and the tick run backward is the tick run forward from the turned-over state. The bottom, however, also fires at every setting: every Node-clock starts with its time gear at rest (the notch before equal to the notch now), so any first step, up or down, is a bottom or a top; the turned-over setting is again at rest, and its reversed step fires again. The great clock never leaves its setting. The bang's own bottom needs no trigger: at a setting at rest, the retrace after the top arrives at the setting and runs on into the same rise, by the step alone.

**The rate's dial.** The rate of a Node-clock is a dial whose teeth count is the full rate, the great clock's own: the load counts slowings, and a stretch below the full rate's would make the clock run faster than the great clock. When the rate would complete that whole turn, the turn leaves as the turnover, and the clock retraces its fall. No load is read below 0, because none is ever reached.

**The light cone.** A Node-clock's six Meshes together may turn at most as the vacuum's six (Rule3's own edge: the spring plus twice the six grips at most four teeth). The Meshes' tensions share that whole among the six Spokes; where their sum would pass it, the excess slows the Node-clock's reading of its Meshes, the lapse, so that the six together hold the vacuum's whole. No tension is held in a range.

**The drive.** The time gear's drive lands whole: its numerator is read alike in both goings and its carry is reversed by the flip, so nothing of it needs a hold. Where the stretch would fall through the full rate within one tick, the clock turns over and no driven carry is kept there.

## 4. The proofs

**P1 (the reflection that runs a carry back is the flip, uniquely).** A carry (q, r') = Rule3(u + r, w) with u read alike in both goings. A reflection tau of the fine tooth runs it back if, for every u and r in [0, w), Rule3(u + tau(r'), w) = (q, r'') with tau(r'') = r. Writing u + r = q w + r', u + tau(r') = q w + (r' - r + tau(r')); the quotient is q for every r in [0, w) only if 0 <= r' - r + tau(r') <= w - 1 for r = 0 and r = w - 1, so w - 1 - r' <= tau(r') <= w - 1 - r'. So tau(x) = w - 1 - x, and then r'' = w - 1 - r and tau(r'') = r. PROVED. The mirror (2 (w div 2) - x) mod w equals w - x at an even w for x >= 1 and 0 at x = 0: it runs the carry back except where r = 0 or r' = 0 (CHECKED: the drive's fine tooth, 1,454 failures in about 10,000 at even D, 0 at odd D, 0 with the flip).

**P2 (the going: T T = 1, T S T = S^-1, N S = S N at every teeth count).** For every w >= 1, o(w, 0) + o(w, 1) = w div 2 + (w - 1) div 2 = w - 1 (at w = 2m: m + m - 1; at w = 2m + 1: m + m). Then:
- T T = 1: w - 1 - (w - 1 - r) = r and the going advanced twice is b (with its carry booked).
- P(T x) = -P(x): w (before - now) + (w - 1 - r) - o(w, 1 - b) = -w (now - before) - r + o(w, b) = -P.
- The carry's rounding in the reversed going is the negated rounding: for every n and w, (-n + w - 1) div w = -(n div w) (the identity ceil((n - w + 1) / w) = n div w). With n = P + o(w, b): (-P + o(w, 1 - b)) div w = (-(P + o(w, b)) + w - 1) div w = -((P + o(w, b)) div w), and the remainders are w - 1 - ((P + o(w, b)) mod w).
- T S T = S^-1 from teeth w to w+ (the reversed step runs w+ to w, the regulator's own reversal giving the teeth back, since the mean teeth (T div 2)(X- + X) is symmetric in the two notches): T y = (now_x, now_y, w+ - 1 - r+, 1 - b) with stride -P+; the reversed step adds the same s F + K (read from the notch now, now_x, and T-even), giving -P; its carry in the going 1 - b at w gives the quotient -(now_x - before_x) and the remainder w - 1 - r: the state T x.
