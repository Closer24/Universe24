## 4. The key conflict: where the order of ticks lives

**THE RULING (for the plan, in plain words).** Each Node-clock holds one more integer, its beat count c. Its next beat is due when each of its six neighbours stands at a count at least c; the engine computes the due bit as the product of six nonneg(c_nb - c), read across its six Meshes (a carry on counts, local, no if). Every due beat the runner allows advances c by one and turns the rate dial by Rule3, (k, ph') = carry(ph, Gamma, W). The gears step only where k is 1; where k is 0 ("no tick this tick", always for a stopped clock) the gears, the two slots and their local times tb and tn stand. A Node-clock at count c reads each neighbour as of c: the neighbour's now slot where its tn is at most c, else its before slot (one choice by one bit, written once, in link). The runner chooses only which due beats fire. The main run fires every Node-clock in every sweep: the synchronous, whole-board pass, the head's order, the whole-board bang. The asynchronous run is an option on the same rule: random masks (the order test, from stage 3) and, for huge boards, regions with halos. The engine sees counts and slots, never a round, a mode or a region. A hold the runner needs (the bang's barrier in the option) is passed as a "no tick" on the rate bit, never as a withheld beat.

- **(a) Where the order lives: CORRECTED on both sides.** The due bit belongs in the engine (Sofer 4.3): it is the Node-clock's own law (line 98, "each Node-clock stepping only when the arrivals it needs from its six neighbours stand"), a local carry on its Meshes' counts. The schedule, which due beats fire, belongs to the runner (design 4; Sofer 4.4 agrees). The design's "the engine sees no round" stands: c is the Node-clock's own count, equal to the runner's round only in the synchronous run.
- **(b) One count against tb and tn: CORRECTED, all three kept.** Once rates part, c (beats) and tn (the tick that wrote the now slot) differ: a "no tick" advances c and leaves tn. Sofer 4.6's slot "chosen by the count difference" reads a stale slot after a "no tick": the probe's order test fails 0 of 40 when rates part; read by tn it passes 40 of 40. tb serves lin only. The state is 4G + H + 5 integers per Node-clock (the design's 4G + H + 4 and c), fixed for the run (line 98).
- **(c) The arrivals rule against the beat: CONFIRMED as one rule.** The design's beat (every Node-clock beats once a round) is Sofer's rule with every due beat fired. The design's regions ("round n of a region starts once its adjacent regions have finished round n - 1") are the same rule at a region's grain, so the huge-board runner adds no law. The design's refutation of the next-tick order (its point 2) does not touch Sofer's rule, which reads counts, not next-tick times. Determinacy (Kahn's): the state at count t is a function of the start within t Meshes, whatever the order.
- **(d) A stopped clock: Sofer as written REFUTED, the corrected rule CONFIRMED.** Sofer 4.4 writes new = go x stepped + (1 - go) x old with go = due x allowed x (the rate bit), so a "no tick" advances neither the count nor the dial: a stopped clock's count stays, its neighbours stop one count ahead, theirs two, and the board freezes within its diameter (probe: a ring of 10, one clock at rate 0, the counts stay between 0 and 5 for ever), against Postulate 4 (line 34, "no Node-clock stays frozen for ever") and the design's point 3, which the owner was told. Also as written, a dial whose bit is 0 never turns again. With the beat advancing c and the dial at every allowed due beat, the stopped clock blocks no one (probe: every count reaches 30; 40 of 40 random schedules equal the synchronous pass). Sofer 4.3's rewind rule (step back when no neighbour is ahead) stands: k at the beat being undone is 1 exactly where tn = c, so the dial runs back by Rule3 with its remainder.
- **(e) Synchronous main run against "asynchronous from the first stage": CORRECTED to "asynchronous-ready from the first stage".** The count, the due bit and the allowed mask stand in the first beat written (stage 2), and the order test runs from stage 3, so no signature changes late (Sofer 4.8's reason, kept). The main run is synchronous, the whole board one big bang (the owner's 15:0x); the asynchronous runner is an option, built at stage 10.

Two of Sofer 4 stand unchanged: a Node-clock never writes into a neighbour and the word is the Mesh's odd number (4.1, 4.7: a two-ended click is two one-ended acts); the two slots are the channel's whole buffer (4.2), which holds only if everything a Node-clock reads across a Mesh is a notch with its before slot. ## 7. The width

- The integers are Python integers in numpy object arrays, dtype=object in every array of the state and the layout. The probe (numpy 2.5.3): the carry is exact at 112 bits and beyond (q d + r = n, 0 <= r < d, negatives included); einsum and the scatter-add (np.add.at) work on object arrays; an int64 array wraps silently (2^62 x 4 gives 0), so int64, and any fixed-width dtype, is refused. numpy has no 128-bit integer, and 112 bits leave no margin under it anyway.
- The width is a key of the universe file, no limit of the engine (line 106: no bound): the files' checker reads it, and a reading reports the largest bit length in each record; no act compares against it, clamps or raises.
- The engine never makes an array without dtype=object; float() and np.float anywhere is refused by the gate (design 4).
- Speed for huge boards is the runner's, not the engine's: regions over processes. A fixed-width form (limbs in int64 whose overflow is a carry into the next limb, law 11's own picture of a bound) is a later option, behind the same signatures; it is not built in this plan.

## 8. Sofer's five meeting points

- **(i) Law 9's draw "in the order of their values" (line 1079): RESOLVED inside the closed list, no law changed.** A sort is not needed for the order: the rank of each digit is a count, rank_i = the sum over j of [v_j < v_i] + the sum over j < i of [v_j = v_i], each bracket a product of nonneg bits; the permutation matrix is P[p, i] = equality(rank_i, p); the composed number is the sum over p of place[p] times (P v)[p], the place values the mixed radix's products, from the bases. Sums, products and carries only, no loop and no branch, n n bits for n digits (n is fixed: the Node-clock's and its six Meshes' digits). Probe: equal to the sorted composition and independent of the digits' order on 300 random draws, so alike images draw alike (line 94). The copy rule (a remainder equal to an earlier one's, or w less it, enters nothing) is the same equality bits with the triangular mask. Sofer 6 item 3 is answered: neither a comparison network nor a move to the readings. One caution for David: the lattice's F = gcd(w, D_i, R_ij) is a loop of carries; it is on the list only as a layout integer computed at load (if its terms are file integers); if any term is state, the draw's lattice is OPEN with OPEN 10.
- **(ii) The draw's one whether and the take's loop: CONFIRMED and CORRECTED.** The one whether is one carry's quotient read as a bit, nonneg(q - 1) for q >= 0; SCRIBE-GATE-1.md's "the one comparison" is corrected to that (Sofer 6 item 2). The take (one whole unit of the light from the Node-clock and its six Spokes) is no loop: the amount from place j is min(avail_j, max(0, need - prefix_j)), with prefix_j a contraction with the triangular ones table and min and max built from nonneg; the places taken in the rank order of (i), not in a typed Spoke order, so the 24 hold. Its formula is David's (OPEN 14); the list holds it.
- **(iii) One hand per quantum against fixed arrays (item (a)): OPEN, the owner's; the architecture is neutral.** Line 98 fixes the integers per Node-clock; a list that grows at run time is refused. The row count enters only the layout (G), never a signature, so whichever the owner rules (Sofer's slots by the kind's cap, or one hand a family whose winding counts its quanta), no function changes. Fixed rows that cap the quanta are a bound (law 11) unless the cap is a clock's carry; that is the owner's to weigh at 16:00.
- **(iv) The twin hands' ring: CORRECTED, a structural finding for David (OPEN 3).** The half turns are exact because their lifts (Q8: i, j, k) act on the four integer rows as signed permutations, and a signed permutation commutes with an element-wise carry whose tie rule is odd. The third turns cannot: if every lift in 2T acted on the two spinor coordinates by monomial matrices, the lines would be permuted by an index-2 subgroup of 2T, and 2T = SL(2,3) has none; over the four integer rows, the only signed-permutation action of 2T with the centre as -1 is induced from Z6 and carries the other spinor pair (E1/2 x omega and its conjugate), not E1/2. So no ring and no basis of four or eight rows (Z[i] or Z[zeta8]) lets element-wise carries keep the 24 bit for bit on the twin hands: Z[zeta8] holds the lifts exactly but not as signed permutations. Probe (twin_probe.py, exact): the induced signed-permutation character and E1/2's on four rows differ on all 16 lifts of the third turns (-1 against 2). The routes are David's: carries on what the 24 permutes (the twin hand's axis form (rho, S), kinds A1 and T1), or the departure kept by name (Sofer 6 item 7). No twin train loads before it closes (stage 6).
