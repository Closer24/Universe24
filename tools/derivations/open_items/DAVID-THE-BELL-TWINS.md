## The derivation

1. **A detector is a turn of the twins.** A hand turns by a Gaussian integer: its two gears are mixed by integers. The twins, up and down, are the doublet, and the doublet turns by a quaternion of integers (a, b, c, d). The up twin u and the down twin v go to (a - di) u + (-c - bi) v and (c - bi) u + (a + di) v, and their winds are multiplied by a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + d^2. For example, (12, 0, 5, 0) turns the twins' axis z to (120, 0, 119) over 169.
2. **The click is the split.** After its detector's turn, the click splits the twins by the split's own draw: the up twin wins where c (W_up + W_down) < room W_up, the winds being the twins' squared Gaussians.
3. **The first child's twins stand equal,** as half of one singlet, so its split is half and half in every turn. Its answer closes the partner's twins to the opposite twin, turned by the first detector's quaternion.
4. **The partner's detector turns those twins back by its own quaternion's conjugate, and splits them.** Born's share comes out of the twins' squared Gaussians alone: there is no cosine, no dot product and no root in the code.
5. **The settings.** The - end turns z to x with (1, 0, 1, 0), or keeps z with (1, 0, 0, 0). The + end turns z to (120, 0, 119) over 169 with (12, 0, 5, 0), or to (-120, 0, 119) over 169 with (12, 0, -5, 0). An exact 45 degrees needs a root, so the + end's directions are the nearest rational ones, at 44.76 and 45.24 degrees from the - end's. Born's exact cells are then -120/169, +120/169, -119/169 and -119/169, so S = -478/169 = -2.8284, 0.00003 below Tsirelson's.
