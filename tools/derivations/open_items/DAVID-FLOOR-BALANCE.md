### 2.4 The square form, reversed bands, and which case holds

- **Big Pic's candidate** (6542519437 item 6): every form of the law is the square of its own cell's value. A band is

  two clocks t/2, so each clock's own spring is half the sum of its bands, laid by no one. The form is the sum of the
  bands' squares, the Gram form of their incidence ((t/2)(D + A) for equal plain bands, D the clocks' counts of bands),
  never below 0: the bound holds on every structure.
- **Which case holds is the structure's (DERIVED).** On a connected group the form has a free swing exactly when some x
  not 0 has x_i = -sigma x_j on every band (sigma = 1 for a plain band, -1 for a reversed one), that is, when around
  every loop the product of the -sigma is 1: every loop's parity and reversal agree. That is Harary's balance of the
  network signed by -sigma (F. Harary, "On the notion of balance of a signed graph", Michigan Math. J. 2 (1953)
  143-146): an even loop carries an even number of reversed bands, and an odd loop an odd number. Then the group sits at
  the floor's edge with one free swing, and a group with no loop always does. It holds by its springs alone exactly when
  some loop's parity and reversal disagree: frustration.

  0 hold t/2 each and its common swing 2t. The plain triangle is the smallest frustrated loop. With one band reversed,
  an odd loop sits at the edge and an even loop holds; an even loop with every band reversed sits at the edge.
- **The ring rule** (ring_balance.py, exact, rings of 3 to 10 clocks): under the square form a ring's form is
