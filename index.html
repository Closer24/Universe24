Universe24

The aim of Universe24 is to represent reality as it is, inside its own simulator, the engine: a lattice of Nodes stepped by one rule in whole numbers and read through clicks, a world that shows what nature shows.

What this is

What if the universe is just numbers? Every point of space holds whole numbers and follows one simple rule with its six neighbours. From this come the speed of light, clocks that run slow near matter and in motion, a direction of time only where something is measured, and a black hole's shadow the size telescopes measured, within their error. Every step runs back exactly.

The paper's one claim is that Universe24 helps compute things in nature. Every number in the first paper is reproduced by a script in the repository, and the time paper's build checks every integer of its text against its run, so anyone with a computer can run the same counting and read the same numbers.

What is new here. The rule is one line of integer arithmetic on a cubic lattice, the simplest a local Node can do in whole numbers: two levels in time, six arrivals, one division, the remainder kept. It carries no free number but the declared pairs, the level weight and the vacuum content, and what a world needs beyond it is declared by name. From that line come, in lattice form, the Klein-Gordon equation with Schroedinger's slow limit under three conditions, Planck's relation in the band's form, Lorentz's composition of velocities at c exactly, clocks slowed in motion and near matter in the known forms at first order, and, under one assumption fixed by two measurements, the weak-field metric with its redshift and the bending of light. No Planck constant is put in: one quantum is one unit of T sin omega_0 with T the file's integer, the mass is one integer, and light is born as the beat of two rotations. On the same board, under the declared counting rule, interference reads as clicks, one quantum is one click and never two, and Bell's and GHZ's correlations follow; in the law, on a hypothesis by name (a family of two planes), the two beams of Stern and Gerlach and g = 2 are derived, outside the paper and with no world run. Every step runs back exactly, bit for bit, so the board has no arrow of time, and the click alone has one. What it does not do, plainly: the probability rule of measurement is declared, not derived; spin one half is a declared shape; helium's correlation energy is a miss the paper names, open in further research; the anomalous moment is a finding by name, its lattice part computed and negligible, its loop part open.

Run it yourself

The whole project is open: the engine, the law, the papers and the runs the papers report stand in the repository on GitHub. With Python 3.14 or later, six commands from a downloaded copy run the two-slit experiment's world, read its detectors' counts beside the prediction written down before the first run, and draw the board frame by frame: see Run it in the README.

The experiments

Everything on these pages comes from whole numbers at each point of space stepped by one rule, Rule 3, and the declared counting rule; nothing is drawn by hand. Rule 3 is one integer line: from a point's two numbers, the six arrivals from its neighbours and its kept remainder it forms one sum, divides once, and keeps the new remainder. The runs were recorded and embedded in their pages; the levels on them are the integers the engine wrote, and a page computes nothing beyond the sums its bars and graphs draw. Each link opens the experiment's own page in a new tab. Press Play, drag the slider. The numbers are the lattice's own, not nature's.

The two slits

The paper's experiment: light through two gaps in a wall, with detectors waiting along a screen read in twelve regions, 130 intervals of Rule 3. The bars are the screen's twelve regions, the record's share in whole quanta, 203 by the shares, beside the detectors' clicks, 500 at the window 1 (29, 37, 49, 36, 12, 63, 83, 26, 34, 51, 42, 38), in the counting window under the click's read of the board (2026-10-20; 285 clicks at the clean engine's one seed, history); the dashed curve is the blind row, 633 in the record's own unit (273 before 3.0, history), written down before the first run from the Huygens sum of the two openings, the pattern they should give. The three high regions of the bars stand at 5, 6 and 9 against the blind's 2, 6 and 9, the lows at 4 and 7 against the blind's 4 and 8: interference, read as clicks, out of whole numbers alone. The clicks are the measurement; the bars are what the page draws from them. Watch the board frame by frame and see the light from the two gaps meet. The law's own line for this row, 284.7, was computed after the run and stands in the paper. Approved experiment.

Which way

Which gap did the light pass? The same wall is read three ways. With both gaps open the screen's twelve regions read [15, 13, 21, 15, 7, 26, 33, 8, 11, 24, 18, 12], 203 by the shares, the two slits' world byte for byte; with the lower gap closed they read [0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 14, 26, 12, 21, 14, 12, 13], 112, one gap's pattern. With both gaps open and a channel detector behind the lower gap taking everything that passes it, the channel reads 119 and the screen 109, in the row [0, 0, 0, 0, 119, 13, 25, 11, 20, 15, 11, 14]: the one-gap row within the draw's scatter, and the two-slit fringes are gone, the central maximum at the region 6 down from 33 to 25, the blind's minimum at the region 8 filled from 11 to 20. In nature this is the which-way experiment, where knowing the path erases the interference; on the board no rule is added to do it, the channel's detector credits the quanta that pass it, so they never reach the screen. These are the pages' readings under the click's read of the board (2026-10-20, the bars by the shares as the pages draw them; the clean engine's one-seed rows of 2026-10, history: [22, 21, 27, 16, 9, 36, 56, 11, 12, 26, 24, 25] at 285, [0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 19, 32, 15, 26, 17, 16, 17] at 142, the channel 149 and the screen 137 in [0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 18, 35, 12, 22, 23, 14, 13]). The packet emission

How does an atom give its light? On a board of 627 Nodes along one axis, one atom in its excited state emits its one quantum at the interval 48, its window's close, with no draw: alone in the exact dark the lifetime is the window, and the quantum is laid as a packet along +x, the law's first declared direction; the page draws the packet's Nodes as it goes, its front at the far face by 60. Twenty-eight Links along the axis, at the reach the law's line derives, a region named reach waits for it; the blind expected one click there. The region credits nothing within the run's 168 intervals: the packet is at the floor there, the count 1 a miss the folder names and does not adjust. The page shows an emission as a travelling packet, and a far detector that does not click. World.

The resonance

Who can take the light an atom gives? On a ring of 48 Nodes a giver atom and a taker atom stand apart; in the resonant world the taker is tuned to the giver's frequency, in the detuned world it is tuned away. The giver emits at 48, the window's close in the exact dark, and the pulse runs the ring; the tuned and the detuned taker are lit at 66 and their first close falls at 161, beyond the run of 96, so neither takes within it, the two runs one to the frame; the pair cannot be run to that close as declared, the giver's own second close at 143 reading its circulating light below the draw's total (a two-Node body at the exact vacuum, refused by name); the tuned against the detuned take is the next round's, the bodies re-declared over three Nodes as the chain's (2026-10-21). The readings of the generator's day, 35 of 100 seeds taking in the resonant world and 0 of 100 in the detuned, stand in the folder's blind_and_reading.md as history. The born light's frequency is read by who can take it: this is the beat of two levels, the law's line "the wavelength is the giver's beat". World.

Two bodies fall toward each other

Two bodies of matter are laid at rest on a board of 49 x 17 x 17 Nodes, 24 Links apart, with no velocity given, and Rule 3 runs for 900 intervals. The board carries a second row of numbers, the content, declared in the universe file: each body writes a well into it, and where the content is deep the clock runs slow. For about 300 intervals little moves. Then the two drift toward each other, faster and faster, their distance falling under a third of its start, and they fall into one well, where they breathe as one body. No force is added: the fall is Rule 3 alone, stepped on a board where the clocks differ. Watch the two wells on the content strip draw together. Lattice reading, no blind.

A bound body in motion

One body is pushed once, at the start, along an axis, and then left to the rule for 200 intervals. It moves at 0.0948 Links per interval, 2 percent under the speed its band predicts, and it stays whole: at every interval at most one quantum stands beyond a sphere of radius 9 around its centre. The level at its centre changes sign every 3.85 intervals, the half period of its rest rotation: the body's own clock, ticking as it travels, the thing the law calls time. The push was put in by hand once; everything after it is the rule's. Lattice reading, no blind.

The atom

The real atom first: the proton is one Node holding one quantum of a frozen plane, its sign writing a well into the holder's row, and the electron is one quantum of a charged plane laid standing in that well at the read 3 on a cube of 11 Nodes a side, its pair turning at every Node by the level's rate and the well's, 1.387 per interval at the centre and 0.887 one Link out. The electron laid in the proton's well reads its own walk: it stands 400 intervals to the overlap 0.990 with its lay at the rotation 0.6638 against the exact frame's 0.66374, and the 1s and the 2s laid together as one quantum breathe at the centre at 0.18803 per interval against the lay's line 0.1794 and the builder's 0.18072, within one bin of 400 intervals: the atom's line is the difference of two standing rates, read from the board, no light family in the universe to carry it. The standing-lay worlds beside it, at the read 1: the ground level on a cube of 25 Nodes, one quantum spread with the well's own angle at each Node and Rule 3 run for 120 intervals with nothing added, the cloud standing (its share within four Links of the proton 0.446 at the lay and 0.440 after 100 intervals, the peak at the centre at every interval, the whole cloud rotating as one clock at 0.835 per interval against a free electron's 0.841, the binding read as a redshift of the rotation); the free Gaussian lump on the same proton breathing inward instead, no fixed point; at T = 72,000,000 on a cube of 33 Nodes a side the 1s and the 2p standing 60 intervals each (the rotations 0.7828 and 0.8388, the 2p's node plane at zero exactly), and their sum the line, run for 224 intervals, the centre of charge swinging along x with the period 112.4 intervals against the blind's 112. The lattice's integers are the toy's, nothing nature's: its fine-structure constant is 0.138 at T = 36,000,000, nineteen times nature's, and 0.276 at 72,000,000. Lattice reading for the levels and the line.

The singlet's two clicks

One quantum of a two-plane family declared antisymmetric under exchange is laid at the centre of a chain of 33 Nodes as two packets, half a quantum each, travelling to the two ends, where two detectors wait, each declaring an axis and both reading the parts as they are, the plain identity pattern: the antisymmetric pair is the family's one word and no detector's. Over 40 intervals the two packets reach the two regions; at the window's close the two detectors draw one after another, each on its own region's read, the first writing its realised port into the record at its birth along the record's own light cone and the second reading the rewritten record, the pair's amplitude the determinant of the two sides' parts, and the two click once each with one combination realised. At the four Bell settings the joint shares give E = -119/169, 119/169, -120/169, -120/169, the singlet's -cos of the angle between the axes exactly, and S = 478/169 = 2.828, above the classical bound 2, with every marginal 1/2 and the two sides never in the same port at equal axes: the numbers of the earlier declaration, the crossed pattern [[0, 1], [-1, 0]] on one detector, to the digit, the determinant at two parts being that pattern term by term. On the same waves, a probe let each detector draw alone on what reaches it: S = 1.42; fixed the pair's direction at the emission and drew locally: S = 1.41; read each side's favoured port with no draw: S = 2 exactly. The write at the birth through the record's own past is the one non-local act, and it alone gives the correlation; a draw at the click adds noise to a local picture and never correlation. The page shows the (a, b) world's run with the gate's reading beside its blind. The page is the click's page: the first detector draws alone on its own region's read, the record's own light cone runs back to its birth, where the realised port is written into the record, and forward to its partner with the realised term, the rest of the board neither read nor written, the partner draws alone on the rewritten record; the look shows the passes with the cone shaded, shrinking Link by Link to the lay's Nodes and growing back. Four more worlds are born of one emission: an atom at the Node 12 gives the pair as a source in time on both parts alike at the interval its own draw picks, the left region clicks and writes at the birth, and the board carries the realised term to the right region, which clicks in the next window. The gates, a results page

Two checks whose result is a number and not a motion, Bell and GHZ (the quantum Zeno and the pulsed Zeno until 2026-10-20, their worlds deleted at the ninth, their numbers standing at the tag v1.1.0 and the archive), each with its blind, written before the run, beside the engine's reading. Bell's gate: at four settings the two sides' correlation reads E = 119/169, -119/169, 120/169, 120/169 and S = 478/169 = 2.8284, above the classical bound 2 and below Tsirelson's 2 sqrt 2 by 2.5 x 10^-5, the blind's fraction exactly. The GHZ gate: the three-sided credit -1, -1, -1, +1 at the four settings x y y, y x y, y y x and x x x, M = -4, where the local bounds are -2 to +2. The counting rule is declared, in the law's own word, and the correlations follow from it on the board: the gates are the engine's checks of its own counting rule against the blind written before the run, not experiments of nature. The page shows blind beside result for every number. The GHZ gate's (x, x, x) page shows the click: its three detectors draw one after another, each alone at its own place, the record's own light cone running back to its birth, where each realised port is written into the record, and forward to the next with the realised term between the draws, the rest of the board neither read nor written, the same E = +1; its page shows the passes with the cone shaded on the board seen from above.
