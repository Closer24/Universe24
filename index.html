Universe24

The aim of Universe24 is to represent reality as it is, inside its own simulator, the engine: a lattice of Nodes stepped by one rule in whole numbers and read through clicks, a world that shows what nature shows.

What this is

What if the universe is just numbers? Every point of space holds whole numbers and follows one simple rule with its six neighbours. From this come the speed of light, clocks that run slow near matter and in motion, a direction of time only where something is measured, and a black hole's shadow the size telescopes measured, within their error. Every step runs back exactly.

The paper's one claim is that Universe24 helps compute things in nature. Every number in the first paper is reproduced by a script in the repository, and the time paper's build checks every integer of its text against its run, so anyone with a computer can run the same counting and read the same numbers.

See it move

The board on these three pages runs on whole numbers and one rule. The engine steps every point of the board with Rule3, one integer line: from a point's two numbers, the six arrivals from its neighbours and its kept remainder it forms one sum, divides once, and keeps the new remainder. A holder of content is declared in the universe file, no force, no velocity law and no wave equation; the runs were recorded and embedded in their pages, the levels on them are the integers the engine wrote, and a page computes nothing beyond the sums its bars and graphs draw. Each link opens in a new tab. Press Play, drag the slider. The numbers are the lattice's own, not nature's.

  • Two bodies fall toward each other. Two bodies of matter are laid at rest on a board of 49 x 17 x 17 Links, 24 Links apart, with no velocity given, and Rule3 runs for 900 intervals. The board carries a second row of numbers, the content, declared in the universe file: each body writes a well into it, and where the content is deep the clock runs slow. For about 300 intervals little moves. Then the two drift toward each other, faster and faster, their distance falling under a third of its start, and they fall into one well, where they breathe as one body. Nothing pulls: the fall is the same integer line, stepped on a board where the clocks differ. Watch the two wells on the content strip draw together.
  • A bound body in motion. One body is pushed once, at the start, along an axis, and then left to the rule for 200 intervals. It moves at 0.0948 Links per interval, 2 percent under the speed its band predicts, and it stays whole: at every interval at most one quantum stands beyond a sphere of radius 9 around its centre. The level at its centre changes sign every 3.85 intervals, the half period of its rest rotation: the body's own clock, ticking as it travels, the thing the law calls time. The push was put in by hand once; everything after it is the rule's.
  • The two slits. The paper's experiment: light through two slits, with detectors waiting along a screen, 130 intervals of Rule3. The bars are the detectors' clicks per region of the screen, 285 in the counting window; the dashed curve is the blind row, 273 written down before the first run from light's line in real arithmetic, the pattern the two openings should give. The three high regions of the bars stand where the blind has them, the lows at the regions 4 and 7 against the blind's 4 and 8: interference, read as clicks, out of whole numbers alone. Watch the board frame by frame and see the light from the two gaps meet. The law's own line for this row, 284.7, was computed after the run and stands in the paper.

Run it yourself

The whole project is open: the engine, the law, the papers and the runs the papers report stand in the repository on GitHub. With Python 3.14 or later, six commands from a downloaded copy run the two-slit experiment's world, read its detectors' counts beside the prediction written down before the first run, and draw the board frame by frame: see Run it in the README.
