Universe24

The aim of Universe24 is to represent reality as it is, inside its own simulator, the engine: a lattice of Nodes stepped by one rule in whole numbers and read through clicks, a world that shows what nature shows.

What this is

What if the universe is just numbers? Every point of space holds whole numbers and follows one simple rule with its six neighbours. From this come the speed of light, clocks that run slow near matter and in motion, a direction of time only where something is measured, and a black hole's shadow the size telescopes measured, within their error. Every step runs back exactly.

The paper's one claim is that Universe24 helps compute things in nature. Every number in the first paper is reproduced by a script in the repository, and the time paper's build checks every integer of its text against its run, so anyone with a computer can run the same counting and read the same numbers.

The experiments

Everything on these pages comes from whole numbers at each point of space stepped by one rule, Rule 3, and the declared counting rule; nothing is drawn by hand. Rule 3 is one integer line: from a point's two numbers, the six arrivals from its neighbours and its kept remainder it forms one sum, divides once, and keeps the new remainder. The runs were recorded and embedded in their pages; the levels on them are the integers the engine wrote, and a page computes nothing beyond the sums its bars and graphs draw. Each link opens the experiment's own page in a new tab. Press Play, drag the slider. The numbers are the lattice's own, not nature's.

The two slits

The paper's experiment: light through two gaps in a wall, with detectors waiting along a screen read in twelve regions, 130 intervals of Rule 3. The bars are the detectors' clicks per region of the screen, 285 in the counting window; the dashed curve is the blind row, 273 written down before the first run from light's line in real arithmetic, the pattern the two openings should give. The three high regions of the bars stand where the blind has them, the lows at the regions 4 and 7 against the blind's 4 and 8: interference, read as clicks, out of whole numbers alone. The clicks are the measurement; the bars are what the page draws from them. Watch the board frame by frame and see the light from the two gaps meet. The law's own line for this row, 284.7, was computed after the run and stands in the paper. Approved experiment.

Which way

Which gap did the light pass? The same wall is read three ways. With both gaps open the screen's twelve regions read [22, 21, 27, 16, 9, 36, 56, 11, 12, 26, 24, 25], 285 clicks, the two slits as shipped; with the lower gap closed they read [0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 19, 32, 15, 26, 17, 16, 17], 142 clicks, one gap's pattern. With both gaps open and a channel detector behind the lower gap taking everything that passes it, the channel reads 149 clicks and the screen 137, in the row [0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 18, 35, 12, 22, 23, 14, 13]: the one-gap row within the draw's scatter, and the two-slit fringes are gone, the central maximum at the region 6 down from 56 to 35, the minimum beside it at the region 8 filled from 12 to 22. In nature this is the which-way experiment, where knowing the path erases the interference; on the board nothing is added to do it, the channel's detector credits the quanta that pass it, so they never reach the screen. These are the readings at the clean engine, one seed, from the folder's blind_and_reading.md. World.

One photon on two atoms

Can one quantum of light be caught twice? One light record holding one whole quantum is laid between two atoms on a chain of 96 Nodes, and spreads toward both as a wave. At the interval 48 one atom, body 0, absorbs it and goes from its ground state to its excited state; the other stays in the ground state. In the folder's reading over 200 trials of this world the two atoms never both absorb; in the control world, two light records laid on the same two atoms, both absorb at the interval 48. The board's light is a wave that reaches both atoms, and the click is one: one quantum is one click, never shared. World.

The packet emission

How does an atom give its light? On a board of 627 Nodes along one axis, one atom in its excited state emits its one quantum at the interval 61 as a packet travelling along the axis; the page draws the packet's Nodes as it goes. At the board's far end a region named reach waits for it. The region records no click in this run and in every seed of the folder's reading: at this board's size the packet is at the floor there, a finding by name in the folder, written as such and not adjusted. The page shows an emission as a travelling packet, and a far detector that does not click. World.

The resonance

Who can take the light an atom gives? On a ring of 48 Nodes a giver atom and a taker atom stand apart, and the giver emits its pulse at the interval 27. In the resonant world the taker is tuned to the giver's frequency and absorbs the pulse, its credit at the interval 96; in the detuned world the taker is tuned away and does not. Both pages run at the design's seed 1, one of the 35 seeds of 100 at which the tuned taker absorbs; the detuned taker absorbs at 0 of 100 seeds, and the full reading stands in the folder's blind_and_reading.md. The born light's frequency is read by who can take it: this is the beat of two levels, the law's line "the wavelength is the giver's beat". World.

Two bodies fall toward each other

Two bodies of matter are laid at rest on a board of 49 x 17 x 17 Links, 24 Links apart, with no velocity given, and Rule 3 runs for 900 intervals. The board carries a second row of numbers, the content, declared in the universe file: each body writes a well into it, and where the content is deep the clock runs slow. For about 300 intervals little moves. Then the two drift toward each other, faster and faster, their distance falling under a third of its start, and they fall into one well, where they breathe as one body. Nothing pulls: the fall is the same integer line, stepped on a board where the clocks differ. Watch the two wells on the content strip draw together. Lattice reading, no blind.

A bound body in motion

One body is pushed once, at the start, along an axis, and then left to the rule for 200 intervals. It moves at 0.0948 Links per interval, 2 percent under the speed its band predicts, and it stays whole: at every interval at most one quantum stands beyond a sphere of radius 9 around its centre. The level at its centre changes sign every 3.85 intervals, the half period of its rest rotation: the body's own clock, ticking as it travels, the thing the law calls time. The push was put in by hand once; everything after it is the rule's. Lattice reading, no blind.

The gates, a results page

Four checks whose result is a number and not a motion, each with its blind, written before the run, beside the engine's reading. Bell's gate: at four settings the two sides' correlation reads E = 119/169, -119/169, 120/169, 120/169 and S = 478/169 = 2.8284, above the classical bound 2 at the quantum 2 sqrt 2, blind and result identical. The GHZ gate: the three-sided credit -1, -1, -1, +1 at the four patterns, M = -4, where the local bounds are -2 to +2. The quantum Zeno gate: the body ends excited in 480, 243, 188, 110 and 68 of 480 trials at 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 readings, against the blind's 1.0000, 0.5000, 0.3750, 0.2346 and 0.1334, every point within one standard error; the pulsed Zeno gate reads 480, 241, 174, 101, 62, 31 and 14 of 480 at 1 to 64 probes, every point within two standard errors of the blind. In the law's words the counting rule is declared and the correlations follow from it on the board: the gates are the engine's checks of its own counting rule against the blind written before the run, not experiments of nature. The page shows blind beside result for every number. Gate.

Run it yourself

The whole project is open: the engine, the law, the papers and the runs the papers report stand in the repository on GitHub. With Python 3.14 or later, six commands from a downloaded copy run the two-slit experiment's world, read its detectors' counts beside the prediction written down before the first run, and draw the board frame by frame: see Run it in the README.
