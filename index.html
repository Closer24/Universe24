Universe24

The aim of Universe24 is to represent reality as it is, inside its own simulator, the engine: a lattice of Nodes stepped by one rule in whole numbers and read through clicks, a world that shows what nature shows.

What this is

What if the universe is just numbers? Every point of space holds whole numbers and follows one simple rule with its six neighbours. From this come the speed of light, clocks that run slow near matter and in motion, a direction of time only where something is measured, and a black hole's shadow the size telescopes measured, within their error. Every step runs back exactly.

The paper's one claim is that Universe24 helps compute things in nature. Every number in the first paper is reproduced by a script in the repository, and the time paper's build checks every integer of its text against its run, so anyone with a computer can run the same counting and read the same numbers.

What is new here. The rule is one line of integer arithmetic on a cubic lattice, the simplest a local Node can do in whole numbers: two levels in time, six arrivals, one division, the remainder kept. It carries no free number but the declared pairs, the level weight and the vacuum content, and what a world needs beyond it is declared by name. From that line come, in lattice form, the Klein-Gordon equation with Schroedinger's slow limit under three conditions, Planck's relation in the band's form, Lorentz's composition of velocities at c exactly, clocks slowed in motion and near matter in the known forms at first order, and, under one assumption fixed by two measurements, the weak-field metric with its redshift and the bending of light. No Planck constant is put in: one quantum is one unit of T sin omega_0 with T the file's integer, the mass is one integer, and light is born as the beat of two rotations. On the same board, under the declared counting rule, interference reads as clicks, one quantum is one click and never two, and Bell's and GHZ's correlations follow; in the law, on a hypothesis by name (a family of two planes), the two beams of Stern and Gerlach and g = 2 are derived, outside the paper and with no world run. Every step runs back exactly, bit for bit, so the board has no arrow of time, and the click alone has one. What it does not do, plainly: the probability rule of measurement is declared, not derived; spin one half is a declared shape; helium's correlation energy is a miss the paper names, open in further research; the anomalous moment is a finding by name, its lattice part computed and negligible, its loop part open.

Run it yourself

The whole project is open: the engine, the law, the papers and the runs the papers report stand in the repository on GitHub. With Python 3.14 or later, six commands from a downloaded copy run the two-slit experiment's world, read its detectors' counts beside the prediction written down before the first run, and draw the board frame by frame: see Run it in the README.

The experiments

Everything on these pages comes from whole numbers at each point of space stepped by one rule, Rule 3, and the declared counting rule; nothing is drawn by hand. Rule 3 is one integer line: from a point's two numbers, the six arrivals from its neighbours and its kept remainder it forms one sum, divides once, and keeps the new remainder. The runs were recorded and embedded in their pages; the levels on them are the integers the engine wrote, and a page computes nothing beyond the sums its bars and graphs draw. Each link opens the experiment's own page in a new tab. Press Play, drag the slider. The numbers are the lattice's own, not nature's.

The two slits

The paper's experiment: light through two gaps in a wall, with detectors waiting along a screen read in twelve regions, 130 intervals of Rule 3. The bars are the detectors' clicks per region of the screen, 285 in the counting window; the dashed curve is the blind row, 273, written down before the first run from the Huygens sum of the two openings, the pattern they should give. The three high regions of the bars stand where the blind has them, the lows at the regions 4 and 7 against the blind's 4 and 8: interference, read as clicks, out of whole numbers alone. The clicks are the measurement; the bars are what the page draws from them. Watch the board frame by frame and see the light from the two gaps meet. The law's own line for this row, 284.7, was computed after the run and stands in the paper. Approved experiment.

Which way

Which gap did the light pass? The same wall is read three ways. With both gaps open the screen's twelve regions read [22, 21, 27, 16, 9, 36, 56, 11, 12, 26, 24, 25], 285 clicks, the two slits as shipped; with the lower gap closed they read [0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 19, 32, 15, 26, 17, 16, 17], 142 clicks, one gap's pattern. With both gaps open and a channel detector behind the lower gap taking everything that passes it, the channel reads 149 clicks and the screen 137, in the row [0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 18, 35, 12, 22, 23, 14, 13]: the one-gap row within the draw's scatter, and the two-slit fringes are gone, the central maximum at the region 6 down from 56 to 35, the blind's minimum at the region 8 filled from 12 to 22. In nature this is the which-way experiment, where knowing the path erases the interference; on the board no rule is added to do it, the channel's detector credits the quanta that pass it, so they never reach the screen. These are the readings at the clean engine, one seed, from the folder's blind_and_reading.md. World.

One photon on two atoms

Can one quantum of light be caught twice? One light record holding one whole quantum is laid between two atoms on a chain of 96 Nodes, and spreads toward both as a wave. At the interval 48 one atom, body 0, absorbs it and goes from its ground state to its excited state; the other stays in the ground state. In the folder's reading over 200 trials of this world the two atoms both absorb in 0 of 200; in the control world, two light records laid on the same two atoms, both absorb at the interval 48 on its page, and in 159 of its 200 trials. The board's light is a wave that reaches both atoms, and the click is one: one quantum is one click, never shared. World.

The packet emission

How does an atom give its light? On a board of 627 Nodes along one axis, one atom in its excited state emits its one quantum at the interval 61 as a packet travelling along the axis; the page draws the packet's Nodes as it goes. Twenty-eight Links along the axis, at the reach the law's line derives, a region named reach waits for it; the blind expected one click there. The region records no click in this run and in every one of the 14 seeds whose packet went its way: the packet is at the floor there, a miss the folder names and does not adjust. The page shows an emission as a travelling packet, and a far detector that does not click. World.

The resonance

Who can take the light an atom gives? On a ring of 48 Nodes a giver atom and a taker atom stand apart, and the giver emits its pulse at the interval 27. In the resonant world the taker is tuned to the giver's frequency and absorbs the pulse, its one draw at the run's end, the interval 96; in the detuned world the taker is tuned away and does not. Both pages run at the design's seed 1, one of the 35 seeds of 100 at which the tuned taker absorbs; the detuned taker absorbs at 0 of 100 seeds, and the full reading stands in the folder's blind_and_reading.md. The born light's frequency is read by who can take it: this is the beat of two levels, the law's line "the wavelength is the giver's beat". World.

Two bodies fall toward each other

Two bodies of matter are laid at rest on a board of 49 x 17 x 17 Nodes, 24 Links apart, with no velocity given, and Rule 3 runs for 900 intervals. The board carries a second row of numbers, the content, declared in the universe file: each body writes a well into it, and where the content is deep the clock runs slow. For about 300 intervals little moves. Then the two drift toward each other, faster and faster, their distance falling under a third of its start, and they fall into one well, where they breathe as one body. No force is added: the fall is Rule 3 alone, stepped on a board where the clocks differ. Watch the two wells on the content strip draw together. Lattice reading, no blind.

A bound body in motion

One body is pushed once, at the start, along an axis, and then left to the rule for 200 intervals. It moves at 0.0948 Links per interval, 2 percent under the speed its band predicts, and it stays whole: at every interval at most one quantum stands beyond a sphere of radius 9 around its centre. The level at its centre changes sign every 3.85 intervals, the half period of its rest rotation: the body's own clock, ticking as it travels, the thing the law calls time. The push was put in by hand once; everything after it is the rule's. Lattice reading, no blind.

The atom

The Node makes the levels and the line, the click makes the shells. The proton is one Node holding one quantum of a frozen plane; its sign writes a well into the holder's row, 1,484 deep at its Node and falling as 477 / r. The electron is laid as the ground eigenvector of that well, one quantum spread over a cube of 25 Nodes a side with the well's own angle at each Node, and Rule 3 runs for 120 intervals with nothing added. The cloud stands: its share within four Links of the proton reads 0.446 at the lay and 0.440 after 100 intervals, the blind's 0.446 within 0.01; the peak stays at the centre at every interval, its level 525 at the lay and 525 one interval later; and the whole cloud rotates as one clock, 0.835 per interval at every radius read within eight Links, slower than a free electron's 0.841: the binding, 0.7 percent of the rest rotation, read as a redshift of the rotation. A free Gaussian lump of the same width on the same proton breathes inward instead, its centre level 260 to 635 to 593 and its inner share 0.42 to 0.53 to 0.51, no fixed point. At T = 72,000,000 on a cube of 33 Nodes a side two levels of one atom stand for 60 intervals each: the 1s, 3,037 at its Node at the lay and 3,052 at the end with 67 percent of its share within two Links and the rotation 0.7828, and the 2p, two lobes at three to four Links along x with its node plane at zero exactly at every interval and the rotation 0.8388. The line is their sum, half a quantum each, run for 224 intervals, two beats: the cloud's centre of charge swings along x as a cosine of the period 112.4 intervals by a cosine fit and 112.5 from its zero crossings against the blind's 112, and the amplitude 0.73 Nodes by the fit (0.75 at most) against the blind's 0.75, the two levels' difference 0.056 per interval read on the board, and the electron's own row of the sign holder breathes at the same beat; no light family stands in the universe, so nothing is emitted and the line beats undamped. The shells are clicks: a toy atom of four parts, two ground parts gu and gd holding one quantum each and two excited parts eu and ed empty, declared antisymmetric under exchange and nothing else, meets two photons one window apart on a chain of 112 Nodes. The first photon is taken into one excited part; at the second close the list's weight into the part already holding a quantum is 0 exactly and the empty part's 9,190,267,331,637,924, and the second photon enters the empty part: no count above 1 with no capacity declared, the exclusion from the one word. The control declares the symmetric sign: the weight into the populated part doubles to 64,961,076,134,244,168 and the second photon piles in, both quanta in one part. The lattice's integers are the toy's, nothing nature's: its fine-structure constant is 0.138 at T = 36,000,000, nineteen times nature's, and 0.276 at 72,000,000; the levels and the line are lattice readings with no click, the shells clicks of a toy atom with the photon's resonance declared. Lattice reading for the levels and the line, clicks for the shells.

The singlet's two clicks

One quantum of a two-plane family declared antisymmetric under exchange is laid at the centre of a chain of 33 Nodes as two packets, half a quantum each, travelling to the two ends, where two detectors wait, each declaring an axis and both reading the parts as they are, the plain identity pattern: the antisymmetric pair is the family's one word and no detector's. Over 40 intervals the two packets reach the two regions; at the window's close the two detectors draw one after another, each on its own region's read, the first writing its realised port into the record at its birth along the record's own light cone and the second reading the rewritten record, the pair's amplitude the determinant of the two sides' parts, and the two click once each with one combination realised. At the four Bell settings the joint shares give E = -119/169, 119/169, -120/169, -120/169, the singlet's -cos of the angle between the axes exactly, and S = 478/169 = 2.828, above the classical bound 2, with every marginal 1/2 and the two sides never in the same port at equal axes: the numbers of the earlier declaration, the crossed pattern [[0, 1], [-1, 0]] on one detector, to the digit, the determinant at two parts being that pattern term by term. On the same waves, a probe let each detector draw alone on what reaches it: S = 1.42; fixed the pair's direction at the emission and drew locally: S = 1.41; read each side's favoured port with no draw: S = 2 exactly. The write at the birth through the record's own past is the one non-local act, and it alone gives the correlation; a draw at the click adds noise to a local picture and never correlation. The page shows the (a, b) world's run with the gate's reading beside its blind. The page is the click's page: the first detector draws alone on its own region's read, the record's own light cone runs back to its birth, where the realised port is written into the record, and forward to its partner with the realised term, the rest of the board neither read nor written, the partner draws alone on the rewritten record; the look shows the passes with the cone shaded, shrinking Link by Link to the lay's Nodes and growing back. Four more worlds are born of one emission: an atom at the Node 12 gives the pair as a source in time on both parts alike at the interval its own draw picks, the left region clicks and writes at the birth, and the board carries the realised term to the right region, which clicks in the next window. The gates, a results page

Four checks whose result is a number and not a motion, each with its blind, written before the run, beside the engine's reading. Bell's gate: at four settings the two sides' correlation reads E = 119/169, -119/169, 120/169, 120/169 and S = 478/169 = 2.8284, above the classical bound 2 and below Tsirelson's 2 sqrt 2 by 2.5 x 10^-5, the blind's fraction exactly. The GHZ gate: the three-sided credit -1, -1, -1, +1 at the four settings x y y, y x y, y y x and x x x, M = -4, where the local bounds are -2 to +2. The quantum Zeno gate: the body ends excited in 480, 243, 188, 110 and 68 of 480 trials at 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 readings, against the blind's 1.0000, 0.5000, 0.3750, 0.2346 and 0.1334, every point within one standard error; the pulsed Zeno gate reads 480, 241, 174, 101, 62, 31 and 14 of 480 at 1 to 64 probes, every point within two standard errors of the blind. The counting rule is declared, in the law's own word, and the correlations follow from it on the board: the gates are the engine's checks of its own counting rule against the blind written before the run, not experiments of nature. The page shows blind beside result for every number.
