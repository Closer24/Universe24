## 2. Theorem: no rule of the six selects a content

**On the GameBoard.** A body's content is a count of units on its
record, `held`. What changes it: a click that joins what arrived (the
amount of the rows), a release that pays `h s` per birth, a give at a
contact (binding-v1), a `become`. Each acts per unit: a body of content
M is M units each reading alone (24.2's additivity (ii), the equivalence
principle as a rule, registered as `push_m = m x push_1`), and a row of
amount w is w rows of amount 1 (additivity (i)). So every rate that
changes a content is linear in the contents: the exchange between two
bodies is the linear map of the map's section C, whose total is
conserved and whose difference shrinks by `1 - 2 k` per interval; every
equal split of every total is a fixed point, none is selected. That is
the masses design's theorem (its section 2 and 7), checked there on the
engine integer by integer and here on the closed form. The collision
table is content-blind (its classes read slot codes only); the phase
closure of bohr-v1 selects radii, not contents; the smallest
self-referential rule that makes a ladder (a window against the reader's
own clock) makes a harmonic ladder with 3068 unseen leptons. What the
structure does fix: the floors of 16.2 (one unit the least mass; a
charged family's least content the reduced denominator of its charge
per unit, 1 for the electron and the proton, 3 for the quarks; a bound
composite's content the sum of its parts less the give). **A theorem of
the declared rules**: the masses and the charges are inputs, and no
rule of the law's kind (a key on a family, a table entry, a column) can
make them otherwise, because every such rule acts per unit of content.

**What this means for the paper.** P8 lists the family table as an
input; that is where the Standard Model stands on the same questions,
and the design's referee sentence stands: a model whose masses are all
inputs is not thereby wrong; the claim to avoid is a derivation.
