[TERMINOLOGY.md](../../TERMINOLOGY.md), "The readings"): Inside is inside
the GameBoard, the Nodes, the integer rows and the tick, where no one
measures; Outside is the game above the board, the detectors and their
clicks only. A number Inside is a GameBoard reading; a click Outside is a
detector reading. The time axis (record 1196, the physics-rule reviewer's
rule folded by the Boss; record 1217, the owner: "by the clock you mean

the tick of its click line is the record's ordering, GAMEBOARD
([ENGINE.md](../../ENGINE.md), "the tick of a line"); a one-Node `wave`

## 1. What the engine itself does during a run: nothing is written into the Inside by the host

Read first, since every experiment runs through it. The runner loads the
world once and steps the law's interval `count` times with an observer
that only writes record lines (`src/event_universe/runner.py:33-60`,
`events/run.py:33-63`: `record` appends to `events.jsonl`; the simulation
is constructed with `observer=record` and never touched again). The
interval is `NatureBeamSimulation.step` (`engine.py:577`): the clocks'
frame, the bodies' steps, the law, the clocks' turn and count; `_place`
(`engine.py:564`) moves a body's Nodes in the engine's index and is called
only by the law's own step and contact. No random draw exists in
`src/event_universe/` (no `random`, no `np.random`; the birth wheel's `u`
is the count of the birth, `amplitude.py:23-24`). The world parser refuses
by name every key of the earlier engines that placed fields, seeds or
emissions during a run (`world.py:368-391`, `OLD_KEYS`: `seeds`,
`spatial_seeds`, `emissions`, `external_bodies`, `initial_field`,
`standing_field`, ...), and accepts no key that schedules a placement
after tick 0 (`world.py:396-430`, `WORLD_KEYS`: the only placements are
`measured`, `in_transit` and `detectors` at load; `become` with `at` is
the body's own clock trigger, the law's). The workspace UI starts and
stops jobs and edits no running world (`ui.py:64-142`). The one host
mutation of state is the inverse interval `engine.py:635`, the law's own
inverse, called by the bijection tests alone. So column (c) is NO for
every registered world and every shipped tool: what writes into the Inside
is found only in unit tests of one rule, listed in section 3, which set a
body's momentum or a row's label between two intervals to exercise the
verb.

The clock stamp is on `main` (PR #834) but declared by 20 worlds only:
`examples/events/moving_detector/*.json` (six), `orbit_lamp/r*_k17*.json`
(four), `newton_side/*.json` (three), `docs/designs/new_rows/worlds/sagnac_k*.json`
(four) and `docs/designs/fail_rows/worlds/j4_muon_*.json` (three; the
registered series, `hubble_stars`, `covariant`, `c_measured`, `weak`,
`clock_word`, `shell_clock`, `amplitude`, `bohr`, `nucleus`, `quarks`,
`binding`, `massive_rows`, declare it nowhere). A detector's own count is
therefore on the record of twenty worlds and off the record of the rest,
which is the root of most of column (d): a tool cannot read a count the
record does not carry.

**Two things counted in no column.** The books' `balanced` flag and a
digest of `state.json` are the engine's own gates (the conservation
check, the bit-identity replay), not results: a tool or test that fails
on them reports a broken engine, not a reading. They are named where they
decide a tool's exit and left out of (b). Likewise a test of one rule on
a minimal GameBoard (the `test_nature_beam_*` suites and their kin) reads
the state by design, since the vector and local tests of a rule are made
on the state; such a test is a row only where it pins a registered
world's number or writes into a running board, and column (f) for it is a
label, not a code change.

## 4. What is dangerous

- **The clock stamp changes no physics but every record** (item 1): a
  world that declares it writes one more field per line; the digests of
  `state.json` are untouched, the digests of `events.jsonl` in the gate set
  and the replay blocks (`quarks/expectations.json`, `massive_rows`) are
  not, so the key must stay off in the gate worlds and the replay worlds,
  or their digests be re-registered under a dated line.
- **Inside a crowd the count and the tick part** (items 2, 4, 7): the
  paper's rows 3 and 4b stand at r = 1.0000 because the centre detector
  sits in no crowd; series X's detector does not (`tests/test_shell_clock.py:295`),
  and the shell's inside ratio 1.0029, MET against 1.003917, would move
  with the detector's dwell before the paper's shell claim is re-read.
- **The fraction of a tick** (item 6): D3's periods are interpolated
  crossings (`tools/orbit_lamp_readings.py:246`); in whole counts the
  ratio 1.677 becomes a ratio of integers with the grain of the ordinal,
  and its bracket 2.00 +- 0.18 must be re-derived by the algebra before the
  restated number is compared with anything.
- **A pin that reads the host's store is a host number even when the field
  is the body's own** (item 8): the weak `counts` pins were read by the
  generator off `simulation.measured[n].counted` at host ticks
  (`make_worlds.py:539-549`); re-deriving them from a run's `become` lines
  is the same integers today and a different provenance, which the
  register must say.
- **Nothing here touches `src/event_universe/`**: every change is in a
  world file's declaration, a readings tool, a test or a register entry;
  no engine change is needed for the count: a `wave` pixel is a body and
  carries one already, an open face is not an entity of the world and
  carries none by construction (record 768); so of the three rows that
  read at a detector without a stamp, 10's build-up and 13's phase rate
  read at pixels that carry `clock` after item 1 (their tools then read it
  in place of `first_tick` and the line's tick, items 10 and 15), and 5a's
  faces need what item 5 proposes, a receiver body on the face (the light
  clock's); the open question is a world-file one for the owner, which
  worlds get a receiver body, not an engine one.
