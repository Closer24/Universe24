## 3. THE TWO BUILDS

### 3.1 Build (i): the field per family and Node

The state of a recorded family at a Node x is one phase-count vector
f_x in Z[Z_N] (N counts, one per phase step; the merge's image, ALGEBRA
2.3), carried by the record's label (the record, the branch, the label
pair) so that one click reads one record: in a world with one live
record at a time (the paper's worlds: rows 2a, 2b, 10) there is one
vector per family per Node. One interval, in the interval's order (ALGEBRA
2.11):

1. **Translate (T).** Every Port p of x sends f_x to the neighbour x + e_p:
   the same element through all six Ports (equal weights) or the element
   times the Port's declared weight (section 5).
2. **Turn (P).** What passes a Link is turned by the family's phase per
   Link, t_L steps: f -> x^(t_L) f, a cyclic shift of the N counts (the
   permutation of the phase index; the turn's whole part per Link is the
   flight table's, `by_clock`, and its remainder is the record's, as
   today).
3. **Sum (G).** The Node's new element is the group-ring sum of the six
   arrivals, f_x <- sum over p of x^(t_L) f_(x - e_p), the cancel
   [p + N / 2] = -[p] applied, the counts whole (no division).
4. **Click (E, D).** At a detector's Node the element is evaluated, the
   pointer sum over p of f_p (C[p], S[p]) at the tables' scale 256, the
   offer its square per Node summed per set, as the layer does today; the
   ladder chooses the cell by the record's u (section 4).

Three verbs carry the field: (T), (P) and (G); the click adds (E) and
(D). The counts are whole numbers: after k intervals the total count of
one record is A K^k, A the birth's amount and K the split's outputs per
Node (6 for the Ports). That is the cutoff: on the int64 the law
computes in, 6^24 = 4738381338321616896 is below 2^63 and 6^25 is above,
so a whole-count field of one record lives 24 intervals; on the two-slit
world's fan of 91 directions, 9 intervals (91^9 = 4.28 x 10^17 < 2^63 <
91^10); on row 10's fan of 601 directions, 6 (601^6 = 4.71 x 10^16 < 2^63
< 601^7) (COMPUTATION). A whole-count field is therefore not the build;
the field is the record form's merge (build (ii)) with the weight shared,
and the cutoff written there is the one in force.

### 3.2 Build (ii): the local split rule in the record form, the coin, and the cutoff (REFUTED BY ITS PINS, CORRECTIONS.md M11; kept as the record of the form tried)

The row as the law carries it: (amount w, multiplicity m, phase p,
direction D, age tau, record, branch, the label pair, the flight
accumulator). The split's domain, the code's property (Reviewer 3's
D1): a recorded row, `record != NO_RECORD`, born by a lamp under the
amplitude law, of a family without the flag `massive`; the light rows of
every amplitude world carry content 1 per unit (the `light` family's
quantum 1, the cost quantum x turn at the birth), so the domain is not
"a row of no content"; a free family's rows (no record: a crowd) and a
massive family's rows do not split under this design.

**The matrix, not the copy (the chief physicist's finding of 06:50Z,
on Reviewer 3's A1); refuted by its pins at 07:40Z, CORRECTIONS.md M11:
the coin's pattern is speckle at every wavelength and every turn of the
coin, the build held until the owner chooses the form.** amplitude-v1's table rule (section 2.1) sends ONE
arriving row to K outputs, (w, m) -> K copies (w a_i, m A): an isometry
of one input, right for a lamp's fan and for a re-emitter's declared
outputs. At a free Node under the split the inputs are the K arrivals
from the K directions, and the copy rule applied to each is the K x K
matrix (1 / sqrt K) J (every output the sum of every input over sqrt K),
of rank one: it forgets the direction of what arrived, and the record's
amplitude then spreads as a diffusion with phases, not as a wave (in
one dimension the copy rule gives a_k(t + 1) = sqrt 2 cos k a_k(t), an
amplifying heat kernel with no front; the pins of section 7 are not its
readings). The split at a free Node is therefore the multiplication by
a unitary matrix over the outputs, verb (B) with declared integers, and
the one such matrix that declares nothing but K is Grover's coin,

    G = (2 / K) J - I:   the output on D_i is (2 / K) (sum over j of psi_j) - psi_i,

in the record form: the arrivals (w_j, m) at the Node (the same m for
every row of one record at one age, section 3.2 below) become the rows
(2 S - K w_i, m K^2) on the outputs D_i, S = sum over j of w_j and w_i
the arrival on D_i (0 where none arrived), the phase carried on each
w_j as its element of Z[Z_N] (the sums are group-ring sums, the cancel
applied). The norm is exact and CONSERVED: sum over i of (2 S - K
w_i)^2 / (m K^2) = sum over j of w_j^2 / m, since G is unitary; a single
arrival (S = w) gives the K - 1 forward outputs 2 w / K and the backward
output (2 - K) w / K, Huygens' forward wave with a weak backward one,
and the whole front, summed, walks at the lines' pace. (The one-input
copy rule is the K x 1 column (1, ..., 1) / sqrt K, which G contains up
to sign only for K = 2; the two agree on a lamp's birth and differ at
every free Node.) The Node's outputs D_i are the world's fan (section
5); the turns t_i are 0 at a free Node (a `rerelease` entry keeps its
`label_turns`); the record, the branch and the label pair are copied;
the age is kept; the flight accumulator of an output on a new line is
seeded as `reseed_flight` seeds a turned row, the table's own count at
the row's age on the new line (`FamilyFlight.accumulator`; Reviewer 3's
D5), so that two outputs of one age on one line are identical in `made`
and `residue` (the merge's identity fields) and merge; seeded at the
half-wall they would never merge and the rows would grow as K^tau. The
rows then walk, and at the next Node the merge (G) sums the identical
ones (the same Node, direction, age, phase modulo N / 2, record,
branch): the field of build (i) is what the merge leaves.

**The collision on a recorded row (Reviewer 3's D2).** The collision as
built acts at every free Node on the single units (amount 1) per
(number, content) class in the eight slots of the six headings
(`_collide`, `nature_beam.py` 3360: eligible are the rows on a heading
or at rest at an unoccupied Node; a slot with one row of amount 1 is a
single, with more or with an amount above 1 a crowd; the fan's
directions are spectators), and a lamp's rows have amount 1. Under the
split the outputs of one record at one Node on the headings are singles
of one class whose directions the cyclic shift would permute every
interval, mixing outputs of different phases and ages. The rule of this
design: a recorded row is exempt from the collision (`record !=
NO_RECORD` excluded from `eligible`); the split at every Node is the
recorded row's own transport, and what the collision did for two rows
of one record (the Sagnac finding of record 1219, the collision on two
rows of one record at one Node) is under the split the coin's mixing
of the same two arrivals, read again in the re-run beside. The
collision stays the rule of rows of no record (a crowd's singles), as
it is. Three tests: generic PASS (a property of the row, its record,
not a name); vector PASS ((P) simply not applied to the recorded row;
(B) in its place); local PASS.

**The multiplicity as the age function, and the cutoff written out.**
Every row of one record at age tau has the multiplicity m_0 K^(2 tau)
under the coin (m_0 K^tau under the copy rule), the same for all, so
the merge never refuses a pair and the multiplicity is not a stored

The amounts are the digits of the wave and grow with it: a surviving
row's share w^2 / m is at least 1 / W (the grain, section 4), so w is at

every exact integer wave: a rational unitary evolution's denominators
grow as K^tau and its numerators with them; the grain bounds the number
of rows, not their digits. The build therefore carries the amounts of a
recorded row as Python integers (the store's `amount` column an object
column for the recorded families, int64 for the rest), exact, at a
HOST cost of ten to fifty times the int64 arithmetic per row (section

**The count of rows.** Without the merge the rows of one record after k

## 4. NO COMPLETION; THE CLICK'S TRIGGER; THE VANISHING; THE GRAIN

**No completion.** The layer's rule "the record is read at its completion,
when every row ended" (amplitude-v1 section 3.2, the live count) is
removed by the owner's word: under the split the record never completes
on the GameBoard (its rows are all the futures; on a periodic axis they
never end), and the record ends only at the click.

detector's SENSITIVITY or a SINGLE-QUANTUM reading; his word of 05:55Z

default (the wheel's own grain, record 1263's bookkeeping) and
declarable per detector; the single-quantum reading is its special case
with the detectors the only sets. A first crossing of the sensitivity is
not a trigger (it truncates the front, breaks the fringe and the

the form the code can build, with the three tests and the pins, are
[CORRECTIONS.md](CORRECTIONS.md) M2.

**Where the trigger comes from, the GameBoard or the detector (the
owner's question of 2026-09-23).** The click is the detector's action:
the detector receives the rows that reach it, its offer (the square of
its Node's summed pointer) is its cell's weight in the ladder, its own
count n_D stamps the click and the record ends there. What the detector
cannot decide is WHEN the ladder is read, for one reason: two paths of
one record of different lengths (the two slits to one pixel) reach the
set at two different intervals, the set accumulates its pointer
coherently over the intervals, and the square of the accumulated sum
rises and falls (an antiphase row arriving later lowers it; amplitude-v1
section 13, departure 3). A click at the first arrival reads the nearest
pixel, not the fringe. The ladder therefore needs the sum over ALL the
record's futures, which is the owner's sentence itself: the choice among
the futures needs them all to have arrived. Today that condition is the
record's live count reaching 0 (the completion, fed through the
apparatus's Ports); under the split it is the sensitivity's counting form, the same
condition of the record on the GameBoard in another form. The detector
stamps the click; the record decides that the time has come.

**Heisenberg under the split (the owner's condition of 2026-09-23: the
wave may not break the uncertainty relation).** It does not, and this is
a theorem of the algebra rather than a separate check: the reading at
the detector is the evaluation (E), the Fourier transform of the
record's element over the aperture's Nodes (section 7), and for a
Fourier pair Kennard's relation in the variance form bounds the product
of the widths from below (ALGEBRA 3.4, record 1141; Reviewer 3's C1).
Row 10's number is not the relation itself but the uniform aperture's
own product at the full width at half maximum, w x FWHM(sin theta) /
lambda = 0.886, the row's pin; a reading well below it would mean a
pattern narrower than the aperture's Fourier transform, which the
relation forbids, so the refutation threshold 0.85 is also a Heisenberg
check in that sense. The law has caught one such break already: the fan hole of
RUN_10 (no direction within 1.22 degrees of the axis at the width 48)
read 0.360 at w = 9, narrower than the Fourier width, an artefact of the
fan and not of the wave, fixed by the 601-direction fan (0.879); under
the split at every Node there is no fan to have a hole, so that source
of a break is gone. A single row on the GameBoard is sharp in both its
Node and its direction; this breaks nothing: a row is one future, a ray,
a GAMEBOARD number, and the relation constrains what the detector reads,
the sum of all the futures (DETECTOR). Row 10's 27 lamp rows and 601
directions per opening Node are all sharp, and their clicks are spread
by exactly the Fourier width (COMPUTATION, `run_10_pins.out`).

**The vanishing.** At the click the record's other rows, wherever they
are, are deleted from the GameBoard (the one deletion, ALGEBRA 3.1, the
one non-local step that the law already has: the pair's gather deletes
both arms' rows). Nothing is placed or cancelled at a distance beyond
what the deletion already does today at the completion.

**The grain (the owner's bookkeeping, record 1263).** A row whose share w / m is below 1 / W (its offer would
take no rung) is not counted: it is deleted at the Node where it falls
below, an event of the row's own record (its own accumulator against the
wheel, verb (D)), nothing kept at the Node. Under equal weights the share
of a single unmerged path after k splits is 1 / K^k of the birth's, so a
stray path (no merge) falls below the grain after log_K(W) intervals:
4.6 for K = 6, 1.8 for K = 90, 1.3 for K = 620 (COMPUTATION); what
survives the grain is what the merge piled up, the coherent paths, the
wave. The birth's amount A (the lamp's `rate` numerator, 2^30 in the
two-slit world) sets how far the wave lives: a field of A units over the
front's Nodes at age tau holds A / (Nodes at tau) per Node, and the
front's Nodes in a slab of one z are 2 tau^2 + 2 tau + 1: 8321 at tau = 64,
so 2^30 / 8321 = 1.29 x 10^5 units per Node at tau = 64 (COMPUTATION), far
above one unit; the grain bites where the wave is thin, its shadow and
its far sidelobes below 1 / W of the record: it deletes those sidelobes
and narrows nothing at the half maximum, only the normalization
(Reviewer 3's C2). The sensitivity is a world-file input of kind 3: the
key `sensitivity` on a `detectors` entry, an integer count of units of
the wheel, 1 by default (s_D = 1 / W), a value below 1 refused at load
(below one rung), named in ENGINE.md's world-file section at the build.

**Where the exhaustion sits among the tests (Reviewer 3's A2).** The
exhaustion, "the record clicks when no counted future is in flight",
decides WHEN the law acts on the whole record: it is a condition of the
whole record, non-local as the completion it replaces and as the
deletion at the click; the local test applies to the split, the merge,
the grain and the detector's own comparison, and the exhaustion passes
only as the click's own non-local step, the one the law already has

for the owner: his sensitivity read as a first crossing is the local

timing stays the record's. Under the sensitivity in its counting form an

the registered pair worlds this is the expected outcome (section 8,
