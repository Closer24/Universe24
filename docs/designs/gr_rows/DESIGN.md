## 5b. Snell's law and Fermat's least time

**The pin from the rule's integers, before the numbers.** The turn per
interval is `f (n_s / d_s)(T_D / Q^2) |V_perp|` and, by 5.1's **V** `=
-(Q c / dwell) grad(A)`, `|V_perp|` is the transverse gradient of the age
moment, so along a path the rule is the ray equation `d theta / dl =
grad_perp(f k_a) = grad_perp(n)` at n near 1: Fermat's Euler-Lagrange
form. Across a boundary where n jumps by `n_2 - n_1` a row at the angle
`theta_1` to the normal turns by `Delta theta = -(n_2 - n_1) tan theta_1`
(the gradient's transverse part `(dn / dx) sin theta` integrated over
`dx / cos theta`), which is Snell's `n_1 sin theta_1 = n_2 sin theta_2`
differentiated at `n_1 = 1`: Snell to first order in `n_2 - n_1 = f (k_2
- k_1)`, toward the denser crowd, the residual of order `(n_2 - n_1)^2`.
The numbers (the script): at `k_2 = 1 / 256` (`n_2` = 1.0078) and
`theta_1 = 30` degrees Snell gives 29.744 and the rule 29.742; at `k_2 = 1
/ 16` (`n_2` = 1.125) and 30 degrees, 26.388 against 25.865, the
difference 0.5 degrees at `(n_2 - 1)^2 = 1.6 x 10^-2`; at 60 degrees and
`1 / 16`, 50.34 against 47.60. On the fan the turn is whole steps (2.4
degrees at series K's directions) and the wheel's dither makes the mean
over a beam exact. **No world yet** makes a slab of crowd: a crowd of `M
/ r` has no sharp boundary, so the pin as it stands is the ray
equation's, and Snell's form holds in the limit of a thin transition (two
sources' crowds meeting, or a plane of sources, a world to design if the
owner asks for it). **Reached in form** to first order; the exact Snell
(the `1 / n` in the ray equation, `d theta / dl = grad_perp(ln n)`) would
need the turn's rate divided by the wall's factor `d_s + f n_s A`, a
division with the remainder kept (verb 6) that the design leaves out
until a strong crowd asks for it.

**Fermat's least time and the fan's choice of direction.** The ray rule
above is the local form of Fermat's principle (its Euler-Lagrange
equation), so a single row follows the least-time path between the
Nodes it passes, to the fan's grain. The wave form, the least-time path
as the path of stationary phase, is the fan's: at a re-emitter the fan by
angle (TWO_SLITS.md section 7) sends a row on every direction within P
with the angular measure as its weight, and the click reads the exact
phase of each path; the stationary-phase path dominates the click (that
is Young's spacing, section 7 of DERIVATIONS_BEAM.md). It follows from
the fan's choice only if the phase a row gains along its path measures
the TIME of the path, that is if the family's phase advances per
interval (a clock, the slits' declaration `[n, 2^30]` per interval) and
not per Link (the `phase_per_link` form, under which a slowed row gains
the same phase per Link and the stationary path is the shortest, not the
least-time, one). So: Fermat's ray form follows from the rule; Fermat's
wave form follows from the fan and the rule together, for families
whose phase is a clock.

*Amended per the review*: should-fix 5.

## 7. What changes in the registered worlds if the rule were on

A named list, not a run. **Without the key nothing changes**: every
registered world is byte identical in `events.jsonl` and `state.json`,
including the 27 worlds with a nonzero `suspension` pair (by group:
`catalog/clock_near_mass` and `catalog/neutron_star`; `coupling/6` and
`coupling/one_content`; `hubble` four worlds; `hubble_stars` twelve;
`redshift/scalar` and `redshift/age`; `weak/j1_*` and `weak/j3_*` five),
which the key alone keeps as they are. **With the key**, in a world that
declares it:

- **Two sources' fields no longer add exactly** (5.5 (ii)): a source's
  rows read another source's crowd, are slowed where it is dense and dwell
  longer there, so its presence and age moment rise by `1 + f k_a` where
  it is slowed; Gauss's law of the stream stays exact in the steady state
  (no row is lost), the timing and the local density change.
- **A beam refracts the crowd it crosses**, and light reads light: the
  crowd's rows read the beam's age moment (section 4's back-reaction),
  and two lamps' beams read each other at their amounts (`lens_meeting`'s
  two lamps; `hubble_stars`' 24 lamps would bend one another's rows by
  about 0.5 percent in pace along a crossed beam at `[1, 65536]`, moving
  the registered z readings with rms 0.0044, if the key were declared
  there).
- **Series E (the clocks' redshift, `suspension` `[1, 1]` and `[1, 2]`)**:
  its 6985 events of family m each hold 4096 units at `release` `[1,
  4096]`, so every one releases one row per direction per self-creation
  and their rows read one another's crowds (other numbers, k = 2 to 9):
  the rows' pace inside the crowd would fall to `1 / (1 + 2 k)` = 0.05 to
  0.2 and the registered rate ratios of 5.2 would move; unchanged without
  the key.
- **Series C's field pair (the presence `M / r^2`, the age moment `M /
  r`)**: one source, one number, its rays read no crowd of their own;
  unchanged with or without the key.
- **The beam and the cone (the bar of section 2.2, the flight's
  isotropy)**: unchanged without the key; with it, a beam crossing another
  number's crowd slows and bends.
- **Series K under the pin world**: section 4's table, the run to make.
- **Series D's orbit (`s32_r24`) and the thrown orbit (12b.2)**: under
  form B the body's drive wall carries `T_D` and scales by the same `1 +
  f k_a` at the cap term, so a moving body's pace in Nodes per interval
  falls in the potential as a row's does, at the order of its speed over

  not computed here; on main today `step_axis` carries no `T_D`, so the
  body's case holds only once form B is on main.
- **Series G2's stars and the growing wall**: two walls on one flight,
