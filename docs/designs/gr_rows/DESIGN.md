## 1. The pins, before any number

One source per number.

- **The bending of light.** Nature: `theta = 4 G M / (b c^2)` toward the
  mass, twice Newton's `2 G M / (b c^2)` for a particle at c (the time
  part and the space part of the metric one each); at the Sun's limb `k =
  G M / (r c^2) = 2.12 x 10^-6`, `4 k = 1.751` arcseconds (Dyson,
  Eddington and Davidson 1920, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 220, 291: 1.98 +-
  0.16 and 1.61 +- 0.40 arcseconds; VLBI: `gamma = 0.99992 +- 0.00012`,
  Lambert and Le Poncin-Lafitte 2011, A&A 529, A70, to verify against the
  source). The two references in the register: series K on main, the
  deflection 0.000 pixel in every world (the flight blind to the crowd,
  [the lensing README](../../../examples/events/lensing/README.md)); series
  K under `meeting-v1`, the centroid toward the mass by -1.79, -4.36 and
  -2.30 pixels at (M, b) = (2^12, 6), (2^13, 6), (2^12, 3), the form `M / b`
  with a constant that is a grain of the fan (5.4).
- **The Shapiro delay.** Nature: `Delta t = (2 G M / c^3) ln(4 r_1 r_2 /
  b^2)`, the potential `M / r` integrated along the path, a logarithm
  (Shapiro 1964, Phys. Rev. Lett. 13, 789); at Cassini's geometry (the
  Earth at 1 au, Saturn at 8.43 au, the ray at 1.6 solar radii) 131
  microseconds; the bound on the coefficient `gamma - 1 = (2.1 +- 2.3) x
  10^-5` (Bertotti, Iess and Tortora 2003, Nature 425, 374). The
  register's reference: the mean age 89.40 intervals in every world of
  series K (a delay of 0.00); under the meeting the mean age moved only by
  the bent path's extra Links, and the meeting's phase reads the FLOW (`M
  / r^2` integrated, `1 / b`), not the potential (5.4).
- **The redshift's second order.** Nature: `1 + z = 1 / sqrt(1 - 2 k) = 1
  + k + (3 / 2) k^2 + (5 / 2) k^3 + ...` between a clock at k and one at
  0 (Pound and Rebka 1960, Phys. Rev. Lett. 4, 337, the first order at
  `2.5 x 10^-15`; the second order is unmeasured on any clock in nature,
  the pin is the formula's). The law today: the clock at `1 / (1 + k)`
  (5.2), so `1 + z = 1 + k` exactly, no second-order term (the lattice
  reads `1 - k + k^2` for the rate against `1 - k - k^2 / 2`).
- **Snell's law** (the owner's addition, record 300). Nature: at a
  boundary of two media `n_1 sin theta_1 = n_2 sin theta_2` with n the
  pace ratio (Snell 1621, published by Descartes 1637; Fermat's least
  time 1662). In the law a medium is a crowd: n is `1 + 2 k_a` under the
  rule, and the boundary is where the age moment changes. The pin: the
  rule must turn a row crossing the boundary toward the denser crowd by
  Snell's angle, to first order in `n_2 - n_1 = 2 (k_2 - k_1)`.
- **What must not change.** Series C's field pair (the presence `M / r^2`
  and the age moment `M / r` from the same rays, `k_s r^2 = 41.5`, `k_a r
  = 36.1`), the equivalence principle (`push_m = m x push_1`), Gauss's
  law exact, and every registered world with `suspension` 0 bit for bit.

## 2. Why the meeting does not reach the pins, and what the pins ask of a rule

Section 5.4: under `meeting-v1` a paid row turns toward the crowd's `-V`
by one step of the direction table per N crowd units met and its phase
gains the crowd met. Two things are missing. (i) The constant: the turn's
size is a grain of the fan (`delta_theta / N`), not tied to the number a
clock reads at the same Node, so the bending has the form `M / b` and no
factor. (ii) The delay: the phase reads the flow, `M / r^2` along the
path, `1 / b`, where nature's delay reads the potential, `M / r`, a
logarithm; and the flight table is one speed, so no delay in time at all.

What the pins ask is one number and one quantity. The number: the clock
at a Node reads `k_a = (n_s / d_s) A` with A the age moment of the crowd
there and `[n_s, d_s]` the world's suspension pair (5.2), and section 5.2
identifies `k_a` with `G M / (r c^2)` at first order. The bending
`4 G M / (b c^2)` is `4 k_a(b)`, the Shapiro delay `(2 / c) integral of k_a
dl`, both in the clock's own k. So the rule must read the SAME age moment
the clock reads, with the SAME pair, and carry ONE more integer, the
factor 2 (the refractive index of the weak field, `n = 1 + 2 k_a`, the
time part and the space part one each). The quantity: a row's pace must
fall as `1 / (1 + 2 k_a)` (the delay reads the potential along the path)
and its direction must turn by the transverse gradient of `2 k_a` (the
bending is Fermat's on that index); and 5.1 gives the gradient without a
neighbour: the flow the push reads is `V = -(Q c / dwell) grad(A)`, so
the transverse flow at the row's own Node IS the transverse gradient of
the age moment.

## 3. The rule, `optical-v1`

**The state read at the Node** (the crowd of LOCALITY-1, fixed K): the
moments of the rows present at the row's own Node, excluding the rows of
the row's own number (the emitter's number, a field of the record, the
same class the collision and the click use; a comparison of numbers, not
a branch on a name): the age moment A (order 0 of the age) and the flow
**V** (order 1), the two readings the bodies' clock and push already take
(5.1, 5.2, 3.3). Nothing at the Node: the moments are readings of the
rows present, recomputed per interval.

**Verb 1, the wall of the flight reads the age moment** (the translation
whose wall is a count, F's feedback block, as the growing wall of 15.2):

    the flight's accumulator: acc += 2 S_1 Q d_s per interval (the rate, unchanged up to the pair's scale);
    the wall:                 2 T_D (d_s + 2 n_s A)                       (integers; the carry, one Link, as today).

The pace is `c_0 / (1 + 2 k_a)` in Nodes per interval with `k_a = n_s A /
d_s`: the refractive index `1 + 2 k_a`. Under form B the body's drive
carries `T_D` as its wall (15.5), so the same rule scales a body's drive
by the same factor: a moving body slows in the potential in Nodes per
interval as a row does, at the order of its speed (section 7).

**Verb 2, the direction turns by the transverse flow** (two translations
and two comparisons, the drive's deficit form; the arc step a
permutation on the fan, the meeting's `pi_t`):

    per transverse axis a (the two axes not along the row's direction D):
        w_a += 2 n_s T_D V_a                     per interval (the turn accumulator; V_a the flow's component in label units);
        if abs(w_a) >= d_s Q^2 THETA:            the direction label steps to the fan's neighbour toward sign(w_a) on axis a,
                                                 w_a -= sign(w_a) d_s Q^2 THETA   (the remainder kept),

with THETA the fan's step at the row's direction as a declared integer at
load (the angle between D and its neighbour on the table, scaled once by
the declared grain, a rounding at load like `T_D` and `u_d`). The turn per
interval is `2 (n_s / d_s)(T_D / Q^2) |V_perp|` radians, and along the
whole path it integrates to `4 k_a(b)` (the arithmetic of section 4). The
accumulator is seeded at birth with the row's wheel value, `w_a = u x d_s
Q^2 THETA // W` (the rung's dither, as the click's), so that a turn
smaller than one step is made by the right fraction of the rows and the
mean over a beam is exact; a single row turns whole steps.

**What the rule does not do.** It changes no row's amount, content,
phase or number (the delay is in the age, the bending in the direction);
it reads no neighbour's record (the moments are the Node's own reading);
it adds no new pair (the clock's `[n_s, d_s]` serves) and one integer,
the factor 2, written into the wall and the turn as the same integer;
at `suspension` 0 it is silent, so every registered world with
`suspension` 0 (series K included) is unchanged bit for bit.

**The three tests, one by one.**

## 5. The second-order redshift: a companion term on the clock, not the rows

A static crowd stretches no stream: a row's arrival rate equals its
release rate whatever its pace along the way (the pace is the same for
every row of the stream), so the redshift is the clocks' (5.2), and the
rows' rule leaves it at `1 + z = 1 + k` exactly. The second order is
reachable by one bilinear term on the owed rate: the wait per
self-creation `w = k' + (3 / 2) k'^2` with `k' = k n_s / d_s`, in integers

    owed = by_drive(acc_owed, 2 k n_s d_s + 3 k^2 n_s^2, 2 d_s^2),

a rate at most bilinear in the state (`k x k`), no root, the remainder
kept. Then the rate `1 / (1 + w) = 1 - k' - k'^2 / 2 + ...` and `1 + z = 1
+ k' + (3 / 2) k'^2`, nature's series to second order, the third order
`(5 / 2) k'^3` left out (the script: at `k' = 1 / 16` the law today
1.0625, with the term 1.0684, nature's 1.0690, the residual `6.9 x 10^-4`
= the third order; at `1 / 1000` the residual `2.5 x 10^-9`). The pin: a
lamp at `k' = 1 / 16` read by a clock at `k' = 0` (a weak-field world;
series E's `k = 2 .. 9` is beyond nature's horizon at `k = 1 / 2`, where
GR has no value and the lattice's rate never reaches 0, 5.2). Under the
same identity or its own; the three tests pass as for the owed count
today (13.2 (b)), the cost one multiply more.

## 5b. Snell's law and Fermat's least time

**The pin from the rule's integers, before the numbers.** The turn per
interval is `2 (n_s / d_s)(T_D / Q^2) |V_perp|` and, by 5.1's `V = -(Q c /
dwell) grad(A)`, `|V_perp|` is the transverse gradient of the age moment,
so along a path the rule is the ray equation `d theta / dl = grad_perp(2
k_a) = grad_perp(n)` at n near 1: Fermat's Euler-Lagrange form. Across a
boundary where n jumps by `n_2 - n_1` a row at the angle `theta_1` to
the normal turns by `Delta theta = -(n_2 - n_1) tan theta_1` (the
gradient's transverse part `(dn / dx) sin theta` integrated over `dx /
cos theta`), which is Snell's `n_1 sin theta_1 = n_2 sin theta_2`
differentiated at `n_1 = 1`: Snell to first order in `n_2 - n_1 = 2 (k_2
- k_1)`, toward the denser crowd, the residual of order `(n_2 - n_1)^2`.
The numbers (the script): at `k_2 = 1 / 256` (`n_2` = 1.0078) and
`theta_1 = 30` degrees Snell gives 29.744 and the rule 29.742; at `k_2 = 1
/ 16` (`n_2` = 1.125) and 30 degrees, 26.388 against 25.865, the
difference 0.5 degrees at `(n_2 - 1)^2 = 1.6 x 10^-2`; at 60 degrees and
`1 / 16`, 50.34 against 47.60. On the fan the turn is whole steps (2.4
degrees at series K's directions) and the wheel's dither makes the mean
over a beam exact: the pin is a beam's centroid past a slab of denser
crowd, not one row. **Reached in form** to first order; the exact Snell
(the `1 / n` in the ray equation, `d theta / dl = grad_perp(ln n)`) would
need the turn's rate divided by the wall's factor `d_s + 2 n_s A`, a
division with the remainder kept (verb 6) that the design leaves out
until a strong crowd asks for it.

**Fermat's least time and the fan's choice of direction.** The ray rule
above is the local form of Fermat's principle (its Euler-Lagrange
equation), so a single row follows the least-time path between the
Nodes it passes, to the fan's grain. The wave form, the least-time path
as the path of stationary phase, is the fan's: at a re-emitter the fan by
angle (TWO_SLITS.md section 7) sends a row on every direction within P
with the angular measure as its weight, and the click reads the exact
phase of each path; the stationary-phase path dominates the click (that
is Young's spacing, section 7 of DERIVATIONS_BEAM.md). It follows from
the fan's choice only if the phase a row gains along its path measures
the TIME of the path, that is if the family's phase advances per
interval (a clock, the slits' declaration `[n, 2^30]` per interval) and
not per Link (the `phase_per_link` form, under which a slowed row gains
the same phase per Link and the stationary path is the shortest, not the
least-time, one). So: Fermat's ray form follows from the rule; Fermat's
wave form follows from the fan and the rule together, for families
whose phase is a clock.
