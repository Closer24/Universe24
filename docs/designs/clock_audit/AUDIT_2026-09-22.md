THE SIX LINES
(1) The information: what moves is a count of clicks between a clock on the GameBoard and a detector: a lamp's births at its declared rate [r, W] (the record's birth ordinal, `record & 0xFFFFFFFF`, and its wheel value u) or a body's self-creations (its `age`, its `counted` at a `become`, the row's `age` on a `click` line), carried by the rows through the Links and Ports to the detector's Node, where the click line keeps the ordinal, the age and the phase; what is lost on the way is nothing of the clock; what the host adds is the line's `tick`, its own ordering, which is not a thing of the law. (2) The generic solution, one primitive for every family alike: a measured duration, period or rate is a ratio of two click counts read at a detector, each count off a clock on the board: the numerator the clock measured (a lamp's ordinals or a body's age), the denominator a second clock, the detector's own (a body's age; for a fixed body with no crowd and `suspension` 0 it equals the tick count exactly, record 569) or a lamp of the reader's own (series S, examples/events/reader_clock: "a lamp of its own so that its births are its own clock"). The tick of a line orders the record and never stands alone as a time. No patch beside it: the same rule reads c (a row's age at a face), a period (the recurrence of a lamp's ordinals), a redshift (ordinals per the reader's own count), a lifetime (a body's `counted` at its `become` per the lab lamp's count) and a Hubble time.
