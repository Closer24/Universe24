## 1. The rule as built against the owner's rule (a)

**PASS (the rule's letter).** Record 128 (docs/LOG_2026-09-20.md:513-515): "a measured event whose clock owes an interval neither releases nor READS in that interval; a message enters a body only at its self-creation; to wait is not to communicate in either direction." Built as one line of step 4, `src/event_universe/events/nature_beam.py:2294-2297`: the rule matrix reads `PASS_RULE` for every family of an entry whose `creating` is False. `creating` is the body's own frame state (`engine.py:404-412`: `owed > 0` pays one, `waited += 1`, `creating = False`, else `creating = True`), set before `nature_beam` runs (`engine.py:350-351`). The release side was already gated by `creating` (`nature_beam.py:3168`), and so is the clock trigger of `become` (`:3181-3190`). The met set is `~own & arrived & (rule != PASS_RULE)` (`:2448`), so under `pass` nothing is admitted: no push, no click, no re-emission, no transformation by a click. Generic: no family, name or number is read; one condition on the reading. Local: the body reads its own record only; the rows at its Node are left in the store and walk by the flight table as at any Node (`:2131-2240`); no register, fixed work per row. Byte-identical under `suspension` 0: `count_owed` returns 0 (`engine.py:114-127`), `creating` is always True, the matrix is unchanged.

**NOTE (the count during a wait is record-only, and the docs present it as part of the rule).** BEAM_LAW note 17 (docs/BEAM_LAW.md:1137-1139), MIGRATION (docs/MIGRATION.md:90-92) and the PR body say "its clock still counts the presence at its Node" as a clause of the rule. In the engine the count taken in a waiting interval feeds nothing physical: `_suspend` runs only under `creating` (`engine.py:363-366`), the `become` crowd gate only under `creating` (`nature_beam.py:3168, 3188`); `entry.presence`/`entry.counted` of a waiting interval (`nature_beam.py:2776-2781`) reach only the frame's record fields and the run's mean presence (`engine.py:986`). So whether the count is "kept" or "silent" during a wait changes no integer of any owed count; the docs should say so (a record convention, not a physical clause), or the count should be reported as 0 in a waiting interval for cleanliness. Under the owner's words the count is not a read: a read is the table's action (push, click, `read` record); the count is the clock's, what makes the wait; nothing enters the body.

**NOTE (what does change the owed count, and it is a consequence, not a choice).** The one physical change of a count is at the self-creation after a wait: the rows that passed unconsumed are still at the Node on a rest slot of their digital line (a heading moves 32 of 55 intervals, `nature_beam.py:2139-2141`; the Hubble headings' table printed by the reviewer) and the clock's count is "every row of another number at the set, rest and moving alike" (`nature_beam.py:2378-2384, 2650-2652`; BEAM_LAW step 5, docs/BEAM_LAW.md:493-497), as it always was for a `read` body. Hence the probe of k = 8 counts 16 after a wait and owes 4 where it owed 2 (`tests/test_nature_beam_clock.py:315-350`). This is the law's existing count applied to rows that are now there; the alternative (count the arrivals only) would be a new choice. Its consequence should be named in note 17 in one more clause: a wait raises the next count by up to the rows resting from the waited interval, so the owed count after a wait is up to twice the steady one, and the wait fraction of a body in a crowd grows (the pushing_scalar detector's self-creation rate 0.2925 to 0.2100 in the PR's own numbers; the pushing_age detector waited 10 by tick 400 under the rule against 8 without it, the reviewer's run). A feedback of the count on itself, bounded by the rest slots of the line, is not a register and not a defect, but the reader of note 17 must be told the waits multiply.

**NOTE (the own number's rows are not under the rule).** `home = own & arrived` (`nature_beam.py:2396-2426`) is taken whether or not the body is creating: a waiting body still takes its own returning rows home and, for a paid family, their labels join its momentum in the waited interval (`:2393-2394`, the rows leaving the store at `:2422`). Whether "a message enters a body only at its self-creation" covers the body's own message is the owner's, and the PR should say the case is left as it was.

## 2. The books, the bijection and the third law

**PASS (books and bijection).** The passed rows stay rows of the store with their own state; nothing is kept at the Node; `books()["balanced"]` holds at every checked tick in (g) and in the reviewer's two runs. The walk is the flight table's (a bijection) for those rows.

**BLOCKING, for the owner's word (the decision's own test is not met, and the residual is not a coincidence on the bound pair).** Record 128 (1) fixes the test: "W1 of record 123 under the signed drive and this rule, the pair's momenta balanced to the integer over 3000 intervals" (docs/LOG_2026-09-20.md:515). Test (g) as re-pinned (`tests/test_step_drive.py:410-424`; docs/TEST_EXPECTATIONS.md:1501-1509) asserts the opposite: the sum -P from tick 1044, -P or -2P after, -P at the end. The mechanism the PR gives is right and the reviewer re-derived it: with rows in flight and no recoil at release, a wait at t removes the read of the partner's release of t - 1 and the release the partner would read at t + 1; balance needs the partner's own wait to remove the matching pair, and a partner's wait exactly one flight later (one interval at one Link) removes an already empty read and one real release, hence -P. But this is not a rare coincidence on a bound pair: the two clocks count each other's releases (the proton counts a crowd proportional to 1840, the neutron to 1837), so their waits fall in the same interval or one apart at every cycle. The reviewer's run of `weak/j3_deuteron_crowd.json` for 500 intervals: on main the pair holds with p = (0, 0, 0) exactly on both bodies after 57 waits each (the paired waits cancel under the old rule: a wait at t and one at t + 1 give +u then 0, the reviewer's derivation); on the branch, 56 waits each, p_x = -1 591 812 747 852 on the proton and -2 150 138 108 800 on the neutron (the sum about -12 P), two `step` records (484, 499), the proton at (8, 10, 10) and the neutron at (11, 10, 10) at tick 500 (three Links apart, so "still at one Link" in the J re-read holds at its end tick, not at every tick), and 501 558 face clicks against 501 035 (the unconsumed rows escape). So on the register's deuteron rule (a) replaces the old rule's imbalance of one push per UNPAIRED wait (record 126's lost wait) with an imbalance of one push per wait pair offset by one interval: a bound pair that was balanced becomes self-propelled (the J re-read's 7 Links in 700). Note 17's sentence "Until this rule ... a bound pair gained one push net per wait" (docs/BEAM_LAW.md:1126-1130) is therefore inaccurate: it gained one push per lost wait. The rule's letter is the owner's; the rule's consequence contradicts the owner's stated test; the reviewer does not fill that gap. Disposition: the owner reads this before the merge; note 17, TEST_EXPECTATIONS (g) and the day's log record must say that the record-128 criterion is not met, why, and that the residual on a bound pair is systematic and one-signed, with the j3 evidence. The structural reason (a free message carries no recoil, so no rule at the waiting body alone closes the pair for every offset) is record 132's section 7 question, not this PR's to design.

**PASS with the above (the deuteron's drift is admissible as the rule's consequence, not a defect of the code).** Every attempted step toward the partner is a hand-over; the drift is the drive of a net momentum the rule lets the pair keep; the books stay exact. Admissible as physics only if the owner accepts a self-propelled bound pair, which is the blocking question.
