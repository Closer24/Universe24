# Light approval, reversible records and click correlations

**Research date:** 7 November 2026. **Candidate base:** `5894510ada12d22f17a70f93a41ec311aef13265`. This study changes no engine, coupling construction, component recipe or Presenter. All preparations use one fixed `+++` tensor and evolve solely through Rule3. This new study remains separate from the released component library.

The investigated idea is that the board continues to evolve representations of competing possibilities, an ordinary signal approves a local outcome, and a click records that approval. The research asks whether this can produce quantum entanglement in the click statistics.

The current evidence supports signal-dependent arithmetic events and prepared common-cause correlations. It does **not** establish quantum entanglement, an autonomous unique-path approval mechanism, or a persistent single-click latch. A precise conditional local calculation supplies a possible write–wait–confirm interface, but its required complete-board input waveform remains unestablished.

## The unchanged model and the meaning of an alternative

At every `(x,g)`, Rule3 updates `(a,b,r)` using the signed sum `u` of the six spatial inputs:

```text
ell, r_next = divmod(2*u + r, 6)
a_next = b
b_next = ell - a
```

A complete initial state and fixed tensor determine one full trajectory. Several routes can be simultaneously represented by patterns in that state; a real internal comparison would also require its own counter/remainder representation and causal interactions. An ensemble of separately prepared simulations is not evidence that one board explores those simulations internally.

An approval signal must be an ordinary disturbance generated by the same closed preparation. A read-only complexity score or click observer cannot select, inject, suppress or erase states. Information absent from one outlet must remain accounted for in the complete reversible state.

## The 84-run causal and statistical audit

The protocol was frozen before new trajectories; its SHA-256 is `3c95321cce84de5bec0da1942f27a32d7d25250b68410c3da979203bad2c3e9d`. This is a recorded research registration, not a third-party timestamp certification.

The board has side `L=97`, background `(0,0,2)`, and horizon `T=16`. Receivers are at `(32,24,48)` and `(32,72,48)`, each connected to a source eight sites away by an initially prepared guide. Their toroidal separation is 48, exceeding the sum of the two radius-16 backward cones. Initial supports cannot reach a periodic boundary within the tested horizon.

Each local setting changes only its receiver's initial remainder pair between `(4,0)` and `(0,4)`. A source phase is one of `(0,1)`, `(1,0)`, `(0,-1)` and `(-1,0)`. These are local preparation settings and designed common initial data, **not** derived physical analyzer orientations or physical photons.

The ensembles are all four shared phases crossed with four setting pairs (16 runs), all sixteen independent phase pairs crossed with four setting pairs (64 runs), and four source-absent controls. Every phase has the declared equal weight independently of settings. There is no random sampling seed and no discarded trial.

For each receiver, the primary outcome is the sign of `ell` at the first guarded C event in steps 1–16, or zero if none occurs. A guarded event requires `ell != 0` and a changed complete local triple. This sign is an external statistic of the retained trajectory, not a demonstrated persistent internal signed record. All 48 receiver channels and subsequent events remain available.

For `E_ab = mean(A*B)` over every trial, the preregistered statistic is `S = abs(E00 + E01 + E10 - E11)`.

| Preparation ensemble | Runs | `E00, E01, E10, E11`, all trials | Full-trial `S` | Both-click conditioned `S` | Joint click probability per setting pair |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Shared source phase | 16 | `1/2, -1/2, -1/2, 1/2` | `1` | `2` | `1/2` |
| Independent source phases | 64 | `1/4, -1/4, -1/4, 1/4` | `1/2` | `2` | `1/4` |
| Sources absent | 4 | `0, 0, 0, 0` | `0` | Undefined: no joint clicks | `0` |

The exact independently recovered local response is `A(x,p)=(-1)^x d(p)` (and the same form for Bob), where `d(p)=1` for phases 0 and 3 and zero for phases 1 and 2. Detected signs are therefore fixed by the local setting. Sharing the source phase correlates detection; it does not introduce a new pattern of conditional signs. The same conditional correlations appear with independent sources. These facts identify an ordinary common-cause explanation of the measured correlation.

The 84 runs retain 1,428 complete states and 1,344 transitions. The independent matrix oracle reproduced every forward and inverse transition, checked the tensor, initial preparations, hashes and all-trial denominators, and recomputed the detector observations. Four null whole boards remained stationary. The 80 clicked receiver instances first clicked at steps 8–11; every one had a later primary event, and none satisfied either strict first-event retention predicate. Local detector traces were unchanged when the remote setting changed, as expected from the causal domain.

## The separately registered two-arrival probe

An additional eight runs test the missing input interface directly. On an `L=101` board through step 32, a receiver initially has remainder pair `(2,4)`. Sources are preloaded at offsets `-8` and `+16` on one prepared guide. The left source has `(a,b)=(0,1)`; the right source has one of two registered phases, `(0,1)` and `(0,-1)`. Each phase family includes both sources, each isolated source and neither source. There is no runtime insertion.

The acceptance predicate demands four consecutive pending states with pair `(4,2)` and zero receiver counters, no C in either isolated-source control, exactly one receiver C-bearing update in the joint preparation within steps 15–19, and a specified confirmed remainder/magnitude record for eight states. This is stronger than merely observing two arrivals.

Both phase families fail (`0/2` accepted). The first-only source writes the intended pending state at step 8, but it lasts only two states; receiver C events begin at step 10 while the second source is absent. Joint runs also have C events before the declared approval window and fail the eight-state confirmed-record criterion. All null whole boards are stationary and all recorded full-state rewinds pass. The probe contains 264 states and 256 transitions; together the studies contain **92 runs, 1,692 states and 1,600 transitions**.

The independent audit reconstructed every secondary initial preparation and reproduced all 256 forward and inverse transitions with its separate matrix oracle, including all 48-channel detector states and six-neighbor input sums. Across both studies, 1,600 transitions were verified in each direction. Two corruption regressions passed, including detection of a tampered frame whose stored hash had also been changed.

The publication candidate passed [all 129 tests](../research/light_approval/evidence/publication_v1/validation.json), with no failures, errors or skips, in a fresh Git checkout after the older pinned evidence was restored. The report identifies the exact tested tree; subsequent changes added only the literature paragraph and validation documentation, with no executable or test changes.

This identifies a concrete missing interface: the scalar threshold calculation is correct, but the complete preparation supplies further inputs and feedback that prevent the declared quiet wait. The two preloaded sources also do not constitute an internally computed unique-path approval, even if their coincidence interface were to pass. The separate [approval record](../research/light_approval/evidence/approval_v1/result.json) retains all failed predicates and actual paired input sums.

## Why an approval filter can imitate a Bell violation

The audit also contains a **separate synthetic mathematical control**, not a Rule3 experiment. Let the uniformly prepared variable be `lambda=(u,v,s)` with `u,v` binary and `s` equal to `+1` or `-1`. Define

```text
A(a, lambda) = s                 if a=u, otherwise 0
B(b, lambda) = s*(-1)^(u*v)      if b=v, otherwise 0
```

These response functions are local. Keeping every attempted trial gives `S=1`. Keeping only trials in which both outputs are nonzero gives `S=4`, although each setting pair has the same joint acceptance rate and the selected local signs have equal marginals. The accepted hidden-variable composition changes with the settings. This is a counterexample to interpreting selected correlations as a valid Bell violation without auditing selection.

Exhaustive independent enumeration additionally checked all 16 binary and 81 ternary deterministic local response tables; their maximum full-trial absolute CHSH value is 2. The enumeration confirms the algebraic proof, rather than replacing its stated assumptions.

## Board time, heralds and observable time

A click counter can remain unchanged while the board executes many steps. During that interval ordinary signals can still travel. If approval communicates information between the two apparatuses before their records are fixed, the local-response premise of a board-separated Bell experiment no longer applies.

This is not a proof against every emergent spacetime interpretation. It identifies the missing physical derivation: calibrate clocks, distances, setting events, record events and signal speeds through fixed observables, then show which events are spacelike in the proposed observed geometry. Hidden board communication must be included in that analysis rather than made invisible by counting only clicks.

There is a constructive reason to investigate this distinction. Toner and Bacon showed that shared randomness and one setting-dependent classical bit can exactly simulate singlet projective-measurement statistics [Toner–Bacon, 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0304076). Many board steps between records could provide communication outside the separated regime tested here. This is neither a Rule3 implementation nor a simulation of all quantum experiments: their construction uses shared real unit-vector directions, an encoded message and specified response computations. Universe24 would have to derive a controlled integer approximation or construction, its reversible information accounting and the operational spacetime map from the permitted dynamics. An externally programmed chooser would not meet that requirement.

A valid preparation herald can select trials independently of the settings. Establishing the herald before fresh independent settings is one sufficient design; it is not the only valid chronology. What matters is the justified independence of the accepted ensemble and the local response assumptions. A both-click or timing-coincidence filter is not automatically such a herald.

Bell nonlocality and quantum entanglement are distinct. Werner's construction demonstrates that some entangled states admit a local hidden-variable description for the considered measurements. Therefore `S <= 2` alone does not exclude all quantum entanglement. Here, however, no independent quantum state, subsystem or measurement correspondence has been derived. Correlation or a single visible record cannot supply that correspondence by itself.

## Decision and next experiments

The next useful target is the local input/record interface. A Bell interpretation should follow a working internal record mechanism and an operational measurement model.

| Question | Deliverable | Computation budget | Success | Failure and continuation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Can the existing compact guided packet write a quiet pending state at a receiver defect? | Nine registered even remainder pairs, each with source-present/absent controls, actual input traces and inverse audit | 18 runs, `L=129`, horizon 24, 60 CPU seconds; complete guide/halo and fixed no-wrap disturbance geometry | Declared changed remainder record, zero receiver counters, no receiver C and eight quiet subsequent updates; unmodified-guide control agrees with packet translation | Record every failed preparation; the translation proof does not cover the defect; proceed only after a pending-record candidate passes |
