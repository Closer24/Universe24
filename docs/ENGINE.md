# The engine

This document describes the engine as the code holds it on `main`, beside [ALGEBRA.md](ALGEBRA.md) (the law) and [HIGHLIGHTS.md](HIGHLIGHTS.md) (the decisions in force). The engine is the law's implementation and nothing else (the owner's decision (c) of 2026-09-29 on #1495): a GameBoard of Nodes, every family's NodeState at every Node, and one interval of acts in the law's order, each a call of Rule3 (`src/event_universe/core/rule3.py`; Rule3, named for the three directions of the vector it works with, the Link's paces: one read coefficient per Port, R_ij = 2 num p_i^2 Q_ij on the Link to that neighbour, the product of the Node's pace squared, p_i = p_0^2 / Gamma from the Node's own content, the composed clock p_0 = Gamma (1 - 1 / Gamma)^c twice (`src/event_universe/core/paces.py`, the law's two functions of the content), and the Link's factor Q_ij, the Link's tension booked squared as one integer per Link in the unit G^2 of the run's Link unit G, read the same from both ends (ALGEBRA.md #the-paces, The clock is the Node's, the tension is the Link's); the six Ports the three directions twice; three levels in time) on whole-board arrays, every neighbour read through a Port (`src/event_universe/core/ports.py`). The engine holds no number, no formula, no family's name and no flag: every physical value comes from the run's files. No act of the interval takes a square root and no act reads a Node to decide anything: the guard reads the whole initial state once at load, as squares. Beside Rule3's acts the loop takes the NodeReaders', from outside the Node as the lay and the receding face are: where the world declares the `draw` the NodeReaders draw inside the run, window after window, and write their click on the GameBoard at one Node (`src/event_universe/credit.py`, `features/click`; HIGHLIGHTS.md at 7ed11fdf, the owner's decision of 2026-10-02, after a click the paths are cancelled on the GameBoard, keeping Rule3); the Node knows nothing of it, and a world without the key runs as before bit for bit. The repository holds the engine, the tools that lay, run, gate and read a world, and the worlds that gate the engine, the two slits and Bell, with the GHZ gate beside Bell's, the same reader over three sides (HIGHLIGHTS.md at 7ed11fdf **One experiment and one gate**); sections 4 to 6 are the input and the output explained with the two slits and Bell as the examples, and section 9 is the recipe for a new experiment as the shipped folders have it.

## 7. How to add a family

A family is a row of the universe file, never a line of the engine: its `name`, its `pair`, its `reads` (the holders it reads by name, each at its weight; `{}` for none) and its `dimension`, or for a held family `held` {`sources`, `level_weight`, `write_weight`, optionally `rest` on the massless row of the content and `act` on the holder of the sign}. ## 9. How to build an experiment

The recipe a reader with the repository alone follows to declare a world, write its blind first, run it and read it, derived from the shipped folders of section 4 (the paper's own experiment, `examples/events/two_slits/`, is the pattern; `examples/events/which_way/`, `examples/events/bell/`, `examples/events/ghz/` and `examples/events/matter_alone/` follow it), from the loader (`src/event_universe/loader/`, `src/event_universe/world_files.py`), the reports (`src/event_universe/reports.py`) and the tools (`tools/`); every key and command below is the code's on `main`. An experiment is one folder under `examples/events/` holding its design (`design.json`), its universe file, its world file with the generator's mode file beside it, its builder (`build_world.py`) and its blind (`expectation.json`); the engine holds no number, so every number of the experiment stands in these files and in nothing else, and the folder is the whole of it.

1. **The universe file**, one JSON object of exactly two keys, `integers` and `families` (`loader/universe.py`, `universe_of`; any other key is refused by name, as everywhere in the files). `integers` holds exactly four, each an integer from 1: `node_clock` (Gamma), `quantum_action` (T), `width` (the bits of the run's integers: at or under 63 every array is the hardware's 64-bit integers, above it Python's, exact and about a hundred times slower) and `link_unit` (G, a power of two, refused otherwise);
