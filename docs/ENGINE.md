### The logical system: the clocks

1. **The great clock and its small clocks.** The system is a board of clocks, each of them a train of gears, and the gears of neighbouring clocks mesh with one another. The board is a lattice of Node-clocks closed on itself, a torus whose three sides the piece file gives. Every Node-clock is the same small clock, differing from another only in its place and in the numbers it holds; it is meshed through one Mesh to each of its six neighbours, along +x, -x, +y, -y, +z and -z, one through each Spoke, and to nothing else (locality). Time belongs to the clock, its count, its beat and the order of its reads; the arithmetic belongs to the gear, every number a notch and every act a sum, a product or Rule3's carry. Space is three dimensions and time is each clock's own count, so the system is three dimensions of space and one of time, and its symmetry is the cube's 24 turns, the only rotations a lattice has (the 48 are those and their 24 mirror images).
2. **Gears, hands, trains and kinds.** A gear is a whole number on a dial: a coarse wheel, the notch, and a fine wheel, the fine tooth, the remainder. A hand is two gears, a Gaussian integer; a train is all the gears of one kind at every Node-clock, real or a Gaussian pair; clocks that meet share one dial and act as a composite clock. A kind is the way the 24 turns exchange and reflect a train's gears; there are five, A1 the scalar, A2 the pseudoscalar (its sign turns under the turns that exchange two axes), E a pair, T1 the vector and T2 the shears, and whatever of nature is a tensor splits into them: a vector is T1, a symmetric tensor of rank 2 on the three axes is A1 + E + T2, and the metric in four dimensions, ten numbers, is the time gear A1, the flows T1 and the spatial block A1 + E + T2, general relativity's ten. Light is A1 and Gaussian.
3. **The tick.** A Node-clock beats only where every neighbour stands at a beat count at least its own (the queue, which keeps the order of the reads). Its dial turns by the clock's rate, and a full turn of the dial is a tick; in the plan's main run every dial stands at the full rate, so every beat is a tick (TENSOR24_PLAN.md, section 11). At each tick every gear takes one step: the same row's gear at each of the six neighbours pulls it through the Mesh between them (the meshing), its spring pulls it back, and the other gears at the same Node-clock drive it by the ratios the 24 allow (the coupling: two readings multiplied through the table of their kinds into a third kind). The step ends in Rule3's carry: where a whole tooth gathers, the gear moves one tooth, the quotient leaving as an event and the remainder staying on the fine tooth. Three carries run in series, the dial's (does the clock tick), the drive's and the gear's.

### The tensor system: the clean tensor

**The dimensions, in one line.** The board is a cube of N Node-clocks, N the product of its three sides, on three axes with six neighbours each and the cube's 24 turns as its symmetry; at each Node-clock the state is a column of 4 G whole numbers, G gear rows in four arrays (a, b, r and rd, each of shape G by N), the Node-clock holding no other number; the act is one multilinear map of degree at most three followed by Rule3's carries, every Node-clock stepping in every round; it takes in a Node-clock's column and its six neighbours' (seven columns) and puts out one column of the same shape.

1. **What makes it tensorial.** Every quantity turns with the great clock under the 24 turns, and the one act commutes with them: turn the input and the output turns the same way.
2. **The five kinds and their sizes.** The kinds are the representations of the 24 (A1, A2, E, T1 and T2 are their standard names; the engine makes each from the six Spokes alone and types no table):
   - **A1:** one number that no turn changes, a scalar (a density, an energy, the time gear's load);
   - **A2:** one number whose sign turns under the turns that exchange two axes, a pseudoscalar (the sign of the axes' permutation);
   - **E:** two numbers, the traceless diagonal part of a symmetric tensor (xx - yy, 2 zz - xx - yy);
   - **T1:** three numbers that turn as a vector (a current, a gradient, a velocity);
   - **T2:** three numbers, the off-diagonal part of a symmetric tensor (xy, yz, zx: the shears), A2 times T1.

   The sizes 1, 1, 2, 3 and 3 give 1 + 1 + 4 + 9 + 9 = 24, the order of the turns. The metric in four dimensions is the time gear A1 (1), the flows T1 (3) and the spatial block A1 + E + T2 (6), 1 + 3 + 6 = 10, a symmetric tensor of rank 2 as in general relativity; light is A1 and Gaussian (2). A gear holds one component of a kind as a whole number, a hand holds each component as a Gaussian integer, and a train is one kind, real, or a Gaussian pair with twice the rows.
3. **The state.** Per gear, four whole numbers: the now slot a, the before slot b, the fine tooth r and the drive's fine tooth rd; the Node-clock holds no number beside its gears' (since G1b of the plan, 2026-10-29: no dial, no beat count, no time and no going), so the state is 4 G integers per Node-clock, and a hand holds no integer of its own, its Gaussian components being rows of its train. Each gear's teeth W are read at the now slot: its rest plus what the clocks' linear read of the gears' now counts lands on its row, with no table and no bound on the read (the metric gears hold the densitized inverse metric, a slot each, by decision 41, F11 of docs/BIG_PICTURE.md 13.2); the clicks' stage (G5, the plan's stage 9) adds one integer per train and Node-clock for the books, its windings (David's page of 05:31, step 4), and one more per train and Node-clock for the take on light's gears, its kept (his page of 06:02, section 7), a count fixed for the run (ALGEBRA.md line 98).

5. **The one act, as tensors.** Every Node-clock steps in every round. It reads, per gear, five readings: the value, the three centred differences along the axes (the gradient, which adds one vector index) and the force. Then:
   - **linear, the meshing:** the force F = L a, with L the Meshes' grips on the six neighbours (each Spoke's grip times the neighbour's now less the Node-clock's own), a sparse operator acting on each row alone and mixing no kinds, with no spring (the spring left the engine at G1);
   - **bilinear, the coupling:** the source J_x = the sum over two readings R_a and R_b of g C[x; a, b] R_a R_b, with C the 24's integer table of the product of two kinds into a third (T1 times T1 into A1 is the dot product, 3 x 3 into 1; into T1 it is the cross product, 3 x 3 into 3) and g the file's strength; there are 125 tables, 43 of them not zero, all made from the 24 alone; J is the only part of the act that is not linear, and it is what couples the trains;
   - **Rule3's two carries in series:** the drive's, q = (J + r_d) div T_d with its remainder kept in the drive's fine tooth; then the gear's, a+ = a + (W (a - b) + s (F + T_k q) + r) div W with W the gear's teeth at the now slot, its remainder kept in the gear's fine tooth: each quotient leaves as the event and each remainder stays. On the force's path T_d = 1 and the drive is J itself.

   Before its carries the whole step is one multilinear map of degree at most three and it commutes with the 24; the carries are its only nonlinearity.
6. **What it puts out.** Its new column, of the same size, so that a state maps to a state of the same shape; no record for the run back: the run back is the step on the mirrored state (the slots swapped, each fine tooth flipped in the teeth it was kept at), mirrored again, exact inside the named domain (the teeth at least 1, every fine tooth within its teeth, no clock falling) and owed whole at G2; the runner writes the record's header and end and, beside it, the ledger (one line per round, Node-clock and train, with the train's now counts at the site); and, from stage 9, the clicks: a hand's whole carry is a click (the drive's carry on the receiving train's channel row, read at the train's winding), written by the runner (stage 10) into the click record that the decoder reads, each reading it takes shown, not shown or not read.
7. **Its properties.** Local: a Node-clock reads itself and its six neighbours, seven columns, and nothing else. Equivariant under the 24: turn the great clock and every train's rows by its kind, and the step is the same. Run back by the step on the mirrored state, exact inside its named domain. Whole numbers alone: the sum, the product and Rule3's carry, with no loop, no if and no float in the step.
