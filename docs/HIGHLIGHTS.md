# 2. Intuitive picture: multidimensional Snake

The whole model, as decided on 2026-09-17, is four definitions and one principle; section 3 states each in full.

1. **Event and ray** (3.3, 3.20). A ray is a straight trajectory between two events and carries information. An event is where information splits, at a Node, by the declared coupling, into at most six rays. There is no third thing. (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)
2. **Detector** (3.19, 5.4). A Node with a bit. On 1 the ray passes, and that pass is the only measurement. On 0 the Node returns the ray untouched, without reading it; the return cancels that ray's share of its event and carries it to the sibling lines. No register, nothing at a distance. (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)
3. **Phase** (3.3). Every ray is a wave ray. Its phase advances at the rest rate of its family, which is its mass; light's rate is zero and it carries its emitter's clock. Interference is steering of content by phase difference, the Born rule as a declared table.
4. **Field** (3.5, 3.28). A ray's field is its own information spreading in ray form in all directions. It makes an event only where it meets something it changes: delay, bending, a changed trajectory, and the field ray returning with the opposite momentum. The computation field, which is gravity, obeys the same rule. (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)

The principle (3.15): every declared invariant is exact, in bounded integers, at every event, because only then can an event be rebuilt from its parts when they return. (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)

Since 2026-09-18 (the law of the bit, section 5.4) the four definitions read with one bit: every ray is a thing (1) or a shadow (0); a mark draws nothing, what arrives was decided at birth (point 14); the Born table is computed from N (point 17); a thing's clock is its content / K (point 19); the field is the thing's shadow set, given with the board, spreading by the Node's mixing (point 24), and its return after a push is a field, not a walk (point 3); nothing is delayed, every ray moves one Link per interval (point 21). Superseded on 2026-09-18 (the evening) by the law of the shadow, section 5.4: there is no bit; every ray is a shadow that spreads from an event by the Node's mixing, an event is a whole quantum at held content, absorbed, held or released again by the family's table, a click is such an absorption, nothing walks a path of its own and nothing returns; the Born table from N, the phase at content / K for every ray and one Link per interval stand. (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: the definitions are one, the event; nothing splits below a whole, nothing waits at a Node, nothing returns, and an event in transit does not turn; section 5.4.)

Think of Universe24 as a kind of multidimensional Snake game with obstacles and interactions. Multiple rays move through the same 3D Node network across compatible property and field channels. They may cross without responding, respond weakly, or interact very strongly according to their selected coupling; crossing or co-location alone is not a collision. Here, weak and strong describe response strength, not automatically the fundamental weak and strong forces. Multidimensional includes independent state channels, not extra spatial dimensions. Obstacles are an analogy for declared interactions, not invented opaque walls or a capacity fix. Ordinary evolution is deterministic; only an actual Detector generates a draw (no draw since 2026-09-18, section 5.4, point 14). The central specification remains the precise definition. (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)

# 3. Foundational postulates

## 3.1 Discrete local space

The adopted board is a connected three-dimensional cubic network. Each local Node connects to six neighboring Nodes through directional Ports and Links. The selected layout is periodic; boundary choices belong to explicit experiment configuration. NodeState is the unit of local state. No fixed private-register decomposition or 36-register layer is required. (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)

## 3.2 Ordinary Node dynamics are local

An ordinary Node operation uses only its own bounded state and information that has causally arrived through its Links. It cannot read another Node's live state or apply an instantaneous distant field, movement or inventory update. There is no exception: the Detector of section 3.19 is a Node like any other, and the number a pair shares travels on the returning ray (section 5.4). Section 3.18, which described a shared quantum-resource exception, was deleted on 2026-09-17. Superseded on 2026-09-18 (the evening) by the law of the shadow, section 5.4: no number travels on a returning ray, since nothing returns; Bell is two events of one field at two places, each born of the local field and the local setting (reading (vii) there). (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)

## 3.3 Ray-form propagation is fundamental

**Two definitions.** The board contains Node states and their properties, not a separate fundamental object called a ray. A ray names the universal form in which those properties propagate through neighboring Nodes. The system has exactly two definitions, event and ray: a ray carries information, and an event is where that information splits; a ray is what was split off from an event. Superseded on 2026-09-18 (the evening) by the law of the shadow, section 5.4: the two definitions are shadow and event; every ray is a shadow, a whole quantum of a family in flight, and an event is a whole quantum at a Node that holds content, from which the shadows spread ("The shadow spreads like a ray from the event"); what a split is as a table is round 8's. (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: one definition, the event; nothing splits below one, and a single event leaves a Node whole in one direction.)

**Interaction and event.** An interaction is a meeting of rays at a Node, decided by the coupling declared between their families, and its result is at most six events, one per Port. An event is a change of trajectory: a new straight line leaving the interaction. A ray is the trajectory of one event between two interactions, reversible in time; a Node that a ray merely crosses hosts no event. From its event, every ray carries the number of steps it has made (for a shadow, retired on 2026-09-18: its return is a field with no step count, section 5.4, point 3). Outside a Detector every trajectory an interaction permits actually happens; alternatives arise only at interactions. Superseded on 2026-09-18 (the evening) by the law of the shadow, section 5.4: no ray is the trajectory of one event and none counts steps, since nothing walks a path of its own; a shadow spreads from its event by the Node's mixing, and an interaction is a whole quantum at held content, absorbed, held or released again by the family's table. (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)

**Phase.** Every ray is a wave ray and carries a phase; a plain ray is a special case of the wave ray, not a second kind. Light is a wave ray with no mass and no charge. Three things change a phase and nothing else: each interval advances it at the rest rate its family declares, which is the ray's mass as a clock, and that rate is zero for light, whose phase does not advance along its own line; an interaction changes it as the declared coupling says; a bound group advances it once per interval it is held. A light ray carries the phase of the clock that emitted it at the moment of emission and delivers it unchanged, so the frequency of light is the rate of its emitter's clock. Since 2026-09-18 the rate is not declared: a thing advances its phase by its content / K per interval, one K for the world, and a shadow has no clock (section 5.4, point 19). Superseded on 2026-09-18 (the evening) by the law of the shadow, section 5.4: the phase of every ray rotates by its content over K, a shadow's included, light's content being its amount ("the phase rotates like a ray"); a release stamps the holder's phase, and a quantum absorbed and released again carries the holder's phase and number (reading (iv) there); the clock of held content is its release, which slows in a potential (reading (iii)). (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)

**Amplitude and interference.** There is no amplitude as a number and no probability field: the phase and the ray's conserved content together are the discrete stand-in for the quantum amplitude, the phase as its angle and the conserved content as its size. Because every trajectory an interaction permits actually happens, the intensity at a place is how much content arrived there, and interference is steering: where rays meet in one layer, the declared coupling reads their phase difference and decides through which Port the shared content leaves, with every invariant exact; nothing is erased. The basic steering coupling is the Born rule stated as a coupling: for a phase difference δ it splits the shared content between the two candidate Ports in the ratio cos²(δ/2) to sin²(δ/2), as a declared table of bounded integer ratios with the remainder owned as section 3.17 requires, so that click intensities follow the Born rule with nothing read by any Detector. It is declared, not derived. Since 2026-09-18 the table is computed from the family's phase width N and declared by no one (section 5.4, point 17). The Detector reads none of this; the probability of a click is already in how much content reached it. Interference of light follows from this alone: two paths of different length reach one place at one time only if their rays were emitted at different times, so they carry different emission phases, and the difference is the emitter's rate times the difference in path length; nothing is accumulated on the way. Superseded on 2026-09-18 (the evening) by the law of the shadow, section 5.4: the shared content leaves by the Node's mixing (point 24), for every ray; a click is the whole quantum the parked parts make where they reach one, one absorption at one Node, and the distribution over many is the amount's, Born by counting (reading (vi) there). (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: "every trajectory actually happens" holds of a bundle, whose shares reach wholes at a crowded Node; a single quantum takes one path, by its momentum, and does not interfere with itself, interference being of many of one number; and "nothing is accumulated on the way" stands again, an event in transit does not turn.)

**Field and matter.** A traveling ray releases a field, and the field is itself made of rays (section 3.5). There is no additional substance called matter (section 3.4); references to rays below refer to this propagation and its interaction patterns. Since 2026-09-18 the field of a thing is its shadow set, given with the board and not released per interval (section 5.4, "A thing does not emit"). Superseded on 2026-09-18 (the evening) by the law of the shadow, section 5.4: matter is content held at a Node, it releases shadows every interval in proportion to its content ("A thing emits"), every ray is a shadow, and nothing is given with the board. (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)

**There is no light and no matter (model owner, 2026-09-18).** There is one kind of content, the ray, and nothing else: an electron, a proton, the light an atom emits and the field a charge releases are the same ray with different declared properties (rate, charge, amount, binding) and one bit. The words light, matter, field, photon and radiation name patterns and roles, never kinds; no rule of the model may read "light" or "matter" as a category. What the bit says, the thing or its shadow, is the only distinction the engine knows (section 5.4, the law of the bit). Superseded on 2026-09-18 (the evening) by the law of the shadow, section 5.4: there is no bit; the one kind of content stands, and the only distinction is between a quantum in flight, a shadow, and content held at a Node, matter (the orchestrator's reading). (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)

## 3.4 Matter is emergent

**Research objective.** Particles, mass and the ordinary appearance of solid matter are intended to emerge as stable patterns of Node properties and their ray-form propagation and interactions. They are not additional fundamental substances. This is a research objective, not a claim that calibrated particle dynamics, mass or stable matter have already been derived. There is no matter in the model at this stage.

**Matter is a bound group.** Matter is the name for rays bound in one Node by a declared binding coupling (a neutron ray and a proton ray held together by the strong binding, an electron ray around them whose trajectory is changed at every step by the field rays the bound pair releases). Binding is the interaction whose result is zero events: the rays stay at the Node and interact again every interval. Mass is the retained energy of a bound group; the group's phase advance is its clock. Superseded on 2026-09-18 (the evening) by the law of the shadow, section 5.4: matter is content held at a Node, the only place events happen, releasing shadows in proportion to its content and absorbing the whole quanta that reach it by its family's table (reading (i) there); whether bound contents exchanging quanta hold together and give the ladder is what round 8 must show. (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)

**Lifetime and unbinding.** A bound group has no lifetime of its own: it lasts as long as the binding interaction repeats at that Node without releasing an event. The binding may be nothing more than a very large output-clock delay (section 3.28) that the bound rays create together, a large mass making the Node very slow, so that the rays do not leave. It is unbound the same way anything else happens on the board: a ray arrives (a high-energy light ray, for instance; there is no photon, only a ray) and the coupling declared for the families present produces events that leave. Nothing else creates or destroys matter. Everything is the same generic ray with the same generic interaction, and everything follows from the generic transactions of the declared couplings; what remains is to close the binding couplings. (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)

**Binding is a periodic orbit of the meeting rule (model owner, 2026-09-17).** A ray never stops; "bound" does not mean "resident", it means "back at the same place in the same state". A bound group is a set of rays whose meetings, under the ordinary coupling table of section 5.2, reproduce the rays that entered them: the outputs leave, walk their Links, meet again, and the meeting gives the same amounts, the same phases modulo the circle and the same headings, so the pattern repeats forever; a pattern whose meeting does not close disperses. There is no binding rule: a bound group is a fixed point of the meeting table over a loop, and the only declared thing is the table. On the cubic lattice the smallest loop is a unit square of four Nodes with rays circulating both ways, each corner meeting every interval two rays that leave through each other's Ports; a group therefore lives on a ring of Nodes, not at one Node, its size is the ring, its clock is the period of the loop, and its mass is its content, exactly section 3.28. This is where the ladder of hypothesis 12 comes from: only the contents and phases that close a loop under the table exist, and experiment A10 counts them. Consequences: the field of a bound group is released by rays in motion, five headings each (section 3.5); the motion of a group as a whole must come from its loop, the corners shifting, not from a register on a Node; the held-ray binding of feature 8 (a rule with delay 1 and no outputs, and its ray_delay wait) is the interim form, which holds any content and therefore has no ladder, and the register-driven motion of feature 8c is the interim form of a group's motion; both are superseded by feature 14, binding as a loop, after features 12, 8c, 2b and 8b land. The two paragraphs above that speak of rays that stay at the Node describe the interim form. Since 2026-09-18 the field of a group is its shadow set, given with the board (section 5.4, "A thing does not emit"), and no ray releases per interval. Superseded on 2026-09-18 (the evening) by the law of the shadow, section 5.4: nothing walks a path of its own, so no ray circulates on a ring; a bound group is contents held at Nodes exchanging whole quanta, and that this gives the ladder of hypothesis 12 is what round 8 must show; the field of a group is the shadows its held content releases every interval ("A thing emits"). (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)

## 3.5 Fields are emergent descriptions

**What a field is.** A field is a local description of ray state, flux or interaction structure. It is not an additional material substance independent of the underlying rays. Every ray is the same ray; a field ray is not a second kind. A ray has a field: the field is the ray's own information spreading in ray form to the Nodes around it, without an event, and the field's presence at a Node is an interaction that makes no event. The field is released in all directions. Since 2026-09-18 (section 5.4) the field of a thing is its shadows, rays of its own family with bit 0 (point 12), given with the board ("A thing does not emit") and spreading by the Node's mixing (point 24); the release in all directions is the form before that date. Superseded on 2026-09-18 (the evening) by the law of the shadow, section 5.4: there is no bit; every ray is a shadow, released every interval by held content in proportion to its content ("A thing emits"), spreading by the Node's mixing, never disappearing, and leaving an open board only at its edge. (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)

**When a field acts.** A field never makes an event unless it meets something it changes; the first event a field is involved in is that meeting, and the returning ray that carries the recoil is split off from it, like every ray from its event. Where a field ray meets a ray whose declared coupling responds, the meeting is an ordinary interaction and its events change that ray's trajectory; everywhere else the field crosses without an event. One of those events is the field ray itself returning reversed: the return is the opposite momentum of the field, carried back along the field ray's line to the ray that released it, which recoils when the return arrives, at finite speed. This is how section 3.14 is satisfied: the recoil is a ray. Until its field meets something, a traveling ray pays nothing for it. Sharpened on 2026-09-18 (the law of the bit, section 5.4): the recoil is the field ray itself turned back, its shadow returning on its own steps with its amount and the opposite of the momentum it gave, not a fresh ray that spreads on; the meeting creates no momentum, and what the ray it met gained, its source loses when the return arrives. Amended the same day (point 3): the return is a field, the shadow turned back with its heading reversed and its momentum inverted, mixing at every Node like any shadow, with no steps of its own; it is absorbed wherever it reaches its owner. Superseded on 2026-09-18 (the evening) by the law of the shadow, section 5.4: there is no return and no home; a shadow that reaches held content is absorbed as a whole quantum, the push being that quantum's momentum with the family's sign times the holder's cross-section, and the third law is the symmetry of the two fields, what escapes to the open edge carrying equal and opposite momenta (reading (ii) there). (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)

**No self-field.** A ray traveling straight never meets its own field: the field is born where the ray is and leaves at the causal speed, ahead of the ray or away from it, and the ray is never faster than its field; no exclusion rule is needed. Only after a change of trajectory can a ray cross field it released earlier, and that is a meeting like any other. A ray's phase per step comes from its family rate alone (section 3.3), not from its own field. Superseded on 2026-09-18 (the evening) by the law of the shadow, section 5.4: no ray travels straight, every ray spreads, and a held content sits in the field it releases; that a content in uniform motion absorbs no net momentum from its own field (inertia) is what round 8 must show. (Under the law of events, 2026-09-19: read as section 5.4's law of events says, there being no ray, thing, shadow or bit, no draw and no return; what this describes is an event, in transit or measured, handled by the Node's one computation, and a Node keeps nothing.)

**The release does not wait for the clock (model owner, 2026-09-17; superseded on 2026-09-18 by section 5.4, "A thing does not emit": there is no release per interval, the field is the standing set of shadows given with the board).** A field release is information, not a departure of matter: every interval, the content resident at a Node says in all six headings that it is there, with the strength its amount gives, and this is booked as a source; the Node's output clock delays only what leaves it as matter, never its field. ## 5.5 Acceptance tests and open decisions

Every supported operation needs declared initial ownership, input, operator, expected output and model time, conserved readouts, edge cases and failure conditions. Accepted principles are not reopened by missing code or tests. Numerical family rules and Detector distributions need explicit closure where still undefined. The once-only encounter requirement needs independent tick traces and implementation evidence. A stated acceptance requirement is not a passing test.

**How tests are written and run (model owner, 2026-09-17).** The engine is generic and its rules are separated, so a test exercises one generic rule in isolation on a minimal board and nothing else: one test module per rule, one per feature of the ray-event model, with the expected integers written down before the first run. No test pins the numbers of an example world, compares two worlds or reproduces a known experiment; those are research runs (section 6), made once, recorded with a fingerprint and a date, and never repeated as tests. A change is checked only against the tests that depend on what it changed, selected by the import graph; the whole suite runs together only when the shared core changes (the Node and its Ports, the order of the cycle, the bounded integers, the phase), and then once, in parallel. A physical milestone, a phenomenon that several rules produce together, is one run of the engine with a fingerprint and a dated record, not a suite. ### Redundancies and inconsistencies, with the one name recommended

1. **presence / scalar / count** — one quantity, three names (`Reading.scalar`, `Measured.presence`, `Readings.presence`; `Readings.count` is `outside`; "count" also names `owed` and the `beam` record). Recommend: `presence` for the scalar everywhere (`reads: "presence"`), `arrived` for `outside`, `owed` for the clock, `clicks` for the beam record.
2. **flow / vector** — `Reading.vector`, `Readings.flow`, "net flow", "label moment". Recommend: `flow` (`reads: "flow"`), and `label_flow` for V.
3. **pointer / amplitude / coherent sum / coherent reading / the wave** — one (X, Y). Recommend: `pointer` (with `amplitude` for the row's `32 x amount` only).
4. **here / rest** — `here` is the reading of rows that did not step (rest rays and dwelling rays); a rest direction is index 0 or 1; `HERE = 0` reuses the rest index as the arrival code. Recommend: `here` for the reading, `rest` for the two directions, a distinct constant `NO_ARRIVAL` for the code.
5. **suspension / owed / the count / the clock's count** — recommend: `suspension` for the key, `owed` for the state and the prose.
6. **label / momentum / push** — the row's label is written as `push` on a detector click and as `momentum` on a face click and a step; `push` should mean a push taken. Recommend: `momentum` on every record line that carries a label; `push` only in `read`/`rerelease`/`home` (what the reader took).
7. **kind / quantum / KINDS** — `kind` is deleted for families; `configuration_validation.KINDS` is the preflight's kind of document. Recommend: rename `KINDS` to `DOCUMENT_KINDS` or drop it (one kind).
8. **heading / direction** — keep `direction` for a ray's field and the table; `heading` only for the six unit vectors (`PORT_HEADINGS`, the collision's slots).
9. **tick / interval** — the code and the records say `tick`; the documents and Highlights say "interval". Recommend: `tick` in every record key and identifier, "interval" as the prose noun defined once in TERMINOLOGY as "the step tick t -> t + 1".
10. **turn / turns / phase_steps** — `FlightTable.turns` (T_d) is a homonym of the clock's `turn`; `Measured.phase_steps` (the cumulative turn) is a homonym of `RayWorld.phase_steps` (N). Recommend: `FlightTable.resolution` for T_d, `Measured.turned` for the cumulative turn, `N` kept as `phase_steps`.
11. **K / clock** — resolved on 2026-09-20: the key `K` is `NatureBeamWorld.K` as declared and resolves to the pair `turn_rate`; the turn is the release at that rate.
12. **width** — the push's `width`, the phase window's "width" (TERMINOLOGY), the detector set's "width" (Highlights), the suspension's "fractional width". Recommend: `width` for the push only; "window" for `phase_window`; "extent" for a set's Nodes; "rate" for `suspension`.
13. **amount / content / held / mass / carried / measured_content** — `amount` is units for a ray and content for a measured event; `mass` on a ray is the emitter's `held`; `carried` is `amount x content`. Recommend: `content` for a measured event's key (in place of `amount`), `emitter_content` for the column `mass`, `carried` documented once as `amount x content`.
14. **units / amount** — `face_units`, `escaped_units`, "in units". Recommend: `amount`.
15. **events / clicks** — `Measured.events` holds the clicks (`run.json` says `clicks`). Recommend: `clicks`.
16. **node / position** — `node` on event lines and in the store, `position` in `state.json`, the world and `Measured`. Recommend: `node` for the triple in every record (TERMINOLOGY: the Node is the location).
17. **model / model_id** — `run.json` writes `model`. Recommend: `model_id`.
18. **measured** — the run's list, the ledger line, the tally by rule and the reader's number on an event line. Recommend: `reader` for the event line's field (its partner `number` -> `emitter`), `taken` for the tally, `measured` for the line and the list.
19. **RULES vs TABLES** — `measured.RULES` (`home, read, measure, rerelease`, the tally keys) is not the rule set (`world.TABLES`: `read, measure, rerelease, pass`). Recommend: `TALLIES`.
20. **reads / reading / Reading / Readings / read** — five near-homonyms. Recommend: `reads` (the component key), `reading` (the detector's law, `beam`/`wave`), `Moments` for the class `Reading`, `BoardDiagnostics` for `Readings`, and the record field `reading` -> `component`.
21. **push_form** — five documents name a function that does not exist; the push is computed inline in `nature_beam` step 4. Recommend: either extract `push_form(...)` as the documents say (one place, the same integers) or correct the five documents to "the push of step 4 in `nature_beam`".
22. **Leftovers of the deleted engines in live names**: `test_repository_hygiene.duplicate_files(ignore_run_duration)` keys on `disturbance_types`; `OLD_KEYS` (23 refused keys) and `EVENTS_KEYS`, `REVERSIBLE_KEYS` are refusal tables (keep, they name the law in the refusal); `ui.py`'s renderer CSP and `.html` branch (audit A9); the `events` package name (kept by the design: a ray is an event in transit); `Sec-Fetch-Site` is an HTTP header, not the deleted noun.
23. **`face:*` as a user detector name** — accepted by the parser, two `face:+x` in the record. Recommend: refuse a detector name in `FACE_NAMES`.
